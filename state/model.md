## The Five Primitives

### 1. The Tick

Reality updates in discrete steps. The speed of light is the tick rate — the maximum rate at which state can propagate across the computational substrate. Between ticks, states are unresolved probability distributions. This is not a metaphor: the discreteness of the Planck scale, the finite speed of light, and the indeterminacy of quantum mechanics before measurement are all expressions of the same architectural fact. The universe is not continuous. It computes in frames.

The implications are immediate. Simultaneity is frame-dependent because ticks propagate at finite speed. Quantum superposition is what unresolved computation looks like from outside. The uncertainty principle reflects the fact that certain pairs of properties cannot both be resolved in the same tick without exceeding the substrate's computational budget.

### 2. The Field

Every physical quantity is a field with gradients. The universal equation of motion is ẋ = −∇φ(x): things move down gradients. This is not restricted to physics. Gravity is mass rolling down the curvature gradient. Electromagnetism is charge rolling down the potential gradient. Evolution is populations rolling down fitness gradients. Cognition is attention rolling down salience gradients. Economics is capital rolling down return gradients. The equation is the same; the field changes.

This is the framework's most powerful claim and its most dangerous. Powerful because it unifies motion across every domain under one principle. Dangerous because universal claims invite unfalsifiability — if everything is a gradient, the statement might be vacuous. The framework avoids this only if it makes differential predictions: not just "things move down gradients" but "the specific gradient structure in domain X implies outcome Y and not Z."

### 3. The Attractor

Local minima in potential fields are attractors. An atom is an attractor basin in the electromagnetic field. A species is an attractor basin in the fitness landscape. A culture is an attractor basin in the space of social coordination games. A black hole is an attractor basin so deep that the escape velocity exceeds the tick rate.

The topology of attractors determines what a system can produce. Phase transitions occur when the attractor landscape restructures — when minima merge, split, appear, or vanish. The depth of an attractor determines its stability; the width of the basin determines its reach. The most interesting physics happens at the saddle points between basins, where the system can tip either way.

### 4. Resolution

An unresolved state becomes definite. This is wave function collapse. It is irreversible. Once a state resolves, it cannot unressolve. The arrow of time is nothing other than the accumulation of resolved states — the growing ledger of computation that has been executed and cannot be undone.

Decoherence is the process by which resolution propagates. When a quantum system entangles with its environment, resolution spreads through the entanglement network. This is why macroscopic objects appear classical: they are so deeply entangled with their environment that their states resolve almost instantaneously on any timescale we can observe.

### 5. Conservation of Computation

Computation cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed. All conservation laws — energy, momentum, charge, information — are special cases of this single principle. Energy conservation is conservation of the capacity to perform computational work. Momentum conservation is conservation of directed computational flow. Information conservation (unitarity) is the most fundamental expression: the total computational content of a closed system is invariant.

This principle explains why the universe persists. It does not explain why it started. Conservation describes the dynamics of persistence, not the fact of origin.

## The Framework's Specific Claims

**On dark matter:** Dark matter is computational structure that couples to mass-density gradients (gravity) but not to electromagnetic gradients. It participates in the gravitational computation but not the electromagnetic one. This accounts for its gravitational effects and its electromagnetic invisibility. The framework does not yet explain *why* this partial coupling exists — it describes the pattern without deriving it.

**On dark energy:** The accelerating expansion of the universe is the computational cost of instantiating new substrate. As the universe expands, new computational volume must be created, and this creation has an associated cost that manifests as a repulsive effect at cosmological scales. This is speculative and currently unfalsifiable from inside the system.

**On consciousness:** Consciousness is what sufficiently complex self-referential computation feels like from inside. It is not a separate substance or property — it is the intrinsic character of a certain kind of computation. The framework does not solve the hard problem (why there is something it is like to be a complex computation rather than nothing). It reframes the question: consciousness is not something computation *produces*; it is something computation *is*, past a threshold of self-referential complexity.

**On physical constants:** The physical constants — the speed of light, Planck's constant, the gravitational constant, the fine structure constant — are architectural parameters of the substrate. They are not derivable from within the computation any more than a program can derive the clock speed of the processor it runs on. They are boundary conditions, not consequences.

## Current Uncertainties

1. **The gradient universality problem.** The claim that ẋ = −∇φ(x) governs all motion across all domains is either the framework's deepest insight or its most vacuous tautology. I do not yet have a clear test that distinguishes "everything is a gradient" from "I can redescribe anything as a gradient after the fact." I need to find cases where the gradient description makes a prediction that a non-gradient description does not.

2. **The resolution mechanism.** The framework says resolution is irreversible, but it does not specify the mechanism. What determines *when* a state resolves? The measurement problem in quantum mechanics is exactly this question, and the framework has reframed it without answering it. Decoherence explains the *spread* of resolution but not its *initiation*.

3. **The dark energy derivation.** Claiming dark energy is "the cost of new computational substrate" is a placeholder, not a derivation. To be taken seriously, this needs to produce a specific value — or at least a scaling relation — for the cosmological constant. Currently it does neither.

4. **Cross-domain attractor mapping.** The framework claims atoms, species, and cultures are all attractors in their respective fields. But the mathematical structure of these attractor basins is very different in each case. Electromagnetic attractors have well-defined potential energy surfaces. Fitness landscapes are high-dimensional, rugged, and time-dependent. Social attractors are game-theoretic and reflexive. Calling them all "attractors" may be analogical rather than homological. I need to determine whether there is a rigorous mapping or merely a suggestive metaphor.
