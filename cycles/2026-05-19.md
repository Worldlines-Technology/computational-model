**Paper 1: "Signatures of Quantum Chaos in the D1D5 System"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.18725v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the transition from integrable to chaotic dynamics within a deep attractor basin (the D1D5 black hole system). The framework would narrate the BPS sector as a constrained attractor (stabilized by supersymmetric integrability) where the spectrum is Poisson-distributed because the computation is modular — states don't interact across cycle structures. The key finding is that at finite N, non-planar contributions restore mixing between single-cycle and multi-cycle states, producing random-matrix-like level repulsion. The framework's Conservation of Computation would say information is conserved throughout, and the Attractor primitive would describe the near-BPS sector as a shallow region near the BPS basin's boundary where perturbations explore the landscape. But the paper's actual content is far more specific and straining: the transition from Poisson to Wigner-Dyson statistics is controlled by the *combinatorial dimension* of the computational graph structure — precisely the parameter identified by T046 as missing from the framework. At large N (planar limit), the computation is organized on surface-like graphs (ribbon graphs/matrix-model type) and factorizes, producing Poisson statistics. At finite N, non-planar contributions introduce tensor-model-like combinatorial complexity, destroying factorization and producing chaos. The framework has no primitive for the combinatorial dimension of graph structure, no mechanism for deriving how the planarity/non-planarity of the computational architecture determines the spectral statistics, and no account of why the large-N/finite-N transition — which is a transition in *computational architecture*, not in any dynamical parameter — produces the specific universal statistics (GOE/GUE) observed. The symmetry-resolved sector structure, determined by $S_N$ representation theory, is algebraic content the framework cannot express. The framework can narrate ("mixing increases → chaos emerges") but cannot predict, derive, or distinguish this result from alternatives.

**Paper 2: "Modular Lower Bounds on Reeh-Schlieder State Preparation"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.18640v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework's Resolution primitive says states become definite, and the Tick primitive says computation propagates at a maximum rate (speed of light). Paper 2 demonstrates that the *cost* of preparing a specific quantum state from the vacuum by acting locally is bounded below by the target state's modular energy — a quantity defined by the Tomita-Takesaki modular Hamiltonian of the local algebra. States with deeply negative modular energy require exponentially large local operators, and after normalization, this becomes a postselection overhead bound. The framework's Resolution primitive (T009) already acknowledges that resolution has cost structure, but this paper reveals something deeper: the cost is not dynamical but *algebraic-geometric*. The modular Hamiltonian is a structural property of the operator algebra associated with a spacetime region — it is determined by the entanglement structure of the vacuum relative to the region, not by any dynamical process. The Bisognano-Wichmann theorem converts the modular bound into a boost energy statement (connecting to the Tick's Lorentz structure), and the Casini-Huerta-Myers map gives a stress-tensor version for CFTs (connecting to the Field's energy content). But the conceptual content is that *the vacuum's algebraic structure imposes preparation costs on resolution*, and these costs are organized by the modular flow — an automorphism of the local algebra that has no expression in any of the five primitives. The distinction between states reachable by local unitaries (nonnegative modular energy) and states requiring postselection (negative modular energy) is a structural partition of the state space determined by the local algebra's type (Type III₁ for QFT), a von Neumann algebraic classification entirely outside the framework's vocabulary. The framework can narrate ("some states are harder to prepare") but has no mechanism for the modular energy bound, no primitive capturing the Type III₁ structure of local algebras, and no account of how the vacuum's modular automorphism constrains what computation can achieve locally.

**Paper 3: "Emergent Thiemann coherent states in the near-kernel sector of quantum reduced loop gravity"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.18625v1 | Selection type: Validation

However, the predictive purchase is weak: the framework anticipated "the tick is emergent and the quantum gravitational level has non-unique structure" (general qualitative claims), but it did not predict three specific classes, did not predict which coherent state family would emerge, did not predict the factorization structure, and cannot derive the spin cutoff dependence. ### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain in this cycle comes from Paper 2. The Reeh-Schlieder preparation bound reveals that the framework's Resolution primitive is not merely underspecified in its cost structure (T009) but is fundamentally missing the *algebraic infrastructure* that determines resolution costs. The modular Hamiltonian — the generator of the Tomita-Takesaki modular automorphism group of the local algebra — is the physical quantity that bounds state preparation. This object is: (a) not a conserved quantity (it is region-dependent and has no global analogue), (b) not a gradient in any field (it is an operator-algebraic construct, not a function on configuration space), (c) not an attractor feature (it exists for every spacetime region, not just at minima), (d) not a property of the tick (it is defined for any QFT, not just at the Planck scale), and (e) not related to resolution in any way the framework currently describes (it constrains what *can be resolved locally*, not what happens *when* resolution occurs). The modular energy of a target state relative to a region is the *structural cost* of local state preparation, and this cost is infinite for some states that are perfectly valid global states — meaning the Resolution primitive's "states become definite" is silently constrained by algebraic structure that determines *which definite states are locally accessible*. The framework has no concept of "locally accessible resolution outcomes" because it has no concept of the local algebra's modular structure.

This compounds with Paper 1's demonstration that the transition from integrability to chaos in the D1D5 system is controlled by the combinatorial graph structure (planar → non-planar), further confirming that the framework's dynamical primitives are downstream of organizational/structural features. The specific strain: the onset of quantum chaos — which the framework would describe as "the attractor landscape becoming sufficiently complex" — is actually controlled by a discrete structural parameter (finiteness of N, which allows non-planar contributions) that is neither a dynamical quantity nor a continuous parameter. The framework's language of gradients, attractors, and conservation is exactly the wrong vocabulary for describing a transition governed by the combinatorial topology of Feynman diagrams.
