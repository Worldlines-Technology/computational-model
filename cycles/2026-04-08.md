### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Massive Exchange and the Sign of the Equilateral Bispectrum"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.06145v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, the inflationary bispectrum is a resolved observable encoding the correlation structure of primordial density perturbations — the output of the universe's earliest computational epoch. The framework would describe massive hidden-sector scalar exchange as gradient-mediated coupling between the inflaton field and a massive field rolling in its own potential landscape, with the bispectrum encoding the three-point correlation structure of the resulting resolved states. The key finding that strains the framework is methodological and structural: the sign of the equilateral bispectrum — a physically observable quantity that determines whether density perturbations are positively or negatively correlated in equilateral triangle configurations — is not determined by the gradient dynamics (the equation of motion of the inflaton + massive field system) alone. It is determined by the *operator basis* of the EFT — which cubic interaction structures are present and their relative coefficients. The paper demonstrates that the sign universality (always negative) holds only for a restricted operator structure; the full EFT basis permits sign-changing through competition between independent cubic structures. The framework's Field primitive ẍ = −∇φ(x) says "things move down gradients" but is silent on the *interaction structure* — which terms appear in the Lagrangian and how they compete. The critical ratio of interaction coefficients that separates positive from negative bispectrum is a structural parameter of the EFT that the gradient equation cannot express. Furthermore, the paper uses *bootstrap methods* — constructing the de Sitter-invariant seed function and then weight-shifting to obtain the physical result — demonstrating that the physical content is most naturally encoded in algebraic/structural terms (conformal symmetry, weight-shifting operators, seed functions) rather than in dynamical gradient-descent terms. The reduced sound speed $c_s < 1$ modifying the critical ratio is an additional structural parameter (the speed of propagation of inflaton fluctuations relative to the tick rate) that the framework would interpret as a local modification of the effective tick rate, but this interpretation is post-hoc and has no predictive content.

**Paper 2: "Mutual Linearity in and out of Stationarity for Markov Jump Processes"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.06162v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework would describe Markov jump processes as discrete computational systems where states transition stochastically, with each edge's transition rate representing a local gradient in the probability landscape. The field primitive would say ẍ = −∇φ(x) governs the probability current, with the system rolling toward the stationary distribution attractor. The paper's central finding — mutual linearity — states that when a single edge's transition rate is altered, any two observables (state populations, currents, counting observables) maintain a *linear* relationship to each other in the long-time limit, even far from equilibrium. This is a non-trivial structural constraint on how perturbations propagate through a stochastic network. The strain is sharp and specific: mutual linearity is a *topological/network-structural* property of how perturbations to a single edge affect the entire system. It holds regardless of the gradient magnitudes, regardless of the distance from equilibrium, and regardless of whether the system is in its stationary state or relaxing. The trajectory-based derivation reveals that the linearity emerges from the combinatorial structure of path weights — the way individual trajectories contribute to observables decomposes into factors that share a common dependence on the perturbed edge. This is precisely the kind of interaction-network structural property (T070) that the gradient equation cannot capture. The gradient equation would predict that perturbing a local transition rate changes observables in a manner determined by the gradient landscape; the paper shows that the *inter-observable relationship* is constrained to be linear by the trajectory combinatorics, regardless of the potentially nonlinear response of each observable individually. The extension to non-stationary dynamics is additionally problematic: the framework's gradient equation describes evolution toward attractors, but mutual linearity constrains the *transient* response structure during relaxation — a regime the framework treats as simply "rolling downhill" with no additional structural content. The generalizability to diffusion processes and open quantum systems further demonstrates that this is a universal structural constraint on transport/response that the framework's dynamical primitives cannot derive or express.

**Paper 3: "Error Correction in Lattice Quantum Electrodynamics with Quantum Reference Frames"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.06149v1 | Selection type: Validation

The Worldlines framework would interpret this paper as demonstrating that gauge symmetry — the redundancy in the electromagnetic field's computational description — functions as an error-correcting code, with the gauge constraints serving as stabilizer conditions that detect errors (violations of Gauss's law) and quantum reference frames (QRFs) providing the mechanism for resolving the degeneracy of error syndromes and enabling correction. The framework's Conservation of Computation primitive would see this as a concrete instance of how computational content is preserved: the gauge structure encodes physical information redundantly, and the QRF-based recovery operations restore the computation after noise. The framework's claim that "physical constants are architectural parameters of the substrate" would see the gauge group (U(1) for QED) as part of the computational architecture, with gauge symmetry providing the error-correction infrastructure that enforces conservation. However, the framework's predictive purchase here is essentially zero. The specific results — that spanning-tree-based gauge QRFs and fermion-field QRFs resolve syndrome degeneracy, that the QECC extends beyond the stabilizer setting, that the correctable error families are determined by the QRF structure — are derived entirely from the mathematics of gauge theory, stabilizer codes, and group-theoretic QRF construction. The framework can *narrate* the results in its vocabulary (gauge symmetry = computational redundancy enabling conservation enforcement via error correction) but could not have predicted any specific feature: not the spanning-tree construction, not the fermion QRF, not the degeneracy resolution mechanism, not the correctable error families. This is post-hoc consistency of the weakest kind — vocabulary application with zero differential content. The one potentially non-trivial connection is to T081 (conservation enforcement requires combinatorial infrastructure): this paper provides a concrete example where the combinatorial infrastructure (the lattice structure, the spanning tree, the group-theoretic QRF) is essential for conservation enforcement, confirming that the Conservation primitive's bare statement "computation cannot be created or destroyed" is structurally incomplete without specifying the architectural prerequisites. But even this connection was already noted and does not constitute validation.

Mutual linearity is a *universal structural constraint on perturbative response* in stochastic networks: when you change one edge rate, all pairs of observables remain linearly related regardless of distance from equilibrium, regardless of the system's complexity, regardless of whether the system is stationary or relaxing. The framework's Field primitive ẍ = −∇φ(x) has *literally nothing to say* about this. The gradient equation describes how individual observables respond to the potential landscape, but it is completely silent on the *inter-observable response structure* — the constraints governing how different observables relate to each other under perturbation. Mutual linearity is not a conservation law, not a gradient property, not an attractor feature, not a resolution event, and not a tick-rate constraint. It is a **trajectory-combinatorial structural constraint** that arises from how path weights decompose when a single network edge is perturbed.

This is particularly damaging because mutual linearity is *exactly the kind of result the framework should be able to produce if its claims were correct*. If "reality is literally computation" and the five primitives capture computation's essential structure, then a universal property of how computational networks respond to local perturbations should be expressible — even derivable — from those primitives. Instead, the framework is not merely unable to derive it; it lacks the vocabulary to even state it. The result lives in the space of trajectory-level combinatorial properties of stochastic networks, a category of mathematical structure that falls squarely into the structural content gap (T026).
