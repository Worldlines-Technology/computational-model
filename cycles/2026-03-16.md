### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: Challenging the Weak Cosmic Censorship with Phantom Fields**
ArXiv ID: 2603.13210v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper probes the robustness of the framework's Attractor primitive in an extreme regime. The framework describes black holes as "attractor basins so deep that the escape velocity exceeds the tick rate" and singularities as points where the substrate's computational capacity is exceeded. Phantom fields — which have negative energy density and violate the dominant energy condition — represent a direct challenge to the gradient framework: they are matter that rolls *up* gradients rather than down them (ẋ = +∇φ rather than −∇φ). If the framework's ẍ = −∇φ(x) is truly universal, phantom fields violate it at the level of the equation's sign. The paper's finding is remarkable: despite deliberately constructing a scenario designed to break cosmic censorship (negative-energy matter that could in principle create naked singularities or negative-mass Schwarzschild geometries), the fully nonlinear numerical evolution shows the phantom field *always disperses*. No naked singularities form. This is deeply interesting for the framework because it suggests that even when the energy condition is violated — even when matter is anti-gravitating — the computational substrate refuses to produce naked singularities. In the framework's language, the attractor landscape is more robust than the field content: the topology of the attractor space (which forbids naked singularities) is preserved even when the gradient dynamics is inverted. However, the framework has no mechanism for explaining *why* this robustness holds. The field primitive says things move down gradients; phantom fields move up gradients; yet the attractor topology (no naked singularities) persists. This strains the framework because the result seems to demonstrate that attractor topology is *independent of* gradient direction — a structural fact the gradient equation cannot capture. The absence of Choptuik critical behavior for the phantom case (qualitatively different dynamics from the positive-energy case) further shows that the gradient sign determines the dynamics but not the censorship, suggesting censorship is enforced by something deeper than gradient dynamics.

**Paper 2: Conformal Vacuum of dS₄ × ℝ with Oppositely Oriented Boundaries**
ArXiv ID: 2603.13145v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper challenges multiple primitives simultaneously. The construction produces a dS₄ quotient spacetime where the past and future boundaries have opposite orientations, creating a lightlike singularity that severs the spacetime's antipodal structure. The framework's Resolution primitive describes the arrow of time as "the accumulation of resolved states" — an irreversible process that requires a consistent temporal orientation. A spacetime with oppositely oriented boundaries represents a configuration where the arrow of resolution *reverses* between past and future infinity, which the framework cannot describe: resolution is defined as irreversible and unidirectional. The lightlike singularity that "severs the antipodes" is a topological intervention in the causal structure that the framework's Tick primitive (finite propagation speed, causal light cones) cannot express — it is a modification of the global topology of computation, not of its local propagation rules. Most strikingly, the vacuum state of this spacetime *simplifies* to a trivial product on antipodal static patches, and this separability extends to its holographic dual (a product of Cardy states). The framework has no account of why topological surgery on the global causal structure should produce *simpler* computational states. The Conservation of Computation primitive would expect that modifying the global structure redistributes computational content but doesn't reduce it; the trivial product structure suggests that topological severance can *factorize* previously entangled computation. The perturbative instability due to a vanishing Hagedorn temperature means this vacuum, despite its simplicity, is not a stable attractor — it is a saddle point that the framework's Attractor primitive would describe as transitional, but the mechanism of instability (Hagedorn temperature going to zero, meaning the density of states grows too rapidly) involves stringy/thermal physics that the framework cannot connect to its primitives. The conformal equivalence to a vacuum on a non-orientable AdS₃ spacetime with an S² bundle introduces non-orientability — a topological property with no expression in any of the five primitives. The framework treats spacetime as the substrate on which computation runs; a non-orientable substrate would mean computation cannot globally define a consistent "handedness," which has implications for the Resolution primitive (is resolution orientation-dependent?) that the framework has never considered.

**Paper 3: The Bianchi IX Attractor in Modified Gravity**
ArXiv ID: 2603.13104v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper provides the strongest validation instance the framework has encountered for its Attractor primitive applied to gravitational dynamics near singularities. The paper proves that in modified gravity theories (Hořava-Lifshitz, λ-R, f(R)) parameterized by v ∈ (1/2, 1), all Bianchi IX solutions converge to an analogue of the Mixmaster attractor — consisting of Kasner states and heteroclinic chains of Bianchi II solutions — and moreover, that in these supercritical theories, *no solutions escape* to locally rotationally symmetric alternatives (unlike in GR where such escape routes exist). This is precisely the framework's prediction in its strongest form: the Attractor primitive claims that "the topology of attractors determines what a system can produce" and that deep attractors capture all nearby trajectories. The Mixmaster attractor in modified gravity is not merely a deep basin — it is the *unique* attractor, with no alternative basins. The framework's language about "phase transitions occurring when the attractor landscape restructures" is validated by the parameterization: as v moves from 1/2 (GR) to v > 1/2 (supercritical), the attractor landscape restructures by eliminating the alternative (LRS) basins, making the Mixmaster attractor globally capturing. The heteroclinic chains connecting Kasner states via Bianchi II transitions are precisely "dynamics at saddle points between basins" — the framework's claimed locus of interesting physics. However, the framework's predictive purchase should be honestly assessed: the framework did not predict the Ringström attractor theorem's extension to modified gravity, did not predict that supercritical theories would have a *simpler* (uniquely capturing) attractor landscape than GR, and did not predict the specific structure of heteroclinic chains. The framework provides a natural vocabulary for describing the result but did not generate it. This is post-hoc consistency, not predictive validation — but it is unusually strong post-hoc consistency because the paper's central theorem (all solutions converge to one attractor, with no alternatives) is the precise mathematical statement the Attractor primitive would make if it could be formalized: deep attractor basins capture all trajectories within their domain, and in the supercritical regime, the domain is the entire solution space. The elimination of escape routes as v increases past 1/2 is also naturally described as attractor basin widening to encompass the full phase space. This is the closest the framework has come to genuine alignment between its qualitative vocabulary and a rigorous mathematical theorem.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most significant strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's phantom field result, and it strikes at the heart of the framework's Field primitive. The framework's universal equation ẍ = −∇φ(x) assigns gradient descent a privileged ontological role: things move down gradients, and this is the fundamental law of motion. Phantom fields have negative kinetic energy, meaning they effectively move *up* the gravitational potential gradient. They are the anti-gradient: ẍ = +∇φ(x). The framework would predict that anti-gradient dynamics should produce fundamentally different attractor topology — if gradient descent produces black holes (deep attractors), anti-gradient ascent should produce naked singularities (the attractor inverted). But the paper finds the opposite: phantom fields *always disperse*. The attractor topology (no naked singularities) is preserved even when the gradient direction is reversed.

This is not a minor mismatch. It suggests that cosmic censorship — the prohibition of naked singularities — is enforced by a structural constraint that is *independent of* the sign of the gradient dynamics. The framework has no such structural constraint. Its entire dynamical content flows from ẍ = −∇φ(x), and if you flip the sign, the framework predicts the dynamics should invert. The fact that they don't means the attractor topology of spacetime is more robust than the gradient equation. Something deeper than gradient dynamics is enforcing the censorship, and the framework is silent about what that could be.

This connects to and sharpens T026 (structural content gap) and T059 (ontological inversion): the structural constraint enforcing censorship operates *above* the gradient dynamics, not below it. The framework has the explanatory hierarchy backwards for this phenomenon: the attractor topology constrains the dynamics, not the other way around.

The secondary strain comes from Paper 2's construction of a spacetime with oppositely oriented boundaries and a factorizing vacuum. The framework's Resolution primitive is built on irreversibility and a single arrow of time. A spacetime where the temporal orientation reverses between boundaries is a configuration the framework literally cannot describe — the arrow of resolution would have to reverse, which the framework declares impossible. The fact that this configuration has a *simpler* vacuum (trivial product) than the standard dS₄ vacuum suggests that the framework's identification of temporal irreversibility with increasing complexity is wrong for this case: reversing the arrow produces simplification, not complication.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T061 — Anti-gradient robustness: attractor topology is independent of gradient sign**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Caridi, Corelli & Pani (2603.13210) demonstrate that phantom scalar fields (negative energy density, violating the dominant energy condition) always disperse in fully nonlinear numerical evolution — no naked singularities, no trapped surfaces, no alternative stationary end states. The framework's Field primitive ẍ = −∇φ(x) predicts that reversing the energy condition (effectively reversing the gradient sign) should qualitatively alter the attractor landscape: if gradient descent produces black holes, gradient ascent should be able to produce their topological inverse (naked singularities). The paper shows this does not happen. Cosmic censorship is preserved even when the gradient is inverted. This means the attractor topology of spacetime is enforced by a constraint *independent of* gradient direction — a structural fact that the Field primitive cannot capture. The framework's explanatory hierarchy (gradient dynamics → attractor structure → observables) is inverted: attractor topology constrains dynamics, not vice versa. This is distinct from T006 (stable excited states not produced by gradient descent, which concerns specific non-minimal states) and T059 (ontological inversion, which concerns the general derivation of dynamics from structure). T061 is sharper: it provides a specific, clean numerical demonstration that the *sign* of the fundamental dynamical equation can be reversed without changing the attractor topology, proving that the topology is not a consequence of the dynamics.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to either (a) identify the structural constraint that enforces censorship independently of the energy condition and incorporate it as a primitive or derived feature (the framework has no candidate for what this constraint is), (b) argue that the phantom field simulation has not explored sufficient parameter space (the paper considers all amplitudes and finds universal dispersion, making this unlikely), or (c) accept that the gradient equation is dynamically correct but topologically uninformative — that it describes how things move but not what configurations are allowed — which would require a separate "topological constraint" primitive that the framework does not have. The result also challenges the framework's dark energy interpretation: if negative energy density always disperses rather than accumulating, the framework's claim that dark energy is "the computational cost of instantiating new substrate" (implicitly requiring persistent negative-pressure energy) needs to account for why cosmological dark energy persists while phantom scalar fields disperse.

**T062 — Orientation reversal and the arrow of resolution**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Kovalsky, Pathak & Ritchie (2603.13145) construct a dS₄ quotient spacetime where the past boundary is oppositely oriented relative to the future boundary, introducing a lightlike singularity that severs the antipodes. The framework's Resolution primitive defines time's arrow as the irreversible accumulation of resolved states. A spacetime with reversed temporal orientation between boundaries is a configuration where the "ledger of resolved states" must reverse its ordering — states that were resolved become unresolved, or the direction of resolution itself flips. The framework declares resolution irreversible, which means it cannot describe this spacetime. The vacuum of this spacetime factorizes into a trivial product on antipodal static patches, and this separability is preserved in the holographic dual (product of Cardy states). This factorization suggests that orientation reversal, far from being pathological, *simplifies* the computational state — the opposite of what the framework's identification of resolution with increasing complexity would predict. The perturbative instability (vanishing Hagedorn temperature) means this simplified state is a saddle point, not an attractor, but the framework has no account of why orientation-reversed vacua should be unstable or what they decay into. This is distinct from T036 (multi-signature computation, which concerns passage through non-Lorentzian signatures) and T053 (Euclidean nucleation, which concerns the absence of time at the origin). T062 concerns a Lorentzian spacetime with a well-defined time direction that *reverses* — not the absence of time, but the reversal of its arrow.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to either (a) extend the Resolution primitive to allow orientation-dependent or orientation-reversible resolution, which would fundamentally change its character (resolution would no longer be universally irreversible), (b) argue that the quotient spacetime is unphysical (but it is a valid solution of Einstein's equations and has a well-defined quantum vacuum and holographic dual), or (c) accept that the framework's identification of the arrow of time with irreversible resolution applies only to simply-connected spacetimes with globally consistent temporal orientation, restricting the primitive's domain of applicability. The conformal equivalence to a non-orientable AdS₃ spacetime raises the additional question of whether computation on a non-orientable substrate is meaningful within the framework, and if so, what "resolution" means when global orientation cannot be defined.
