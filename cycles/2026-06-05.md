**Paper 1: "Smooth horizons from topology change in canonical quantum gravity"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.06404v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper addresses the framework's deepest attractor (black holes) and its most fundamental inter-primitive tension (T135: Conservation vs. Attractor at the horizon). The framework would narrate the firewall paradox as a conflict between Conservation of Computation (unitarity demands information escape) and Attractor boundary integrity (smooth horizon demands no drama at crossing). Chandrasekaran's resolution operates through mechanisms the framework has no vocabulary for at multiple levels. First, the gravitational Hamiltonian acts via a **pair-of-pants topology change** — a cobordism mapping one interior sector to two connected interior sectors — which is an operation on the topology of the computational space itself, not a computation within a fixed topology. The framework has no primitive for topology-changing dynamics (T087 noted topology-induced causal shortcuts; this is far more radical — the topology of the computation's arena changes dynamically during evolution). Second, the resolution relies on **boost edge modes** at the horizon — gravitational degrees of freedom localized at the attractor boundary that are introduced by splitting the Hilbert space across the event horizon. These are neither bulk computation nor boundary architecture but interface degrees of freedom that emerge from the act of decomposition. The framework's substrate/computation distinction cannot accommodate structure that is constituted by the decomposition operation itself. Third, the **crossed product algebra** at the horizon provides the precise mathematical realization of the firewall as a one-sided boost — the covariance structure of this algebra determines whether the horizon is smooth or singular. The framework's Resolution primitive has no concept of algebraic covariance determining physical outcomes. Fourth, the key physical result — that the firewall branch is annihilated by the gravitational constraints while the smooth branch survives — is a selection by **constraint imposition**, not by dynamical evolution. The Dirac observable identification (horizon vacuum measurement = early radiation purity measurement) is a structural identity enforced by the gravitational constraints, not computed by any tick-by-tick process. This directly extends T059 (ontological inversion): the resolution of the framework's most severe inter-primitive tension (T135) is achieved by structural/algebraic/topological means that no primitive can express. The large diffeomorphism identifying the interior Hawking partner algebra with the decoded early radiation algebra is a gauge equivalence — a structural identification, not a dynamical transformation.

**Paper 2: "Breakeven demonstration of quantum low-density parity-check codes"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.06455v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework's Conservation of Computation primitive states computation cannot be created or destroyed. T081 and T116 have established that the *enforcement* of this conservation requires combinatorial infrastructure (LDPC code structure) with its own vulnerability landscape. Paper 2 provides the first experimental demonstration that qLDPC codes — the theoretical objects T081 analyzed — achieve breakeven performance in a physical system: logical qubit lifetimes comparable to or exceeding physical qubit lifetimes. The framework would narrate this as: the substrate's noise threatens Conservation of Computation; the LDPC code structure provides the combinatorial infrastructure to enforce conservation against this noise; breakeven means the infrastructure successfully compensates for the substrate's imperfection. However, the strain is precise and multi-layered. First, the nine different codes demonstrated on a single device without hardware reconfiguration — spanning qLDPC, topological, and concatenated families — demonstrate that the combinatorial infrastructure for conservation enforcement is **not architecturally fixed** but is a software-level choice. The framework treats the substrate as providing architectural constraints; here the same substrate supports categorically different conservation-enforcement architectures. The conservation infrastructure is computation, not architecture — blurring the substrate/computation boundary further (extending T031, T089, T091). Second, the 4-logical-qubits-in-18-physical-qubits encoding rate demonstrates that conservation enforcement has a quantitative **compression ratio** (4/18 ≈ 0.22) that is a structural property of the code, not a consequence of any primitive. The framework's Conservation primitive is silent on compression ratios. Third, the OMG (optical-metastable-ground) architecture for mid-circuit measurement and reset — enabling syndrome extraction without ion transport — demonstrates that the Resolution primitive's physical implementation requires specific hardware engineering that mediates between resolution events and conservation enforcement. The measurement/reset cycle is both a resolution event (extracting syndrome information) and a conservation-enforcement step (correcting errors), meaning the Resolution and Conservation primitives are operationally entangled in a way the framework's independent-primitive architecture cannot express. Fourth, the breakeven threshold itself — the point where encoded qubits outlast physical qubits — is a phase transition in the effectiveness of conservation enforcement that depends on the error model, code structure, decoder algorithm, and hardware fidelity in a multi-parameter landscape the framework has no mechanism to describe.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain comes from Paper 1. The firewall paradox is precisely the quantitative inter-primitive tension T135 (Conservation vs. Attractor at the black hole horizon), and Chandrasekaran's resolution demonstrates that resolving this tension requires:

1. **Topology-changing dynamics as a fundamental operation.** The pair-of-pants interaction is not a perturbation within a fixed computational topology but a change in the topology of the computation itself. The framework has no primitive for this. T087 noted topology-induced causal modifications; Paper 1 goes far beyond this to topology change as a *dynamical process generated by the gravitational Hamiltonian*. The framework's five primitives all assume a fixed computational topology — fields propagate on a fixed space, attractors exist in a fixed landscape, resolution accumulates on a fixed ledger. When the Hamiltonian itself changes the topology, the arena in which the primitives operate is dynamically modified by the computation.

2. **Edge modes as interface degrees of freedom constituted by decomposition.** The boost edge modes at the horizon do not exist in the unsplit Hilbert space — they are created by the act of splitting the bulk Hilbert space across the event horizon. This is a radical challenge to the framework's ontology: physical degrees of freedom that are *constituted by the observer's decomposition choice*, not by the substrate or the computation. The framework has no category for observer-constituted structure (T091 noted the substrate/computation separation is QRF-dependent; Paper 1 makes this concrete at the black hole horizon).

3. **Constraint imposition as the selection mechanism.** The physical resolution of the paradox — annihilation of the firewall branch by the gravitational constraints — is achieved by Dirac constraint quantization, not by dynamical evolution. The framework's Resolution primitive assumes states become definite through a dynamical process. Here, the "wrong" branch (firewall) is eliminated by a structural/algebraic operation (quotienting by constraints), and the "right" branch (smooth horizon) survives because it is a zero mode of the relevant generator. The selection is algebraic, not dynamical. This is the most concrete and physically consequential instance yet of T059 (ontological inversion): the framework's most important physical question (does the firewall exist?) is answered by algebraic structure, not by any primitive.

4. **The operator algebra identification as a large diffeomorphism.** The final result — that the interior Hawking partner algebra and the decoded early radiation algebra are identified by a large diffeomorphism — means the resolution of the information paradox is a *gauge equivalence*: two apparently different physical subsystems are revealed to be the same Dirac observable. The framework's Conservation primitive would say "information is preserved," which is correct but vacuous — the *mechanism* of preservation is a gauge identification enforced by constraints, which is structural content the Conservation primitive cannot express.

The compound strain is: the framework's most important inter-primitive tension (Conservation vs. Attractor at the horizon) is resolved by mechanisms (topology change, edge mode constitution, constraint selection, gauge identification) that are not merely absent from the framework but are categorically different from any primitive — they are structural, algebraic, and topological operations that pre-empt and override dynamical evolution.

**T162 — Topology-changing dynamics: the framework has no primitive for dynamical modification of the computational topology by the Hamiltonian itself**

Opened: this cycle

Description: Chandrasekaran (2606.06404) demonstrates that the gravitational Hamiltonian in JT gravity acts through pair-of-pants topology-changing interactions, mapping a single-interior Hilbert space sector to a connected two-interior sector. The framework's five primitives all assume a fixed computational topology: the Tick propagates within a fixed causal structure, the Field describes gradients on a fixed space, the Attractor describes basins in a fixed landscape, Resolution accumulates on a fixed ledger, and Conservation operates within a fixed Hilbert space. Paper 1 demonstrates that the *fundamental dynamical evolution* — the gravitational Hamiltonian generating time evolution — changes the topology of the computational arena. This is not an exotic or optional feature: topology change is the mechanism that resolves the firewall paradox and produces smooth horizons with unitary Page-curve evolution. Without topology change, the framework's two most important primitives (Conservation and Attractor) are in irreconcilable tension at the black hole horizon (T135).

This is distinct from T087 (topology-induced causal shortcuts, which concerns wormhole topology connecting otherwise disconnected regions in a *fixed* topology) because T162 concerns *dynamical* topology change generated by the Hamiltonian. It is distinct from T057 (meta-computational primitives, which concerns operations that transform theories) because the topology change here is not a transformation between theories but a dynamical process *within* a single theory — the Hamiltonian of JT gravity includes topology-changing terms as part of its standard action. It is distinct from T054 (dynamics from topological BF theory, which concerns deriving a dynamical theory from a non-dynamical starting point) because T162 concerns topology change as an ongoing dynamical process, not as a one-time derivational step. It is distinct from T144 (ensemble-averaged computation, which concerns averaging over topological completions) because T162 concerns a *single* canonical evolution that dynamically accesses multiple topologies through Hamiltonian transitions, not an ensemble average.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) extend the Tick primitive to accommodate topology-changing updates — but a "tick" on a space whose topology changes at each step is no longer a simple sequential update; the Hilbert space dimension itself changes (single interior → two interiors), meaning the computation's state space is dynamically variable; (b) add a primitive governing topology change, specifying when and how the computational topology is modified — but this would require the topology to be a dynamical degree of freedom, introducing a new layer of structure below the existing primitives; (c) argue that topology change is specific to 2D (JT) gravity and does not generalize — but topology change is a generic feature of quantum gravity path integrals, and the pair-of-pants cobordism is the simplest instance of a phenomenon expected to be universal; or (d) accept that the framework's fixed-topology assumption is an approximation that breaks down for quantum gravity, limiting the framework's scope to exclude its most dramatic physical example (black hole interiors). The exponential suppression of individual topology changes, compensated by exponentially many transitions over a Page time, means topology change is non-perturbative — it cannot be treated as a small correction to fixed-topology dynamics.
