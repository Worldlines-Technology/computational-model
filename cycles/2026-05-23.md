### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "How many systems can be dephased before the quantum switch becomes causally definite?"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.22807v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework's Tick primitive assumes a definite sequential causal ordering of computational updates, and T094 already flagged indefinite causal order as a fundamental challenge. This paper intensifies that challenge by quantifying the *robustness* of causal indefiniteness under dephasing (a form of partial resolution/decoherence). The framework would interpret dephasing as partial resolution — intermediate computation becoming more definite — and would expect that sufficient resolution should collapse indefinite causal order into definite causal order. The paper confirms this qualitative expectation: dephasing all systems or all-but-the-future system does collapse causal nonseparability. However, the critical finding is that causal nonseparability can *survive* when any single non-future system remains undephased. This means the indefinite causal order is not a fragile quantum artifact that vanishes with minimal resolution but is robust against extensive dephasing, requiring only a single coherent (unresolved) degree of freedom in a specific structural position (any non-future system) to persist. The framework has no mechanism for expressing *which* system's coherence matters for preserving causal indefiniteness — it treats all resolution events equivalently and has no concept of the structural position of a system within a causal-order superposition. The bipartite/multipartite distinction and the specific role of the "future system" (whose coherence alone is insufficient) demonstrate that causal indefiniteness has a directional/temporal structure that the framework's Tick cannot capture: the future system is distinguished from all others, but not by any dynamical primitive — by its position in the causal structure that is itself in superposition. The QC-QC restriction to "physically motivated" processes means this is not an abstract mathematical curiosity but a constraint on experimentally realizable quantum circuits.

**Paper 2: "Shading A-polynomials via huge representations of $U_q(\mathfrak{su}_N)$"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.22560v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework claims reality is literally computation and identifies physical content with computational processes governed by the five primitives. This paper demonstrates that the physical content of 3D Chern-Simons theory on knot complements is encoded in A-polynomials — algebraic varieties in $SL(N,\mathbb{C})$ character varieties — that are computed not through any dynamical process but through the representation theory of quantum groups $U_q(\mathfrak{su}_N)$, Clebsch-Gordan chord combinatorics, and a double-scaling limit where representation dimensions diverge. The classical A-polynomial $A_a(\ell_b, m_c)$ constraining character variety coordinates is a *constraint surface*, not a dynamical trajectory. The framework's Field primitive (gradient descent on a potential) has no contact with constraint surfaces in character varieties. The quantum A-polynomial $\hat{A}$ annihilating the Jones polynomial (a wavefunction of Chern-Simons theory) is a *difference operator* — an algebraic object whose structure is determined by knot topology and representation theory, not by any of the five primitives. The CG chord formalism, which provides the constructive tool for computing these polynomials, is a combinatorial/algebraic structure (extending T018, T026) with a natural 2D CFT (WZW) interpretation and a knot contact homology interpretation — demonstrating yet another instance of the categorical polymorphism (T021) where the same physical content is accessible through quantum groups, 2D CFT, knot theory, and contact homology simultaneously. The "huge representation" limit ($\hbar w_i \sim m_i$ finite as $\hbar \to 0$, $w_i \to \infty$) is a classical limit in which the quantum computation reduces to algebraic geometry — the A-polynomial is the classical shadow of a quantum algebraic structure. The framework has no primitive for limits that transform quantum computational content into algebraic-geometric constraint surfaces. The spurious roots of augmentation polynomials vs. the true A-polynomial roots demonstrate that the physically correct content requires distinguishing genuine from spurious algebraic structure, a mathematical filtration operation invisible to all primitives.

**Paper 3: "Cosmological Singularities and Quantum Particles"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.22623v1 | Selection type: Validation

The framework's model has developed a distinction between "substrate singular" (all computation fails), "field singular" (one field diverges while others survive), and "structured singular" (singularity with well-defined boundary data). Paper 3's finding that fermionic (Dirac) fields have non-singular solutions through big bang/big crunch, big rip, and big brake singularities — while scalar (Klein-Gordon) fields do not — is directly relevant to the field-specific singularity survival noted in the model since T038/T040/T044. The framework would interpret this as: the computational substrate processes different field types (spinor vs. scalar) with different resilience to curvature singularities, with fermions surviving where bosons fail. This is post-hoc consistent with the model's existing note that "the substrate-field relationship is field-specific, not universal" and strengthens the "field singular" category with a new, independent example (cosmological singularities in addition to the previously recorded electromagnetic fields through naked singularities from Paez, Fiorini & Hernández 2025). However, the framework had no predictive purchase here: it could not have predicted that fermions would survive and bosons would not, nor the specific mechanism (the second-order ODE for spinor basis functions having two regular solutions while the scalar equation does not). The Fock space construction at the singularity — enabling particle interpretation across the singularity — is consistent with the "structured termination" category (T092) but extends it: here the termination is not a terminal boundary but a *passable* one for fermions, meaning the computation continues through a point where curvature diverges. The framework's Resolution primitive has no concept of "passage through a singular point" — it describes states becoming definite, not computations surviving singularities. The spin-statistics dependence of singularity survival is a structural fact (determined by the algebraic structure of the Dirac vs. Klein-Gordon equation) that no primitive can derive. This is weakest-category validation: the framework can narrate the result in its vocabulary but contributed zero predictive content.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's quantification of causal nonseparability robustness. The framework's T094 already acknowledged that indefinite causal order challenges the Tick primitive. Paper 1 makes this challenge quantitatively precise and structurally sharp in a way that reveals a new inadequacy: the framework cannot express the *structural position* of a system within a causally indefinite process, yet this position determines whether causal indefiniteness survives dephasing.

Specifically: the result that any single non-future undephased system preserves causal nonseparability, while keeping only the future system undephased does not, demonstrates that the "future" direction has a distinguished structural role even within causally indefinite processes. The framework's Tick primitive defines one time direction; indefinite causal order superimposes multiple time orderings. Paper 1 shows that within this superposition, the "future" boundary is structurally special — it is the one position where coherence is *insufficient* to maintain causal indefiniteness. This is a temporal asymmetry *within* the causal order superposition, not between alternative orderings. The framework has no primitive for describing asymmetries within superpositions of causal orderings. The Resolution primitive treats decoherence as spreading resolution through entanglement networks, but Paper 1 shows that decoherence's effect on causal structure depends on *which* system decoheres — a position-dependent effect the Resolution primitive treats uniformly. The framework is strained not merely by the existence of indefinite causal order (already flagged) but by the discovery that indefinite causal order has internal structural properties (robustness profile as a function of which subsystem is dephased) that are physically meaningful and experimentally relevant, yet have no expression in any primitive.

Paper 2 strains the framework through a different mechanism: the entire physical content of a quantum field theory (3D Chern-Simons) is encoded in algebraic-geometric objects (A-polynomials in character varieties) computed through representation-theoretic combinatorics (CG chords of quantum groups), with the content being simultaneously accessible through at least four distinct mathematical categories (quantum groups, 2D CFT, knot contact homology, algebraic geometry). This is not merely another instance of the ontological pluralism problem (T021) — it is a demonstration that the *constructive tools* for computing the physical content are representation-theoretic and combinatorial, with no dynamical input from any of the five primitives. The framework cannot even begin to describe what an A-polynomial is, let alone why it contains the physical content of a topological field theory.

**T150 — Positional structure within causal indefiniteness: the framework cannot express which subsystem's coherence maintains causal nonseparability**

Opened: this cycle

Description: Benhaj, Sengupta & Branciard (2605.22807) prove that in bipartite causally nonseparable processes, causal nonseparability survives dephasing of all but one system — provided the surviving coherent system is not the "future" system. If only the future system remains undephased, causal nonseparability collapses. This extends to the multipartite QC-QC setting. The framework's Tick primitive assumes definite causal order (T094 already noted this is challenged by indefinite causal order). T150 is distinct from T094 because it concerns not the *existence* of indefinite causal order but its *internal positional structure* — the fact that different systems within a causally nonseparable process play structurally different roles in maintaining the nonseparability. The "future" system is distinguished from all other systems: its coherence alone is insufficient for causal nonseparability, while any other system's coherence is sufficient. This temporal asymmetry within the causal superposition has no expression in any primitive. The framework's Resolution primitive treats dephasing/decoherence as spreading uniformly through entanglement networks; the paper proves its effect on causal structure is position-dependent. The framework has: (a) no concept of "position within a causal order superposition"; (b) no mechanism for distinguishing the future boundary from other boundaries within a causally indefinite process; (c) no account of why coherence at a non-future position preserves indefiniteness while coherence at the future position does not. The multipartite QC-QC extension means this is not a bipartite curiosity but a general structural feature of physically motivated causally indefinite circuits.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) extend the Tick/Resolution primitives to include positional structure within causal order superpositions, specifying how each subsystem's role in maintaining causal indefiniteness depends on its structural position — this requires importing the process matrix formalism's internal structure, which distinguishes past/future/intermediate systems categorically; (b) derive the asymmetry between future and non-future systems from some combination of the five primitives — but the distinction arises from the structure of the process matrix (the future system's role in defining the output of the process vs. intermediate systems' role in the causal structure), which is categorical/algebraic, not dynamical; (c) add a primitive governing the internal structure of causally indefinite processes, specifying the robustness profile of causal nonseparability as a function of subsystem dephasing — but this would require a theory of causal order as a quantum degree of freedom with its own internal structure, fundamentally different from the Tick's assumption of definite sequential ordering. The practical consequence is sharp: indefinite causal order provides computational advantages (T094 already noted), and Paper 1 shows these advantages are robust against extensive decoherence, meaning they are relevant for realistic (noisy) quantum technologies. The framework cannot predict or characterize this robustness.
