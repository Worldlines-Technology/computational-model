### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Quantum theory based on real numbers cannot be experimentally falsified"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.19208v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper strikes at the framework's core ontological claim that "reality is literally computation" by demonstrating that the *number field* over which the computation is defined — complex vs. real — is experimentally underdetermined. The framework implicitly assumes complex quantum mechanics (the standard formalism), and its primitives (Tick, Resolution, Conservation/unitarity) are formulated in terms of complex Hilbert spaces. The paper proves that every finite network correlation achievable in complex QT is also achievable in real QT with identical locality structure, provided source independence is imposed operationally rather than mathematically. This means the framework's computational substrate could equally well be running real-number computation or complex-number computation, and no experiment can distinguish these. The framework claims physical constants are "architectural parameters of the substrate" — but the number field is arguably more fundamental than any constant, and it is now shown to be empirically inaccessible. The critical distinction the paper draws — between product-state independence (a mathematical constraint on the Hilbert space structure) and operational independence (defined by absence of observable correlations) — reveals that the *same observed correlations* can arise from "markedly different pictures of the correlation structure underlying the same observed world." This is a new and sharp instance of the ontological pluralism problem (T021): not just different mathematical categories encoding the same physics, but different number fields supporting the same operational predictions. The framework has no primitive or mechanism for addressing which number field the computation uses, and the paper proves this question is empirically unanswerable.

**Paper 2: "Reduction of Triadic Interactions Suppresses Intermittency and Anomalous Dissipation in Turbulence"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.19180v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper demonstrates that the defining statistical features of three-dimensional turbulence — anomalous dissipation, intermittency, multifractal scaling — are not consequences of the Navier-Stokes gradient dynamics alone but require the *full combinatorial richness* of the triadic interaction network. When the network is decimated (fractally or homogeneously), the system retains the same gradient equation (Navier-Stokes) but loses its anomalous character: structure-function exponents collapse to dimensional (Kolmogorov K41) values, the multifractal spectrum contracts, and anomalous dissipation vanishes. The framework's Field primitive ẍ = −∇φ(x) describes gradient dynamics but says nothing about the combinatorial structure of the nonlinear interactions. Yet this paper proves that the gradient dynamics is *insufficient* to produce the characteristic physics — the combinatorial network topology of triadic couplings is what generates intermittency and anomalous dissipation. This is a devastating empirical demonstration of a point the framework has been accumulating evidence for theoretically (T018, T026, T046): the *organizational structure* of how computational modes couple to each other — which modes talk to which, the density and topology of the interaction graph — determines the qualitative physics, and the gradient equation alone does not. The framework cannot distinguish between full Navier-Stokes and decimated Navier-Stokes because both obey ẍ = −∇φ(x); the difference is entirely in the combinatorial structure of the coupling network, which is invisible to all five primitives.

**Paper 3: "Black hole superradiance in Poincaré gauge theory"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.19140v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper examines whether the framework's description of black holes as deep attractor basins with energy extraction via superradiance is modified when spacetime geometry includes torsion (Poincaré gauge theory). The key result — that torsion enables Dirac fermions to extract energy from rotating black holes via chiral asymmetry while preserving the Pauli exclusion principle — is consistent with but not predicted by the framework. The framework says black holes are attractor basins where "escape velocity exceeds the tick rate," and superradiance is a known mechanism for energy extraction from rotating attractors. The novel element (torsion enabling fermionic superradiance through chiral asymmetry) maps onto the framework's language: torsion modifies the geometric structure of the attractor, and the chiral asymmetry provides a new channel for computational flow out of the basin. However, the framework had no predictive purchase here — it did not predict that torsion would enable fermionic superradiance, did not predict the chiral mechanism, and cannot derive any of the specific conditions (the modified Teukolsky equation, the chiral coupling structure). The framework's description of the result is pure post-hoc translation into its vocabulary. The result is weakly consistent with the Attractor primitive (deep attractors have rich internal structure, as noted in the 2026-03-07 update on vortex core excitations), but the specific mechanism (torsion → chiral asymmetry → fermionic superradiance) involves geometric structure (torsion, Poincaré gauge symmetry) that the framework treats as emergent or secondary. The paper actually reinforces the structural content gap (T026): the physical mechanism depends on the gauge structure of spacetime geometry, not on any dynamical primitive.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 2 (turbulence decimation). The framework's Field primitive is ẍ = −∇φ(x) — the claim that motion is universally gradient descent. Paper 2 demonstrates, via direct numerical simulation, that *the same gradient equation* (Navier-Stokes) produces qualitatively different physics depending on the combinatorial topology of the triadic interaction network. Full triadic coupling → intermittency, anomalous dissipation, multifractal scaling. Decimated triadic coupling → Kolmogorov K41 scaling, no anomalous dissipation, regularized velocity fields. The gradient is identical in both cases. What changes is the *wiring diagram* of which Fourier modes interact with which. The framework has literally nothing to say about this distinction. None of the five primitives can express, represent, or constrain the triadic interaction network. The result is not merely that the framework is incomplete (a gap to be filled) — it is that the framework's *central dynamical claim* (gradient dynamics is the universal equation of motion) is demonstrated to be insufficient for one of the most important phenomena in classical physics. The gradient determines the *mean-field* behavior (K41 scaling), but the physically important phenomena (intermittency, anomalous dissipation) arise from the combinatorial richness that the gradient equation carries implicitly but the framework cannot express. This converts the structural content gap (T026) from an abstract philosophical concern to a concrete, experimentally demonstrated failure: the framework cannot distinguish between two versions of the same physical system that differ only in their interaction network topology and produce qualitatively different physics.

Paper 1 adds a different but equally fundamental strain: the number field of the computation is empirically underdetermined. The framework says "reality is literally computation" but cannot specify — and now provably *cannot determine through experiment* — whether the computation uses real or complex numbers. This is not a gap in our knowledge that could be closed with better experiments; it is a proven impossibility. The framework's implicit use of complex quantum mechanics is a choice, not a fact, and the alternative choice (real quantum mechanics) produces identical operational predictions. The framework's claim that reality *is* computation requires the computation to have a definite structure, but the number field — arguably the most fundamental structural choice in defining a computation — is shown to be empirically vacuous.

Description: Hoffreumon & Woods (2603.19208) prove that real quantum theory (RQT) and complex quantum theory (QT) produce identical correlations in all finite network experiments when source independence is defined operationally. The framework claims "reality is literally computation," which requires the computation to have a definite mathematical structure. This choice is now proven experimentally inaccessible — not merely unknown, but unknowable from within. The framework faces: (a) if reality is literally computation, the computation has a definite number field, but we can never know which — making the framework's core ontological claim unfalsifiable at its most basic structural level; (b) if the number field doesn't matter (operational equivalence implies ontological equivalence), then "reality is computation" is weaker than claimed because the computation's structure is not unique even at the number-field level; (c) the distinction between product-state independence and operational independence reveals that the *same operational facts* support "markedly different pictures of the correlation structure," meaning the framework's claim that reality has a definite computational structure is challenged not by alternative mathematical categories (T021) but by alternative instantiations within the *same* mathematical category (quantum theory) using different number fields. This is sharper than T010 (duality, where different computations give the same physics) because here the two "computations" are not dual in any mathematical sense — they are genuinely different theories (different Hilbert space structures, different tomographic properties, different entanglement structures) that happen to be operationally indistinguishable.
Why unresolved: The framework has no mechanism for selecting between ℝ and ℂ, and the paper proves no experiment can select between them. The framework could (a) declare the number field an architectural parameter alongside c, ℏ, G, and α — but unlike those constants, this parameter has no measurable consequences, making it a purely metaphysical commitment; (b) adopt operational equivalence as ontological equivalence and accept that the computation's structure is underdetermined — but this weakens "reality is literally computation" to "reality is literally a class of operationally equivalent computations," which is significantly less specific; (c) argue that internal consistency (local tomography, which RQT lacks) selects ℂ — but the paper proves this theoretical preference has no experimental consequence, making the selection criterion aesthetic rather than physical. The paper also notes that RQT and QT support "markedly different pictures of the correlation structure underlying the same observed world," meaning the framework's Resolution primitive (which produces definite outcomes from superpositions) operates identically in both theories while the underlying correlation structure differs — the operational content of resolution is preserved while its structural substrate is underdetermined.

**T070 — Combinatorial interaction-network topology determines qualitative physics: the gradient is necessary but not sufficient**
Opened: this cycle
Description: Kankaria et al. (2603.19180) demonstrate via DNS that decimating the triadic interaction network of 3D Navier-Stokes — while preserving the equation's form — systematically suppresses intermittency and eliminates anomalous dissipation. Structure-function exponents collapse to K41 dimensional values, the multifractal spectrum contracts, and the velocity field regularizes. The gradient dynamics (Navier-Stokes equation) is identical in full and decimated systems; what differs is the *combinatorial density and topology* of Fourier-mode couplings. The framework's Field primitive ẍ = −∇φ(x) captures the gradient dynamics but is completely blind to the interaction-network topology. This is distinct from T046 (combinatorial dimension in matrix vs. tensor models, which is a mathematical physics result about large-N limits) because T070 concerns a concrete physical system (turbulence) with direct numerical evidence. It is distinct from T018 (algebra of computation, which concerns the organizational structure of computational operations) because T070 concerns not how operations compose but which *subsets of modes are permitted to interact* — the topology of the coupling graph, not the algebra of the coupling. It is distinct from T029 (anomalous transport universality classes) because T029 concerns fluctuation statistics during transport, while T070 concerns the *existence* of anomalous scaling as a function of interaction-network completeness. The specific finding that anomalous dissipation vanishes means the K41 theory (which follows from dimensional analysis of the gradient equation alone) is *correct* when the interaction network is sparse — the gradient equation is sufficient in the decimated case. The deviation from K41 (intermittency, anomalous dissipation) arises *solely* from the combinatorial richness of the full interaction network. The gradient equation is necessary but not sufficient for the qualitative physics of turbulence.
Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) extend the Field primitive to include the interaction-network topology as a structural parameter alongside the gradient — effectively specifying not just ẍ = −∇φ(x) but also "which modes couple to which" — which would require the primitive to become a network-theoretic object rather than a differential equation; (b) derive the interaction-network structure from the existing primitives, which is impossible since the gradient equation does not specify it (the gradient is the same for full and decimated systems); or (c) add a new primitive governing the combinatorial structure of mode couplings, which would be a major structural change. The continuous dependence of intermittency suppression on decimation fraction means this is not a threshold effect but a graded phenomenon: the qualitative physics varies continuously with network density, and the framework has no continuous parameter to track this variation. The result also has implications for the framework's dark energy claim: if the "computational cost of new substrate" interpretation is correct, the rate of computational expansion should depend on the interaction-network structure, not just the gradient dynamics — but the framework cannot express this dependence.
