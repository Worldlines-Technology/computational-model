### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "The formation of a weak null singularity in the interior of generic rotating black holes"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.04877v1 | Selection type: Strain

This paper proves that the interior of generic rotating (Kerr) black holes develops a weak null singularity at the Cauchy horizon: the spacetime metric is continuously extendible ($C^0$) across it but not Lipschitz extendible. This is the definitive resolution of Strong Cosmic Censorship for Kerr interiors, establishing the precise regularity class at which deterministic evolution fails. The Worldlines framework describes black holes as "attractor basins so deep that the escape velocity exceeds the tick rate" and describes resolution as states becoming "definite." This paper demonstrates that the physical content of the black hole interior — whether deterministic computation survives past the Cauchy horizon — depends entirely on a regularity distinction ($C^0$ vs. Lipschitz) that the Resolution primitive cannot express. The metric is continuous (you can extend through) but not Lipschitz (the Einstein equations lose uniqueness). The framework says "resolved states are definite," but "definite" at what regularity? If $C^0$-definite, the computation continues past the Cauchy horizon into a region of non-uniqueness (multiple possible futures). If Lipschitz-definite, the computation terminates at the Cauchy horizon. The framework's binary resolution (unresolved/definite) collapses this physically decisive spectrum into a single undifferentiated concept. The strain is sharpened by the proof method: the result depends on showing that the dynamical (nonlinear) Teukolsky field is well-approximated by the linear Teukolsky field, whose instability was previously proven. This means the attractor's interior structure (T067) is determined by a *stability argument* — the linear approximation controls the nonlinear dynamics — which is a structural/analytical fact about the PDE, not a consequence of any dynamical primitive. The framework's Attractor primitive describes the black hole's depth and basin width but has no mechanism for deriving the regularity of its interior boundary, which is determined by the interplay of blue-shift instability and Price law decay — quantities the gradient equation cannot access.

**Paper 2: "Boltzmann-Loschmidt dispute reloaded quantum 150 years later"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.04879v1 | Selection type: Strain

This paper demonstrates analytically and numerically that quantum chaos diffusion (cold atoms in a kicked harmonic trap) can be time-reversed with up to 100% efficiency, in sharp contrast to classical evolution where exponentially small perturbations destroy time reversibility. The framework's Resolution primitive declares that "once a state resolves, it cannot unresolve" and that "the arrow of time is nothing other than the accumulation of resolved states." This paper strains the framework in two directions simultaneously. First, quantum evolution is perfectly time-reversible even in a chaotic regime — the unresolved quantum computation can be run backwards with complete fidelity, meaning that prior to resolution, there is no arrow of time whatsoever. The framework already acknowledges that "between ticks, states are unresolved probability distributions," but the paper makes the implication explicit and quantitative: the quantum system retains *complete* reversibility that classical chaos destroys. Second, and more critically, the paper demonstrates that the classical arrow of time in chaotic systems arises from sensitivity to exponentially small perturbations — precisely the Precision-Induced Irreversibility mechanism identified in T075 (non-normality + finite precision + amplification). The quantum system avoids this because quantum evolution is unitary (norm-preserving, thus "normal" in the operator-algebraic sense) and discrete (quantized phase space with minimum cell size ℏ^d), which provides a natural precision floor. The framework's claim that the arrow of time comes from Resolution is contradicted: the classical arrow comes from precision loss in chaotic dynamics (PIR), the quantum system has no arrow at all prior to measurement, and the transition between them is governed by the Ehrenfest time scale — a quantity the framework has no primitive to express. The paper effectively provides the experimental proposal for testing PIR (T075) by contrasting quantum and classical reversibility in the same dynamical system.

**Paper 3: "Canonical Uncertainty Relations for Madelung Variables in Curved Spacetime"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.04784v1 | Selection type: Validation

This paper derives uncertainty relations for the hydrodynamic (Madelung) variables of quantum fields in curved spacetime, showing that gravitational fields modulate quantum fluctuations through the lapse function and spatial metric. The framework would interpret this as: the Tick primitive (setting the maximum propagation rate, here encoded in the lapse function N) and the Field primitive (curvature gradients, encoded in γ_{ij}) jointly determine the resolution constraints (uncertainty relations) for quantum fields. Specifically, the lapse function — which the framework would identify with the local tick rate (proper time progression relative to coordinate time, modulated by gravity) — appears directly in the uncertainty bounds, consistent with the framework's claim that the tick rate and resolution are connected. However, this is purely post-hoc consistency with zero differential predictive content. The Madelung representation is a standard reformulation of quantum mechanics; the canonical commutation relations in curved spacetime follow from standard QFT on curved backgrounds; the appearance of N and γ_{ij} in the uncertainty relations is a mathematical consequence of the ADM decomposition, not a prediction of any framework. The framework could narrate the result ("gravity modulates the computational substrate's resolution capacity") but could not have predicted the specific form of the uncertainty relations, could not have distinguished this result from its non-occurrence, and adds nothing beyond vocabulary to the derivation. The claim of relevance to "scalar field dark matter models" is consistent with the framework's description of dark matter as structure coupling to gravitational gradients, but again this is shared with all standard dark matter physics and provides no differential validation. This is the weakest category of consistency: the framework's language can be applied after the fact, but the framework's primitives contributed no predictive, constraining, or distinguishing content.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's resolution of Strong Cosmic Censorship for Kerr black holes. The strain is not that the framework contradicts the result — it is that the framework is *constitutively unable to express* the physical content. The decisive physical fact is: the Kerr Cauchy horizon is $C^0$-extendible but not Lipschitz-extendible. This means deterministic evolution (the ability of the computation to uniquely determine its own future) depends on a regularity threshold that falls *between* two specific mathematical function classes. The framework's Resolution primitive has exactly one concept — "definite" — where it needs a graded spectrum of regularity classes ($C^0$, Lipschitz, $C^1$, $C^2$, ..., smooth, analytic). The framework literally cannot formulate the question "does computation survive past the Cauchy horizon?" because it has no vocabulary for the regularity of the resolution output.

This is compounded by Paper 2, which demonstrates that the arrow of time — the framework's signature output of the Resolution primitive — has a completely independent source in the classical domain (sensitivity to exponentially small perturbations in chaotic systems, i.e., PIR) and is *entirely absent* in the quantum domain prior to measurement. The framework attributes the arrow exclusively to "accumulation of resolved states," but Paper 2 shows: (a) quantum systems have no arrow prior to resolution, confirming that the pre-resolution state is perfectly reversible, but (b) classical systems develop an arrow from precision loss, not from resolution. The framework's single-source account of irreversibility is falsified as an exhaustive description. The quantum-classical contrast — same dynamics, same Hamiltonian, opposite reversibility — is determined by whether the system has a natural precision floor (quantum: yes, ℏ^d phase-space cells; classical: no), which is a structural property the framework cannot express.

Together, the two papers attack the Resolution primitive from opposite ends: Paper 1 shows that resolution's *endpoint* requires regularity distinctions the primitive lacks; Paper 2 shows that resolution's *signature output* (the arrow of time) has an independent source the primitive ignores. The Resolution primitive is simultaneously under-specified (lacks regularity classes) and over-claimed (attributes all irreversibility to itself).
