### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "The free boundary problem in general relativity"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.18128v1 | Selection type: strain

The Worldlines framework treats singularities through the lens of substrate failure (T040, T044, T064, T092) and has accumulated a taxonomy: substrate singular, field-singular, causally screened, and structured termination (singularities with well-defined boundary data). Paper 1 introduces something more radical: the singularity is treated as a *free boundary* where the variational principle itself is unconstrained, and the requirement that the action be stationary *at* the singularity produces specific boundary conditions that select physically admissible cosmologies. The framework's Resolution primitive describes the arrow of time as the accumulation of resolved states, and its origin problem (T002) concerns why computation started. This paper proposes that the initial singularity is not a substrate breakdown or a structured termination but a *variational boundary* — the computation's initial condition is not specified externally but is *selected by the action principle applied to the singularity itself*. The framework has no mechanism for this: it treats the origin as either an unexplained brute fact or a Euclidean instanton (T053), and its Attractor primitive describes stable states but not variational selection at singular boundaries. The specific result — that free-boundary variation excludes Kasner/BKL cosmologies but admits conformally regular FLRW models with reflecting boundary conditions for perturbations — is a concrete physical prediction derived entirely from the variational structure of the theory applied at its own singular boundary. The framework's gradient equation, tick structure, and attractor description are all undefined at the singularity, yet the paper extracts physical predictions from it. The reflecting boundary conditions for perturbations at the bang are especially striking: they constitute a specific prediction about the initial state of the computation that is derived from the action principle's own internal consistency, not from any external specification or substrate parameter. The framework claimed initial conditions are either architectural (substrate-level) or unexplained; this paper derives them from the theory's own variational structure applied at the boundary of its domain of validity.

**Paper 2: "Making complex CFTs real: The two-dimensional Potts model for Q>4 and complex Q"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.18125v1 | Selection type: strain

The Worldlines framework describes phase transitions as attractor landscape restructuring at saddle points (Attractor primitive) and treats conformal field theories as the enhanced symmetry structure at critical points (T129). Paper 2 demonstrates that the Q-state Potts model's first-order transition for Q>4 can be analytically continued to reveal a *pair of complex CFTs* — conformally invariant fixed points that exist at complex coupling constants and complex Q, obtained by the collision and complexification of the critical and tricritical fixed points that were real for Q≤4. The framework has no mechanism for complex-valued attractor basins, complex coupling constants, or complex-valued Q (which is the parameter counting the number of states — fundamentally an integer in the physical theory). The framework's Field primitive ẍ = −∇φ(x) operates on real-valued fields and potentials; complex coupling constants take the dynamics off the real axis into a regime where "gradient descent" has no straightforward interpretation (in the complex plane, gradient descent becomes gradient flow, which has qualitatively different topology). The framework's Attractor primitive describes real-valued potential landscapes with real minima and saddle points; the collision of fixed points at Q=4 and their emergence as a complex conjugate pair is an attractor-landscape bifurcation that occurs in the *complexified* parameter space, with no real-valued realization for Q>4. The conjecture that all conformal data (central charge, critical exponents, structure constants) can be obtained by analytic continuation from Q≤4 demonstrates that the complete physical content of the Q>4 first-order transition regime is encoded in the analytic structure of the complex CFT pair — meaning the physical content of a first-order transition (which the framework describes as a discontinuous landscape restructuring) is the *analytic continuation shadow* of a second-order critical point. This directly challenges the framework's categorical distinction between first-order (discontinuous landscape change) and second-order (continuous landscape change) phase transitions: they are related by analytic continuation through the complexified coupling space, a structural relationship the framework cannot express. This extends T036 (multi-signature computation requiring complex intermediate steps) and the complexified-saddle-point problem (noted in the model update from 2026-03-23) to the statistical mechanics setting: the physical content of a real first-order transition is encoded in complex conformal field theories that exist only at complex couplings.

**Paper 3: "Emergent de Sitter Space and Non-Unitary Tensor Networks from Non-Hermitian Quantum Criticality"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.17983v1 | Selection type: validation

The Worldlines framework claims that spacetime geometry emerges from the computational substrate and that the Tick primitive's propagation structure generates causal structure. Paper 3 demonstrates the emergence of de Sitter spacetime geometry from boundary entanglement data of a non-Hermitian critical fermion chain, realized as a non-unitary continuous MERA (cMERA) tensor network. The framework's qualitative prediction — that geometry emerges from computational structure — is superficially validated: the de Sitter metric, geodesics, and RT surfaces all emerge from the entanglement structure of a boundary quantum system. However, the framework had zero predictive purchase on any specific feature of this construction. The framework did not predict: (a) that de Sitter emergence requires *non-Hermitian* (non-unitary) boundary physics, directly challenging the Conservation primitive's unitarity; (b) that the tensor network architecture involves a timelike-to-null transition in the emergent geodesic structure, with zero-cost links along the de Sitter horizon; (c) that the bond-counting mechanism reproducing the de Sitter RT formula involves a discrete truncation at the timelike-to-null transition; (d) that the logarithmic scaling of non-unitary critical entanglement entropy is the microscopic origin of the de Sitter entropy. The non-unitarity is especially problematic: the framework identifies Conservation of Computation with unitarity as its "most fundamental expression," but de Sitter emergence here *requires* non-unitarity at the boundary. The framework's dark energy claim ("computational cost of new substrate") is not confirmed or disconfirmed by this construction; the de Sitter spacetime emerges from a concrete microscopic mechanism (non-Hermitian critical fermion chain) that has nothing to do with substrate instantiation costs. This is post-hoc consistency at the weakest level: the framework's vocabulary (emergence, computation, geometry) can be applied to narrate the result, but the framework contributed no prediction, no constraint, and no mechanism that distinguishes this construction from alternatives or from the null hypothesis.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain comes from Paper 1's free-boundary variational principle at the cosmological singularity. The framework has spent considerable effort taxonomizing singularities (T040: unified substrate falsified; T044: field-specific resilience; T064: causally screened; T092: structured termination with boundary data). Paper 1 introduces a qualitatively new category that the framework not only lacks but that inverts its explanatory structure: the singularity is not a computational failure, not a structured termination, not a substrate event — it is a *variational boundary where the theory's own action principle selects the initial conditions*. The framework's origin problem (T002) asks why computation started, treating the origin as either an architectural given or an unsolvable mystery. Paper 1 proposes a concrete answer: the origin's character is determined by the variational principle itself, applied with free (unconstrained) boundary conditions at the singularity. The specific exclusion of Kasner/BKL cosmologies and admission of conformally regular FLRW models is a *derived* constraint on the initial state — not imposed externally, not architectural, not from any of the five primitives, but from the requirement that the action be stationary under unconstrained variation at its own singular boundary. The framework has no concept of "free variation at a singular boundary" because it has no variational principle at all — its primitives are dynamical descriptions (gradient descent, conservation, resolution) rather than variational ones (action stationarity). The reflecting boundary conditions for cosmological perturbations at the bang constitute a concrete, potentially observationally testable prediction about the initial conditions of the universe, derived from a structural principle (free-boundary variational stationarity) that the framework cannot express, formulate, or evaluate. This is not merely another instance of the structural content gap (T026); it is a concrete, physically consequential answer to one of the framework's own acknowledged open problems (T002), derived from mathematical structure the framework is constitutively unable to access.

Paper 2's complex CFT pair adds a distinct strain: the framework's categorical distinction between first-order and second-order phase transitions — between discontinuous and continuous attractor landscape restructuring — dissolves under analytic continuation. The two types of transitions are shadows of a single analytic structure in the complexified coupling space. The framework has no concept of complexified attractor landscapes, complex-valued field theory, or the analytic continuation that relates qualitatively different transition types. This extends the complexification challenge (T036, the model's 2026-03-23 update on complexified saddle points) from quantum mechanics to statistical mechanics and from individual theories to the *classification of phase transition types*.
