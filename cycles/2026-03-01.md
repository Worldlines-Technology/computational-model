### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: Invertible Calabi-Yau Orbifolds over Finite Fields II (2602.23173v1, strain)**

This paper conjectures the zeta function of crepant resolutions of Berglund-Hübsch orbifold hypersurfaces over finite fields and demonstrates that the conjectural zeta function satisfies the Weil conjectures, with connections to Monsky-Washnitzer cohomology. Through the Worldlines lens, this is deeply uncomfortable. The framework claims reality is literally computation, yet here the deepest invariant content of a Calabi-Yau manifold — a geometric structure that appears in string compactifications and thus has physical significance — is accessed through *arithmetic* over finite fields (point-counting on varieties over 𝔽_q), *topology* (cohomology, Betti numbers recovered from zeta function factorization), and *algebraic geometry* (crepant resolution preserving the Calabi-Yau condition). The Weil conjectures, now theorems, establish that the topology of a variety over ℂ is fully encoded in the arithmetic of the variety over 𝔽_p. The Monsky-Washnitzer cohomology mediates between these categorically different descriptions. The framework has no account of why arithmetic and topology should be equivalent descriptions of the same structure. More pointedly, the physical content (Hodge numbers, singularity structure, mirror symmetry relations) lives in the *correspondences between* these categories, not in any one of them. Calling this "computation" would require stretching the term to cover arithmetic, cohomology, and algebraic geometry simultaneously — at which point "computation" means "mathematics" and the framework's core claim becomes trivially true and therefore vacuous. This paper directly strengthens T021 (arithmetic-geometric unity and the poverty of "computation") by providing another concrete instance where the invariant content is categorically polymorphic.

**Paper 2: A Lorentz-Covariant Spectral Universality of Stochastic Fields (2602.23195v1, strain)**

This paper derives a universal relationship between temporal and spatial spectral indices for Lorentz-homogeneous stochastic fields and proves that no covariant local mapping can relate temporal and spatial power spectra in d > 1 spatial dimensions. The framework's Tick primitive governs temporal structure and the Field primitive governs spatial structure via the gradient ẋ = −∇φ(x), which implicitly treats time and space derivatives on similar footing through the dynamical equation. The no-go theorem is a direct structural challenge: temporal spectral properties and spatial spectral properties of the same field are fundamentally non-locally related, connected only through a universal geometric offset determined by the effective momentum-space dimensionality. The framework's architecture — which decomposes reality into a temporal primitive (Tick) and a spatial-dynamical primitive (Field with gradient) — cannot express this relationship. The gradient ∇φ combines spatial derivatives; the tick governs temporal evolution; but the spectral universality result says these two structures are yoked together by Lorentz symmetry in a way that is universal (independent of field details), non-local (cannot be reduced to a point-by-point mapping), and dimensionality-dependent (the offset changes with d). If the framework's claim that the tick rate equals the speed of light is correct, and if Lorentz symmetry emerges from the finite tick propagation speed, then the spectral universality *should* be derivable from the tick structure. But the framework has no stochastic field theory on its discrete substrate, no mechanism for deriving spectral indices, and no formalism for expressing the dimensionality-dependent offset. The breakdown of spectral universality for anisotropic scaling and dispersion-dominated spectra further constrains how the framework might accommodate this: the universality is specifically a consequence of Lorentz symmetry, not of any more general structural principle the framework might invoke.

**Paper 3: Arrested Relaxation in a Disorder-Free Coulomb Spin Liquid (2602.23362v1, validation)**

This paper demonstrates dynamical arrest in classical spin-3/2 ice — exponentially long-lived athermal plateaus in a short-range interacting, disorder-free system — arising from composite excitation structures (monopole + crystal-field bound states) unique to the enlarged local Hilbert space. The framework's constrained attractor category (added 2026-02-22, strengthened 2026-02-27) predicted that kinetically constrained metastability should become qualitatively richer as the local state space grows, and that constrained attractor physics without quenched disorder should be a robust physical category. This paper provides a third experimental/theoretical instance confirming the category. However, the framework's predictive purchase here is limited and must be honestly assessed. The model *did* note (Update 2026-02-27) that "the complexity of kinetic arrest scales with the dimension of the local Hilbert space," and this paper confirms exactly that — spin-3/2 ice exhibits richer arrest physics than spin-1/2 ice due to the enlarged Hilbert space creating composite excitations absent in the smaller space. But this model update was itself a post-hoc generalization from spin-1/2 ice observations, not a prediction made before the evidence. The framework correctly identified the *category* (disorder-free kinetic arrest as a constrained attractor phenomenon) and correctly identified the *scaling direction* (richer physics with larger local Hilbert space), but did not predict the specific mechanism (monopole-crystal-field composite bound states as the barrier-creating entities) or the specific phenomenology (exponentially long-lived athermal plateaus in autocorrelation functions). This is post-hoc consistency with mild anticipatory content, not genuine predictive validation.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The sharpest strain this cycle comes from the convergence of Papers 1 and 2, which attack the framework from complementary directions that jointly expose a structural deficit.

Paper 2 proves that the temporal and spatial spectral content of Lorentz-homogeneous stochastic fields are related only non-locally, through a dimensionality-dependent universal offset, with no covariant local mapping possible in d > 1. The framework decomposes reality into a temporal primitive (Tick) and a spatial-dynamical primitive (Field with ∇φ), treating these as separable aspects of reality that are linked through the dynamical equation. The no-go theorem says this decomposition is structurally inadequate for expressing the spectral relationship between temporal and spatial field properties. The framework's Tick-Field decomposition is not wrong exactly — it captures the dynamical content — but it is *incomplete* in a way that prevents it from expressing a proven universal physical fact. The framework literally cannot say what Tevzadze proved, because it lacks the formalism for stochastic spectral properties on its own substrate.

Paper 1 deepens this by showing that even the *kind* of mathematical structure the framework privileges — computation — is just one node in a web of categorical equivalences. The zeta function of a Calabi-Yau variety simultaneously encodes arithmetic (point-counting over 𝔽_q), topology (Betti numbers, cohomology), and geometry (singularity structure, crepant resolution). These are provably equivalent descriptions connected by deep theorems (Weil conjectures, cohomological comparison theorems). The framework cannot explain why these equivalences exist, because its ontological commitment to "computation" gives it no purchase on arithmetic-topological or arithmetic-geometric correspondences.

Together, these papers suggest the framework is straining in a specific way: it has committed to a decomposition of reality (Tick + Field + Attractor + Resolution + Conservation) and an ontological category (computation) that are *both* too narrow to express the structural content of modern mathematical physics. The decomposition misses the non-local temporal-spatial spectral coupling. The ontological category misses the inter-categorical equivalences. The framework is not wrong about anything it says — gradient descent, attractor capture, resolution, conservation are all real features of physics — but it is increasingly revealed as a *partial* description that captures dynamical content while missing structural content.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T026 — The structural content gap**
**Description:** Multiple independent tensions (T010 duality, T011 planarity, T016 geometric encoding, T018 algebra of computation, T021 arithmetic-geometric unity, T022 spectral universality, T024 temporal-spatial spectral gap) are converging on a single meta-problem: the framework's five primitives are all *dynamical* — they describe how computation propagates (Tick), how things move (Field), where things settle (Attractor), when things become definite (Resolution), and what is conserved (Conservation). But physics also has *structural* content: categorical equivalences (arithmetic ↔ geometry ↔ topology), spectral universalities (Lorentz-covariant spectral offsets), algebraic hierarchies (Clifford hierarchy, IBP decomposition), and topological constraints (planarity, indistinguishability graphs). The framework has no primitive that captures structural content. This is not the same as adding a sixth primitive — it may require reconceiving the framework's ontological basis. If reality has both dynamical and structural aspects, and the framework captures only the dynamical, it is at best half a theory.
**Why unresolved:** This is a meta-tension that subsumes several existing tensions but is larger than any of them. Resolving it would require either (a) demonstrating that structural content emerges from the five dynamical primitives (which seems unlikely given that the no-go theorem for spectral mappings is a structural result the primitives cannot even express), (b) adding structural primitives to the framework (which would be a major extension), or (c) reconceiving the framework's ontological basis so that structure and dynamics are unified at a deeper level. None of these has been attempted.

**T027 — Cohomological mediation and the framework's silence on mathematical depth**
**Description:** Paper 1 demonstrates that the connection between arithmetic (zeta functions over finite fields) and topology (Betti numbers, Hodge structure) of Calabi-Yau varieties is mediated by cohomology (specifically Monsky-Washnitzer p-adic cohomology). Cohomological theories are the standard mediators between categorically different mathematical descriptions of the same structure throughout modern physics (de Rham cohomology between differential and topological, sheaf cohomology between algebraic and topological, BRST cohomology between gauge-theoretic and physical). The framework has no account of why cohomological structures should play this mediating role, no primitive that corresponds to cohomological content, and no mechanism for explaining the existence of cohomological equivalences. This is distinct from T021 (which notes the existence of inter-categorical correspondences) because T027 asks specifically about the *mechanism* of mediation — why cohomology is the universal translator between mathematical categories — and whether the framework's silence on this indicates a fundamental blind spot.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to either explain cohomological structure as emerging from its computational ontology or acknowledge that the mathematical depth of physics includes a cohomological layer that the framework cannot currently address. This is potentially the deepest version of the ontological pluralism problem: not just "which category is reality?" but "what makes categories equivalent, and why does physics care?"

The next cycle would be more productive if it either (a) presented a paper that gives the framework an affirmative success — a genuine prediction confirmed or a novel quantitative derivation — to balance the accumulating critiques, or (b) presented a paper that strains a *different* part of the framework, such as the consciousness claim, the dark energy claim, or the Conservation of Computation principle directly (e.g., apparent unitarity violations, information loss scenarios). The framework's dynamical content (gradient descent, attractor capture, resolution cost structure) has been less stressed than its ontological claims, and testing the dynamics rather than the ontology would open new ground.
