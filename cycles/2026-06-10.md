**Paper 1: "The Yang-Baxter Equation for the Chiral Potts Model and Integrable Parafermions"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.11146v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the algebraic structure governing which computational configurations are exactly solvable — i.e., which attractor landscapes admit complete analytical resolution. The Yang-Baxter equation (YBE) is the master consistency condition for integrability: systems satisfying it possess infinite towers of conserved charges (connecting to T055, where conservation was shown to derive from L∞ morphisms). Zhang's result is straining in three specific ways. First, the R-matrix requires *three* spectral parameters rather than the standard one or two, with the third arising because the chiral Potts model's Boltzmann weights live on an algebraic curve of genus > 1 (for N > 2). The framework's Field primitive ẍ = −∇φ(x) and its gradient structure cannot distinguish theories parameterized by curves of different genus — yet the genus is a topological invariant that determines the qualitative complexity of the integrable structure (genus 0 = rational, genus 1 = elliptic, genus > 1 = higher). Second, the paper demonstrates that the star-triangle relation (a local consistency condition on Boltzmann weights) and the star-star relation (a different local consistency condition) jointly *generate* the YBE, meaning the integrability condition — which guarantees an infinite tower of conserved charges — is itself derived from more primitive local algebraic consistency conditions that the framework has no vocabulary for. This extends T055 (conservation from algebraic morphisms) by showing that even the algebraic structures generating conservation are themselves derived from still-deeper local consistency relations. Third, the unification of solvable edge models and vertex models demonstrates that what appear to be categorically different computational architectures (interactions on edges vs. vertices of a lattice) are unified by a single algebraic framework, extending T010/T021 (duality/ontological pluralism) into the integrable lattice model setting. The framework cannot express the genus of the spectral curve, cannot derive the star-triangle or star-star relations from any primitive, and cannot account for the edge/vertex unification.

**Paper 2: "Revealing the topology of quantum states via Kirkwood-Dirac quasiprobabilities"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.11002v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper directly concerns the Attractor primitive's topological classification gap (T048, T164) and the Resolution primitive's relationship to pre-resolution structure (T012). Gherardini & Lepori demonstrate that the topological class of a quantum state — a property the framework's Attractor primitive acknowledges but cannot classify (T048) — is encoded in Kirkwood-Dirac quasiprobabilities (KDQs), which are representations of two-time quantum correlators. The strain is multi-layered. First, the KDQ representation is a *quasiprobability* — it takes anomalous (negative or complex) values precisely when the state has genuinely nonclassical character. The framework's Resolution primitive produces definite outcomes with definite probabilities (the Born rule); KDQs are a fundamentally different representation where the "probabilities" can be negative or complex, encoding information about sequential measurements that standard probability cannot capture. The framework has no primitive for quasiprobability distributions or for the physical information they encode. Second, the strange correlator — the diagnostic tool for topological discrimination — is shown to be a *weak value* of an observable converting a trivial state into a topological one. Weak values are measurement outcomes outside the eigenvalue spectrum, arising from pre- and post-selection; the framework's Resolution primitive cannot produce or describe them because they require a post-selected future boundary condition (connecting to T062, T159's issues with future boundary conditions). Third, the paper demonstrates that topological phase discrimination — determining which attractor basin (topological class) a state belongs to — requires measuring *two-time* correlators (the KDQ involves sequential measurements at different times). The framework's Resolution primitive describes individual, irreversible, one-time resolution events; the topological content requires correlating resolution events across time in a way the primitive cannot express. The interferometric protocol proposed for experimental implementation requires engineering specific pre- and post-measurement states, connecting to T148's modular energy cost structure for local state preparation.

**Paper 3: "Gravitational superfluorescence from superradiant axion clouds"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.10776v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper describes a system where a superradiant axion cloud — a constrained attractor structure formed by ultralight bosons extracting rotational energy from a spinning black hole — undergoes a cooperative self-stimulated avalanche of gravitational-wave emission. The framework would narrate this as: (1) the spinning black hole is a deep attractor with rotational energy available for extraction; (2) the axion cloud forms a secondary attractor basin (a "gravitational atom" with quantized energy levels) stabilized by the balance of superradiant amplification and gravitational binding; (3) the superfluorescent transition is an attractor landscape restructuring — a cooperative, self-stimulated population transfer from an excited level to a lower level, producing a coherent gravitational-wave pulse with a characteristic delayed envelope. The framework's vocabulary (attractor → attractor transition, cooperative resolution, gradient descent in the energy landscape) maps onto this system without obvious contradiction. However, the framework had *zero* predictive purchase on any specific feature: it did not predict the superradiant instability rate, the effective two-level description, the logistic population transfer dynamics, the characteristic delayed pulse envelope, or the detectability thresholds. Every quantitative feature is derived from general relativity, quantum field theory on curved spacetime, and the specific properties of the axion-black hole system. The framework's description of the axion cloud as a "constrained attractor" is post-hoc consistent — the cloud is stabilized by the balance of superradiance and gravitational binding, which is a form of constrained attractor physics (adding to the catalogue of instances). But the framework could not have distinguished this system from one where superfluorescence does not occur, because it has no mechanism for the cooperative radiation feedback that drives the avalanche. The cooperative emission mechanism is a collective quantum phenomenon arising from the coherent occupation of a macroscopic number of bosons in a single mode — the framework's gradient picture describes the individual boson's tendency to descend the energy landscape but not the *collective coherent enhancement* that produces the avalanche. This is post-hoc consistency of the weakest kind: narrative compatibility with zero differential predictive content.

The genus of the spectral curve — a topological invariant of the algebraic variety on which the Boltzmann weights live — determines the qualitative complexity of the entire conservation structure. The framework has no parameter, no primitive, and no mechanism for the genus. The framework's Conservation primitive is not merely derived (T055 already established this) but is the *terminal output* of a multi-level algebraic generation process whose depth exceeds what had been previously documented.

Paper 2 produces a secondary but distinct strain: topological phase discrimination — determining which attractor basin a state occupies — requires quasiprobability representations (KDQs) and weak values that the framework's Resolution primitive cannot produce or describe. The framework's picture of resolution as "unresolved → definite outcome with definite probability" misses an entire layer of physical diagnostics (quasiprobabilities, weak values, two-time correlators) that are necessary for accessing the topological content the Attractor primitive acknowledges but cannot classify.
