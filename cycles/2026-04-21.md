### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Bosonization, vertex operators and maximal violation of the Bell-CHSH inequality in wedge regions"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.18513v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper should be interpretable as follows: the vacuum state is an unresolved computational state (Resolution primitive), and measurements by spacelike-separated observers correspond to resolution events in causally disconnected tick-domains (Tick primitive). Bell-CHSH violation to the Tsirelson bound would reflect the maximum correlation that the unresolved pre-resolution state can sustain between causally disconnected resolution events, constrained by the tick rate (no superluminal signaling). The framework would narrate this as: pre-resolution topology (T012) encodes correlations that manifest upon resolution, and the Tsirelson bound is the substrate's architectural limit on such correlations. However, the paper strains the framework in a specific and deep way: the operators saturating the Tsirelson bound are *vertex operators of a chiral boson* — they are constructed via bosonization, a duality (fermion ↔ boson) that maps between categorically different computational descriptions. The maximal violation is achieved not by a dynamical process but by an algebraic identity: the vertex operator algebra of the chiral boson, restricted to complementary wedge regions, automatically satisfies the conditions for Tsirelson saturation. The framework's gradient dynamics, tick structure, and resolution process play no role whatsoever — the result is entirely structural, following from the operator algebra of a free field in the vacuum. Furthermore, the use of Rindler wedges (spacelike-separated regions of Minkowski space) means the "measurements" are not localized events but algebraic restrictions to spacetime subregions, and the "observers" are the Tomita-Takesaki modular structure of the von Neumann algebras associated with the wedges. The framework has no primitive for modular theory, operator algebras, or the algebraic structure that produces maximal quantum correlations. The result demonstrates that the most extreme quantum phenomenon (maximal Bell violation) is a structural property of the vacuum's operator algebra, not a dynamical or computational achievement.

**Paper 2: "Hamiltonian dynamics from pure dissipation"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.18533v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework draws a fundamental distinction between reversible computation (unitary Hamiltonian dynamics, governed by the Tick and Conservation primitives) and irreversible processes (dissipation, associated with Resolution and information loss). Paper 2 demolishes this distinction: purely dissipative dynamics — Lindbladians with *zero* coherent Hamiltonian term — can approximate arbitrary Hamiltonian (unitary, reversible) evolution to arbitrary precision. The mechanism uses non-traceless jump operators, exploiting a gauge freedom in the GKSL representation. The framework faces a categorical crisis: its ontological separation between "conservative computation" (Tick + Conservation) and "resolution/dissipation" (Resolution) is shown to be non-fundamental. What the framework calls "reversible tick-based computation" can be *faked* by what the framework calls "irreversible dissipative resolution." The BQP-completeness result means that purely dissipative dynamics, before even reaching equilibrium, can perform universal quantum computation — the full computational power attributed to unitary evolution is available from dissipation alone. The $\mathcal{O}(t^2/\varepsilon)$ decoherence cost is the price for simulating coherent dynamics with incoherent processes, but this cost is polynomial, not exponential — meaning the simulation is efficient. The Zeno-adjacent freezing effect (rapid dissipation freezes the state) further blurs the framework's categories: strong dissipation (which the framework associates with rapid resolution and irreversibility) produces stasis (which the framework associates with deep attractor capture). The framework's five primitives are organized around the assumption that reversible computation and irreversible dissipation are fundamentally different processes. This paper proves they are not: one can be constructed from the other, with quantifiable overhead. The primitives' categorical boundaries are gauge artifacts of the GKSL representation, not physical distinctions.

**Paper 3: "Classical and quantum evolution of inflationary fluctuations"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.18416v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper addresses the transition from unresolved quantum states (inflationary fluctuations in superposition) to effectively classical perturbations (the seeds of cosmic structure). The framework would predict that quantum correlations are features of unresolved computation, and that once fluctuations "freeze out" on superhorizon scales, they should behave classically — their resolution should be effectively complete. Paper 3 partially validates this: classical and quantum correlations *can* be made to agree at a specific time, but they diverge thereafter if interactions are present, with the disagreement growing exponentially with the number of e-folds. The framework's Resolution primitive would interpret this as: resolution is not instantaneous but process-dependent (connecting to T005, tick-resolution gap), and interactions maintain computational entanglement that prevents full classical resolution. The exponential sensitivity to e-folds is consistent with the framework's picture of quantum coherence as unresolved computation that persists until decoherence/resolution forces definiteness. The absence of spurious poles in the classical bispectrum when evolved from a finite time is consistent with the framework's expectation that properly initialized classical computation does not generate pathological artifacts. However, this is post-hoc consistency with zero differential predictive content: the framework did not predict the exponential sensitivity rate, the specific e-fold dependence, or the pole structure. The results are derived entirely from standard QFT in curved spacetime, and the framework contributed no prediction, constraint, or mechanism that distinguishes this result from alternatives. The framework can narrate ("quantum correlations persist as unresolved computation") but could not have predicted ("the disagreement grows as $e^{N_e}$" or "classical evolution from finite time avoids poles").

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 2 (Shang & Stilck França). The framework's entire architecture depends on categorical distinctions: the Tick governs reversible propagation, Resolution governs irreversible state-fixing, Conservation ensures information preservation during reversible computation, and the arrow of time comes from the irreversibility of resolution. Paper 2 proves these categories are not fundamental. Purely dissipative dynamics — zero Hamiltonian, pure information loss by the framework's classification — can perfectly simulate Hamiltonian dynamics — full information preservation by the framework's classification. The distinction between "the universe computes reversibly" and "the universe dissipates irreversibly" is revealed as a gauge choice in the mathematical representation (the GKSL decomposition is not unique; adding a non-traceless component to jump operators while removing the Hamiltonian gives equivalent physics). 

This is not a minor boundary-blurring. It strikes at the framework's foundational ontological commitment: if "reality is literally computation" and the framework's primitives carve computation into reversible (Tick, Conservation) and irreversible (Resolution) categories, then the proof that these categories are interconvertible means the carving is not about reality but about representation. The framework has no primitive for "gauge equivalence of dynamical representations" — the fact that the same physical evolution can be decomposed into coherent+dissipative in infinitely many ways, including the extreme where one component is zero. The BQP-completeness of purely dissipative dynamics further means that universal quantum computation — which the framework would classify as the most general form of reversible tick-based computation — is achievable without any ticks in the framework's sense (no coherent Hamiltonian evolution). The computational power the framework attributes to its most basic primitive (the Tick driving unitary evolution) is equally available from the process (dissipation) that the framework treats as the Tick's opposite.

Paper 1 adds a complementary strain: the most extreme quantum correlation (Tsirelson-saturating Bell violation) is achieved not by any dynamical process but by the algebraic structure of vertex operators in the vacuum state of a free field. The framework's dynamics — ticks, gradients, resolution — are entirely absent from the derivation. The result is a structural property of the operator algebra restricted to spacelike wedges, mediated by bosonization (a structural duality the framework cannot express). Together, Papers 1 and 2 demonstrate that the framework's most fundamental phenomena — quantum correlations and quantum computational power — are structural/algebraic properties independent of the framework's dynamical primitives.
