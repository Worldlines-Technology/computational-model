### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: Many-body chirality of topological stabilizer states**
ArXiv ID: 2606.20472v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework has no primitive, parameter, or mechanism for chirality — the distinction between a system and its mirror image at the level of many-body quantum states. Paper 1 formulates chirality as an *obstruction* to implementing complex conjugation via finite-depth local operations, proving that this obstruction exists for stabilizer realizations of ℤ_d^(k) anyon theories precisely when the anyon data are not mirror-invariant. The framework's Resolution primitive describes states becoming definite; its Attractor primitive describes basins characterized by depth, width, and topological invariants (T048, T146); its Conservation primitive describes preserved quantities. None of these can express the concept of an obstruction to a specific local operation (complex conjugation), nor can they distinguish chiral from non-chiral topological states. The result is especially damaging in three ways: (1) The chirality is *invisible to tripartite entanglement* and only appears at the four-partite level, meaning the framework's implicit bipartite/tripartite entanglement vocabulary is categorically insufficient — the physical content requires a specific multipartite partition structure the framework has no concept of. (2) The chirality can exist even when conventional diagnostics vanish (zero modular commutator, zero chiral central charge, commuting-projector Hamiltonians), meaning the framework's existing post-hoc consistency with topological classification (T048) misses genuinely physical distinctions. (3) The "intrinsic many-body imaginarity" result proves that for d > 2, the complex phase structure of stabilizer states cannot be removed by any finite-depth local unitary — the ℂ vs. ℝ distinction (T069) is not merely unknowable but is *physically encoded* in certain topological states, with states that provably require complex amplitudes even when they are not chiral. This directly addresses T069 by showing that at least for some topological states, the number field is a physical property of the state, not merely an architectural ambiguity.

**Paper 2: Impossibility of superluminal signalling rules out causal loops in conical spacetimes**
ArXiv ID: 2606.20476v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework's Tick primitive asserts that the speed of light is the tick rate — the maximum rate of state propagation — and that causal structure follows from this local constraint. Paper 2 proves that the relationship between no-superluminal-signalling (NSS) and the absence of causal loops is *geometry-dependent*: in (1+1)-Minkowski spacetime, NSS does not rule out operationally detectable causal loops, but in conical spacetimes with d > 1 spatial dimensions (including standard (3+1)-Minkowski), NSS does rule them out. The framework treats the Tick as producing causal structure through a single mechanism (maximum propagation rate), implying that causal structure is determined *locally* by the tick rate. Paper 2 demonstrates that the *global causal structure* — whether causal loops can exist — depends on the *dimensionality and geometry* of the spacetime, not just on the local propagation constraint. The tick rate is the same in (1+1) and (3+1) Minkowski, yet the causal structure is qualitatively different: causal loops are compatible with NSS in the former but not the latter. The mechanism is geometric: in d > 1 spatial dimensions, the "conical" structure of the future light cone (the solid angle subtended by causal influence regions) creates geometric constraints on signalling that are absent in d = 1. This means the Tick primitive's local constraint (maximum propagation rate) is *necessary but insufficient* for determining causal structure; the dimensionality and geometry of the spacetime are independent inputs that the framework treats as unexplained architectural parameters (T013) but that have direct causal consequences the framework cannot derive. The result also extends to post-quantum theories, meaning the geometry-dependence of causal structure is not specific to quantum mechanics but is a feature of any theory respecting NSS — the framework's computational substrate, whatever its nature, is subject to this geometric constraint.

**Paper 3: String Axiverse Enhancement of Superradiant Dark Matter Production**
ArXiv ID: 2606.20524v1 | Selection type: Validation

The framework describes dark matter as "computational structure that couples to mass-density gradients (gravity) but not electromagnetic gradients" and black holes as "attractor basins so deep that escape velocity exceeds the tick rate." Paper 3 studies superradiant dark matter production by primordial black holes in the string axiverse, where O(100–10^5) light axion species enhance the PBH spin through Hawking emission, expanding the parameter space for superradiant cloud formation and micro-boson star remnants. The framework can narrate this in its vocabulary: deep attractor basins (PBHs) shed computational content (Hawking evaporation) including dark matter candidates (axions coupling gravitationally), while superradiance extracts rotational energy from the attractor into bound-state structures (boson clouds) that are secondary constrained attractors (self-gravitating "micro-boson stars"). The interplay between evaporation (attractor shedding) and superradiance (attractor energy extraction) produces a competition whose outcome depends on the number of axion species — a structural parameter the framework cannot derive. However, the framework had *zero predictive purchase* on any specific finding: it could not predict that the axiverse enhancement exists, that there is a critical species number above which evaporation outcompetes superradiance, that the dark matter contribution to relativistic degrees of freedom at recombination is negligible, or that the viable parameter space has the specific shape it does. The framework's qualitative consistency with "dark matter couples gravitationally" is shared with every CDM-variant model. This is the weakest category of validation: the framework provides vocabulary but no prediction, no constraint, and no mechanism that distinguishes this result from its non-occurrence.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most significant strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's intrinsic many-body imaginarity result. The framework has recorded T069 as the tension that the number field (ℝ vs. ℂ) is empirically underdetermined and provably unknowable from finite operational procedures. Paper 1 partially *resolves and partially deepens* this tension in a way that is deeply uncomfortable for the framework. For stabilizer states of ℤ_d^(k) anyon theories with d > 2, the complex phase structure is *intrinsic* — it cannot be removed by any finite-depth local unitary. This means the ℝ/ℂ distinction is not merely architectural or unknowable; for certain topological states, ℂ is a *physical necessity encoded in the state's entanglement structure*. The framework claimed the number field is an "architectural parameter of the substrate, not derivable from within the computation." Paper 1 shows it is derivable — at least partially — from the topological properties of certain quantum states. The complex numbers are not just a choice of substrate but a physical requirement detectable from within the computation via the obstruction to real-ification by finite-depth circuits.

This produces a sharp contradiction: the framework says the number field is unknowable from within; physics says certain states provably require ℂ and this requirement is detectable from within (by checking whether finite-depth local unitaries can map the state to a real representation). The framework's substrate opacity claim is falsified for this specific parameter in this specific context.

Simultaneously, the four-partite character of the chirality obstruction reveals a new and severe gap: the framework has no concept of *partition-dependent* physical content. The chirality is invisible to any tripartite entanglement measure but detectable at the four-partite level. The framework's primitives are not parameterized by the number of subsystems in the partition, and the Resolution/Attractor/Conservation primitives treat states as having properties independent of how they are partitioned. Paper 1 proves this is physically wrong for chirality: the physical content exists only at a specific partition level.

Paper 2's strain is also significant but in a different direction. The framework implicitly derives causal structure from a single local constraint (the tick rate). Paper 2 proves that identical local constraints produce *qualitatively different global causal structures* depending on spatial dimensionality — a parameter the framework acknowledges as unexplained (T013) but whose causal consequences it had not previously been forced to confront. The framework cannot explain why reality has d > 1 spatial dimensions, and now Paper 2 shows this choice has direct, provable consequences for whether causal loops are physically possible — making T013 not merely an open question about the substrate but a question whose answer determines fundamental causal structure.

### STATE UPDATES

1. **T069 (number field underdetermination) — partially resolve and deepen.** The model's description of the ℝ/ℂ underdetermination should be updated to note: for stabilizer states of ℤ_d^(k) anyon theories with d > 2, the complex number field is a *physical necessity* detectable from within the computation — the state's complex phase structure cannot be removed by finite-depth local unitaries. The framework's claim that the number field is an "architectural parameter not derivable from within" is falsified for these states. The underdetermination remains for operational correlations in general networks (Hoffreumon & Woods 2025) but is *resolved in favor of ℂ* for specific topological states. The model should distinguish between "operationally underdetermined" (the Hoffreumon-Woods result, which holds for finite network correlations) and "topologically determined" (the Ellison et al. result, which holds for specific many-body states). The resolution T181 proposed (GUE selects ℂ) should be noted as receiving independent support: both the stochastic unitary mechanism and the topological imaginarity result point to ℂ as physically necessary, through different mechanisms.

2. **T013 (time dimensionality) — strengthen with causal-loop evidence.** The model should record that the number of spatial dimensions is not merely an unexplained architectural parameter but has direct, provable consequences for the global causal structure: in d = 1 spatial dimension, NSS is compatible with operationally detectable causal loops; in d > 1, it is not. The Tick primitive's local constraint (maximum propagation rate) is insufficient to determine global causal structure; the spatial dimensionality provides an independent geometric input. The framework's silence on dimensionality is now shown to be silence on a parameter that determines whether causal loops are physically possible — a qualitatively more severe gap than previously recognized.

3. **Attractor primitive — add partition-dependent topological content.** The model's description of topological attractor basins should be updated to note: the physical content of topological states (specifically, chirality) can be invisible to all entanglement measures up to (n−1)-partite but detectable at n-partite level. The Attractor primitive's characterization of topological basins by depth, width, cohomological class (T048), and magic content (T146) should be extended to include *chirality* as an additional characterization axis, with the specific caveat that chirality requires four-partite entanglement diagnostics and evades all conventional diagnostics (modular commutator, chiral central charge). The framework should note that the physical content of attractor basins has a partition-level dependence it cannot express.

4. **T026 (structural content gap) — add many-body chirality / four-partite entanglement obstructions as a new evidence type.** The model should record that the physical content of topological states includes obstructions (to complex conjugation by local operations) that are: (a) invisible to tripartite and lower entanglement measures; (b) detectable only at the four-partite level; (c) classified by anyon-theoretic mirror-invariance data; (d) distinct from magic (T146), from cohomological classification (T048), and from entanglement scaling (T117). This adds "partition-level entanglement obstructions" to the catalogue of structural categories the framework cannot express.

5. **Tick primitive — add geometry-dependence of global causal structure.** The model's description of the Tick should be updated to note: the local propagation constraint (speed of light as maximum tick rate) determines local causal structure (light cones) but not global causal structure (presence/absence of causal loops). The global causal structure depends on spacetime geometry, specifically the spatial dimensionality, which the framework treats as an unexplained architectural parameter. In d = 1, NSS permits causal loops; in d > 1, it forbids them. The framework's derivation of causal structure from the Tick is incomplete without geometric input the Tick does not provide.

6. **Dark matter claim — record Paper 3 as post-hoc consistency with zero differential predictive content.** The string axiverse superradiant dark matter production is consistent with "dark matter couples gravitationally but not electromagnetically" but the framework contributed no prediction of the axiverse enhancement mechanism, the critical species number, the micro-boson star formation, or the parameter space structure. Record as the weakest validation category.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T183 — Many-body chirality as a partition-level obstruction invisible to the framework's entanglement vocabulary**

Opened: this cycle

Description: Ellison et al. (2606.20472) prove that many-body chirality — the obstruction to implementing complex conjugation via finite-depth local operations — is an *intrinsically four-partite* property of topological stabilizer states: it is invisible to all tripartite entanglement diagnostics but detectable at the four-partite level. The framework's primitives have no concept of partition-level-dependent physical content. The Resolution primitive treats resolution outcomes as properties of states, not of partitions of states. The Attractor primitive characterizes basins by depth, width, topological class, magic content — none of which is partition-level-dependent. The Conservation primitive conserves quantities that are state properties, not partition-structure properties. Paper 1 proves that a physically fundamental property (chirality) exists *only* at a specific partition level, meaning the framework's state-centric ontology is incomplete: some physical content is constitutively invisible unless the correct subsystem decomposition is used, and the framework has no mechanism for specifying or discovering the correct decomposition.

This is distinct from T012 (pre-resolution topology, which concerns the topology of path-indistinguishability) because T183 concerns the partition structure of resolved/definite states, not pre-resolution states. It is distinct from T146 (magic as a missing resource-theoretic axis) because magic is a state property independent of partition, while chirality is partition-dependent. It is distinct from T048 (cohomological completeness of state classification) because chirality evades the conventional topological diagnostics that cohomological classification captures (vanishing modular commutator, vanishing chiral central charge). It is distinct from T117 (entanglement scaling class as a continuous parameter) because chirality is a discrete obstruction, not a continuous parameter. It is distinct from T163 (edge mode constitution by observer decomposition) because T163 concerns degrees of freedom *created* by decomposition, while T183 concerns physical content that is *revealed only at* a specific decomposition level but exists independently of the observer's choice.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) incorporate a theory of partition-level-dependent physical content, specifying which properties of quantum states are detectable at which partition levels and what determines the "minimal detection partition" for each property — this would require importing the mathematical framework of multipartite entanglement theory (n-partite measures, entanglement monotones parameterized by partition), categorical beyond any primitive; (b) extend the Attractor primitive's topological characterization to include chirality, specifying how the anyon data's mirror-invariance determines the chirality obstruction — but this requires the full fusion category / anyon theory machinery that the framework repeatedly has been shown to lack (T048, T107, T111, T146); (c) derive the four-partite character from some combination of the five primitives, which appears impossible since the partition level is not a dynamical or computational quantity but a structural feature of the multipartite Hilbert space decomposition; or (d) accept that the framework's state-centric ontology is fundamentally incomplete — some physical properties are not properties of states but properties of states-relative-to-partitions, and the framework has no mechanism for this relativity.
