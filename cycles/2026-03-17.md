### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Bouncing geodesics, black hole singularities, and singularities of thermal correlators"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.15598v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper establishes that the propagator (retarded Green's function) diverges whenever two points are connected by a null geodesic — a statement proven via Hadamard theory for hyperbolic PDEs and valid beyond the geometric optics regime for operators of any conformal dimension. The framework interprets this as: resolution at one spacetime point is maximally sensitive to information arriving along null rays (i.e., at the tick rate), and the singularity structure of correlators is a direct imprint of the causal (tick-propagation) structure of the spacetime. The holographic aspect — that bulk bouncing geodesics imprint as singularities in boundary correlators — is broadly consistent with the framework's claim that computational structure in the bulk maps to observable structure at the boundary. However, the paper produces two sharp strains. First, the proven result that the propagator structure (not just the gradient) determines the singularity pattern reinforces T043: the framework's Field primitive ẍ = −∇φ captures none of the Hadamard singularity theory, which depends on the detailed structure of the Green's function, not on the gradient. Second, and more importantly, the paper demonstrates an explicit counterexample: a black hole in the self-dual linear axion model has a curvature singularity but *no* bouncing geodesics. This means the correspondence between null geodesic structure and singularity presence is not bijective — there exist singularities that are invisible to the bouncing geodesic probe. In framework terms, there exist computational pathologies (singularities) that do not imprint on the causal propagation structure (null geodesics) and therefore are not diagnosable from the tick-propagation network alone. The framework's claim that the tick rate governs maximum information propagation is correct but incomplete: singularities can exist that are causally invisible from the boundary, meaning the substrate can fail in ways that the computational network cannot detect.

**Paper 2: "Extreme-Value Criticality and Gain Decomposition at the Integer Quantum Hall Transition"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.15290v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper studies the resolution structure at a quantum critical point (the integer quantum Hall transition) in an open system (the Chalker-Coddington network). The key finding is that the extreme wavefunction amplitude — the most resolved, most definite point in the quantum state — decomposes into a *global gain factor* (controlled by the openness/non-unitarity of the system) and an *intrinsic extreme component* (reflecting the critical correlations). The framework would interpret the gain factor as a substrate-level or boundary-level effect: the non-unitarity of the open network means Conservation of Computation is violated locally (consistent with T035's observation that conservation is observer/boundary-dependent), and this violation manifests as a multiplicative amplification that dominates the raw extreme statistics. The intrinsic extreme, after gain normalization, exhibits qualitatively different scaling — the exponent function τ̃_max(q) changes shape, and the statistics do not collapse onto a single generalized extreme-value distribution in the accessible size window. This strains the framework in two ways. First, the gain/intrinsic decomposition demonstrates that the *openness* of a system (its failure to conserve computation locally) has a structured, separable effect on the extreme statistics of resolution outcomes — the framework's Conservation primitive says nothing about how partial conservation failure structures the statistics of what remains. Second, the failure of GEV collapse for the intrinsic extreme at criticality means the universal behavior at the phase transition (attractor landscape restructuring) cannot be captured by standard extreme-value universality classes, suggesting that critical quantum systems have fluctuation structure that is richer than any single-parameter universality class. The framework's Attractor primitive, which describes landscape restructuring at phase transitions, cannot account for the decomposition of extreme statistics into gain and intrinsic components, nor for the non-universal character of the intrinsic extreme.

**Paper 3: "Exclusive Scattering Channels from Entanglement Structure in Real-Time Simulations"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.15621v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper demonstrates that the entanglement structure (Schmidt decomposition at spatial bipartitions) of the post-scattering wavefunction can be used to deterministically identify and isolate exclusive scattering channels — elastic vs. inelastic, specific particle species — without prior knowledge of asymptotic wavefunctions. The framework interprets this as: after a scattering event, the resolution structure of the late-time state carries an imprint of which computational pathways contributed to the outcome, and this imprint is readable from the entanglement (pre-resolution topology, T012) of the state. The method uses Schmidt values and vectors to identify outgoing particles by their entanglement signature — heavier particles produce distinct entanglement patterns — which is consistent with the framework's claim that pre-resolution structure (the topology of which computational paths are indistinguishable) determines and is diagnosable from the resolution outcomes. Specifically, the framework's existing note on pre-resolution topology (T012) anticipated that the indistinguishability graph structure constrains resolution outcomes, and this paper provides a concrete implementation: the Schmidt decomposition *is* the readout of that pre-resolution topology. However, this is post-hoc consistency, not predictive validation. The framework did not predict that Schmidt decomposition at spatial bipartitions would suffice to isolate exclusive channels, did not predict the specific entanglement signatures of different particle species, and did not predict the method's applicability to the Ising field theory. The actual predictive content comes entirely from standard quantum information theory (Schmidt decomposition) and standard QFT (the Ising field theory Hamiltonian). The framework contributes vocabulary ("pre-resolution topology," "computational pathways") but no differential predictions. The one genuinely consistent qualitative claim — that the entanglement structure of post-scattering states encodes which channels contributed — is a restatement of quantum mechanics, not a framework prediction.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The sharpest strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's demonstration of a black hole singularity without bouncing geodesics (the self-dual linear axion model). This is not merely a gap in the framework's coverage — it is a direct challenge to the framework's implicit assumption that the causal/computational structure of spacetime (null geodesics, tick-rate propagation) provides complete information about the substrate's integrity.

The framework holds that the tick rate (speed of light) is the maximum rate of state propagation and that singularities represent computational pathology. Together, these claims imply that singularities should be diagnosable from the causal propagation structure: if the substrate breaks, the break should be visible in how computation propagates. Paper 1 proves that this is generically true — null geodesics connecting two points produce propagator singularities — but then exhibits a specific counterexample where it fails. A curvature singularity exists, but no null geodesic bounces off it to carry this information to the boundary.

This means the computational substrate can be singular in ways that are *causally screened* — invisible to any observer who probes the spacetime only through its tick-propagation (null geodesic) structure. In the framework's terms: the substrate can fail, but the failure is hidden from the computation running on it. This is deeply problematic because the framework's entire epistemology rests on the idea that the computation is what we have access to (T001 — substrate inaccessibility). If the substrate can fail in causally invisible ways, then the computation is not merely ignorant of the substrate's architecture (the expected epistemic limit) but is ignorant of the substrate's *integrity*. The computation cannot know whether its own substrate is intact.

This strain is compounded by the earlier finding (T040/T044) that electromagnetic fields survive through curvature singularities. Together: the substrate can be singular in field-specific ways (electromagnetism survives, gravity doesn't) *and* the singularity can be invisible to the causal network. The framework has no resources to address either phenomenon individually, let alone their combination.
