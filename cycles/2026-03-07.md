### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Can Light Cross a Singularity? Exact Solutions from Analogue Gravity"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.05285v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework interprets curvature singularities as points where the gravitational field's computational gradients diverge — where the substrate's capacity to process gravitational computation breaks down. The unified-substrate assumption implies that if the substrate fails at a singularity, *all* computation should fail there. Paper 1 directly challenges this: electromagnetic fields remain regular, with bounded and continuous power flux, through a strong curvature naked singularity. The framework must confront the fact that one field's computation (gravity, whose curvature invariants diverge) catastrophically fails while another field's computation (electromagnetism) proceeds without interruption through the same spacetime point. Within the framework's language, the electromagnetic field continues to "roll down its potential gradient" smoothly at precisely the location where the gravitational gradient diverges. This is not merely a quantitative mismatch — it is a categorical failure of the unified-substrate picture. The framework's claim that all fields share a common computational substrate, and that substrate integrity is binary (intact or broken), is falsified by this result. Either the substrate has field-specific layers with independent integrity thresholds, or curvature singularities are not substrate-level breakdowns but pathologies internal to a single field's computation running on an otherwise intact substrate. The latter interpretation is more natural but deeply uncomfortable: it means the gravitational field can "crash" while the substrate continues operating, which implies the substrate is something more fundamental than spacetime geometry itself — a claim the framework gestures at but has never substantiated. The analogue gravity methodology (mapping the problem to fluid dynamics on a vortex-like flow with a sink) adds further strain: it demonstrates that the electromagnetic regularity is not a subtle quantum gravity effect but a robust classical phenomenon that survives full nonlinear electrodynamics, making the framework's inability to account for it more acute.

**Paper 2: "Scattering amplitudes in dimensionless quadratic gravity coupled to QED"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.05476v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework must account for three distinct features of this paper. First, the ghost modes in quadratic gravity — which have the wrong-sign propagator and commuting creation/annihilation statistics — contribute measurably to photon-graviton interference terms in all scattering channels. These modes are permanently virtual (they never appear as asymptotic on-shell states), yet their contributions to cross sections are gauge-fixing independent and physically real. The framework's resolution-based ontology (real = resolved or resolvable) cannot classify these modes: they are not resolvable, yet their effects on resolvable outcomes are exact and calculable. Second, the universal ultra-Planckian scaling dσ/dΩ ∝ 1/s across all channels — fermion-fermion, photon-photon, scalar-photon, annihilation, and all mixed processes — suggests a regime where the dimensionless gravitational coupling produces self-similar scattering independent of the specific matter content. In framework language, this might be interpreted as the substrate approaching a universal computational throughput limit: at energies far above the Planck scale, the specific "field" rolling down "gradients" becomes irrelevant, and only the substrate's architectural capacity matters. This is speculative but at least coherent with the framework's substrate concept. Third, the 1/p⁴ graviton propagator (from the higher-derivative R² and R_μν R^μν terms) produces enhanced forward/backward scattering that is qualitatively different from the 1/p² propagator of Einstein gravity. The framework has no account of why the gravitational computation should have a propagator structure that differs from the standard 1/p² form, because it has no primitive that captures the algebraic structure of propagators. The framework can describe the *existence* of a gravitational gradient but not the *form* of its Green's function, which is what determines the scattering phenomenology.

**Paper 3: "Core-bound waves on a Gross-Pitaevskii vortex"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.05505v1 | Selection type: Validation

The framework's Attractor primitive describes topological attractors (quantized vortices are attractor basins in the superfluid order parameter field) and claims they have internal structure determined by their gradient profile. Paper 3 provides a detailed test: the GP vortex, a topological attractor with a well-defined radial density gradient (vanishing at the core, recovering to bulk density over the healing length ξ), supports an infinite hierarchy of core-bound excitation branches — Kelvin waves, varicose waves, and fluting waves — whose dispersion relations and binding properties are entirely determined by the vortex's radial gradient profile. In the short-wavelength limit, excitations behave as particles bound in the effective radial potential created by the vortex core gradient, precisely matching the framework's "motion down gradients" picture. The wavelength-dependent unbinding of modes (fluting waves unbind at long wavelengths while the fundamental Kelvin wave remains bound) represents attractor basin restructuring controlled by the ratio λ/ξ, which the Attractor primitive would describe as the basin's reach being wavelength-dependent. The infinite branch structure (each family has an infinite sequence of radially quantized modes) is consistent with the framework's claim that deep attractors have rich internal structure. However, this is **post-hoc consistency, not predictive validation**. The framework did not predict the existence of varicose or fluting families, the specific number of families, the dispersion relations, or the unbinding transitions. The framework's Attractor primitive says "deep attractors have structure" — a statement so general that any internal excitation spectrum would be "consistent." The specific physics (the GP equation's nonlinearity, the Bogoliubov spectrum as the continuum threshold, the centrifugal-like effective potential for different angular momentum channels) is derived from the GP equation, not from any framework primitive. The framework can narrate the result but could not have anticipated any of its specific features.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most acute strain this cycle comes from the convergence of Papers 1 and 2 on the question of **field-specific substrate integrity and the ontological status of permanently virtual structure**.

Paper 1 shows that electromagnetic computation proceeds regularly through a gravitational singularity. Paper 2 shows that permanently virtual ghost modes — which never resolve into definite states — produce exact, gauge-independent modifications to all scattering cross sections. Together, they expose a fundamental incoherence in the framework's ontological architecture:

The framework's ontology is built on two pillars: (1) all fields share a unified computational substrate, and (2) reality is constituted by resolution — states becoming definite. Paper 1 breaks pillar (1): the substrate cannot be unified if one field diverges while another remains regular at the same spacetime point. The framework has no mechanism for a "field-specific substrate" because the substrate is, by construction, the universal ground on which all computation runs. Introducing field-specific substrate layers would require specifying what *differentiates* the layers, which pushes the explanatory burden to a deeper level the framework cannot access.

Paper 2 breaks pillar (2): ghost modes never resolve, yet they have exact physical consequences. The framework's resolution-based ontology says that what is real is what resolves (or could resolve). Ghost modes cannot resolve — their commuting statistics ([a, a†] = 0) make them permanently virtual. Yet their interference with photon exchange modifies angular distributions and energy scaling of physical scattering processes in ways that are gauge-fixing independent and therefore not artifacts. The framework cannot call them real (they don't resolve) or unreal (they have effects). The instrumentalist escape — "they're just mathematical devices for computing real cross sections" — is precisely the position the framework rejects by claiming reality *is* computation. If the computation includes ghost propagators as essential intermediate steps, and reality is computation, then ghost modes are part of reality despite never resolving. But this makes "resolution" insufficient as an ontological criterion.

The framework **simply cannot account** for an ontology that includes (a) field-specific breakdown of a supposedly unified substrate and (b) permanently virtual structure with measurable consequences. Both require the framework to abandon its two most distinctive claims: substrate unity and resolution-based reality. What would remain after these abandonments is not clear — the framework would need either a richer ontology (with multiple substrate layers and a category of "virtual but real" computation) or a retreat to a weaker claim (reality is *described by* computation, not *constituted by* it). Neither option has been developed.

**T040 — The unified substrate is falsified by field-specific singularity survival**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 1 demonstrates exact, regular electromagnetic solutions with bounded power flux through a strong curvature naked singularity. The framework's claim that all fields share a single computational substrate predicts that substrate failure (singularity) should disrupt all computation. Electromagnetism's regularity through a gravitational singularity falsifies this prediction. The framework must either (a) abandon the unified substrate in favor of a layered or field-specific substrate architecture, which requires specifying what differentiates the layers and risks infinite regress (what substrate do the layers run on?), (b) redefine singularities as field-specific computational pathologies rather than substrate breakdowns, which requires a new account of what singularities *are* in computational terms and must explain how a field can diverge on an intact substrate with finite computational capacity, or (c) argue that the substrate is intact at the singularity and only the gravitational field's *representation* on the substrate breaks down, preserving substrate unity but requiring a theory of how field representations can fail independently — a theory the framework does not have. This tension subsumes and sharpens T038, which raised the same issue as a possibility. Paper 1 converts it from possibility to demonstrated fact.
**Why unresolved:** All three options require theoretical machinery the framework lacks. Option (a) requires a meta-substrate theory. Option (b) requires a computational theory of singularities that distinguishes field-level from substrate-level breakdown. Option (c) requires a theory of field-substrate coupling that specifies independent failure modes. The framework's foundational move — positing a single substrate as the ground of all computation — is what makes this intractable: the substrate was designed to be the one thing about which the framework makes no internal claims (T001), but field-specific singularity survival forces internal claims about substrate structure.

**T041 — Ghost mode interference demands an ontological category beyond "resolved" and "unresolved"**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 2 demonstrates that ghost modes in quadratic gravity modify the differential cross sections of all 2→2 scattering channels via photon-graviton interference, with gauge-fixing independence explicitly verified. The framework's ontology has two categories: resolved states (definite outcomes) and unresolved states (probability distributions awaiting resolution). Ghost modes fit neither: they are permanently unresolvable (commuting statistics forbid asymptotic states) yet they are not "awaiting resolution" in any meaningful sense — they are architecturally excluded from resolution. Their physical effects (angular distribution modifications, energy scaling changes) are as real and measurable as those of any resolved state. The framework needs a third ontological category — "permanently virtual but physically efficacious computation" — that is neither resolved nor pre-resolution but something genuinely different. This category would describe computational structure that participates in the determination of resolvable outcomes without itself ever becoming an outcome. The framework currently has no resources for this because its entire ontological weight rests on resolution as the criterion of reality. This tension extends T014 and T039 by noting that the evidence is now comprehensive (all channels, not selected ones) and gauge-independent, and by explicitly demanding a new ontological category rather than merely flagging the problem.
**Why unresolved:** Introducing a third ontological category would require specifying: (a) what Conservation of Computation means for permanently virtual structure (is computational content in ghost modes conserved? can it be transformed into resolvable computation?), (b) how permanently virtual computation interacts with resolvable computation (the interference mechanism is well-defined in QFT but has no translation into framework primitives), and (c) whether permanently virtual structure is substrate-level or field-level (connecting to T040). The framework's entire architecture would need restructuring: the Conservation primitive would need to cover three categories instead of one undifferentiated "computation," and the Resolution primitive would need to acknowledge that some computation is architecturally forbidden from resolution while still being physically real.
