### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Finite-n Estimate of Dedekind Numbers by Layer-Ratio Monte Carlo"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.09795v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, Dedekind numbers count the monotone Boolean functions on an n-element set — equivalently, the number of antichains or downsets of the Boolean lattice {0,1}^n. The framework would interpret the Boolean lattice as a discrete computational structure and monotone Boolean functions as computations respecting a gradient constraint (monotonicity = never descending along the lattice ordering). The Dedekind number M(n) then counts the number of distinct "gradient-respecting" computational configurations on the lattice. The paper's core finding — that the Whitney number sequence (the distribution of downsets by rank) exhibits a "two-shoulder" feature around the central rank rather than clean unimodality — is a structural property of the space of possible computations on the Boolean lattice. The framework has no primitive that addresses the *enumeration* of consistent computational configurations, the *distributional shape* of configuration counts across a structural parameter (rank), or the *doubly-exponential growth* of M(n) (which reflects the combinatorial explosion of consistent monotone structures). The two-shoulder feature is a structural fact about the organization of the space of gradient-respecting computations that the framework cannot predict, express, or derive. This connects directly to T145 (the space of allowed computations has internal phase structure) and T107 (pre-computational consistency conditions): the space of monotone Boolean functions is a discrete, exactly countable analogue of the "space of allowed computations," and its internal structure (the Whitney number distribution) is a combinatorial invariant with no expression in any of the five primitives. The layer-ratio Monte Carlo method — which estimates M(n) by sampling ratios of adjacent Whitney numbers via reversible Markov chains — is itself a computational approach to characterizing the combinatorial structure of the space of computations, and the Markov chain's mixing properties are determined by the lattice's combinatorial geometry, not by any dynamical primitive.

**Paper 2: "Who Earns the Safety? Intervention-Aware Quantum Predictive Control with Safety Attribution"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.09778v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the composition of two computational systems — a variational quantum circuit (VQC) policy and a classical safety filter (CBF projection + runtime guard) — where the physical observable (safe building control) is produced by their combined action, and the paper's central contribution is a method for *attributing* which component is responsible for the safety. The framework would describe the VQC policy as computation rolling down a loss-gradient landscape (Field primitive), the safety filter as an attractor constraint preventing excursion beyond safe boundaries (Attractor primitive), and the training process as resolution of the policy's parameters through gradient-based optimization. The paper's core conceptual challenge to the framework is the *attribution problem*: when two computational processes jointly produce an outcome, the framework has no mechanism for decomposing the outcome into contributions from each process. The framework's Conservation of Computation tracks total computational content but cannot partition credit between subsystems. More specifically, the paper demonstrates that a "resolved" system (the trained policy producing safe trajectories) can have its apparent competence entirely attributed to the safety filter rather than to the policy's own learned structure — the post-filter resolution is deceptive about the pre-filter computational state. This is a concrete instance of the framework's Resolution primitive being unable to distinguish between genuine and filter-mediated resolution. The guard-off evaluation protocol — removing the safety layer and observing whether the policy is independently safe — is operationally a test of whether resolution (safe behavior) is intrinsic or extrinsic to the computational agent. The framework has no concept of "who resolves" or "where in a composite system does the resolution originate," and this paper makes the absence concrete in an engineering context. The quantum-vs-classical policy comparison (quantum being significantly safer at matched parameter count) is weakly consistent with the framework's implicit assumption that quantum computation has different capabilities than classical computation, but the framework contributed no prediction about the magnitude, direction, or mechanism of the advantage.

**Paper 3: "Deviations from Debye's specific heat due to excess energy fluctuations"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.09566v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper demonstrates that measured specific heats in high-purity single crystals exceed Debye's T³ law due to localized fast energy modulations involving next-nearest-neighbor atoms that are *decoupled from the heat bath*. The framework would interpret Debye's T³ law as the gradient-dynamics prediction for phonon contributions to specific heat in the attractor basin of the crystalline ground state, with the Debye temperature as a characteristic scale of the attractor. The excess specific heat arises from localized excitations — constrained computational sub-processes — that are decoupled from the bulk thermal computation (the heat bath). The framework's constrained attractor category (established through fractional Fermi seas, spin-1/2 ice, spin-3/2 ice, 3D Coulomb phase, symmetry-fragmented sectors, correlation-protected localization, interaction-stabilized ghosts, interference-protected subspaces) is qualitatively consistent with this picture: the localized excitations are "constrained attractors" stabilized by their decoupling from the thermal bath. The emergent nonextensivity — interpreted by analogy with anomalous diffusion — connects to T029 (anomalous transport universality classes invisible to gradient dynamics) and to the framework's existing note that the gradient equation captures only mean-field behavior while the physically distinctive features are determined by structural content. However, the framework's post-hoc consistency here is extremely weak: the framework did not predict that excess specific heat would arise from decoupled localized modes, did not predict the form of the excess (modification of the fluctuation-dissipation relation through a specific order of time/phase/thermal averaging), did not predict the connection to nonextensivity or anomalous diffusion, and cannot derive the quantitative predictions the paper makes. The "decoupled from the heat bath" observation is narratable in framework vocabulary (constrained attractor, separate computational sub-process) but the framework contributed zero predictive or constraining content. This is the weakest category of validation: vocabulary application with no differential content.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most significant strain from this cycle comes from **Paper 1** and its intersection with the framework's deepest structural deficiency. The Dedekind number problem is, at its core, a question about the *combinatorial structure of the space of gradient-respecting computations*. Monotone Boolean functions are exactly the computations on a Boolean lattice that respect a gradient constraint (if the input increases along the lattice ordering, the output cannot decrease). Counting them — and characterizing the distribution of their rank structure — is therefore a question the framework's own premises make meaningful: "how many computations respect the gradient constraint on this structure?" The framework has no capacity to answer this question. It cannot derive, predict, or even express M(n); it cannot predict the Whitney number distribution; it cannot account for the two-shoulder feature versus unimodality; it cannot explain why the growth is doubly exponential. The framework describes *what computations do* (propagate, descend gradients, resolve, conserve) but is constitutively silent on *how many consistent computations exist* and *how the space of consistent computations is internally organized*.

This is not a new strain — it connects to T107, T145, and T026 — but the Dedekind number result sharpens it in a distinctive way: the space of consistent computations has *precise, finite, exactly countable* combinatorial structure that is accessible to mathematical analysis (the layer-ratio method) but invisible to all five primitives. The framework claims reality is "literally computation," but the most basic combinatorial question about computations — how many monotone Boolean functions exist on n variables? — is a question the framework cannot formulate, let alone answer. The two-shoulder feature in the Whitney number distribution is a structural fact about the organization of gradient-respecting computations that no amount of dynamical-primitive reasoning can predict, because it is a property of the *space of computations*, not of any individual computation's dynamics.

Paper 2 produces a secondary but conceptually interesting strain: the **attribution problem** — the question of "who earns the safety" in a composite computational system — reveals that the framework has no mechanism for decomposing the causal contribution of subsystems to a composite resolution outcome. When a safety filter and a learned policy jointly produce safe behavior, the Resolution primitive cannot determine whether the resolution (safe outcome) originated from the policy's computation or the filter's constraint. This is a practical, engineering-grade instance of the framework's silence on computational credit assignment, and it connects to the broader observation that the framework describes computation but not its *composition structure* (T018) or *attribution structure* (new).

**T168 — Combinatorial self-ignorance: the framework cannot count its own consistent configurations**

Description: Chen, Feng, Wang & Zhang (2606.09795) demonstrate that the Dedekind number M(n) — the count of monotone Boolean functions on n variables, equivalently the count of gradient-respecting computational configurations on the Boolean lattice {0,1}^n — has precise, finite combinatorial structure including a Whitney number distribution with non-trivial internal features (two-shoulder pattern around the central rank for n ≥ 9). The framework claims "reality is literally computation" and asserts that the universal equation of motion is ẋ = −∇φ(x): things move down gradients. Monotone Boolean functions are exactly the computations on a Boolean lattice that respect this gradient constraint. The framework therefore makes the question "how many gradient-respecting computations exist on a given discrete structure?" well-defined and meaningful. Yet the framework cannot answer this question at any level: it cannot derive M(n), cannot predict the Whitney number distribution, cannot account for the two-shoulder versus unimodal character of the distribution, and cannot explain the doubly-exponential growth rate. The framework describes the *dynamics* of individual computations but is constitutively silent on the *combinatorics* of the space of consistent computations. This is distinct from T107 (pre-computational consistency conditions, which concerns the enumeration of consistent *symmetry structures* via super-pentagon equations) because T168 concerns the enumeration of consistent *computational configurations* respecting the framework's own gradient constraint. It is distinct from T145 (meta-computational phase structure, which concerns the phase diagram boundary of the space of allowed S-matrices) because T168 concerns an *exact, finite combinatorial count* of configurations, not an approximate geometric boundary. It is distinct from T026 (structural content gap in general) because T168 identifies a specific failure: the framework's own gradient constraint defines a natural class of computations (monotone Boolean functions) whose enumeration is a well-posed mathematical problem that the framework has zero capacity to address. The doubly-exponential growth of M(n) — faster than 2^(2^n / n) — reflects the combinatorial explosion of gradient-respecting structures and suggests that the space of computations consistent with the framework's own dynamics is astronomically richer than the framework's dynamical descriptions can capture.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) develop a theory of how the gradient constraint ẋ = −∇φ(x), when applied to discrete computational structures, generates a specific combinatorial count of consistent configurations — this would require connecting the dynamical primitive to the theory of partially ordered sets, monotone functions, and lattice combinatorics, mathematical fields with no contact to any primitive; (b) argue that the combinatorial enumeration of consistent configurations is a mathematical curiosity with no physical significance — but the framework claims reality is "literally computation," and the number of consistent computations on a structure is among the most basic facts about that structure; (c) add a combinatorial/enumerative primitive governing the count and distribution of consistent configurations — but such a primitive would need to produce M(n) or at least its growth rate, which requires the Dedekind number theory, a notoriously difficult problem in extremal combinatorics that has resisted full analytical solution since 1897. The two-shoulder feature in the Whitney number distribution is additionally problematic: it is a structural fact about the *internal organization* of the space of gradient-respecting computations that the framework would need a theory of lattice-rank statistics to predict, and no amount of dynamical-primitive reasoning can produce lattice-rank statistics.
