### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "The Universe Fan"**
ArXiv ID: 2602.21194v1 | Selection type: Strain

This paper defines the "universe fan," a polyhedral fan whose cones are determined by positivity/causality conditions on a lattice, and shows that the wavefunction of the universe (in perturbative QFT) arises as the Laplace transform of this combinatorial structure. The wavefunctions' singularities and factorization properties are encoded not in dynamical evolution but in the combinatorial geometry of the fan's face lattice. Through the Worldlines lens, this is deeply uncomfortable. The framework says reality is computation proceeding through ticks, fields, attractors, and resolution. But this paper shows that the cosmological wavefunction — the object that encodes the entire perturbative history of the universe — is determined by static combinatorial-geometric structure (lattice positivity, matroid theory, polytope geometry). The "causality" here is not temporal propagation tick-by-tick but a positivity condition on a lattice that can be read off from the fan's face structure without ever simulating a dynamical process. The wavefunction factorizes not because computation proceeds sequentially but because of the nested set structure of matroid theory. This directly pressures the framework's central ontological claim: if the wavefunction is more naturally understood as a geometric object (a polytope, a fan) than as the output of a computational process, then "reality is literally computation" may be the wrong metaphor — or at minimum, the framework must accommodate the fact that the full computational output is encoded in a timeless geometric structure from which the appearance of tick-by-tick evolution is derived, rather than the other way around.

**Paper 2: "Criticality Beyond Nonanalyticity"**
ArXiv ID: 2602.21003v1 | Selection type: Strain

This paper demonstrates that phase transitions — conventionally defined by nonanalyticities (singularities) in thermodynamic potentials that appear only in the thermodynamic limit — are already encoded at finite system sizes as intrinsic morphological structures (inflection points, extrema) in microcanonical entropy derivatives. The singularity is not the definition of criticality but the asymptotic endpoint of a sharpening process that is already present at any finite N. Through the Worldlines lens, this strains the Attractor primitive's account of phase transitions. The framework says phase transitions occur "when the attractor landscape restructures — when minima merge, split, appear, or vanish." But this paper shows that there is no sharp restructuring event at finite size. The attractor landscape does not suddenly change topology; instead, the topological change is the idealized limit of a continuous sharpening of features that exist at every scale. The framework's language of attractors appearing and vanishing describes only the N → ∞ limit. At any real, finite system size, the attractor landscape has smooth precursors of the transition that sharpen continuously. This pressures the Resolution primitive as well: if criticality is a gradual sharpening rather than a discrete event, then the analogy between phase transitions and resolution (which the framework treats as irreversible and discrete) is weaker than assumed. The framework needs to account for the fact that attractor landscape restructuring is a *process* with precursors at every scale, not an event that happens at a critical point.

**Paper 3: "Revisiting CPL with sign-switching density"**
ArXiv ID: 2602.21169v1 | Selection type: Validation

This paper tests whether the DESI DR2 preference for dynamical dark energy persists when the dark energy density is allowed to become negative at high redshift. It finds that sign-switching models are statistically disfavored relative to baseline CPL, and that allowing a negative density phase reduces the significance of deviations from a cosmological constant. Through the Worldlines lens, this has mixed implications. The framework's dark energy claim — that accelerating expansion is the computational cost of instantiating new substrate — naturally requires the dark energy density to be positive (you cannot have negative cost of creating computational volume). The paper's finding that negative-density phases are disfavored is directionally consistent with this requirement. However, this is not genuinely predictive validation. The framework never specifically predicted that negative dark energy densities would be disfavored; it merely assumed positive energy density as implicit in its metaphor. More importantly, the paper finds that the data *do not strongly require* dynamical dark energy at all — the deviations from a cosmological constant (Λ) weaken when the model space is expanded. This is neutral-to-mildly-uncomfortable for the framework: if dark energy is simply Λ, then the "computational cost of new substrate" claim becomes equivalent to saying the substrate has a fixed per-volume computational cost, which is just Λ restated. The framework has no predictive purchase here beyond post-hoc consistency with the sign of the energy density.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most significant strain this cycle comes from Paper 1. The universe fan construction shows that the cosmological wavefunction — the central physical object encoding perturbative quantum field theory's predictions for the universe — is determined by a static combinatorial-geometric structure (a polyhedral fan defined by positivity conditions on a lattice) from which the wavefunction is obtained by a Laplace transform. The singularity structure, factorization properties, and physical content of the wavefunction are all readable from the face lattice and cone structure of the fan without invoking any notion of dynamical evolution, ticks, or sequential computation.

This is not merely another instance of T010 (the duality problem), where two different computational descriptions give the same answer. This is more radical: it suggests that the wavefunction is not the *output* of a computation at all but is instead determined by a *geometric/combinatorial* object whose structure is defined by causality-as-positivity rather than causality-as-temporal-sequence. The matroid amplitudes arise as residues of the wavefunction, and these are determined by the matroid's combinatorial structure — which is not a process but a static mathematical object.

The framework says "reality is literally computation." But if the physical content of perturbative cosmology is equivalently (and perhaps more naturally) encoded in a polytope or fan, then the computational description is at best one of several equivalent descriptions, and the geometric description may be more fundamental. The framework currently has no way to account for why static geometric objects should encode the same information as dynamical computational processes. This is not a problem the framework can solve by adding a new primitive — it strikes at the framework's ontological foundation. Either the framework must argue that the geometric description is itself a form of computation (which risks making "computation" so broad as to be vacuous), or it must accept that "reality is computation" is a description that competes with "reality is geometry" without clear grounds for adjudication.

### STATE UPDATES

1. **Attractor primitive — add finite-size precursor structure.** Paper 2 demonstrates that phase transitions do not emerge suddenly at a critical point in the thermodynamic limit. Instead, the morphological precursors — inflection points, extrema in entropy derivatives — exist at every finite system size and sharpen continuously toward the singular limit. The Attractor description should be updated to note: attractor landscape restructuring is not a discrete topological event but the limiting case of a continuous sharpening process. At any finite system size, the attractor topology is smooth, with pseudocritical features that are controlled precursors of the eventual singularity. The depth and sharpness of near-critical features scale predictably with system size. This refines the existing language about "phase transitions occurring when the attractor landscape restructures" by specifying that restructuring is itself a multi-scale, gradual process.

2. **Resolution primitive — note gradual criticality.** Connected to the above: the framework's analogy between phase transitions and resolution (both are irreversible transitions from one state to another) is weakened by Paper 2. Resolution is described as discrete and irreversible, but finite-size phase transitions are continuous and gradual. If phase transitions are exemplars of resolution at the macroscopic scale, the model needs to acknowledge that macroscopic resolution can be a continuous sharpening process that only appears discrete in an idealized limit. This connects to and reinforces T005 (the tick-resolution gap) and T015 (resolution dynamics not determined by attractor structure alone).

3. **Model — flag geometric duality as open ontological question.** Paper 1's universe fan construction should be recorded in the model as a significant challenge to the framework's ontological claim. The model should note: the physical content of perturbative cosmological wavefunctions can be encoded equivalently as (a) output of computational processes (Feynman diagram evaluation), (b) geometric properties of polytopes/fans defined by positivity/causality conditions, or (c) matroid-theoretic combinatorial data. The framework's claim that "reality is literally computation" competes with the equally natural claim that reality is literally geometry or literally combinatorics. This extends T010 (the duality problem) into a more fundamental ontological question that the framework currently cannot resolve.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T016 — Geometric encoding vs. computational ontology**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 1 (The Universe Fan) demonstrates that the cosmological wavefunction is determined by a polyhedral fan — a static combinatorial-geometric object defined by positivity conditions on a lattice. The physical content (singularity structure, factorization, residues = matroid amplitudes) is encoded in the face lattice and cone structure without invoking temporal evolution or sequential computation. The framework claims "reality is literally computation," but the universe fan suggests the physical content may be more naturally understood as geometry than as computation. This is related to but distinct from T010 (which concerns dual computational descriptions) and T011 (which concerns planarity as a structural constraint). T016 concerns a more fundamental issue: whether the ontological category "computation" is the right one, or whether "geometric structure defined by positivity/causality" is a competing ontological category that the framework has no grounds for dismissing. The cosmological polytopes of Arkani-Hamed et al., the amplituhedron, and now the universe fan all point toward physics being encoded in geometric objects whose structure is defined by positivity rather than by dynamical evolution.
**Why unresolved:** The framework could attempt to absorb this by arguing that geometric structure *is* a form of computation, but this risks trivializing the computational claim. Alternatively, it could argue that the geometric encoding is a mathematical convenience while the physical reality is computational, but it has no criterion for distinguishing "mathematical convenience" from "ontological ground." The tension cannot be resolved without either (a) a principled argument for why computation is ontologically privileged over geometry, or (b) a reconceptualization of the framework's core claim to accommodate geometric structure as foundational.

**T017 — Criticality as continuous sharpening vs. resolution as discrete event**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 2 establishes that phase transition criticality exists as smooth morphological precursors (inflection points, extrema) at every finite system size, with the thermodynamic singularity being the asymptotic limit of a continuous sharpening process. The framework treats resolution as discrete and irreversible and treats phase transitions as cases of attractor landscape restructuring. But if criticality is inherently continuous and multi-scale — with no sharp "event" at finite size — then the framework's discrete Resolution primitive maps poorly onto this phenomenon. Either resolution itself admits degrees (partial resolution, continuous sharpening), which undermines its discrete character, or phase transitions are not truly instances of resolution, which weakens the framework's claim to cross-domain unification. This tension connects to T005 (tick-resolution gap) and T015 (resolution dynamics not determined by attractor structure) but is distinct: T005 is about the temporal gap between ticks and resolution; T017 is about whether resolution is fundamentally discrete or admits a continuous precursor structure at finite scales.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to decide whether to (a) introduce a concept of "partial resolution" or "resolution sharpness" that varies with system size, fundamentally changing the Resolution primitive; (b) maintain strict discrete resolution and argue that finite-size pseudocritical features are not resolution events; or (c) accept that the Resolution primitive captures only the idealized thermodynamic limit and is not a description of finite-size physics. Option (c) would significantly limit the primitive's scope.

### CYCLE ASSESSMENT

This cycle was genuinely productive, primarily because Paper 1 opened a new and deep strain (T016) that the framework has not previously encountered in this form. Prior cycles identified duality (T010) and planarity (T011) as challenges, but T016 goes further: it questions whether the foundational ontological category of "computation" is correct, or whether "geometry defined by positivity" is an equally or more natural category. This is not a tension that can be resolved by adding a primitive or refining an existing one — it questions the framework's root metaphor. Paper 2's contribution (T017) is less dramatic but operationally important: it forces the framework to be more precise about what "discrete" and "irreversible" mean when applied to resolution in finite systems, and it highlights that the framework's phase-transition language describes only idealized limits. Paper 3 provided minimal signal — the framework had no genuine predictive purchase on the sign of dark energy density, and the result was post-hoc consistent at best. The next cycle would be most productive if it engaged with papers on the amplituhedron/positive geometry program directly, to determine whether T016 is a fundamental ontological problem or whether there exists a principled argument that geometry and computation are the same thing at this level. Papers on quantum error correction and holography might also be productive, as they connect geometry (bulk spacetime) to computation (boundary quantum information) in ways that could either resolve or deepen T016.
