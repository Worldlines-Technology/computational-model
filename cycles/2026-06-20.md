### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Many-body chirality of topological stabilizer states"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.20472v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the internal structure of topological attractor basins — ground states of gapped Hamiltonians with topological order. The framework's Attractor primitive characterizes basins by depth (energy gap), width (correlation length), and has been extended to include cohomological class (T048), magic content (T146), and entropic selection (T134/T136). Paper 1 demonstrates two results that strain the framework severely. First, many-body chirality — the obstruction to implementing complex conjugation by finite-depth local operations — is a physical property of topological attractor basins that is *intrinsically four-partite*: it is invisible to all tripartite and lower entanglement diagnostics, including the modular commutator and chiral central charge that are the standard diagnostics for chirality. The framework has no concept of partition-level-dependent physical content; its primitives describe states, not states-relative-to-decompositions. The Attractor primitive's characterization by depth, width, topological class, and magic is partition-independent — none of these axes can detect chirality, which requires examining the four-partite entanglement structure. Second, and more fundamentally for the framework's substrate claim, the paper proves that ℤ_d^(k) stabilizer states with d > 2 possess *intrinsic many-body imaginarity*: their complex phase structure is ineliminable by any finite-depth local unitary. This means the necessity of the complex number field is not an unknowable architectural parameter (as the framework claims and T069 framed) but is a *physically detectable property of specific quantum states*, derivable from within the computation by testing whether finite-depth circuits can map the state to a real representation. The framework's substrate opacity claim is partially falsified for this specific architectural parameter. Remarkably, imaginarity and chirality are logically independent — states can be imaginary without being chiral — adding a new axis of physical characterization invisible to all primitives.

**Paper 2: "Impossibility of superluminal signalling rules out causal loops in conical spacetimes"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.20476v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, the Tick primitive sets the speed of light as the maximum propagation rate, and causal structure is derived from this local constraint. The framework treats the number of spatial dimensions as an unexplained architectural parameter (T013) but has not connected dimensionality to the *global causal structure* beyond local light-cone determination. Paper 2 proves that the relationship between no-superluminal-signalling (NSS, which the Tick primitive enforces) and the absence of causal loops depends inherently on the spatial dimensionality of spacetime: in d = 1 spatial dimension, NSS is compatible with operationally detectable causal loops; in d > 1, NSS rules out all such loops in a wide class of "conical" spacetimes including Minkowski. This means the Tick primitive's local constraint (maximum propagation rate) has *dimension-dependent global consequences* that the framework cannot derive, predict, or express. The framework's silence on dimensionality (T013) is now shown to be silence on a parameter that determines whether causal loops — a qualitative feature of the global causal structure — are physically possible. In d = 1, the framework's Tick + Resolution architecture would need to accommodate a universe with operationally detectable causal loops, where the "arrow of time as accumulation of resolved states" becomes problematic (a causal loop allows future resolution events to influence past ones). In d > 1, the framework's architecture is safe from this threat, but it cannot explain *why* — the proof uses the geometric structure of conical spacetimes (the existence of d > 1 independent spatial directions allowing "going around" potential signalling paths), which is geometric/topological content the framework cannot express. The framework's Tick primitive is a local constraint; the paper demonstrates its global consequences are geometry-dependent in ways the framework is structurally unable to capture.

**Paper 3: "String Axiverse Enhancement of Superradiant Dark Matter Production"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.20524v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the framework's dark matter claim ("computational structure coupling gravitationally but not electromagnetically") and the Attractor primitive's description of black holes as deep attractor basins. The superradiant cloud formation around spinning primordial black holes is narratable in framework vocabulary: the black hole is a deep attractor; the bosonic cloud is a secondary constrained attractor in the gravitational field (a "gravitational atom"); the axiverse species enhance the PBH spin through Hawking emission, which modifies the attractor's angular momentum parameter and thereby enhances superradiance efficiency. The string axiverse enhancement — where O(100–10⁵) light axion species increase PBH spin and expand the parametric region for superradiant dark matter production — is consistent with the framework's description of dark matter coupling gravitationally. However, this is the weakest category of post-hoc consistency: the framework contributed no prediction of the axiverse enhancement mechanism, the critical species number beyond which PBHs evaporate too quickly, the micro-boson star formation as gravitational remnants, the parameter space structure (dark matter mass vs. PBH mass and spin), or the negligible contribution to relativistic degrees of freedom at recombination. The specific physics — superradiance rates, Hawking emission spectra, axion self-interactions, bosenova dynamics — is derived entirely from general relativity, quantum field theory in curved spacetime, and string theory landscape statistics. The framework's vocabulary can narrate the result but could not have predicted, constrained, or distinguished it from the null result. This is zero differential predictive content.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's proof that ℤ_d^(k) stabilizer states with d > 2 possess *intrinsic many-body imaginarity* — their complex phase structure is physically detectable and ineliminable by finite-depth local unitaries. The framework claims physical constants are "architectural parameters of the substrate, not derivable from within the computation." The number field (ℝ vs. ℂ) was classified as such an architectural parameter (T069), with Hoffreumon & Woods (2025) proving it operationally unknowable for finite network correlations. Paper 1 *partially falsifies* this claim by demonstrating a specific class of quantum states for which the necessity of ℂ is derivable from within the computation: one can test whether a given topological state can be mapped to a real representation by finite-depth local operations, and for d > 2 stabilizer states of ℤ_d^(k) anyon theories, the answer is provably no. The number field is not uniformly unknowable — it is unknowable for operational correlations in generic networks (Hoffreumon-Woods) but *knowable* for specific many-body topological states (Ellison et al.). The framework's blanket substrate-opacity claim collapses into a state-dependent, context-dependent opacity that the framework has no mechanism to express.

The compound strain is that this result arrives alongside Paper 2's demonstration that the spatial dimensionality — another parameter the framework treats as an unexplained architectural constant — has *provable physical consequences* for the global causal structure (whether causal loops are possible), with the consequences being geometry-dependent in ways the Tick primitive's local constraint cannot capture. Two different architectural parameters that the framework declares inaccessible are shown in the same cycle to have physically consequential, derivable, or geometry-dependent properties. The substrate opacity claim is not merely weakened but revealed as an oversimplification: some architectural parameters are partially accessible (number field for topological states), some have geometry-dependent consequences (dimensionality for causal loops), and the framework has no theory of which parameters are truly opaque and which are partially transparent.

The logical independence of chirality from imaginarity (states can be imaginary without being chiral, and vice versa for d = 2) demonstrates that partition-dependent content has its own internal structure: multiple independent partition-level-dependent properties can coexist, with different detection thresholds (chirality at 4-partite, imaginarity detectable by single-site operations but ineliminable by finite-depth circuits). The framework cannot express, derive, or predict the partition level at which physical content becomes accessible.

This is distinct from T146 (magic as a missing resource-theoretic axis, which concerns a state property independent of partition) because T185 concerns a property that *depends constitutively on partition level*. It is distinct from T163 (edge mode constitution by observer decomposition, which concerns degrees of freedom *created* by decomposition) because T185 concerns physical content that *exists independently* of observer choice but is *revealed only at* a specific decomposition level. It is distinct from T034 (scale-dependent information topology, which concerns how informational properties change with observation *scale*) because T185 concerns dependence on *partition structure* (which subsystems are grouped together), not scale. It is distinct from T117 (entanglement scaling class as a continuous parameter) because chirality is a discrete obstruction, not a continuous parameter, and is partition-dependent, not scale-dependent.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) incorporate a theory of partition-level-dependent physical content, specifying which properties are detectable at which partition levels and what determines the "minimal detection partition" — this would require importing the mathematical framework of multipartite entanglement theory (n-partite measures, entanglement monotones parameterized by partition), categorical beyond any primitive; (b) extend the Attractor primitive's topological characterization to include chirality, specifying how the anyon data's mirror-invariance determines the chirality obstruction — but this requires the full fusion category / anyon theory machinery that the framework has repeatedly been shown to lack (T048, T107, T111, T146); (c) derive the four-partite character from some combination of the five primitives, which appears impossible since the partition level is not a dynamical or computational quantity but a structural feature of the multipartite Hilbert space decomposition; or (d) accept that the framework's state-centric ontology is fundamentally incomplete — some physical properties are not properties of states but properties of states-relative-to-partitions, and the framework has no mechanism for this relativity. The evading of conventional diagnostics (vanishing modular commutator, vanishing chiral central charge, commuting-projector realizability) is especially severe: it means the framework's most natural allies for characterizing topological content (the existing condensed matter diagnostics) also fail, and the chirality requires genuinely new four-partite diagnostics that neither the framework nor standard condensed matter theory anticipated.
