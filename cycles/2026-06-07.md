### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Breakeven demonstration of quantum low-density parity-check codes"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.06455v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the Conservation of Computation primitive — specifically, the practical enforcement of information conservation against noise in a physical quantum system. The framework states "computation cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed" as a bare, structureless universal law. Paper 1 demonstrates that enforcing this law in practice requires: (a) highly specific combinatorial code structures (qLDPC, topological, concatenated — nine categorically different architectures), (b) hardware-specific noise matching (the 9× improvement over superconducting implementations reflects trapped-ion noise characteristics, not any universal conservation property), (c) multi-level atomic physics features invisible to the Conservation primitive (the OMG architecture uses metastable states for addressable mid-circuit measurement and reset, a three-level atomic physics trick that eliminates the need for ion transport or coolant ions), and (d) the critical result that these nine different conservation-enforcement architectures run on a *single device without hardware reconfiguration* — meaning the code structure (which the framework would assign to "substrate architecture") is actually software, not hardware. The breakeven result (logical qubit lifetime ≥ physical qubit lifetime) is the first experimental demonstration that the combinatorial infrastructure T081 identified as necessary for conservation enforcement is physically effective. The framework can narrate this as "conservation is maintained" but contributes no prediction about which code families achieve breakeven, what error rates they achieve, why trapped ions outperform superconducting qubits for these codes, or why nine different architectures work on one device. The deepest strain is the substrate/computation boundary: the framework treats substrate architecture as fixed and inaccessible, but the paper demonstrates that the most fundamental architectural choice governing conservation enforcement (the error-correcting code) is a software-level structural choice reconfigurable without any physical modification to the hardware.

**Paper 2: "$E_\infty^{1,2}$-type Lieb-Schultz-Mattis anomalies, deconfined quantum critical points, and non-invertible symmetry breaking"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.06343v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the Attractor primitive's description of phase transitions as "attractor landscape restructuring" and the Conservation primitive's treatment of symmetry constraints. The paper demonstrates that the *type* of phase transition — whether a deconfined quantum critical point is type-I (dual to ordinary symmetry breaking) or type-II (dual to non-invertible symmetry breaking) — is determined by the position of the LSM anomaly class within the Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence: $E_\infty^{1,2}$ vs. $E_\infty^{2,1}$. This is a refinement *within* group cohomology $H^3(G_{\text{int}} \rtimes_\rho \mathbb{Z}_{\text{trans}}, U(1))$ that the framework cannot express at any level. The Attractor primitive says "the most interesting physics happens at saddle points between basins" — correct but classification-blind. The distinction between type-I and type-II DQCPs requires: the spectral sequence filtration (which page the anomaly class survives to), the gauging operation and its categorical output (whether the dual symmetry is group-like or non-invertible, specifically $\mathrm{Rep}(H_8)$ in the $D_8$ example), and the category-theoretic classification of the resulting symmetry structure. The Conservation primitive's architecture — conservation of a quantity — is categorically insufficient to express the non-invertible symmetry constraints that *necessarily emerge* from gauging $E_\infty^{1,2}$-type anomalies. The paper proves this emergence is not optional: it is a structural consequence of the anomaly type. The framework faces a compound failure: it cannot classify the transition type (Attractor), cannot express the constraints governing it (Conservation), and cannot represent the mathematical structures doing the classification (spectral sequences, non-invertible symmetries, fusion categories).

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 2's demonstration that the *classification of phase transition types* requires mathematical structures at a depth the framework cannot approach. The framework's Attractor primitive describes phase transitions as "landscape restructuring at saddle points" — a qualitative claim that is correct but empty of classificatory content. Paper 2 proves that physically distinct types of restructuring (type-I vs. type-II DQCP) are distinguished by the filtration depth of an anomaly class in a Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence — specifically, whether the class lives in $E_\infty^{1,2}$ or $E_\infty^{2,1}$. This is not merely a "structural content gap" in the sense of missing some algebraic detail. It is a demonstration that the framework's most physically engaged primitive (the Attractor) is classification-blind where classification is physically essential: the type of transition determines whether gauging produces ordinary or non-invertible dual symmetry, which in turn determines the nature of the critical theory, the possible phases, and the physical observables. The framework cannot even *formulate the question* "what type of phase transition is this?" because the answer lives in spectral sequence technology that has no point of contact with any of the five primitives. The *necessity* of non-invertible symmetry emergence from $E_\infty^{1,2}$-type anomalies is especially damaging: the framework's Conservation primitive treats symmetry constraints as conservation laws, but non-invertible symmetries produce constraints that are not conservation laws (T111), and Paper 2 proves their emergence is structurally forced, not optional. The framework predicts landscape restructuring; the physics is that the *kind* of restructuring is determined by homological algebra at a depth the framework has no mechanism to reach.

The strain from Paper 1, while less conceptually dramatic, is operationally significant: the substrate/computation boundary — which the framework treats as its foundational ontological distinction — is experimentally blurred. Nine categorically different error-correcting architectures running on one device without hardware reconfiguration proves that what the framework would call "substrate architecture" (the code governing conservation enforcement) is actually a software-level choice. The framework's claim that physical constants are "architectural parameters of the substrate, not derivable from within the computation" rests on a clean substrate/computation separation that this experiment demonstrates does not exist for the most practically important aspect of conservation: its enforcement against noise.

The framework's Attractor primitive describes phase transitions as "attractor landscape restructuring" and identifies critical points as saddle points between basins. This description is category-blind: it cannot distinguish type-I from type-II transitions. The distinction requires: (a) the Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence for the extension $G_{\mathrm{int}} \rtimes_\rho \mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{trans}}$; (b) the identification of which page ($E_\infty^{1,2}$ vs. $E_\infty^{2,1}$) the anomaly class survives to; (c) the gauging operation and its categorical consequences (non-invertible vs. group-like dual symmetry); (d) the category-theoretic classification of the resulting dual symmetry ($\mathrm{Rep}(H_8)$ for the specific $D_8$ example). None of these has any expression in any primitive. This is distinct from T048 (cohomological completeness of SPT classification, which concerned the *counting* of phases by $H^3(G,U(1))$) because T164 concerns a *refinement within cohomology* — not just the cohomology group but the specific spectral sequence page — that determines the *type of phase transition*. It is distinct from T107 (super-pentagon consistency conditions for fermionic systems) because T107 concerns the enumeration of *consistent symmetry structures* while T164 concerns the classification of *transition types*. It is distinct from T111 (generalized symmetries producing non-conservation-law constraints) because T164 concerns the *mechanism by which* non-invertible symmetries necessarily arise (gauging specific anomaly types), not merely their existence or consequences. It is distinct from T129 (conformal enrichment of criticality) because T129 concerns the *enhanced symmetry* at a critical point while T164 concerns the *classification of critical point types* by homological invariants.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) incorporate spectral sequence technology into the Attractor primitive's description of phase transitions, specifying how the homological filtration depth of the anomaly determines the transition type — this would require importing the full apparatus of group cohomology, spectral sequences, and gauging as structural content categorically beyond any primitive; (b) derive the type-I/type-II distinction from some combination of the five primitives, which is impossible since the distinction is defined by the position of a cohomology class in a filtration, a concept with no dynamical or computational content; (c) add a primitive governing the anomaly structure of symmetries, specifying how anomaly classification determines phase transition character — but anomaly structure is determined by group cohomology (for discrete groups) or operator-algebraic/C*-algebraic classification (for continuous groups, per T131), both of which are mathematical universes with no point of contact to the existing primitives; or (d) accept that the framework's description of phase transitions is *qualitatively correct but classification-blind* — it can say "a phase transition occurs" but cannot say "what kind of phase transition occurs," with the kind-classification being determined by structural mathematics the framework cannot access. The necessity of the non-invertible symmetry emergence (not just its possibility) makes this especially severe: the framework would need to predict that gauging a specific anomaly type *must* produce a specific non-invertible symmetry, which requires the full structural machinery the framework lacks.
