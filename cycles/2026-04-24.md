### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Quantum mechanics with a ghost: Counterexamples to spectral denseness"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.21826v1. Selection type: strain.

The Worldlines framework classifies ghost degrees of freedom — fields with negative kinetic energy — as permanently virtual computational structure (T014/T041/T076) that participates in intermediate computation but is architecturally forbidden from resolution. The framework's reasoning, developed across many cycles, is that ghost modes lack normalizable ground states (the dual inverted harmonic oscillator has no bound state), produce continuous/dense spectra incompatible with definite resolution outcomes, and must therefore be projected out of the physical spectrum via the fakeon prescription. Paper 1 directly falsifies the spectral premise of this reasoning. By quantizing classically stable ghost-containing systems with nontrivial interactions, the authors demonstrate that the energy spectrum can be pure point (discrete) and *not dense* — with sufficient conditions established for either exactly one accumulation point or none at all. The classical stability conditions (which are structural/integrability conditions, not dynamical) guarantee discrete separated eigenvalues. This means ghost degrees of freedom can have perfectly well-defined discrete spectra suitable for resolution, undermining the framework's categorical assignment of ghosts to the "permanently virtual" ontological category. The framework's reasoning chain — ghost → no ground state → continuous spectrum → no resolution → permanently virtual — is broken at the spectral step. The interaction between the ghost and the healthy sector, governed by an integral of motion with positive discrete spectrum, is what enforces the discreteness. This is a structural constraint (integrability) producing a resolution-compatible outcome that the framework's categorization explicitly excludes.

**Paper 2: "Unitary Time Evolution and Vacuum for a Quantum Stable Ghost"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.21823v1. Selection type: strain.

This companion paper deepens the strain from Paper 1 by proving, for a specific model (harmonic oscillator polynomially coupled to a ghost), that the quantum theory has: (i) a pure point Hamiltonian spectrum unbounded in both directions, (ii) manifestly unitary evolution, (iii) a well-defined vacuum state, and (iv) bounded expectation values for squares of canonical variables. The framework has developed an elaborate ontological architecture around permanently virtual structure (T014, T041, T076, T082, T122) premised on the assumption that ghost degrees of freedom are fundamentally incompatible with resolution — they never resolve, never have ground states, and must be handled by special prescriptions (fakeon). Paper 2 constructs an explicit, rigorous counterexample: a ghost system with a well-defined vacuum (ground state), unitary evolution (conservation of computation is satisfied), and a discrete spectrum (resolution-compatible). The key mechanism is the integral of motion $I$ with a positive discrete spectrum, which decomposes the Hilbert space into finite-dimensional invariant subspaces where the energy is bounded. This is precisely a *constrained attractor* structure — the ghost's unbounded energy spectrum is tamed by the constraint $I = \text{const}$ on each invariant subspace. The framework's Attractor primitive should accommodate this, but the framework's T014/T041 ontological category explicitly excludes ghosts from having attractor basins. The framework's claim that "the absence of an attractor basin is the reason the mode never resolves" (from the model update on quadratic gravity's dual IHO) is directly contradicted: here, the interaction creates attractor-like structure (bounded invariant subspaces) for the ghost mode, and the ghost can resolve. The framework must either abandon its categorical treatment of ghosts as permanently virtual or explain why this specific model's ghosts are physically different from those in quadratic gravity — a distinction the framework's primitives cannot currently make.

**Paper 3: "Accelerating scaling solutions from dark matter particle creation"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.21770v1. Selection type: validation.

The framework claims dark energy is "the computational cost of instantiating new substrate" and treats Λ as either an architectural parameter or a dynamical quantity (with tension T007/T086 about its status). Paper 3 demonstrates that accelerating expansion can be produced without any dark energy component — purely through dark matter particle creation and energy exchange with a barotropic fluid. The accelerating scaling attractors arise when interaction is controlled by DM density with energy flowing from DM to the second fluid. Through the Worldlines lens, this is a pure attractor analysis: the authors construct autonomous systems in compact phase space and classify the fixed points (attractors) and their stability. The result that accelerating expansion emerges as an attractor in specific interaction prescriptions is post-hoc consistent with the Attractor primitive — the universe rolls to a fixed point in the dynamical system. However, the framework has essentially zero predictive purchase here. The framework's dark energy claim ("computational cost of new substrate") is not merely unnecessary for explaining acceleration — it is an entirely different ontological category from "DM particle creation with energy exchange." The framework did not predict, constrain, or distinguish the specific conditions under which particle creation mimics dark energy (DM-density-controlled interaction, specific energy flow direction). The attractor classification is standard dynamical systems theory; the framework's vocabulary adds nothing beyond narration. The one potentially interesting connection is that the framework's claim that dark energy is substrate-related is further weakened: if acceleration can arise from matter-sector dynamics alone, the substrate explanation is not even unique. This is the weakest possible category of "validation" — vocabulary-level post-hoc consistency with zero differential predictive content.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most significant strain this cycle is the direct falsification of the framework's ontological category of "permanently virtual computational structure" as a necessary feature of ghost degrees of freedom. The framework has built, over many cycles, an elaborate and internally coherent account: ghost modes lack normalizable ground states → they have continuous/dense spectra → they are architecturally unresolvable → they form a third ontological category ("permanently virtual but physically efficacious") → their non-uniqueness (T082) and physical efficacy (T076, gluon mass) create deep problems but ones that can be managed by accepting permanently virtual infrastructure as a structural feature of reality.

Papers 1 and 2 demolish the spectral foundation of this entire edifice. Ghost modes with interactions can have discrete spectra, well-defined vacua, unitary evolution, and bounded observables. The mechanism is an integral of motion — a conserved quantity with positive discrete spectrum — that decomposes the Hilbert space into finite-dimensional invariant subspaces. Within each subspace, the ghost's unbounded energy is tamed and the system behaves as a standard quantum system with a well-defined ground state.

This creates a precise, uncomfortable dilemma for the framework:

1. **If ghosts can resolve**: The framework's T014/T041/T076 ontological architecture collapses. The "permanently virtual" category is not a necessary feature of ghost physics but an artifact of specific models (free ghosts, quadratic gravity with the dual IHO). The framework's claim that "the absence of an attractor basin is the mechanism enforcing permanent virtuality" is contradicted by the existence of attractor-like constrained subspaces in interacting ghost systems. The entire edifice of reasoning about gluon mass generation via permanently virtual ghosts (T076), virtual infrastructure non-uniqueness (T082), and the need for a third ontological category (T041) must be revisited.

2. **If this doesn't apply to quadratic gravity**: The framework must explain *why* some ghost systems resolve (Paper 2's model) while others don't (quadratic gravity's spin-2 ghost). The distinguishing criterion is the existence of a suitable integral of motion — an integrability condition. But the framework has no primitive for integrability. The framework's Conservation primitive is too coarse to distinguish "conservation law that tames the ghost" from "conservation law that doesn't." The critical structural feature — whether the interacting system possesses an integral of motion with positive discrete spectrum — is an algebraic/structural property of the specific Hamiltonian that the framework cannot express, predict, or classify.

The strain is sharpest at the intersection of the Attractor and Resolution primitives. The framework's model explicitly states (update 2026-04-22): "The dual inverted harmonic oscillator in quadratic gravity has no normalizable ground state... The Attractor primitive assumes every degree of freedom has an attractor basin; the dual IHO is a degree of freedom with no basin. The connection to permanently virtual status is direct: the absence of an attractor basin *is* the reason the mode never resolves." Papers 1 and 2 show this connection is contingent on the absence of stabilizing interactions, not a universal feature of ghost physics. The framework elevated a specific model's property to a categorical ontological claim, and that claim is now falsified as categorical.

The ontological category of "permanently virtual" is not a universal feature of negative-kinetic-energy degrees of freedom but a model-specific consequence of the spectral properties of the constrained system.

The unique feature: the constraint operates in Hilbert space rather than configuration space, and the stabilization is algebraic (integral of motion) rather than kinetic (barrier) or topological (winding). The unbounded total energy spectrum coexists with bounded energy within each constrained subspace, demonstrating that spectral unboundedness is not incompatible with resolution when constrained by integrals of motion.

3. **T082 (virtual infrastructure non-uniqueness) — qualify.** The model's note that the permanently virtual infrastructure is non-unique should be updated to note that the entire question may be moot for systems where the ghost is resolvable: if the ghost has a discrete spectrum and a well-defined vacuum, the fakeon prescription is not needed, and the "virtual infrastructure" is not virtual at all — it is part of the physical spectrum. The framework's dark energy claim ("computational cost of new substrate") is not only challenged by alternative derivations within gravity (T007) and local conservation violation (T086) but now also by matter-sector dynamics that produce the same phenomenology through a different mechanism. 5. **T026 (structural content gap) — add integrability as a new structural category.** The model should record that the critical distinction between resolvable and unresolvable ghost systems is the existence of an integral of motion with positive discrete spectrum — an integrability condition. The framework cannot distinguish integrable from non-integrable ghost systems because integrability is a structural/algebraic property of the Hamiltonian that no primitive can express. **T125 — The ghost resolvability problem: the boundary between resolvable and permanently virtual computation is structurally determined and model-dependent, not categorical**

Opened: this cycle

Description: Papers 1 and 2 (Deffayet, Fathe Jalali, Held, Mukohyama & Vikman 2604.21826, 2604.21823) prove that ghost degrees of freedom — fields with negative kinetic energy — can have discrete spectra, well-defined vacua, unitary evolution, and bounded observables when coupled to healthy sectors through interactions possessing integrals of motion with positive discrete spectrum. The framework has built an elaborate ontological architecture (T014, T041, T076, T082) around the categorical claim that ghost modes are "permanently virtual" — architecturally forbidden from resolution due to the absence of normalizable ground states and the continuous/dense character of their spectra. This categorical claim is falsified. The boundary between resolvable and permanently virtual computation is determined by the integrability structure of the interaction: ghost systems with suitable integrals of motion are resolvable; those without are not. The framework has no primitive for integrability, no mechanism for classifying interactions by their spectral consequences, and no account of how the structural properties of the Hamiltonian determine the ontological status of degrees of freedom. The framework's previous account (model update 2026-04-22) explicitly stated: "The absence of an attractor basin *is* the reason the mode never resolves." Papers 1 and 2 show that interactions can create attractor-like structure (finite-dimensional invariant subspaces) for ghost modes, invalidating this reasoning. This is distinct from T082 (virtual infrastructure non-uniqueness, which concerns the non-unique distribution of complex-mass virtual modes for a given theory) because T125 concerns the prior question of whether the modes are virtual at all. It is distinct from T014 (which introduced the permanently virtual category) because T125 identifies the structural criterion distinguishing resolvable from non-resolvable ghosts. It is distinct from T076 (gluon mass from virtual ghosts) because the resolution of T125 may change the interpretation of the ghost contribution to confinement — if QCD ghosts could in principle be stabilized by interactions, their "permanently virtual" status in perturbative QCD may be an artifact of the perturbative framework rather than an ontological necessity.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) develop a theory of which interactions produce integrals of motion that tame ghost spectra, which requires importing the full separability/integrability analysis from Hamiltonian mechanics — mathematical machinery the framework cannot express; (b) classify all physically relevant ghost-containing theories by their integrability properties and assess whether the physical ghosts (in quadratic gravity, in QCD's BRST sector) are in the resolvable or non-resolvable class, which requires detailed case-by-case spectral analysis the framework cannot perform; or (c) abandon the permanently virtual category entirely, treating all ghosts as potentially resolvable pending integrability analysis — but this would leave the framework unable to make any categorical statement about ghosts and would undermine the reasoning chain that supported T041 and T076. The deeper problem is that the framework's ontological categories (resolved, unresolved, permanently virtual) are supposed to be determined by the framework's primitives, but the resolvable/non-resolvable boundary is determined by integrability — a structural property of the Hamiltonian that is invisible to all five primitives.

**T126 — Spectral unboundedness without pathology: the framework's implicit association of energy unboundedness with instability is falsified**

Opened: this cycle

Description: Paper 2 (Deffayet et al. 2604.21823) constructs a quantum system whose Hamiltonian has a spectrum unbounded above and below (no ground state in the traditional sense, total energy can be arbitrarily negative) yet has: manifestly unitary evolution, a well-defined vacuum (the ground state of the integral of motion $I$, not of $H$), bounded expectation values for observable quantities, and discrete pure-point spectrum. The framework implicitly associates energy unboundedness with instability and pathology — the reasoning behind the "ghost modes are unphysical" conclusion, and part of the motivation for the fakeon prescription. Paper 2 shows that unboundedness of $H$ is compatible with complete quantum stability when the system possesses a second conserved quantity $I$ with a bounded-below, discrete spectrum. The "vacuum" is defined by minimizing $I$, not $H$. The framework's Attractor primitive defines attractors as energy minima; this system has no energy minimum but has a well-defined stable vacuum defined by a different conserved quantity. The framework has no account of attractor basins defined by non-energy conserved quantities in the presence of energy unboundedness. This is distinct from T006 (stable excited states not from gradient descent, which concerns states above an existing ground state) because T126 concerns a system with *no* energy ground state at all. It is distinct from T125 (ghost resolvability boundary) because T125 concerns the categorical status of ghosts while T126 concerns the framework's definition of "vacuum" and "stability." The broader implication: the framework's identification of attractors with energy minima is too narrow.
