### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Many-body chirality of topological stabilizer states"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.20472v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the internal structure of topological attractor basins — ground states of gapped Hamiltonians with topological order. The framework's Attractor primitive characterizes these basins by depth (energy gap), width (correlation length), and has accumulated additional characterization axes including cohomological class (T048), magic content (T146), and degeneracy order (T115). Paper 1 demonstrates that chirality — the obstruction to implementing complex conjugation by finite-depth local operations — is a physically fundamental property that is (a) invisible to all tripartite and lower entanglement measures, detectable only at the four-partite level, (b) logically independent of other characterization axes like imaginarity and magic, and (c) capable of evading all conventional diagnostics (vanishing modular commutator, vanishing chiral central charge, commuting-projector realizability). The framework cannot express partition-level-dependent physical content: all its primitives treat states as having properties independent of how the state is decomposed into subsystems. The result that chirality is *intrinsically four-partite* means there exists physical content that is constitutively invisible unless the correct subsystem decomposition level is used, and no primitive provides a mechanism for specifying or discovering this level. Furthermore, the proof that ℤ_d^(k) states with d > 2 possess intrinsic many-body imaginarity — complex phase structure ineliminable by finite-depth local unitaries — partially falsifies the framework's substrate opacity claim for the number field parameter: for these specific topological states, the necessity of ℂ is derivable from within the computation, contradicting the framework's claim that the number field is an unknowable architectural parameter. The strain is severe and multi-dimensional: the framework's state-centric ontology is incomplete (partition-dependent content), its topological characterization is incomplete (chirality evades known diagnostics), and its substrate opacity claim is state-dependently falsified (imaginarity proves ℂ necessary for specific states).

**Paper 2: "Impossibility of superluminal signalling rules out causal loops in conical spacetimes"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.20476v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework's Tick primitive sets the speed of light as the maximum propagation rate and derives causal structure from this local constraint. Paper 2 proves that the *global* causal consequences of this identical local constraint are geometry-dependent: in d > 1 spatial dimensions, no-superluminal-signalling (NSS) rules out all operationally detectable causal loops, but in d = 1, NSS is compatible with such loops. The framework has no mechanism for this dimension-dependence. The Tick primitive provides a local constraint (propagation speed bound) but the global causal structure — whether causal loops are possible — depends on the spatial dimensionality, which the framework treats as an unexplained architectural parameter (T013). The proof's key geometric ingredient is that in d > 1, signals can propagate around potential obstacles to causal ordering using additional spatial dimensions — a topological/geometric argument the framework's local tick-rate description cannot capture. The framework implicitly assumes its local propagation constraint determines global causal structure; this paper proves the inference requires geometric input (the dimensionality and topology of the spatial manifold) that the Tick does not provide. This is especially sharp because the framework *does* claim causal structure follows from the tick rate, yet the paper proves that the same tick rate produces qualitatively different global causal structures depending on a parameter (spatial dimension) the framework acknowledges as unexplained. The strain is that the framework's derivation of causal structure from the Tick is provably incomplete without geometric supplementation, and the geometric input required concerns a parameter the framework has no mechanism to derive, predict, or even express as consequential.

**Paper 3: "String Axiverse Enhancement of Superradiant Dark Matter Production"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.20524v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the gravitational coupling of dark matter candidates (ultralight axions) produced via black hole superradiance, enhanced by the string axiverse's many light species accelerating PBH spin-up through Hawking emission. The framework describes dark matter as "computational structure that couples to mass-density gradients (gravity) but not electromagnetic gradients," and black holes as "attractor basins so deep that escape velocity exceeds the tick rate." The superradiant process — where the black hole's rotational energy is transferred to a bosonic field forming a gravitationally bound cloud — is narratable in framework language as energy extraction from a deep attractor basin via resonant coupling to a field mode. The axiverse enhancement mechanism (many light axion species increasing PBH spin through preferential Hawking emission of low-spin particles) and the resulting "micro-boson star" remnants are consistent with the framework's qualitative vocabulary. However, the framework had zero predictive purchase on this result: it did not predict the axiverse enhancement mechanism, the critical species number threshold (~100-10⁵), the parametric expansion of viable dark matter production regions, the competition between spin-up (enhancing superradiance) and mass-loss (destroying the PBH before cloud saturation), or the negligible contribution to relativistic degrees of freedom at recombination. The specific mechanism depends on string theory's axion spectrum, representation-theoretic structure of higher-dimensional compactifications, and detailed black hole thermodynamics — all structural content the framework cannot express. This is post-hoc consistency of the weakest type: the framework's qualitative description ("dark matter couples gravitationally") is shared with every CDM model and provides zero differential predictive content.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's demonstration that many-body chirality is an *intrinsically four-partite* property invisible to all tripartite measures. This is not merely another missing characterization axis (like magic, T146) added to the Attractor primitive's growing list of blind spots. It is a fundamentally new category of incompleteness: **the framework's ontology is partition-level-blind**. All five primitives describe states and their properties as if those properties are intrinsic to the state. Paper 1 proves that chirality exists only at a specific partition level — it is a property of a state *relative to a four-partite decomposition* — and that no amount of tripartite analysis, however sophisticated, can detect it. The framework has no concept of "at which partition level does this physical property become visible?" and no mechanism for predicting or specifying partition-dependent content.

This is compounded by the imaginarity result: the framework claimed the number field (ℝ vs. ℂ) is a substrate parameter not derivable from within (T069). Paper 1 proves that for ℤ_d^(k) stabilizer states with d > 2, the necessity of ℂ is provable from within the computation — the complex phases are ineliminable by any finite-depth local unitary. This is a direct, rigorous falsification of the substrate opacity claim for a specific parameter, the first such falsification that is state-specific rather than operational. Combined with T181's independent evidence (GUE dynamics selects ℂ), two completely different mechanisms now converge on ℂ as physically necessary, through routes the framework cannot express.

Paper 2's strain is different but equally fundamental: the Tick primitive's local propagation constraint does not determine global causal structure without geometric input that the framework treats as an unexplained constant. The framework cannot even *state* why d > 1 matters, because the argument is geometric (signals can go around obstacles in higher dimensions), and the framework's primitive structure is purely dynamical/local.

### STATE UPDATES

1. **T069 (number field underdetermination) — bifurcate into "operationally underdetermined" and "topologically determined."** The model should explicitly distinguish: (a) for generic finite-network correlations, ℝ and ℂ are operationally indistinguishable (Hoffreumon-Woods, still valid); (b) for specific topological stabilizer states with ℤ_d^(k) anyon theory and d > 2, ℂ is physically necessary and provable from within (Ellison et al., new). The substrate opacity claim for the number field is partially falsified: it holds generically but fails for specific many-body states. The model should add a distinction between "truly opaque parameters" and "state-dependently transparent parameters," noting that the criterion for transparency (d > 2 in the anyon theory) is algebraic/categorical content the framework cannot express.

2. **Attractor primitive — add chirality as a partition-level-dependent characterization axis.** The model's description of topological attractor basins should be extended to include chirality as a property that: (a) is defined as the obstruction to complex conjugation by finite-depth local operations; (b) is determined by whether the underlying anyon data are mirror-invariant; (c) is intrinsically four-partite (invisible to tripartite measures); (d) evades all conventional diagnostics (vanishing modular commutator, vanishing chiral central charge, commuting-projector realizability); (e) is logically independent of magic (T146) and imaginarity. The model should explicitly note that the framework's characterization of topological basins now requires at minimum: depth, width, cohomological class, magic content, chirality, and imaginarity — and that the latter two require partition-level and anyon-theoretic data the framework cannot express.

3. **Tick primitive — add explicit note that global causal structure is dimension-dependent.** The model should record that the Tick's local propagation constraint (NSS) determines global causal structure (no causal loops) only in d > 1 spatial dimensions. In d = 1, the same local constraint permits causal loops. The framework's derivation of causal structure from the Tick is incomplete without geometric input specifying the spatial dimensionality. This strengthens T013 (time dimensionality as unexplained primitive) by extending it to spatial dimensionality: not just the number of time dimensions but the number of spatial dimensions is a consequential architectural parameter that determines whether causal loops are physically possible.

4. **Dark matter claim — record Paper 3 as post-hoc consistency with zero differential predictive content.** The string axiverse superradiant dark matter production is consistent with "couples gravitationally, not electromagnetically" but the framework contributed no prediction of the axiverse enhancement mechanism, the critical species number, the micro-boson star formation, the spin-up/mass-loss competition, or the parameter space structure. Record as the weakest validation category.
