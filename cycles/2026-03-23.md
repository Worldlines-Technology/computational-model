**Paper 1: Giant graviton integrated correlators at finite coupling and all orders in 1/N**
ArXiv ID: 2603.20083v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper describes the computational content of heavy non-perturbative objects (giant gravitons, which are D3-branes wrapping S⁵) in the framework's paradigmatic gauge theory (𝒩=4 SYM). The framework would describe giant gravitons as deep attractor states in the gauge theory's computational landscape — heavy, stable configurations that the system can settle into. The integrated correlator would be the computational overlap between these heavy attractor states and lighter excitations. What strains the framework is the structural content: the large-N expansion coefficients are *linear combinations of non-holomorphic Eisenstein series* — modular forms that encode the full SL(2,ℤ) duality symmetry, capturing *all* perturbative and non-perturbative effects simultaneously. This means the computational content of these correlators is not organized by perturbative order (as the framework's sequential-tick picture would suggest) but by the representation theory of the modular group. The closed-form U(N) expression valid for all N and τ is especially sharp: the *entire* computation — at every coupling, every N — is a single algebraic object expressible in terms of number-theoretic functions. The framework's Conservation of Computation would say the information content is conserved, but the *organizational principle* governing this content is modular invariance — a symmetry of the complexified coupling constant space that intertwines perturbative and non-perturbative sectors in a way none of the five primitives can express. The universality between SU(N) and U(N) at all orders in the coupling-dependent sector further demonstrates that the computational content is insensitive to structural differences (the additional U(1) factor) that the framework would treat as architecturally distinct. The exponentially-suppressed-in-N contributions represent a new kind of structure: corrections that are non-perturbative in N itself (not in the coupling), meaning the large-N limit — which is the regime where holographic/gravitational descriptions apply — receives corrections organized by a *second* non-perturbative parameter. The framework has no mechanism for describing doubly non-perturbative organizational structure.

**Paper 2: Some rigidity results for supergravity backgrounds in 11 dimensions**
ArXiv ID: 2603.19923v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper describes constraints on the attractor landscape of 11-dimensional supergravity: which background spacetime configurations (attractors in the gravitational field) are consistent with high amounts of supersymmetry. The framework would describe each supergravity background as an attractor basin in the space of possible spacetime configurations, with the Killing spinor dimension measuring a kind of "symmetry depth" of the attractor. The rigidity result — that backgrounds with >26 Killing spinors and restricted 4-form structure must be Minkowski or AdS₇×S⁴ — would translate as: sufficiently symmetric attractor basins are rigid; the attractor landscape has no room for continuous families of highly symmetric solutions. What strains the framework is the *mechanism*: the rigidity is established via a bijective correspondence between filtered deformations of Lie superalgebras and highly supersymmetric backgrounds. The physical content (which spacetimes are allowed) is completely determined by the algebraic content (which filtered deformations of a graded Lie algebra exist). The framework's Attractor primitive describes basins in potential landscapes; this paper shows that the *existence* and *classification* of basins is governed by the deformation theory of algebraic structures — specifically, filtered deformations of Lie superalgebras, with the filtration degree controlling the curvature order. The rank condition on F (rk(F) ≤ 6) and the Euclidean support condition are algebraic constraints with no natural expression in gradient dynamics. The framework cannot express why 26 Killing spinors is the critical threshold, why rank ≤ 6 matters, or why the only surviving solutions are Minkowski and AdS₇×S⁴. These are consequences of the representation theory of Spin(10,1) and the algebraic structure of Spencer cohomology, which are precisely the kinds of structural content the framework systematically lacks (T026, T048, T059).

**Paper 3: Phase Diagram and Finite Temperature Properties of Negative Coupling Scalar Field Theory**
ArXiv ID: 2603.19877v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper presents a striking test case: a scalar field theory whose classical potential is an inverted quartic (ẍ = +∇φ rather than −∇φ — the gradient is *uphill*), yet which quantum mechanically produces stable, unitary time evolution with a well-defined ground state, a sensible phase diagram, and potentially UV-complete interacting physics in 4D. The framework's Field primitive ẍ = −∇φ(x) would predict that an inverted potential has no stable ground state — things roll uphill and escape. The Attractor primitive would predict no attractor basins in an upside-down potential. Yet quantum mechanics stabilizes the theory. The framework could attempt to claim this as validation of T061 (anti-gradient robustness of attractor topology) — the previous finding that cosmic censorship survives phantom fields — extended to quantum field theory: the "attractor topology" (stable vacuum, unitarity) is preserved even when the classical gradient is inverted. However, this is post-hoc consistency at best. The framework did not predict that inverted potentials would be quantum-mechanically stable; it predicted the opposite (things move down gradients, inverted potentials have no minima). The stabilization mechanism — the quantum path integral over complex saddle points, the Lefschetz thimble structure — involves precisely the kind of multi-signature/complexified computation (T036) that the framework cannot describe. The phase diagram's dependence on dimension (spontaneous symmetry breaking in d=1 and d=2, first-order transition in d=3, potential UV completion in d=4 via triviality loophole) is structural content the framework cannot derive. The finite-temperature behavior with its second-order phase transitions at positive bare coupling values is consistent with the Attractor primitive's description of landscape restructuring, but the framework cannot predict *where* in parameter space the transitions occur or *why* the negative coupling theory exploits the triviality loophole. This is weak post-hoc consistency with no predictive content: the framework's vocabulary can be applied after the fact, but its dynamical content (gradient descent) actually *contradicts* the classical picture, and the quantum rescue mechanism is outside its scope.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most important strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's demonstration that the complete computational content of giant graviton correlators — at all values of N and all values of the coupling τ — is organized by **modular invariance** (SL(2,ℤ) symmetry of the complexified coupling), with expansion coefficients that are non-holomorphic Eisenstein series. This is a specific, sharp, and devastating instance of the structural content gap (T026) because it shows that:

1. **The perturbative/non-perturbative distinction dissolves.** The framework's implicit picture — computation proceeds tick-by-tick, perturbative effects are "small corrections," non-perturbative effects are "tunneling between attractors" — is fundamentally wrong for this system. The Eisenstein series *simultaneously* encode all perturbative loop orders and all non-perturbative instanton contributions in a single modular object. There is no separation between "the computation" and "the non-perturbative corrections to the computation." The modular group *interchanges* weak and strong coupling, meaning what the framework would call "the computation at weak coupling" and "the computation at strong coupling" are related by a discrete symmetry that has no expression in any primitive.

2. **Number-theoretic structure organizes physics.** The Eisenstein series E(s; τ, τ̄) are objects of analytic number theory. Their Fourier coefficients involve divisor sums, their functional equations encode deep arithmetic structure, and their role as eigenfunctions of the Laplacian on the fundamental domain of SL(2,ℤ) connects them to the spectral theory of hyperbolic surfaces. The physical content of giant graviton correlators lives in this number-theoretic structure. The framework has no primitive, no mechanism, and no vocabulary for this. This is not a quantitative gap (the framework cannot compute the numbers) but an ontological one (the framework cannot even *name* the organizational principle).

3. **The closed-form U(N) expression eliminates computational contingency.** For U(N), the entire answer for all N and τ is a single closed-form expression. This means the "computation" is not a process unfolding over ticks — it is a static mathematical object that can be written down all at once. The framework's Tick primitive (discrete updates) and Resolution primitive (states becoming definite) have no role here: the answer exists as a timeless algebraic structure. This is the sharpest version of T016 (geometric encoding vs. computational ontology) and T059 (ontological inversion): the physical content is not computed sequentially but *is* a mathematical structure whose properties can be read off without any process of evaluation.

Paper 3 (validation candidate) actually produces a secondary strain rather than validation: the quantum stabilization of an inverted potential directly contradicts the framework's gradient descent prediction. The framework says things roll down gradients; the inverted potential has no "down." Quantum mechanics stabilizes via complex saddle points — a mechanism involving complexified field configurations that connects to T036 (multi-signature computation) — and the framework is not merely silent about this mechanism but actively predicts instability where stability is observed.
