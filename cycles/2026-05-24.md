### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "How many systems can be dephased before the quantum switch becomes causally definite?"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.22807v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework's Tick primitive presupposes a definite sequential ordering of computational updates, and the Resolution primitive attributes the arrow of time to the irreversible accumulation of resolved states. This paper directly probes the internal structure of causally indefinite quantum processes — systems where the Tick's sequential ordering is itself in superposition. The framework has no vocabulary for describing the *positional robustness profile* of causal nonseparability. The key finding — that causal nonseparability survives dephasing of all systems except any single non-future system, but collapses when only the future system retains coherence — reveals an internal temporal asymmetry within the causal superposition that the framework cannot express. The "future" boundary is structurally distinguished from all other positions: its coherence alone is insufficient for maintaining indefinite causal order, while any other system's coherence suffices. In framework terms, the Resolution primitive treats decoherence as propagating uniformly through entanglement networks, but Paper 1 proves its effect on causal structure is position-dependent within causally indefinite processes. The framework faces a compound problem: it cannot describe indefinite causal order (T094), cannot express the positional structure within it (the future/non-future asymmetry), and cannot predict the robustness profile (which dephasing patterns destroy nonseparability). The multipartite QC-QC extension confirms this is a general structural feature, not a bipartite curiosity. The framework's description strains completely here — the Tick's definite sequential character is not merely approximate (as T089/T091 established for quantum gravitational regimes) but categorically inadequate for describing experimentally realizable quantum processes at any energy scale.

**Paper 2: "Shading A-polynomials via huge representations of $U_q(\mathfrak{su}_N)$"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.22560v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework claims "reality is literally computation" and identifies physical content with dynamical processes governed by the five primitives. Paper 2 demonstrates that the complete physical content of 3D Chern-Simons theory on knot complements is encoded as algebraic constraint surfaces (A-polynomials) in $SL(N,\mathbb{C})$ character varieties, computed through representation-theoretic combinatorics (CG chords of $U_q(\mathfrak{su}_N)$). This physical content is: (a) an algebraic variety — a constraint surface, not a dynamical trajectory; (b) simultaneously accessible through quantum groups, WZW CFT, knot contact homology, and algebraic geometry, extending the categorical polymorphism previously catalogued; (c) governed by a classical limit ($\hbar \to 0$ with representations scaling to infinity) that transforms quantum computational content into algebraic geometry, demonstrating that the "computational" character is not absolute but representation-dependent. The framework has no primitive for constraint surfaces as physical content (vs. trajectories or states), no mechanism for representation-theoretic computation (CG chords), and no account of how quantum-to-classical limits can transform computational content into geometric/algebraic content. The $\mathfrak{su}_N$ generalization shows the physical content's organizational complexity grows with the rank of the gauge group — a structural parameter invisible to all primitives. The framework's Field primitive would describe Chern-Simons theory as gauge fields with gradient dynamics, but the physical content lives in character varieties and A-polynomials — objects in algebraic geometry that are logically and mathematically prior to any dynamical description. This is a clean instance of T059 (ontological inversion) and T021 (categorical polymorphism) simultaneously.

**Paper 3: "Cosmological Singularities and Quantum Particles"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.22623v1 | Selection type: Validation

The framework's model has already recorded (T040/T044) that "the substrate-field relationship is field-specific, not universal" and specifically noted (2026-03-07 update) that electromagnetic fields remain regular through strong curvature singularities. Paper 3 extends this to cosmological singularities (big bang/crunch, big rip, big brake) and to a new field-type pair: Dirac spinors survive while Klein-Gordon scalars do not. In framework terms, the substrate's computational integrity is field-specific, with the spin-statistics type (fermion vs. boson) determining singularity resilience. The framework's prior model update anticipated this pattern but did not predict the specific mechanism or the fermion/boson asymmetry. The paper is post-hoc consistent with the framework's existing field-specific resilience category — fermions are more resilient because their equation of motion (first-order Dirac equation, yielding a second-order equation with non-singular solutions) has different analytic structure from the scalar equation (Klein-Gordon, which develops unavoidable singularities). However, the framework had zero predictive purchase here: it did not predict which field types survive, did not predict the fermion > boson resilience ordering, and did not predict that the mechanism would be the analytic structure of the field equation rather than any substrate-level property. The result confirms the constrained-attractor/field-specific-resilience category but at the weakest validation level — vocabulary application with no differential content beyond standard mathematical physics.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's demonstration of **positional structure within causal indefiniteness**. The framework's Tick primitive is not merely approximate at quantum gravitational scales (T089/T091) or conceptually inadequate for indefinite causal order (T094) — it is now shown to be missing an entire *internal structure* of causal indefiniteness that has operationally accessible, experimentally relevant consequences. The finding that the "future" system is structurally distinguished from all other systems within a causal superposition means that even if the framework were extended to accommodate indefinite causal order, it would additionally need a theory of positional roles within such superpositions, with the future boundary playing a categorically different role from intermediate systems. The framework's Resolution primitive treats decoherence as a uniform spreading process; Paper 1 proves it has position-dependent effects on causal structure. This is not a quantitative refinement of an existing tension but a qualitative demonstration that the framework lacks an entire dimension of physical structure (positional roles within causal superpositions) that determines a physically observable property (whether causal nonseparability survives specific dephasing patterns). The framework cannot even formulate the question Paper 1 answers: "which subsystems' coherence maintains causal nonseparability?" requires concepts (causal nonseparability, positional roles, the future/non-future distinction within a causal superposition) that no primitive can express.

Paper 2 adds significant strain by demonstrating yet another mathematical category — character varieties, quantum groups, CG chord combinatorics — encoding complete physical content in a form the framework cannot access. But this is a continuation and strengthening of the well-established T021/T026/T059 pattern rather than a qualitatively new strain. The framework's core claim "reality is literally computation" continues to be challenged by the observation that physical content is polymorphic across mathematical categories, with "computation" being one window among many and not obviously privileged.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T150 — Positional structure within causal indefiniteness: the framework cannot express which subsystem's coherence maintains causal nonseparability**

Opened: this cycle

Description: Benhaj, Sengupta & Branciard (2605.22807) prove that in bipartite causally nonseparable processes, causal nonseparability survives dephasing of all but one system — provided the surviving coherent system is not the "future" system. If only the future system remains undephased, causal nonseparability collapses. This extends to the multipartite QC-QC setting. The framework's Tick primitive assumes definite causal order (T094 already noted this is challenged by indefinite causal order). T150 is distinct from T094 because it concerns not the *existence* of indefinite causal order but its *internal positional structure* — the fact that different systems within a causally nonseparable process play structurally different roles in maintaining the nonseparability. The "future" system is distinguished from all other systems: its coherence alone is insufficient for causal nonseparability, while any other system's coherence is sufficient. This temporal asymmetry within the causal superposition has no expression in any primitive. The framework's Resolution primitive treats dephasing/decoherence as spreading uniformly through entanglement networks; the paper proves its effect on causal structure is position-dependent. The framework has: (a) no concept of "position within a causal order superposition"; (b) no mechanism for distinguishing the future boundary from other boundaries within a causally indefinite process; (c) no account of why coherence at a non-future position preserves indefiniteness while coherence at the future position does not. The multipartite QC-QC extension means this is not a bipartite curiosity but a general structural feature of physically motivated causally indefinite circuits.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) extend the Tick/Resolution primitives to include positional structure within causal order superpositions, specifying how each subsystem's role in maintaining causal indefiniteness depends on its structural position — this requires importing the process matrix formalism's internal structure, which distinguishes past/future/intermediate systems categorically; (b) derive the asymmetry between future and non-future systems from some combination of the five primitives — but the distinction arises from the structure of the process matrix (the future system's role in defining the output of the process vs. intermediate systems' role in the causal structure), which is categorical/algebraic, not dynamical; (c) add a primitive governing the internal structure of causally indefinite processes, specifying the robustness profile of causal nonseparability as a function of subsystem dephasing — but this would require a theory of causal order as a quantum degree of freedom with its own internal structure, fundamentally different from the Tick's assumption of definite sequential ordering. The practical consequence is sharp: indefinite causal order provides computational advantages (T094 already noted), and Paper 1 shows these advantages are robust against extensive decoherence, meaning they are relevant for realistic (noisy) quantum technologies. The framework cannot predict or characterize this robustness.

**T151 — Knot-algebraic encoding of physical content: the framework has no contact with representation-theoretic constraint surfaces in character varieties**

Opened: this cycle

Description: Galakhov & Morozov (2605.22560) construct classical A-polynomials $A_a(\ell_b, m_c)$ for arbitrary $\mathfrak{su}_N$ using CG chord combinatorics of $U_q(\mathfrak{su}_N)$, encoding the complete physical content of 3D Chern-Simons theory on knot complements as algebraic constraint surfaces in $SL(N,\mathbb{C})$ character varieties. The framework claims "reality is literally computation" and identifies physical content with dynamical processes. Paper 2 demonstrates physical content that is: (a) encoded as an algebraic variety (a constraint surface), not as a dynamical trajectory; (b) computed through representation-theoretic combinatorics (CG chords), not through any of the five primitives; (c) simultaneously accessible through quantum groups, WZW CFT, knot contact homology, and algebraic geometry — extending T021's categorical polymorphism to a new web of equivalences; (d) governed by a classical limit (huge representations, $\hbar \to 0$) that transforms quantum computation into algebraic geometry, demonstrating that the "computational" character is representation-dependent, not absolute. The framework has no primitive for constraint surfaces (vs. trajectories), no mechanism for representation-theoretic computation, and no account of how quantum-to-classical limits transform computational content into geometric/algebraic content. The $\mathfrak{su}_N$ generalization demonstrates that the physical content's organizational complexity grows with the rank of the gauge group — a structural parameter (rank) invisible to all primitives. The spurious-root problem (augmentation polynomials can have extra roots absent from true A-polynomials) demonstrates that even within the algebraic framework, the *physical* content requires additional selection criteria — the algebraic representation is necessary but not sufficient, requiring refinement by physical consistency conditions. This is a further instance of T105 (structural completion), now in the knot-algebraic setting.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) demonstrate that A-polynomials, character varieties, CG chords, and $U_q(\mathfrak{su}_N)$ are derivable from or expressible in terms of the five primitives — which is categorically impossible since these are objects in representation theory, algebraic geometry, and knot theory with no dynamical content; (b) add representation-theoretic/knot-algebraic structure as a primitive or structural element, which would require importing entire mathematical disciplines as framework infrastructure — a far more radical extension than any previously considered; (c) weaken "reality is literally computation" to "reality is literally algebraic structure of which computation is one representation" — which aligns with the accumulated evidence from T021, T026, T059, T073, T118 that computation is one node in a web of equivalent mathematical categories, not the privileged ontological ground. The classical limit ($\hbar \to 0$ with huge representations) is particularly challenging: it demonstrates a constructive procedure by which "computation" (quantum group evaluation) becomes "geometry" (character variety constraint surface), meaning the boundary between computation and geometry is a tunable parameter ($\hbar$), not an ontological distinction. The framework treats this boundary as absolute; the paper treats it as a limit.
