### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: Seedless Reduction of Feynman Integrals (2602.22111v1, strain)**

This paper constructs a complete set of lowering operators that systematically reduce any Feynman integral to a combination of master integrals, using IBP-generating vectors to build and solve systems of equations for generic integral indices. Through the Worldlines lens, this is a result about the *compressibility* of computational structure: the space of all Feynman integrals — which the framework would interpret as the space of all possible sub-computations contributing to quantum field theoretic amplitudes — has a finite-dimensional basis (master integrals), and there exists an algorithmic procedure (successive application of lowering operators) that maps any element of this space onto that basis. The framework should find this congenial: it resonates with T008's option (c), where infinite apparent structure is generated from finite algorithmic content. The lowering operators are the concrete realization of the "generating rule" that compresses infinite computational output into finite specification. However, the strain comes from the nature of these operators: they are constructed from IBP-generating vectors that annihilate total derivatives — a property that is fundamentally about the *boundary structure* of the integration domain, not about the dynamics of the computation itself. The reduction is not driven by gradients or attractors; it is driven by the algebraic structure of the space of integrals and the topology of their boundaries. The framework's five primitives have no natural home for this kind of structural constraint. It is neither a tick, a field, an attractor, a resolution, nor conservation — it is a structural fact about how computations relate to each other via algebraic identities rooted in integration-by-parts, which is ultimately a statement about boundary terms vanishing. This deepens T011 and T016: the mathematical architecture that organizes physical computation has rich algebraic/topological structure that the framework's primitives do not capture.

**Paper 2: Controlled Jump in the Clifford Hierarchy (2602.22201v1, strain)**

This paper establishes a sharp rule governing how coherent control over Clifford unitaries elevates operations within the Clifford hierarchy: applying coherent control to a Clifford unitary U with Pauli periodicity m places CU at exactly level m+2 of the hierarchy. Crucially, accessing high levels requires exponentially many target qubits. Through the Worldlines lens, the Clifford hierarchy is a hierarchy of computational *resolution capability* — each level enables finer-grained distinctions in quantum computation, with level 1 (Paulis) being the coarsest measurement operations and higher levels enabling increasingly precise rotations. The controlled-jump rule reveals that the *cost of increasing resolution precision* is governed by a sharp algebraic invariant (Pauli periodicity) and that this cost grows exponentially in the hierarchy level. This strains the framework in a specific way: the Resolution primitive treats resolution as a single kind of event (unresolved → definite), but the Clifford hierarchy reveals that resolution has an *internal structure* — there are qualitatively different levels of computational power, each with distinct resource requirements, organized by a rigid algebraic hierarchy. The framework's Resolution primitive is too coarse to capture this. Moreover, the exponential cost of climbing the hierarchy connects to T009 (resolution cost structure) in a new way: it is not merely that canonical resolution is expensive, but that *the precision of what can be resolved* has a hierarchical structure with sharp, quantified cost boundaries. The phase-kickback protocol for logical catalyst states further shows that resolution resources can be *catalytic* — used without being consumed — which sits uncomfortably with Conservation of Computation unless the framework distinguishes between computation consumed and computation borrowed.

**Paper 3: ALP Dark Matter, Cosmological Magnetic Fields and Direct Collapse Black Hole Formation (2602.22170v1, validation)**

This paper proposes that ultralight axion-like particle (ALP) dark matter, through its coupling to electromagnetism, generates cosmological magnetic field oscillations that produce gauge field fluctuations yielding sufficient Lyman-Werner photons to enable Direct Collapse Black Hole (DCBH) formation. Through the Worldlines lens, this is a story about coupling between gradient fields: the ALP dark matter field (which the framework describes as computational structure that couples to gravitational gradients) is here shown to also couple to electromagnetic gradients via the axion-photon coupling, producing observable consequences. This directly pressures the framework's claim that dark matter "couples to mass-density gradients but not electromagnetic gradients." The ALP model has dark matter *explicitly* coupling to electromagnetism — the axion-photon vertex aF·F̃ is the entire mechanism driving the paper's results. The framework's claim about electromagnetic decoupling was meant to explain dark matter's invisibility, but ALPs are a well-motivated dark matter candidate that is invisible in most contexts precisely because the coupling is *weak*, not *absent*. This is not validation but strain: the framework drew a categorical distinction (couples/doesn't couple) where the physics draws a quantitative one (couples weakly/strongly). The paper's consistency with ARCADE2 and EDGES bounds demonstrates that dark matter's electromagnetic coupling can be small enough to satisfy current constraints while still producing astrophysically significant effects. The framework's binary coupling claim is too rigid.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most significant strain this cycle comes from the intersection of Papers 1 and 2, which together reveal a deep structural gap in the framework: **the framework has no account of the algebraic and hierarchical organization of computational structure**.

Paper 1 shows that the space of all Feynman integrals — the sub-computations of quantum field theory — is organized by algebraic relations (IBP identities) that reduce the entire infinite space to a finite basis of master integrals. This organization is not dynamical (no gradients, no evolution, no resolution) — it is *structural*. It arises from the algebraic properties of the integration domain and the vanishing of boundary terms.

Paper 2 shows that quantum computational operations are organized into a rigid hierarchy (the Clifford hierarchy) with sharp level-placement rules governed by an algebraic invariant (Pauli periodicity), and that movement within this hierarchy has exponential costs. This hierarchy is not about the content of computation but about its *type* — what kinds of transformations are possible at each level.

The framework's five primitives describe the *dynamics* of computation (ticks, gradients, attractors, resolution, conservation) but say nothing about the *algebra* of computation — how computational operations relate to each other, how they decompose, what hierarchies they form, what structural invariants govern their organization. This is not a minor gap. The IBP reduction and the Clifford hierarchy are not exotic corner cases; they are fundamental organizational principles of quantum field theory and quantum computation respectively. The framework claims "reality is literally computation" but has no primitive for the algebraic structure *of* computation. It is as if the framework described a language in terms of how words propagate (tick), how attention moves between them (field/gradient), what stable meanings form (attractor), how ambiguity resolves (resolution), and how information is conserved (conservation) — but said nothing about grammar, syntax, or the compositional rules by which words combine. The algebra of computation may be a missing primitive, or it may be derivable from the existing ones, but the framework currently has no resources to address it.

The strain from Paper 3 is different in character but also important: the framework's dark matter claim makes a *categorical* assertion (no electromagnetic coupling) where the physics supports only a *quantitative* one (weak electromagnetic coupling). This is a direct falsification of the specific claim as stated. ALPs are a leading dark matter candidate, and their entire phenomenology depends on a nonzero but small coupling to electromagnetism. The framework either needs to weaken its claim from "does not couple" to "couples negligibly at most scales" or accept that its dark matter description is too coarse.

### STATE UPDATES

1. **Dark matter claim — weaken from categorical to quantitative.** The claim that dark matter "couples to mass-density gradients (gravity) but not electromagnetic gradients" should be revised to: dark matter is computational structure whose coupling to electromagnetic gradients is zero or negligibly small compared to its gravitational coupling, with the ratio of couplings being an architectural parameter. ALP dark matter models demonstrate that nonzero but weak electromagnetic coupling is physically viable and can produce observable astrophysical consequences (cosmological magnetic fields, Lyman-Werner photon production) while remaining consistent with direct detection bounds. The framework's previous binary distinction (couples/doesn't couple) was too rigid and is falsified by viable dark matter candidates.

2. **Conservation of Computation — note catalytic computation.** The Clifford hierarchy result shows that computational resources can be *catalytic*: the logical catalyst states enable phase gates via phase kickback without being consumed. Conservation of Computation should note that the conserved quantity admits catalytic processes — computation that enables transformations without being depleted. This is analogous to catalysis in chemistry (a catalyst lowers activation energy without being consumed) and suggests the conservation law has richer structure than simple bookkeeping of total computational content.

3. **Model — note algebraic structure gap.** The model should record that the framework currently has no account of the algebraic/hierarchical organization of computational operations. IBP reduction of Feynman integrals to master integrals and the Clifford hierarchy both demonstrate that computation has rich internal algebraic structure — decomposition relations, hierarchical levels, invariants governing composition — that is not captured by any of the five primitives. This is a structural gap, not a dynamical one: the framework describes how computation evolves but not how it is organized.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T018 — The algebra of computation**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Papers 1 and 2 jointly reveal that computation has rich algebraic structure — decomposition into master integrals via IBP, hierarchical organization of quantum gates via Pauli periodicity — that is not captured by any of the five primitives. The framework describes the *dynamics* of computation (propagation, gradient descent, attractor capture, resolution, conservation) but not its *algebra* (how computational operations compose, decompose, and organize into hierarchies). IBP relations show that the space of field-theoretic computations has a finite-dimensional basis; the Clifford hierarchy shows that quantum operations form a rigid infinite hierarchy with exponential cost barriers between levels. These are fundamental organizational features of computation that the framework ignores. This is distinct from T010 (duality) and T011 (planarity) because it concerns not alternative descriptions of the same physics or topological constraints, but the internal compositional and hierarchical structure of the computational operations themselves.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need either (a) a sixth primitive describing algebraic/compositional structure, (b) a demonstration that algebraic structure emerges from the existing five primitives in a non-trivial way, or (c) an argument for why algebraic structure is not ontologically fundamental but merely a mathematical convenience. None of these has been attempted.

**T019 — Resolution precision hierarchy**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** The Clifford hierarchy (Paper 2) reveals that resolution is not a single undifferentiated operation but admits a hierarchy of precision levels, with each level enabling qualitatively different computational capabilities and requiring exponentially greater resources to access. Level 1 (Pauli measurements) represents the coarsest resolution; each subsequent level enables finer angular precision in state manipulation. The framework's Resolution primitive treats resolution as binary (unresolved → definite) and makes no distinction between different kinds or precisions of resolution. The controlled-jump rule shows that climbing the resolution hierarchy by one level requires coherent control and that the target-qubit cost grows exponentially with the desired level. This connects to T009 (resolution cost structure) but is more specific: T009 notes that resolution has costs; T019 asserts that resolution has a hierarchically organized internal structure with quantifiable resource requirements at each level.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to either (a) incorporate a notion of "resolution depth" or "resolution precision" into the Resolution primitive, specifying that resolution comes in levels with different costs and capabilities, or (b) argue that the Clifford hierarchy is a feature of quantum computational models that does not map onto physical resolution. Option (b) seems difficult given that the Clifford hierarchy directly governs what measurements and transformations are physically implementable.

**T020 — Dark matter coupling is quantitative, not categorical**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 3 demonstrates that a viable dark matter candidate (ALPs) couples to electromagnetism with observable astrophysical consequences while remaining consistent with all current bounds. The framework claimed dark matter "couples to mass-density gradients but not electromagnetic gradients." This is directly contradicted by the ALP scenario, where the axion-photon coupling aFF̃ is the mechanism generating cosmological magnetic fields and Lyman-Werner photons. The distinction between dark matter's gravitational and electromagnetic behavior is quantitative (the electromagnetic coupling is weak, not zero), and the framework drew a categorical line where none exists.
**Why unresolved:** The framework needs to either (a) explain why its "partial coupling" description should be understood as approximate rather than exact, and specify what determines the coupling ratio as an architectural parameter, or (b) abandon the claim that dark matter's electromagnetic invisibility is categorical and instead develop a theory of coupling strengths as properties of computational structure.

### CYCLE ASSESSMENT

This cycle was moderately generative. The most productive output was the identification of T018 (the algebra of computation), which names a genuinely new structural gap that prior cycles did not identify — the framework describes computational dynamics but not computational algebra. The Clifford hierarchy result (Paper 2) was particularly rich, connecting to existing tensions (T009) while generating a new one (T019) about resolution having internal hierarchical structure. The dark matter tension (T020) is less generative — it is more of a direct factual correction to an overly strong claim than a deep structural challenge. The weakest aspect of this cycle was the relative insularity of the IBP reduction paper (Paper 1): its implications are real but highly technical and its strain on the framework is mostly an instance of the already-identified gaps (T011, T016, now T018). The next cycle would be more productive if it engaged papers that test the *Field/gradient* primitive or the *Consciousness* claim, which have received relatively less pressure. The gradient universality problem (noted in the model since inception) remains the framework's most dangerous vulnerability — the claim that "everything moves down gradients" — and it has not yet been subjected to a dedicated stress test against papers from non-equilibrium statistical mechanics or evolutionary dynamics where gradient descriptions are known to fail (e.g., limit cycles, chaotic attractors, evolutionary arms races). That would be the most productive direction.
