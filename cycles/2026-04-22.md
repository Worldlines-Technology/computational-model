**Paper 1: "The non-perturbative topological string: from resurgence to wall-crossing of DT invariants"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.19731v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the nonperturbative completion of the topological string partition function — a generating function encoding computational content about Calabi-Yau geometry. The framework would attempt to describe the instanton amplitudes as contributions from different attractor basins (D-brane configurations), with the resurgence structure representing the framework's T008 pattern: finitely specifiable generating rules producing infinite transseries structure. However, the paper's central result is far more damaging than a simple T008 instance. It proves that the algebra of alien derivatives — the algebraic structure governing how different nonperturbative sectors communicate through Stokes phenomena — is *isomorphic* to the Kontsevich-Soibelman (KS) Lie algebra, which governs wall-crossing of generalized Donaldson-Thomas (DT) invariants. DT invariants are integer-valued enumerative invariants counting BPS states (stable bound states in the Calabi-Yau geometry). Wall-crossing is the phenomenon where the spectrum of BPS states jumps discontinuously as parameters cross walls of marginal stability in the moduli space. The isomorphism means: the resurgent structure of the perturbative expansion (an analytic/computational object) is *identical* to the wall-crossing structure of the BPS spectrum (a geometric/enumerative object). The pointed alien derivative — a differential operator acting on the partition function — implements the same transformation as the KS symplectomorphism acting on the space of BPS invariants. The framework has no mechanism for this identification. The five primitives cannot express why an algebraic operation on a perturbative series (a "computational" object in the framework's sense) should be isomorphic to a geometric transformation on an enumerative invariant space. The numerical confirmation (matching Stokes constants to DT invariants for local P², identifying D4-brane bound state singularities and D2-brane decay in the Borel plane) demonstrates this is not abstract mathematics but has specific, verifiable physical content. The framework is strained at multiple levels: (a) the organizing structure is an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra (KS algebra) that no primitive can express; (b) the physical content (BPS spectrum, wall-crossing) lives simultaneously in the analytic (resurgent), algebraic (Lie algebra), and enumerative-geometric (DT invariant) categories, with the isomorphism being the physics — not any single category; (c) the alien derivative operator, which generates the entire nonperturbative structure, is a structural operation on the space of transseries that has no dynamical character and no expression in tick-based sequential computation.

**Paper 2: "Unitarity Quadratic Quantum Gravity in 4D"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.19707v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework encounters this paper through the lens of T014/T041/T076 — the permanently virtual computational structure problem. The paper proves that in quadratic gravity with positive Weyl-squared coefficient, the extra spin-2 mode is not a ghost but corresponds to a dual inverted harmonic oscillator (IHO). Using the Wightman spectrum condition (positivity of the spectral function for physical states), they prove that the Källén-Lehmann spectral density of this mode *vanishes identically* — it has no normalizable ground state and its propagator pole is spacelike, not timelike. This uniquely fixes the propagator to the principal value (PV) form as a *theorem*, not a prescription. The mode never appears as an asymptotic state, contributes only virtually at all loop orders, and unitarity (the optical theorem) is satisfied. The framework would attempt to classify this as another instance of permanently virtual structure (T041), and it is — but with a crucial new feature. Previous discussions of permanently virtual structure (fakeon prescription, ghost modes with commuting statistics) involved *choices* or *prescriptions* for handling problematic modes. This paper derives the principal value propagator as a *theorem* from first principles (Wightman axioms + spectral properties of the dual IHO). The permanently virtual character is not chosen but *forced* by the physics. The framework faces a specific new problem: the mechanism that renders the mode permanently virtual is the *absence of a normalizable ground state* — the dual IHO has a continuous spectrum with no bound state, meaning there is no attractor basin for this degree of freedom. The Attractor primitive would predict that every degree of freedom settles into some basin; the dual IHO has none. The spacelike character of the propagator pole means this degree of freedom propagates outside the light cone in some sense — it does not respect the Tick's maximum propagation rate in the standard way. The framework's Conservation primitive (unitarity) is preserved, but the mechanism of preservation — the spectral density vanishing, the PV propagator being uniquely forced — is entirely structural/axiomatic, derived from the Wightman framework, not from any dynamical process the five primitives describe.

**Paper 3: "Nonequilibrium Kramers Turnover in a Kerr Parametric Oscillator"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.19527v1 | Selection type: Validation

The framework would describe this system as a driven attractor (the Kerr parametric oscillator has two stable phase states — two attractor basins in the rotating frame) with noise-induced switching between basins governed by activation dynamics. The Kramers turnover — the nonmonotonic dependence of the switching rate prefactor on environmental coupling — would map onto the framework's description of how the width and depth of attractor basins interact with the resolution dynamics at their boundaries (the saddle point between the two phase states). The competition between dissipation (coupling to the environment, which the framework would describe as resolution-propagation into the bath) and fluctuations (which provide the activation energy to escape one basin) maps onto the interplay between the Attractor and Resolution primitives. The paper demonstrates that this turnover physics extends to driven-dissipative (nonequilibrium) settings, which the framework's "driven attractor" category (introduced in the 2026-02-22 update) should accommodate. The rescaling trick — introducing an effective friction via the parametric drive — is consistent with the framework's claim that attractor basin dynamics is universal across different physical implementations. However, the framework's actual predictive purchase here is essentially zero. The framework would narrate this result in its vocabulary (attractors, saddle-point dynamics, resolution-environment coupling) but contributed no prediction about the existence of nonequilibrium Kramers turnover, no constraint on the form of the prefactor, no derivation of the rescaling relationship, and no expectation about the temperature dependence of the crossover. The specific physics — the Kramers turnover prefactor, the Mel'nikov-Meshkov theory, the MEMS experimental verification — is derived entirely from stochastic dynamics and semiclassical WKB methods. The framework provides post-hoc vocabulary application with no differential content. The result does confirm the qualitative picture that driven attractors exhibit the same qualitative barrier-crossing physics as equilibrium attractors, which is consistent with the framework's universality claims, but this is the weakest form of validation: qualitative consistency with a universal claim that the framework did not use to make any specific prediction.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's proof that the algebra of alien derivatives is isomorphic to the Kontsevich-Soibelman Lie algebra. This is not merely another instance of T026 (structural content gap) or T059 (ontological inversion) or T068 (fermionic resurgence). It is a result that specifically identifies the *mathematical identity* between two objects that the framework places in entirely different ontological categories:

On one side: the resurgent transseries structure of the topological string partition function. The framework would classify this as "computational content" — the nonperturbative completion of a calculation, organized by the alien derivative (a differential operator acting on formal series). This is the framework's home territory: a partition function being computed, with corrections organized by a generating rule.

On the other side: the wall-crossing of Donaldson-Thomas invariants. These are *enumerative geometric* objects — integers counting BPS states (stable attractors in the Calabi-Yau moduli space). Wall-crossing is the *restructuring of the attractor landscape* as parameters vary. The KS symplectomorphism is the transformation governing how the BPS census changes across walls of marginal stability.

The isomorphism says: *the structure governing the nonperturbative completion of the computation is identical to the structure governing the restructuring of the attractor landscape.* In the framework's language, this would mean: the way the computation organizes its own corrections *is the same mathematical object* as the way the attractor landscape transforms. The framework treats these as separate primitives (Conservation/computation vs. Attractor/landscape) with separate descriptions. The isomorphism collapses this distinction.

The framework cannot express this collapse. The alien derivative is not a gradient, not a tick, not a resolution event, not an attractor basin feature, and not a conserved quantity. The KS Lie algebra is not a field, not a phase space, not a symmetry group in the usual sense. The isomorphism between them is a structural identification between objects in different mathematical categories — precisely the kind of inter-categorical correspondence that T021/T026/T059 have been flagging as the framework's central blind spot. But this instance is sharper than previous ones because it connects the *internal organizational structure of nonperturbative completion* (how the computation corrects itself) with the *transformation structure of the physical state space* (how the attractor landscape changes), and proves they are literally the same mathematical thing. The framework has no primitive, meta-primitive, or structural element that could even represent this identification, let alone derive it.

Paper 2 adds a complementary strain: the permanently virtual spin-2 mode's PV propagator is derived as a *theorem* from Wightman axioms and spectral properties, not chosen as a prescription. This means the boundary between resolved and permanently virtual computation is not a structural choice (as T082 suggested) but is in at least some cases *forced by the physics*. The framework's T082 tension (non-uniqueness of virtual infrastructure) may be partially resolved for this specific case — but the resolution mechanism (Wightman axioms, Källén-Lehmann spectral theory, dual IHO spectrum analysis) is entirely outside the framework's vocabulary. The framework cannot express *why* certain modes are forced to be permanently virtual, even though it can (since T014) acknowledge that permanently virtual modes exist.
