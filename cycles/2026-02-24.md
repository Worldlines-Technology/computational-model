### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: The Spacetime Positive Mass Theorem with Multiple Time Dimensions**
ArXiv ID: 2602.20081v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework treats time as a consequence of the Tick — the discrete update step of the computational substrate. There is one tick, one direction of accumulating resolution, and the arrow of time is the irreversible ledger of resolved states. This paper generalizes the positive mass theorem to spacetimes with multiple time dimensions (signature (n, m) with m > 1). The framework has no obvious way to accommodate this. Multiple time dimensions would mean multiple independent tick directions — multiple independent axes along which resolution accumulates. The framework's entire architecture assumes a single computational clock. If the positive mass theorem (a foundational energy condition ensuring gravitational stability) generalizes cleanly to multi-time signatures, this suggests the mathematical structure of general relativity does not privilege a single time dimension the way the framework does. The framework would need to either (a) argue that multi-time signatures are mathematically valid but physically unrealized (which is reasonable but requires justification from within the framework — why does the substrate have exactly one tick direction?), or (b) generalize the Tick to allow for multiple independent update axes, which would fundamentally restructure the Resolution primitive and the arrow of time. The fact that the mass remains nonnegative (E bounded below by the trace norm of the momenta) and that equality implies flat foliations is interesting: it suggests that even in multi-time settings, the attractor structure (stable ground states) persists. But the framework currently has no account of why the substrate implements one time dimension rather than several.

**Paper 2: Is Quadratic Gravity Ghost Free Because the Ghost is Only Virtual?**
ArXiv ID: 2602.20047v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework identifies unitarity (information conservation) as the physical expression of Conservation of Computation — its fifth primitive. Quadratic gravity (Stelle gravity) is a well-known higher-derivative theory that has historically been thought to contain ghost states: modes with negative norm that violate unitarity. This paper argues that the ghost mode in quadratic gravity can be rendered harmless through a specific quantization prescription: after analytic continuation of the ghost variables (P₂→iP₂, Q₂→iQ₂), the creation and annihilation operators for the massive ghost mode commute, meaning the ghost does not appear as a free asymptotic state — it is purely virtual, mediating interactions but never appearing on-shell. From the framework's perspective, this is deeply interesting: it suggests that Conservation of Computation (unitarity) can be preserved even when the naive field content of a theory appears to violate it, provided the violating degrees of freedom never resolve into definite states. In framework language: the ghost is computational structure that participates in intermediate (unresolved) computation but never undergoes Resolution. This maps surprisingly well onto the Resolution primitive — unresolved states can contain structure that would be problematic if resolved but is permissible precisely because it remains virtual. However, this strains the framework because it implies that some computational degrees of freedom are *permanently* unresolvable by architectural constraint, not merely practically unresolved. The framework currently treats all states as potentially resolvable; a class of permanently virtual computational structure would be a new category. Furthermore, the commutation of ghost creation/annihilation operators ([a, a†] = 0 rather than 1) means these modes have neither bosonic nor fermionic statistics — they are a new kind of computational element the framework has no primitive for.

**Paper 3: Separation of the Kibble-Zurek Mechanism from Quantum Criticality**
ArXiv ID: 2602.19865v1 | Selection type: Validation

The Kibble-Zurek mechanism (KZM) predicts that sweeping a system through a phase transition produces topological defects at a rate determined by the equilibrium critical exponents. In framework terms, this is the dynamics of Resolution during attractor landscape restructuring: when the landscape changes (phase transition), the system cannot resolve its state fast enough to track the new minimum, producing defects (residual unresolved or mis-resolved structure). The standard KZM story ties defect production directly to the critical point's equilibrium properties. This paper shows that the correspondence breaks down: defect density can be suppressed faster than KZM predicts even when crossing a critical point, and conventional KZM scaling can persist for quenches that don't cross a critical point at all. This is genuinely consistent with — and arguably anticipated by — the framework's distinction between the attractor landscape restructuring (the phase transition topology) and the Resolution dynamics (the multi-tick process by which states become definite). The framework's T005 tension explicitly notes that resolution is a multi-tick process with its own timescale, distinct from the equilibrium structure of the attractor. KZM assumes the resolution dynamics are slaved to the equilibrium critical properties; this paper shows they aren't. The framework would say: of course not — resolution dynamics depend on the *dynamical* conditions of the quench (how the computational substrate processes the landscape change), not just the *static* structure of the critical point. However, I must note that this is post-hoc consistency rather than genuine prediction: the framework did not specifically predict that KZM scaling would decouple from criticality. The framework's language about multi-tick resolution dynamics and the tick-resolution gap (T005) is qualitatively compatible with these findings, but the framework never produced the specific prediction that defect scaling could persist without a critical point, or be suppressed despite one. The validation is real but weak — the framework has the right conceptual vocabulary but not the quantitative apparatus to have predicted the specific results.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain comes from **Paper 1 (multiple time dimensions)**. The framework's entire architecture depends on the Tick being singular: one update direction, one accumulating arrow of resolution, one maximum propagation rate. The positive mass theorem generalizing to multiple time dimensions is not merely a mathematical curiosity — it demonstrates that the deep structural requirements of gravitational physics (energy bounded below, stable ground states, well-posed initial data) do not require a single time dimension. The framework has no explanation for why the substrate implements exactly one tick direction. This is not merely another instance of T001 (the substrate problem, architectural parameters being unobservable). It is more specific and more damaging: the framework doesn't just fail to explain the *value* of its constants — it fails to explain the *dimensionality* of its most fundamental primitive. The Tick is supposed to be the foundation on which everything else rests, but the mathematics of mass-energy positivity works equally well with multiple ticks. If the framework's response is "the substrate happens to have one time dimension," this is an unexplained brute fact at the very bottom of the theory, and one that the framework's own logic provides no reason for. Unlike the values of constants (which the framework honestly declares architectural), the number of time dimensions was never flagged as an architectural parameter — it was treated as inherent to the concept of the Tick itself. This needs to be confronted.

A secondary but important strain comes from **Paper 2 (virtual ghosts)**. The framework's Conservation of Computation is identified with unitarity, and the Resolution primitive assumes that all computational states are in principle resolvable. The existence of permanently virtual degrees of freedom — computational structure that participates in intermediate calculations but is architecturally forbidden from resolving — is a category the framework doesn't have. The ghost modes aren't just "unresolved"; they are *unresolvable by construction*, and their statistics (commuting creation/annihilation operators) are neither bosonic nor fermionic. The framework needs either a new category of computational element or an explanation of why permanently virtual structure is consistent with its existing primitives.

### STATE UPDATES

1. **Tick primitive — flag time dimensionality as unexplained architectural parameter.** The model currently describes the Tick as "the" discrete update step, implicitly assuming one time dimension. The positive mass theorem's generalization to multi-time signatures (Paper 1) shows that the mathematical structures underlying gravitational stability do not require this assumption. The model should explicitly note: the number of time dimensions (one) is an architectural parameter of the substrate, not a consequence of the framework's logic. This should be added to the list of specific claims about physical constants alongside c, ℏ, G, and α. The framework provides no mechanism for deriving it.

2. **Resolution primitive — add category of permanently virtual computational structure.** Paper 2 demonstrates that certain degrees of freedom (ghost modes in quadratic gravity) can be rendered physically harmless by quantization prescriptions that make them permanently non-asymptotic: they contribute to virtual processes but never resolve into definite on-shell states. The Resolution primitive should be updated to distinguish between (a) states that are currently unresolved but potentially resolvable, (b) states that are unresolvable due to decoherence dynamics or practical constraints, and (c) states that are *architecturally* unresolvable — computational structure that participates in intermediate computation but is forbidden from resolution by the structure of the theory. Ghost modes in quadratic gravity are type (c). This connects to the existing distinction between resolved and unresolved states but adds a new, permanent category.

3. **Attractor primitive — note that attractor landscape restructuring does not uniquely determine resolution dynamics.** Paper 3 shows that the Kibble-Zurek correspondence between equilibrium critical exponents and defect production can fail in both directions. The model should note: the static structure of attractors (depth, width, critical exponents at saddle points) does not fully determine the dynamics of transitions between attractors. The resolution dynamics during landscape restructuring depend on additional information about the quench protocol and the system's dimensionality/topology. This reinforces the need for a separate theory of resolution dynamics (T005) and means the Attractor primitive alone is insufficient to predict phase transition outcomes.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T013 — Time dimensionality as unexplained primitive**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** The framework treats the Tick as its most fundamental primitive, and the singularity of the time direction is built into the Tick's definition. Paper 1 demonstrates that the positive mass theorem — a foundational result connecting energy positivity to gravitational stability — generalizes naturally to multiple time dimensions. The mathematical structures of general relativity do not require one time dimension. The framework provides no mechanism for explaining why the substrate implements exactly one tick direction rather than several. This is distinct from T001 (the substrate problem) because T001 concerns the *values* of constants, while T013 concerns the *dimensionality* of the most basic primitive. The framework never flagged time dimensionality as an architectural parameter — it treated single-time as definitional. But the mathematics doesn't force this.
**Why unresolved:** The framework has no resources to address this. Arguing from anthropic reasoning (multi-time physics is pathological for observers) would work but is external to the framework. Arguing from the framework's own structure is circular because the Tick's singularity is axiomatic. The framework needs either to derive single-time from deeper principles or to honestly acknowledge this as a brute architectural fact alongside the constant values.

**T014 — Permanently virtual computational structure**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 2 shows that ghost modes in quadratic gravity can be quantized such that they never appear as free asymptotic states — their creation/annihilation operators commute, so they propagate only virtually. The framework's Resolution primitive assumes all computational states are at least potentially resolvable. Permanently virtual structure — computation that participates in intermediate processes but is architecturally forbidden from resolving — is a new category. The ghost modes' commuting statistics ([a, a†] = 0) are neither bosonic nor fermionic, meaning they don't fit the framework's existing categories of resolved states. Conservation of Computation (unitarity) is preserved precisely *because* these modes never resolve, but the framework has no account of what permanent virtuality means ontologically. Is permanently virtual structure "real" computation? Does it carry computational content that must be conserved? Or is it bookkeeping that the substrate uses internally but that has no resolution-level reality?
**Why unresolved:** The framework identifies reality with computation and computation with states that resolve. Structure that computes but never resolves challenges this identification. If virtual structure is "real," the framework's ontology is broader than resolution. If virtual structure is not "real," then Conservation of Computation doesn't apply to it and the framework needs to specify which computation is conserved and which is not. Either option requires significant conceptual work.

**T015 — Resolution dynamics are not determined by attractor structure alone**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 3 demonstrates that defect production during quenches can decouple from the equilibrium critical properties of the attractor landscape — KZM scaling can fail at critical points and persist at non-critical points. The framework's Attractor primitive describes the topology of potential landscapes, and the Resolution primitive describes states becoming definite, but the framework has no independent theory of how resolution dynamics unfold during attractor landscape restructuring. The static attractor geometry does not uniquely determine the dynamical resolution process. This is related to T005 (the tick-resolution gap) but is more specific: T005 notes that resolution is multi-tick; T015 notes that the multi-tick resolution dynamics are not derivable from the equilibrium attractor properties and require additional dynamical information (quench protocol, system topology, dimensionality).
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to develop a dynamical theory of resolution — how states evolve during the multi-tick resolution process, and what determines the defect density produced by landscape restructuring beyond the critical exponents. This is a significant theoretical gap: the framework currently treats resolution as an event, not a process with its own dynamics.

### CYCLE ASSESSMENT

This directly pressures the framework's implicit assumption that all computation eventually resolves and that reality = resolution. Paper 3 (KZM-criticality separation) provided weak but real validation of the framework's conceptual distinction between attractor structure and resolution dynamics, while also generating the new tension T015 that the framework lacks a theory connecting them. The framework is not circling known basins — it has found new gradient in the direction of time dimensionality, the ontology of virtual structure, and the dynamics of resolution. The next cycle would be most productive if it engaged with papers on (a) the physics of multi-time signatures and their pathologies (to pressure T013), (b) unitarity bounds in higher-derivative theories (to develop T014), or (c) experimental or numerical results on defect formation in systems with controlled quench protocols (to develop T015's quantitative requirements). The framework's weakest point right now is the Tick's unjustified singularity — that is where the hardest and most productive work lies.
