### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Wick-connected theories and Lorentz violation"** (arXiv: 2606.07243v1, strain)

The Worldlines framework treats Lorentz invariance as a consequence of the Tick primitive: the speed of light is the tick rate, the maximum propagation speed, and the resulting causal structure produces Lorentz symmetry. Double Wick rotation — analytically continuing time and reinterpreting a spatial direction as the new time — is a formal operation that connects different field theories. In Lorentz-invariant theories, Wick-connected theories are equivalent: the physics is the same regardless of which direction is designated "time." Paper 1 proves that when Lorentz invariance is broken, this equivalence fails: Wick-connected theories can have different propagating mode content, different unitarity properties, and different renormalizability. The framework strain is severe and multi-layered. First, the paper demonstrates that the *physical content* of a theory (its modes, unitarity, UV behavior) depends on which direction is designated as "time" — i.e., which direction the Tick runs. For Lorentz-invariant theories, this choice is immaterial (the physics is invariant under relabeling). For Lorentz-violating theories, the Tick's directional choice *determines* the physics. The framework treats the Tick as selecting "the" time direction, but the paper shows this selection is not physically neutral when Lorentz symmetry is absent — it actively determines which modes propagate, whether the theory is unitary, and whether it is renormalizable. Second, the paper provides explicit criteria for when unitarity, the mode spectrum, and renormalizability are preserved or destroyed under Wick connection. These criteria are structural properties of the dispersion relation (polynomial structure, root classification) that no primitive can express. Third, the framework's own Tick primitive, if taken literally as a discrete structure at the Planck scale, would generically break Lorentz invariance (discrete lattice structures do not have continuous Lorentz symmetry), meaning the framework's own substrate should produce exactly the regime where Wick-connected theories differ — the framework's foundational discrete structure undermines the equivalence that makes the Tick's directional choice physically innocent.

**Paper 2: "Quantum correlations in QBism's reconstruction program"** (arXiv: 2606.07485v1, strain)

The Worldlines framework treats quantum mechanics as the literal computational structure of reality: superposition is unresolved computation, the Born rule governs resolution outcomes, and the Tsirelson bound on quantum correlations would be interpreted as a substrate-level constraint on correlations between causally separated resolution events. Paper 2 investigates qplex theories — a broader class of probabilistic models motivated by QBism's reconstruction of quantum theory from agent-normative principles. The key finding is a structural asymmetry: the qplex geometric constraints (SIC-POVM-based inner product structure on probability vectors) are sufficient to reproduce the Tsirelson bound ($2\sqrt{2}$) for the CHSH scenario (two outcomes) but *insufficient* to exclude superquantum correlations for the CGLMP inequality $I_{2233}$ (three outcomes). The qplex allows the algebraic maximum of 4, exceeding the quantum bound. The framework strain is sharp: the framework claims the Born rule and quantum correlations are consequences of the substrate's computational architecture. If qplex geometry — a principled, physically motivated generalization of quantum state/measurement structure — reproduces some quantum bounds (CHSH) but not others (CGLMP), then the constraint structure governing correlations is not monolithic. The CHSH bound follows from geometric/inner-product structure alone; the CGLMP bound requires *additional* constraints beyond this geometry. The framework has no account of what these additional constraints are. The paper's conclusion — "additional principles are needed to complete the QBist reconstruction" — directly parallels a challenge to the framework: the five primitives (especially Conservation/Resolution + the substrate's architectural parameters) may suffice for some correlation bounds but not others, and the missing principles are structural constraints on the space of valid probability assignments that the framework cannot express. This connects to T088 (the Grothendieck gap), T121 (maximal Bell violation as operator-algebraic structure), and T026 (structural content gap) but adds a new dimension: the *outcome cardinality* (2 vs. 3) determines whether geometric constraints suffice, meaning the framework's implicit assumption that a single set of architectural parameters governs all correlations is challenged by a dependence on the measurement structure's discrete cardinality.

**Paper 3: "Loss of the Scaling Attractor in Self-Gravitating Domain Wall Networks"** (arXiv: 2606.07296v1, validation)

The Worldlines framework's Attractor primitive describes stable states as local minima in potential fields, with phase transitions occurring when the attractor landscape restructures. Paper 3 demonstrates that the scaling attractor of domain wall networks — a well-known stable fixed point in the phase space of defect evolution on fixed cosmological backgrounds — becomes a *saddle point* when gravitational backreaction is self-consistently included. The scaling regime survives only as a transient, and all trajectories are driven toward a wall-dominated, kinematically frustrated state. This is precisely the framework's language: promoting the expansion rate from an external parameter to a dynamical degree of freedom restructures the phase-space topology, converting a stable attractor into a saddle and eliminating all stable fixed points in the physical phase space. The framework's Attractor primitive correctly predicts the qualitative phenomenology: when the "landscape" is modified by coupling previously external variables into the dynamics, attractor topology can change (stable → saddle), and the system's long-term behavior is qualitatively altered. The framework's Field primitive (ẋ = −∇φ) describes the domain walls as rolling down gradients, and the autonomous system analysis is a concrete implementation of gradient flow in phase space. However, this is **post-hoc consistency, not predictive validation**. The framework did not predict that gravitational backreaction would destabilize the scaling attractor, did not predict the specific saddle structure, did not predict the kinematic frustration end state, and did not predict the non-existence of stable fixed points in the physical phase space. All of these results are derived from the autonomous system analysis of the velocity-dependent one-scale model coupled to the Friedmann equation — standard cosmological dynamics that predates the framework. The framework's vocabulary (attractor → saddle, landscape restructuring, no stable fixed point) maps onto the paper's results, but the framework contributed no prediction, constraint, or mechanism that distinguishes this result from alternative outcomes. The paper does strengthen the constrained/driven attractor category noted in the model: the domain wall network in the frustrated state is not an energy attractor but a state stabilized by kinematic constraints (comoving freezing), consistent with the model's existing notes on constrained attractors. The paper also provides a concrete example of T142 (dynamically emergent attractor landscapes): the relevant phase-space topology is not given by the DW model alone but emerges only when gravitational backreaction is dynamically included — the "landscape" itself is dynamically constructed.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain from this cycle comes from Paper 1's demonstration that the physical content of a field theory — its propagating modes, unitarity, and renormalizability — depends on which direction is designated as "time" when Lorentz invariance is absent. The framework's Tick primitive posits a definite time direction (the direction of discrete updates) and claims Lorentz invariance follows from the tick rate being the maximum propagation speed. But the framework's own substrate, if literally discrete at the Planck scale, would generically break Lorentz invariance. Paper 1 proves that in the Lorentz-violating regime, the choice of time direction is not physically innocent: different choices produce different theories with different mode content, different unitarity properties, and different UV behavior. This creates a *self-undermining loop*: (1) The Tick primitive selects a time direction. (2) The discrete substrate generically breaks Lorentz invariance. (3) In the Lorentz-violating regime, the time direction selection determines the physics. (4) But the framework has no mechanism for why one time direction is selected over another — the Tick is axiomatic, not derived. The framework's foundational choice (which direction the Tick runs) becomes a theory-determining parameter precisely in the regime (Lorentz violation at the Planck scale) where the framework's own substrate structure should apply. The framework cannot account for *why* its Tick runs in the direction it does, and Paper 1 proves this is not a harmless indeterminacy but a physics-determining choice. This is distinct from T013 (time dimensionality, which concerns the *number* of time dimensions) because it concerns the *selection of direction* within a given number of dimensions, and from T094 (indefinite causal order) because it concerns the classical/fixed-background level, not quantum superposition of causal orders.

The second significant strain comes from Paper 2's demonstration that geometric/inner-product constraints on probability assignments reproduce the Tsirelson bound for two-outcome measurements but fail for three-outcome measurements. The framework implicitly assumes a uniform architectural constraint governing all quantum correlations. Paper 2 shows this uniformity fails: the constraint structure is outcome-cardinality-dependent, with two-outcome bounds following from geometry alone and three-outcome bounds requiring additional principles. The framework has no account of what additional principles are needed or why the constraint structure depends on the measurement's discrete cardinality.
