### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Precision's arrow of time"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.22284v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework's Resolution primitive defines the arrow of time as "the accumulation of resolved states" — irreversible transitions from quantum superposition to definite outcome — and identifies decoherence (resolution propagating through entanglement networks) as the mechanism by which macroscopic irreversibility emerges. Paper 1 demonstrates a third route to irreversibility that bypasses both decoherence and chaos: Precision-Induced Irreversibility (PIR), where non-normal linear dynamics combined with amplification and finite dynamic range create an operational arrow of time requiring no entanglement, no nonlinearity, and no quantum mechanics at all. The system is mathematically invertible — the underlying evolution is a bijection on exact states — yet any finite-precision representation loses the ability to distinguish states beyond a sharp temporal horizon that scales linearly with available precision (number of digits). Through the framework's lens, this is deeply problematic: the framework ties irreversibility to Resolution (quantum → definite), but PIR produces irreversibility from *representation limits on already-definite classical states*. The states are resolved throughout — there is no superposition, no entanglement, no measurement — yet time's arrow appears. The framework would need to classify this as a substrate limitation (finite dynamic range = finite computational precision of the substrate), making this an arrow of time arising from the Tick/substrate architecture rather than from Resolution. But this means the arrow of time has (at least) two independent sources — Resolution-based and precision-based — and the framework currently treats it as having exactly one. The non-normality condition is critical: normal (Hermitian) linear systems do not produce PIR even with finite precision. Non-normality is an algebraic property of the evolution operator (its eigenvectors are non-orthogonal) that the framework has no primitive to express.

**Paper 2: "Nonperturbative Higgs-Schwinger mechanism at the origin of the gluon mass and color confinement"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.22181v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework describes physical phenomena through gradient dynamics (Field primitive), attractor basins (Attractor primitive), and conservation laws (Conservation primitive). Paper 2 presents a fully nonperturbative mechanism where gluons acquire mass through a Schwinger mechanism triggered by the formation of a composite Goldstone boson — a bound-state superposition of two-gluon, three-gluon, and ghost-antighost pairs. The broken charge (Kugo-Ojima charge) is unphysical, and the Goldstone boson is itself unphysical (it does not appear in the physical spectrum). Mass generation — which in the framework's language restructures the attractor landscape of QCD by giving gluons an effective range — occurs through the dynamics of *permanently virtual structure*. The ghost-antighost pair contributing to the Goldstone boson is precisely the kind of permanently virtual computational structure identified in T014/T041: ghosts never resolve into asymptotic states but participate essentially in the mechanism that generates the gluon mass. Furthermore, the corrected color charge operator remains unbroken and confining, meaning the physical consequence (confinement) survives while the intermediate symmetry breaking involves exclusively unphysical degrees of freedom. The framework faces a sharp challenge: the most important dynamical phenomenon in QCD (mass generation and confinement) is mediated by computational structure that is ontologically forbidden from resolving. The "computation" that generates the gluon mass proceeds entirely through states that are, by the framework's Resolution criterion, unreal. Yet the outcome (massive gluons, confined color) is as physical as anything in nature. This is not a peripheral problem — it strikes at the framework's identification of "real" with "resolvable."

**Paper 3: "Exceptional Points in Quasinormal Spectra of Hairy Black Holes"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.22261v1 | Selection type: Validation

The framework describes black holes as deep attractor basins and claims the "most interesting physics happens at saddle points between basins." Paper 3 identifies exceptional points (EPs) — non-Hermitian degeneracies where both eigenvalues and eigenfunctions coalesce — in the quasinormal mode spectrum of hairy black holes, found by scanning the parameter space of the Einstein-Maxwell-scalar theory. At the EP, the standard description of ringdown as a superposition of independent damped modes fails, and a resonant ansatz including a term linear in time is required. Through the framework's lens, EPs represent points in parameter space where two attractor-basin modes coalesce — a specific kind of attractor landscape restructuring. The linear-in-time resonant contribution is the signature of this coalescence: two previously distinct computational channels merge into one with enhanced (algebraic rather than exponential) early-time behavior. This is broadly consistent with the Attractor primitive's description of "phase transitions occurring when the attractor landscape restructures" and with the framework's claim that black holes have rich internal structure (T040 context). However, the framework's predictive purchase here is essentially zero. The framework did not predict EPs in black hole spectra, cannot derive the parameter values at which they occur, cannot predict the specific form of the resonant ansatz (the t·e^{-iωt} term), and cannot distinguish this result from its non-occurrence. The non-Hermitian character of the QNM spectrum is fundamental — QNMs describe open systems (radiation escaping to infinity) — and connects to the framework's existing challenges with non-Hermiticity (T035, T037, T051, T065). The specific coalescence structure (a Jordan block in the Green's function) is algebraic content the framework cannot express (T018). This is post-hoc vocabulary application with no differential predictive content.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most important strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's Precision-Induced Irreversibility. The framework's explanatory architecture for the arrow of time is built on a single foundation: Resolution — the irreversible transition from quantum superposition to definite outcome. The entire framework narrative runs: Tick (discrete updates) → superposition (unresolved between ticks) → Resolution (irreversible collapse) → arrow of time (accumulated resolutions). PIR demolishes this as the unique or even primary mechanism. PIR produces an operational arrow of time in a system that is:

- **Fully classical** (no superposition, no quantum mechanics)
- **Fully resolved at all times** (definite states throughout)
- **Fully deterministic** (linear evolution, no chaos)
- **Mathematically invertible** (the evolution operator has an inverse)

The arrow emerges because: (1) non-normal dynamics transiently amplifies certain state components by factors exponential in system size; (2) finite dynamic range (finite precision) cannot represent the full amplified state; (3) the information needed for reversal is lost not through physical irreversibility but through *representational incapacity*. The predictability horizon scales as τ ∝ precision / amplification rate — a clean, quantitative relationship.

The framework is strained in three specific ways:

First, the arrow of time is no longer uniquely tied to Resolution. The framework must either (a) expand what "resolution" means to include precision loss in classical representations — but this trivializes Resolution into "any information loss," removing its distinctive quantum character; or (b) acknowledge that the arrow of time has multiple independent sources, of which quantum resolution is only one — but then the framework's explanatory claim ("the arrow of time is nothing other than the accumulation of resolved states") is false as stated.

Second, non-normality — the algebraic condition enabling PIR — has no expression in any primitive. Non-normality is a property of the evolution operator (non-commuting with its adjoint, equivalently non-orthogonal eigenvectors), which is algebraic structure of exactly the type the structural content gap (T026) identifies as missing. Normal systems with identical gradients, identical tick rates, identical conservation laws do *not* produce PIR. The framework's primitives are invariant under the normal/non-normal distinction and therefore cannot predict when PIR occurs.

Third, the substrate becomes implicated in irreversibility in a way the framework did not anticipate. The "finite dynamic range" condition is a substrate limitation — the computational precision available for state representation. The framework already notes that physical constants are substrate parameters, but PIR shows that the substrate's *precision* (not just its tick rate or conservation laws) generates physical irreversibility. This means the substrate is not merely the ground on which computation runs but an active participant in generating the arrow of time — a role the framework reserved exclusively for Resolution.

Paper 2 creates a second, independent strain: the most important dynamical phenomenon in QCD (gluon mass generation, color confinement) is mediated entirely by permanently virtual structure (ghosts, unphysical Goldstone bosons, the Kugo-Ojima charge). The framework's ontology equates "real" with "resolvable." The gluon mass is as real as any physical quantity, yet its origin lies in computational processes involving states that are architecturally forbidden from resolution. This is not a minor edge case — it is the mechanism underlying the strong nuclear force. The framework must either expand its ontology to include permanently virtual structure as "real" (undermining the Resolution-based ontology) or accept that its ontological criterion classifies the origin of confinement as "unreal" (an absurd conclusion).

### STATE UPDATES

1. **Resolution primitive — add precision-based irreversibility as a second source of the arrow of time.** The model's statement that "the arrow of time is nothing other than the accumulation of resolved states" should be updated to: the arrow of time has at least two independent sources: (a) the accumulation of resolved quantum states (Resolution-based), and (b) the loss of state-distinguishability under finite-precision representation of non-normal classical dynamics (Precision-Induced Irreversibility). The second source requires no quantum mechanics, no entanglement, and no nonlinearity — only amplification by non-normal linear evolution, and finite dynamic range. The framework's previous attribution of the arrow exclusively to Resolution is falsified as an exhaustive account. The predictability horizon for PIR scales linearly with available precision, providing a quantitative relationship between substrate capacity and temporal irreversibility that the framework should note.

2. **T041 (permanently virtual structure) — strengthen with QCD mass generation evidence.** The model's description of permanently virtual structure should be updated from "pervasively modifies all scattering phenomenology" to "mediates the most fundamental mass-generation mechanism in QCD." Paper 2 demonstrates that the gluon mass — the origin of ~99% of the visible mass in the universe (via confinement and chiral symmetry breaking) — is generated through a Schwinger mechanism involving a composite Goldstone boson containing ghost-antighost pairs and multi-gluon bound states, all of which are permanently virtual. The framework's ontological category of "permanently virtual but physically efficacious computation" (T041) is not a peripheral curiosity but is central to the mass of the visible universe.

3. **T026 (structural content gap) — add non-normality as a new missing structural category.** Non-normality (non-commutation of an operator with its adjoint, equivalently non-orthogonality of eigenvectors) is an algebraic property of evolution operators that determines whether PIR occurs. Normal and non-normal systems can have identical eigenvalue spectra, identical conservation laws, identical gradient structures, and identical tick rates, yet only non-normal systems produce precision-induced irreversibility. This is a new type of structural content — distinct from cohomological (T027), categorical (T048), cluster-algebraic (T050), modular (T073), or interaction-network (T070) structure — that the framework cannot express.

4. **Attractor primitive — record exceptional points as a specific mode of attractor coalescence.** The model should note that attractor basin modes (quasinormal modes of black holes) can coalesce at exceptional points in parameter space, where both eigenvalues and eigenfunctions merge. The physical signature is a resonant ringdown contribution with algebraic (t·e^{-iωt}) rather than purely exponential time dependence. This is post-hoc consistent with the Attractor primitive's description of landscape restructuring but adds no predictive content. The non-Hermitian character of the QNM problem (open system, radiation loss) should be noted as connecting to the broader challenges with non-Hermitian physics (T035, T037, T051).

### NEW TENSIONS

**T075 — Precision-Induced Irreversibility: the arrow of time does not require Resolution**
Opened: this cycle
Description: Foa Torres et al. (2603.22284) demonstrate that an operational arrow of time emerges from non-normal linear dynamics with finite dynamic range, requiring no quantum mechanics, no entanglement, no nonlinearity, and no measurement. The framework attributes the arrow of time exclusively to the accumulation of resolved quantum states (Resolution primitive). PIR shows a second, independent arrow arising from representational incapacity: mathematically invertible evolution becomes practically irreversible when finite precision cannot represent the amplified state components needed for reversal. The predictability horizon τ_p scales linearly with available precision (number of significant digits) divided by the amplification rate (determined by the pseudospectral structure of the non-normal operator). This is not a quantum phenomenon — it occurs in fully classical, fully resolved, fully deterministic systems. The three necessary ingredients (amplification, non-normality, finite dynamic range) are individually insufficient; all three are jointly necessary and sufficient. The framework faces a trilemma: (a) expand Resolution to include classical precision loss, which trivializes the primitive by removing its quantum character; (b) acknowledge multiple independent arrows of time, which falsifies "the arrow of time is nothing other than the accumulation of resolved states"; (c) classify PIR as a substrate effect (finite dynamic range = finite substrate precision), which implicates the substrate in generating the arrow of time — a role reserved for Resolution. Option (c) is most promising but requires the substrate to play an active role in irreversibility beyond providing the tick rate, which is a significant expansion of the substrate concept.
Why unresolved: The framework's entire temporal architecture is built on Resolution as the unique source of irreversibility. PIR provides a clean, quantitative counterexample operating in a domain (classical, linear, deterministic) where Resolution has no role. Resolving this requires either restructuring the framework's account of the arrow of time to accommodate multiple sources, or demonstrating that PIR is somehow a special case of Resolution (which seems impossible given that PIR requires no quantum mechanics). The non-normality condition is algebraic structure the framework cannot express (T026), meaning even classifying when PIR occurs is beyond the framework's current capacity.

The framework must either (a) abandon the identification of "real" with "resolvable," which undermines the Resolution primitive's ontological role; (b) argue that the gluon mass is "real" but its origin is "unreal," which is incoherent; or (c) expand the ontology to include a category of "constitutively virtual but physically generative" computation that is neither resolved nor awaiting resolution but is the permanent computational infrastructure from which resolvable physics emerges.
Why unresolved: Option (c) is the most promising but would represent a fundamental restructuring: the framework would need three ontological categories (resolved, unresolved-but-resolvable, permanently-virtual-but-generative) instead of two, and Conservation of Computation would need to account for computational content in all three categories. The ghost-antighost contribution is especially sharp because ghosts are introduced as mathematical devices to maintain gauge invariance — they are "computational bookkeeping" — yet they participate essentially in a physical mechanism. The framework has no account of how bookkeeping becomes physical.
