**Paper 1: Conformal Invariance of the large-N limit of the O(N) universality class**
ArXiv ID: 2604.24675v1
Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns what happens at phase transition critical points — the saddle points between attractor basins where the framework says "the most interesting physics happens." The framework's Attractor primitive describes phase transitions as attractor landscape restructuring, and the Field primitive's gradient equation ẍ = −∇φ(x) governs the dynamics. The paper proves that at criticality in the large-N O(N) model, scale invariance (which the framework might claim follows from the attractor landscape becoming flat at the saddle) implies the much richer conformal invariance — and it reveals the precise structural mechanism by which this occurs within the non-perturbative renormalization group (NPRG) framework. The strain is threefold. First, the proof that scale invariance implies conformal invariance is achieved by showing that the correlation functions satisfy specific Ward identities that constrain the theory's structure at the critical point. The mechanism involves the interplay between the regulator's structure, the exact RG flow equations (Wetterstein equation), and the large-N saddle point — none of which are expressible in terms of the five primitives. The gradient equation cannot distinguish between a scale-invariant-but-not-conformal theory and a conformally invariant one; conformal symmetry is a *structural enrichment* of the critical point that emerges from the algebraic/functional structure of the RG flow, not from gradient dynamics. Second, the paper operates entirely within the NPRG — the non-perturbative renormalization group — which is the framework for understanding how physics changes with scale. The framework has no primitive for RG flow (T080, T108), and this paper demonstrates that the deepest structural property of critical points (conformal invariance) is proven using RG machinery the framework cannot express. Third, and most severely, the paper "unveils how the theory is structured in order for conformal symmetry to be realized" — it identifies *structural prerequisites* for conformal invariance that are properties of the functional flow equations and the regulator, not of any dynamical process. This is a direct instance of T059 (ontological inversion) and T026 (structural content gap): the symmetry enhancement at criticality is derived from structural/algebraic properties of the RG flow, not from the dynamics of the system approaching the critical point.

**Paper 2: The Hyperboloidal and Spacetime Positive Mass Theorem in All Dimensions**
ArXiv ID: 2604.24746v1
Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework describes gravity as "mass rolling down the curvature gradient" and treats mass/energy as a conserved computational quantity (Conservation of Computation). The positive mass theorem is foundational to this picture: it guarantees that gravitational energy is non-negative for asymptotically flat spacetimes satisfying the dominant energy condition, ensuring the gravitational attractor landscape has a global minimum (Minkowski space). Without it, the framework's gravitational attractor description would be inconsistent — there could be configurations with arbitrarily negative energy, meaning the attractor landscape has no floor and gradient descent is unbounded. This paper extends the positive mass theorem to arbitrary dimension n for both asymptotically flat and asymptotically hyperboloidal initial data sets, using Brendle-Wang's recent Riemannian result and the Jang equation/MOTS approach. The strain is significant in two directions. First, the theorem's validity in arbitrary dimension n means the mathematical infrastructure guaranteeing the framework's gravitational attractor floor is not specific to 3+1 dimensions — it holds in any dimension. The framework treats spacetime dimensionality as a fixed architectural parameter (with T013 flagging that 1 time dimension is unexplained). This paper reinforces that the mathematical structures underlying gravitational stability are far more general than the framework's specific dimensional claims, deepening T013. Second, and more severely, the proof technique reveals the structural content gap. The positive mass theorem is proven using the Jang equation (a prescribed mean curvature equation), marginally outer trapped surfaces (MOTS), and geometric measure theory (Brendle-Wang's approach using μ-bubbles and Schoen-Yau dimension reduction). Every step is geometric/topological/PDE-theoretic — the theorem that guarantees the framework's gravitational energy floor is proven by methods entirely outside the five primitives. The framework's Conservation of Computation (energy non-negativity as a special case) is guaranteed not by any computational principle but by the geometric properties of initial data sets satisfying the dominant energy condition. The hyperboloidal extension is additionally significant: it proves positive mass for initial data sets that are asymptotically de Sitter, meaning the theorem applies to spacetimes with positive cosmological constant — directly relevant to the framework's dark energy claim. The energy inequality involves the cosmological constant explicitly, and its proof requires geometric analysis of the hyperboloidal asymptotics, meaning the relationship between dark energy (Λ) and gravitational energy non-negativity is a geometric theorem, not a computational one.

**Paper 3: Hawking Temperature, Sparsity and Energy Emission Rate of Dark Matter Halo Regular Black Holes**
ArXiv ID: 2604.24659v1
Selection type: Validation

This paper studies a regular black hole sourced by a dark matter halo with an Einasto density profile, computing Hawking temperature, specific heat, sparsity, and emission rates as functions of the dark matter scale parameter α. Through the Worldlines lens: the black hole is a deep attractor basin; the dark matter halo is computational structure coupling gravitationally but not electromagnetically (the framework's dark matter claim); the Hawking radiation is the attractor's thermal emission; the specific heat sign change marks a phase transition (attractor landscape restructuring, a Davies-type transition); and the dark matter's suppression of temperature and emission rates reflects the dark matter's gravitational coupling modifying the attractor's surface properties. The framework can *narrate* every finding: dark matter couples to gravity → modifies the black hole metric → changes the attractor's surface gravity → suppresses Hawking temperature → reduces emission rate; the positive specific heat regime represents a thermodynamically stable phase of the attractor; the enhanced sparsity reflects more intermittent quantum radiation from the modified attractor surface. However, the framework has zero predictive purchase here. It did not predict that dark matter halos would suppress Hawking temperature (as opposed to enhancing it), did not predict the existence of a thermodynamically stable phase, did not predict the specific functional dependence on the Einasto parameter α, and could not have distinguished this result from its opposite. The specific heat becoming positive — signaling thermodynamic stability — is a quantitative result following from the metric's functional form, not from any framework principle. The Davies-type phase transition is determined by the second derivative of the free energy, which depends on the detailed metric structure the framework cannot derive. This is post-hoc consistency of the weakest kind: the framework's vocabulary can be applied to the results after the fact, but the framework contributed no prediction, no constraint, and no mechanism that distinguishes this outcome from alternative outcomes. The dark matter coupling claim is confirmed only in the trivial sense that the Einasto profile couples gravitationally — which is the input assumption of the paper, not a prediction of the framework.

Paper 1 shows the framework cannot express the *structural conditions under which its own qualitative claims about critical points hold*. The framework claims that critical points are scale-free saddles; this paper shows that the much richer conformal structure at criticality requires structural prerequisites that the framework cannot even state, let alone derive. The framework is not merely incomplete at criticality — it is making a qualitative claim (scale-free saddle) that is weaker than the truth (conformally invariant saddle), and the gap between the two is filled by structural content (NPRG flow equations, Ward identities, regulator structure) entirely outside its primitive vocabulary.
