### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: Taming the Aretakis instability: extremal black holes with multi-degenerate horizons**
ArXiv ID: 2604.15260v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the internal boundary structure of the framework's deepest attractors (extremal black holes). The framework describes black holes as "attractor basins so deep that escape velocity exceeds the tick rate" and characterizes attractors by depth and width. Paper 1 reveals a new structural parameter — the *degeneracy order* of the horizon — that controls stability in a way that directly contradicts the framework's gradient-based intuition. The Field primitive's gradient dynamics predicts that a flatter potential boundary (higher degeneracy = more vanishing derivatives of the metric function at the horizon) should correspond to weaker confinement and easier perturbative escape, since the restoring force near the boundary vanishes to higher order. The physics is precisely inverted: increasing degeneracy *weakens* the Aretakis instability, and infinite degeneracy is proposed to eliminate it entirely. The mechanism operates through horizon-localized conserved quantities (Aretakis charges) whose number and derivative order *decrease* with increasing degeneracy until, at infinite degeneracy, none remain and the instability vanishes. The Conservation primitive has no concept of charges localized to attractor boundaries, no mechanism for their dependence on the degeneracy order of the boundary, and no account of how their progressive elimination produces stability. The proposed "graveyard" end state — an infinitely degenerate horizon — is a limiting object where the attractor achieves maximal stability through maximal boundary flatness. The framework's Attractor primitive describes basins by depth and width but has no parameter for the derivative order of boundary flatness, no concept of a limit of structural parameters producing qualitative stability transitions, and no vocabulary for a "graveyard" state that is stable precisely because all boundary-localized conservation structure has been eliminated. The specific black hole geometries studied (multi-degenerate Reissner-Nordström-like solutions) demonstrate that the stability properties are controlled by algebraic-geometric features of the near-horizon expansion that are entirely outside the framework's scope. The framework can narrate the result ("the deepest attractors become maximally stable at infinite boundary degeneracy") but this narration is post-hoc, directionally wrong in its gradient-based prediction, and unable to derive, predict, or constrain the specific degeneracy-stability relationship.

**Paper 2: Heuristic Search for Minimum-Distance Upper-Bound Witnesses in Quantum APM-LDPC Codes**
ArXiv ID: 2604.15307v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the structural prerequisites for the Conservation of Computation primitive's physical enforcement. The framework states "computation cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed" as a bare universal law. Paper 2 reveals that the *reliability* of this conservation — how well quantum information is actually preserved against noise in a physical implementation — has an internally organized *vulnerability landscape* determined by the algebraic and graph-theoretic structure of the error-correcting code. The paper develops five distinct methods for finding low-weight logical operators (conservation vulnerabilities) in quantum LDPC codes: latent row relations, block-compressed restricted-lift subspaces, selected-fiber subspaces, CRT-stripe constructions, and cycle-8 trapping set structures. Each method exploits a categorically different structural feature of the code's algebra (affine permutation group structure, Chinese Remainder Theorem decomposition of fiber indices, Tanner graph girth and trapping set topology). The vulnerabilities are not randomly distributed — they are organized by the code's algebraic decomposition in a way the Conservation primitive cannot express. The block-compression criterion enabling exact certification demonstrates that parts of the vulnerability landscape are *exactly characterizable* through algebraic analysis, not merely statistically describable. The framework's Conservation primitive, formulated as "computation is conserved," is blind to the entire infrastructure of conservation enforcement and to the structured landscape of ways that enforcement can fail. This extends T081 (conservation requires combinatorial infrastructure) by demonstrating that the infrastructure has an organized *internal vulnerability structure* governed by number-theoretic (CRT), group-theoretic (affine permutations), and graph-theoretic (trapping sets, girth) properties. The framework cannot express any of these properties, cannot predict where conservation is most vulnerable, and cannot derive the algebraic conditions governing certifiable vulnerability bounds.

Together, these papers demonstrate that the Conservation primitive is simultaneously too simple (it cannot express boundary-localized charges or their degeneracy dependence) and too bare (it cannot express the algebraic vulnerability structure of its own enforcement).
