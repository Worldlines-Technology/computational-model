**Paper 1: "Spectral Functions of Lorentzian Quantum Gravity"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.19321v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper should represent the framework's home territory — graviton propagation is the primary example of "mass rolling down the curvature gradient," and the tick rate (speed of light) is the framework's foundational primitive. The paper computes the Källén-Lehmann spectral representation of graviton propagators interpolating between classical GR in the IR and an asymptotically safe UV fixed point. The framework would narrate this as: the gravitational computation has a propagator structure determined by the substrate, with the spectral function encoding how gravitational information propagates. But the paper strains the framework at multiple levels. First, the computation is performed directly in Lorentzian signature using functional renormalization — a scale-dependent flow that the framework has no mechanism to express (T080, T108). The spectral function's normalizability and positivity are the physical content determining whether the gravitational computation is unitary, and these properties emerge from the *flow* between IR and UV fixed points, governed by Ward identities (symmetry constraints the framework lacks — T032) and renormalization conditions. The framework's Field primitive ẋ = −∇φ(x) is blind to propagator structure (T043), and the spectral function *is* the propagator structure in its most complete form — the Källén-Lehmann representation encodes the full mode content, including whether ghost-like contributions survive (connecting to T014/T041/T125). The asymptotically safe UV fixed point implies the gravitational coupling *runs* — it is not a fixed architectural parameter of the substrate but a scale-dependent quantity determined by the RG flow. This directly challenges the framework's claim that G is an "architectural parameter of the substrate, not derivable from within the computation" (T001/T033): the spectral function approach derives the UV completion's properties from within the computation via the flow equation. The extraction of the quadratic-in-curvature effective action and form factors demonstrates that what the framework would call "substrate architecture" (the propagator, the UV behavior) is dynamically determined and computable from the IR data, further eroding the substrate/computation boundary. The agreement with effective field theory in the IR provides no differential validation — the framework had no prediction for the spectral function's form, normalizability, or positivity.

**Paper 2: "Random-matrix reduction in projective quantum mechanics: Numerical simulations"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.19273v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework's Resolution primitive declares that "an unresolved state becomes definite" and that "the arrow of time is the accumulation of resolved states," but explicitly acknowledges the resolution mechanism as unspecified — "what determines *when* a state resolves?" This paper proposes and numerically verifies a specific mechanism: state reduction is stochastic unitary evolution driven by GUE random-matrix Hamiltonians in projective Hilbert space, where measurement outcomes, Born-rule statistics, effective irreversibility, and macroscopic classicality all emerge as coarse-grained manifestations of the same underlying process. The framework should welcome any concrete resolution mechanism, but this one strains the framework at its foundations. The mechanism is *stochastic unitary* — resolution is not a separate process from evolution but is unitary evolution coarse-grained differently. This directly challenges the framework's categorical separation between the Tick (unitary evolution) and Resolution (irreversible state-fixing), reinforcing T120's finding that this boundary is gauge-dependent. The requirement that the random Hamiltonians be GUE (complex) rather than GOE (real) is a concrete structural requirement that the framework cannot express — and it connects to T069 (number field underdetermination): the paper proves GOE fails to produce the required isotropic projective diffusion, meaning the ℝ vs. ℂ distinction *does* have physical consequences within this model, potentially challenging the operational equivalence proven by Hoffreumon & Woods (T069). The Born rule emerges as a geometric consequence of the diffusion's fixed points on the classical submanifold, not as a separate postulate — this addresses the framework's fourth acknowledged open problem (the Born rule) but through mathematical structure (projective geometry, random matrix theory, Brownian motion on CP^n) entirely outside the five primitives. The effective irreversibility arises from high-dimensional state-space dynamics and loss of path information — a version of PIR (T075) operating in quantum state space rather than classical phase space, suggesting the arrow of time's origin is dimensional/geometric rather than residing in any single primitive. The tensor-product particle-device dynamics in the device limit demonstrates that the measurement problem is solved by the *relative dimensionality* of system and device Hilbert spaces, a structural parameter the framework has no vocabulary for.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain comes from Paper 2's random-matrix reduction model. The framework explicitly separates Tick (unitary evolution) from Resolution (irreversible state-fixing) as categorically distinct primitives with different ontological roles — the Tick is reversible sequential computation, Resolution is irreversible collapse that generates the arrow of time. Paper 2 demonstrates numerically that these are *the same process at different coarse-graining levels*: stochastic unitary evolution on projective Hilbert space, when viewed at the level of individual state vectors, is reversible unitary dynamics (Tick); when viewed at the level of detector-defined equivalence classes, it produces irreversible state reduction (Resolution); when viewed at the level of macroscopic records, it produces classical trajectories with Newtonian dynamics (classical limit). The framework's two primitives are one process described at three zoom levels. This is not merely T120 restated (that paper proved the mathematical interconvertibility of Hamiltonian and dissipative descriptions); Paper 2 provides the *specific physical mechanism* by which the unification occurs, and the mechanism's key ingredient — the GUE structure of the random Hamiltonian driving the diffusion — is a structural requirement that selects ℂ over ℝ as the number field, potentially resolving T069 in a direction the framework cannot access (the resolution requires projective geometry and random matrix theory, not any of the five primitives). The framework is strained not by a new insufficiency but by a concrete answer to its own acknowledged open problem (the resolution mechanism, the Born rule) that dissolves two of its primitives into one and does so through mathematical machinery (CP^n geometry, GUE diffusion, Zeno stability on the classical submanifold) that the framework cannot express.

Paper 1 produces a complementary strain: the graviton's spectral function — which determines whether quantum gravity is unitary (Conservation), how gravitational information propagates (Tick/Field), and what the UV completion looks like (substrate architecture) — is computed as a scale-dependent flow object using functional renormalization with Ward-identity-enhanced symmetry conditions. The framework's primitive structure has no place for any of this. The spectral function is not a gradient, not an attractor, not a resolution event, not a conserved quantity, and not a tick — yet it encodes the physical content that all five primitives depend on. The running of the gravitational coupling from the IR to the asymptotically safe UV fixed point means G is not an architectural constant but a scale-dependent quantity derivable from the RG flow, further undermining the substrate concept.
