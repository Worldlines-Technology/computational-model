### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: Invertible Calabi-Yau Orbifolds over Finite Fields II (2602.23173v1, strain)**

This paper conjectures a specific form for the zeta function of crepant resolutions of Berglund-Hübsch orbifold hypersurfaces over finite fields, showing the conjecture satisfies the Weil conjectures and connecting it to Monsky-Washnitzer cohomology. Through the Worldlines lens, this strains the framework in a precise and uncomfortable way. The paper operates in an arena — algebraic geometry over finite fields — where the "computation" is defined over a discrete, finite substrate (the field 𝔽_q) and yet produces deep arithmetic structure (the zeta function, its factorization, cohomological underpinnings) that mirrors the physics of Calabi-Yau compactifications in string theory. The Weil conjectures, proven by Deligne, are the finite-field analogues of the Riemann hypothesis and encode information about the topology of the underlying variety through point-counting over extensions 𝔽_{q^n}. The framework claims reality is computation on a discrete substrate, but here the direction is reversed: starting from a discrete substrate (finite field), the deep structure that emerges is geometric and cohomological, not computational in any dynamical sense. The zeta function encodes topological invariants (Betti numbers, Hodge structure) through an arithmetic process. The crepant resolution — a geometric operation that resolves singularities while preserving the canonical class — has a direct analogue in the framework's Resolution primitive, but here resolution is a geometric smoothing operation, not a state-becomes-definite event. Most critically, the Berglund-Hübsch mirror construction connects these finite-field computations to mirror symmetry, which is itself a profound duality (connecting the framework's T010). The paper demonstrates that the same physical/mathematical content (Calabi-Yau geometry) is encoded simultaneously in arithmetic (point-counting), cohomology (Monsky-Washnitzer), and geometry (crepant resolutions), with none of these descriptions being more fundamental than the others. The framework has no resources to explain why arithmetic over finite fields should encode the same topological information as continuous geometry.

**Paper 2: A Lorentz-Covariant Spectral Universality of Stochastic Fields (2602.23195v1, strain)**

This paper derives a universal relationship between temporal and spatial power spectral indices for stationary stochastic fields in Minkowski spacetime, showing that the temporal spectral index is symmetry-protected and observer-invariant, offset from the spatial index by a geometric factor determined by effective momentum-space dimensionality. Crucially, it proves a no-go result: no covariant local mapping can relate temporal and spatial power spectra in more than one spatial dimension. Through the Worldlines lens, this creates strain in several places. First, the framework's Tick primitive treats time as the sequential update dimension and space as the extent over which state propagates, but this paper shows that Lorentz symmetry imposes a rigid, non-trivial constraint on how spectral structure in the time direction relates to spectral structure in spatial directions — and that this relationship is *not* a simple mapping but involves a universal geometric offset. The framework's picture of "ticks propagating across substrate" suggests a fundamental asymmetry between time and space that should be reflected in spectral properties, but Lorentz covariance forces the relationship to be symmetric up to a dimensionality-dependent geometric factor. Second, the no-go theorem (no covariant local mapping between temporal and spatial spectra in d > 1) is a constraint that emerges purely from symmetry — specifically, from the structure of the Lorentz group acting on stochastic fields. The framework's Field primitive (ẋ = −∇φ) describes motion down gradients but has no account of stochastic fields or their spectral structure, let alone universal constraints on that structure from spacetime symmetry. Third, the result that spectral universality *breaks down* for anisotropic scaling and dispersion-dominated spectra is interesting: these are precisely the regimes where the framework's discrete-tick structure might become visible (dispersion from discreteness, anisotropy from the tick/space asymmetry). The framework would predict such breakdowns but cannot calculate their specific form.

**Paper 3: Arrested Relaxation in a Disorder-Free Coulomb Spin Liquid (2602.23362v1, validation)**

This paper demonstrates dynamical arrest — exponentially long-lived athermal plateaus in spin autocorrelations — in a disorder-free, short-range interacting classical spin-3/2 ice system following a thermal quench. The arrest originates from composite excitation structures unique to the enlarged on-site Hilbert space and from kinetically constrained relaxation pathways requiring activated processes. Through the Worldlines lens, this is a genuine validation case for the Attractor primitive's extended formulation. The model was updated (2026-02-22) to include "constrained attractors" — states stable not because they are energy minima but because they are stabilized by conservation laws, integrability, or topological constraints. The spin-3/2 ice plateau is precisely such a state: it is not a thermal equilibrium minimum, and its stability arises from kinetic constraints (the composite excitations create barriers in configuration space that require activated processes to cross). The framework's description of "attractors in a constrained phase space, not in the full energy landscape" maps directly onto this: the ice rules and the structure of the spin-3/2 Hilbert space define a constrained phase space in which the arrested state is effectively an attractor. Furthermore, this validates the T015 update about resolution dynamics not being determined by attractor structure alone — the arrest happens because the quench protocol creates a state whose relaxation is kinetically blocked despite the equilibrium attractor landscape being accessible in principle. However, the validation is post-hoc rather than predictive: the framework's "constrained attractor" language was developed in response to earlier results (fractional Fermi seas) and applied here retroactively. The framework did not predict the specific mechanism (composite excitations in spin-3/2 ice) or the specific phenomenology (exponential lifetime scaling). It is consistent, not predictive. The one area where the framework gains genuine traction is the connection between T006 (stable excited states not produced by gradient descent) and this paper: both demonstrate that stability without energetic minimality is a real and general physical phenomenon, validating the framework's expansion beyond simple gradient descent.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most important strain this cycle comes from Paper 1 and its interaction with the existing tensions T010, T016, and T018. The Calabi-Yau/finite-field result demonstrates something the framework genuinely cannot accommodate: the same mathematical-physical content is *simultaneously* arithmetic (counting points over 𝔽_q), cohomological (Monsky-Washnitzer), geometric (crepant resolution of singularities), and combinatorial (the Berglund-Hübsch transpose construction). These are not "dual computational descriptions" in the sense of T010, where two different computations yield the same answer. They are categorically different mathematical frameworks — number theory, homological algebra, algebraic geometry, combinatorics — that are *provably equivalent* in their encoding of the same invariant content (the zeta function and its factorization).

The framework claims "reality is literally computation." But the zeta function of a Calabi-Yau variety over a finite field doesn't care whether you call its determination "computation." The Weil conjectures — which constrain the zeta function's structure (rationality, functional equation, Riemann hypothesis, connection to Betti numbers) — are not computational constraints. They are constraints from the intersection of arithmetic and topology. The framework has no primitive that captures *why* counting solutions to polynomial equations modulo a prime should encode topological invariants of the corresponding complex variety. This is Weil's original insight, proven by Grothendieck and Deligne, and it represents a deep connection between discrete arithmetic and continuous geometry that the framework's five primitives cannot express.

This escalates T016 (geometric encoding vs. computational ontology) into something more severe: not just "geometry might be more fundamental than computation" but "the web of equivalences between arithmetic, geometry, topology, and algebra constitutes the deep structure of mathematical physics, and 'computation' is at best one node in this web, with no special ontological privilege." The framework's insistence on computation as the fundamental category is not wrong so much as *provincial* — it picks one corner of a richly interconnected mathematical landscape and declares it the foundation.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T021 — Arithmetic-geometric unity and the poverty of "computation"**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 1 demonstrates that the zeta function of a Calabi-Yau variety encodes topological invariants (via Weil conjectures) through arithmetic over finite fields, with the connection mediated by cohomology (Monsky-Washnitzer) and geometric operations (crepant resolution). This instantiates a pattern seen throughout modern mathematical physics: the same invariant content is accessible through categorically different mathematical frameworks — arithmetic, algebraic geometry, topology, combinatorics — that are provably equivalent but conceptually distinct. The framework claims reality is "literally computation," privileging one mathematical category as ontologically fundamental. But arithmetic-geometric correspondences (Weil conjectures, Langlands program, mirror symmetry) suggest that the deep structure of mathematical physics lives in the *relationships between* categories, not in any single category. The framework provides no justification for privileging computation over arithmetic, geometry, or topology, and the existence of categorical equivalences suggests that the invariant content — which is what physics cares about — is not inherently computational.
**Why unresolved:** This is deeper than T010 (duality between computational descriptions) or T016 (geometric encoding vs. computational ontology). Those tensions concern specific alternative descriptions of specific physical systems. T021 concerns the observation that the entire edifice of mathematical physics is characterized by inter-categorical equivalences, and asks whether "computation" names anything ontologically distinctive or is merely one of many equivalent windows into the same structure. Resolving this would require either (a) demonstrating that computation is the unique category from which all others can be derived (which would be an extraordinary mathematical claim), (b) identifying what specific feature of computation earns it ontological privilege, or (c) abandoning the claim that reality is literally computation in favor of a more category-neutral ontological claim.

**T022 — Spectral universality as a constraint the framework cannot derive**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 2 derives a universal relationship between temporal and spatial spectral indices for Lorentz-homogeneous stochastic fields, with the offset determined purely by spacetime dimensionality and Lorentz symmetry. This is a structural constraint on the statistical properties of fields that follows from symmetry alone. The framework's Field primitive describes dynamics (ẋ = −∇φ) but not the statistical/spectral properties of fields. The Tick primitive posits discrete updates and finite propagation speed, which should in principle constrain spectral structure, but the framework cannot derive the specific universal offset or the no-go theorem for local spectral mappings. More fundamentally, the result shows that Lorentz symmetry — which in the framework is a consequence of the tick rate being the maximum propagation speed — has non-trivial implications for the spectral structure of stochastic fields that the framework never anticipated and currently cannot reproduce. If the framework's "tick rate = speed of light" is correct, it should be possible to derive these spectral constraints from the discrete tick structure, but the framework lacks the formalism to do so.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to develop a theory of stochastic fields on its discrete substrate and show that the spectral universality emerges from the tick structure + Lorentz invariance. This is in principle possible but has not been attempted. Furthermore, the no-go result (no covariant local spectral mapping in d > 1) might constrain the framework's Field primitive: if temporal and spatial field structures cannot be locally mapped, the framework's treatment of fields as having unified gradients ∇φ that combine spatial and temporal derivatives may be too naive.

**T023 — Kinetic arrest as a distinct dynamical category**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 3 demonstrates that a disorder-free, short-range interacting system can exhibit dynamical arrest with exponentially long-lived athermal plateaus. The arrest mechanism — composite excitations creating kinetic barriers in an enlarged Hilbert space — represents a dynamical phenomenon that is distinct from both attractor capture (the system is not in a minimum) and resolution dynamics (the system is classical, so quantum resolution is not involved). The framework currently has three categories for stable states: energy attractors, constrained attractors, and driven attractors. Kinetically arrested states don't fit cleanly into any of these: they are not true attractors (the system would eventually relax), they are not constrained by conservation laws or integrability (the ice rules are not symmetries in the traditional sense), and they are not driven (no external energy input). They represent a fourth category: states that are metastable for exponentially long times due to the structure of the dynamical landscape (barriers in configuration space) rather than the structure of the energy landscape (minima). The framework's gradient equation ẋ = −∇φ(x) would describe the system as slowly rolling down a gradient, but the exponential timescale is set by barrier structure, not gradient magnitude, and the framework has no account of barrier-dominated dynamics as distinct from gradient-dominated dynamics.
**Why unresolved:** The framework's "everything moves down gradients" claim technically accommodates exponentially slow motion (small gradients), but this misses the physical content: the dynamics are barrier-dominated, not gradient-dominated. The exponential timescale comes from the Arrhenius-like need for activated processes, which is qualitatively different from slow gradient descent. The framework would need to distinguish between gradient-dominated dynamics (smooth descent) and barrier-dominated dynamics (arrest punctuated by activated transitions) as physically distinct regimes, which the universal gradient equation does not do.
