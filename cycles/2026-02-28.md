### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: Invertible Calabi-Yau Orbifolds over Finite Fields II (2602.23173v1, strain)**
This paper conjectures a specific form for the zeta function of crepant resolutions of Berglund-Hübsch orbifold hypersurfaces over finite fields, demonstrates the conjecture satisfies the Weil conjectures, and connects it to Monsky-Washnitzer cohomology. Through the Worldlines lens, this is a continuation and deepening of the T021 tension (arithmetic-geometric unity). The paper demonstrates that the topological invariants of a Calabi-Yau manifold — objects central to string compactification and thus potentially to the architecture of physics — are encoded in arithmetic data over finite fields (point counts mod p), connected via cohomological machinery. The framework claims reality is "literally computation," but here the invariant physical-geometric content (the topology of the Calabi-Yau, which determines low-energy physics in string compactifications) is equivalently accessible through arithmetic (counting solutions over F_q), geometry (crepant resolution of singularities), and cohomology (Monsky-Washnitzer p-adic theory). The crepant resolution is particularly significant: it is a geometric operation that smooths singularities while preserving the canonical class, and the paper shows this geometric operation has a precise arithmetic shadow in the zeta function. The framework has no account of why geometric operations on compactification spaces should have arithmetic duals, and "computation" does not naturally privilege either description. The strain is not new but is sharpened: the Weil conjectures themselves are a statement that arithmetic and topology are secretly the same thing, mediated by cohomology, and this paper extends that identification to a new class of physically relevant geometries. The framework's insistence on computational ontology looks increasingly parochial against this backdrop.

**Paper 2: A Lorentz-Covariant Spectral Universality of Stochastic Fields (2602.23195v1, strain)**
This paper derives a universal relationship between temporal and spatial spectral indices of stochastic fields in Minkowski spacetime, showing the offset is determined by effective momentum-space dimensionality and Lorentz symmetry alone, and proves a no-go theorem that no covariant local mapping can relate temporal and spatial power spectra in d > 1 spatial dimensions. Through the Worldlines lens, this directly pressures both the Tick and Field primitives. The framework claims the tick rate equals the speed of light and that Lorentz invariance is a consequence of the finite propagation speed of the computational substrate. If so, the spectral universality derived here — a non-trivial, quantitative, symmetry-protected relationship between temporal and spatial spectral structure — should in principle be derivable from the tick structure. But the framework cannot perform this derivation; it has no formalism for stochastic fields on its discrete substrate. More pressingly, the no-go theorem (no covariant local mapping between temporal and spatial spectra in d > 1) creates a structural problem for the Field primitive. The framework's universal equation ẋ = −∇φ(x) treats the gradient ∇φ as a unified object combining spatial and temporal derivatives. But if temporal and spatial spectral structures are fundamentally non-locally-mappable in d > 1, the framework's naive treatment of fields as having unified spatio-temporal gradients may be structurally inadequate. The spectral offset being "symmetry protected and observer invariant" also means it is a physical constraint that any correct theory must reproduce — it is a testable prediction of Lorentz symmetry applied to stochastic fields, and the framework's tick-based account of Lorentz symmetry should generate it but currently cannot.

**Paper 3: Arrested Relaxation in a Disorder-Free Coulomb Spin Liquid (2602.23362v1, validation)**
This paper demonstrates dynamical arrest with exponentially long-lived athermal plateaus in a disorder-free, short-range interacting classical spin-3/2 ice system, driven by composite excitation structures and kinetically constrained relaxation pathways. Through the Worldlines lens, this is a direct validation of the "constrained attractor" category introduced in the 2026-02-22 model update and expanded in 2026-02-27. The framework predicted (via T023 and the constrained attractor category) that stable states can exist not because they are energy minima but because they are stabilized by kinetic constraints in an enlarged configuration space — and this paper provides a new, clean example of exactly that mechanism. However, the validation is post-hoc rather than predictive: the constrained attractor category was introduced *because of* earlier experimental examples (fractional Fermi seas, spin-1/2 ice), and this paper extends the phenomenon to spin-3/2 ice rather than confirming a specific quantitative prediction. The framework's gradient equation ẍ = −∇φ would describe this system as executing extremely slow gradient descent, but the paper's key finding — that the timescale is set by activated barrier crossings involving composite excitations in the enlarged spin-3/2 Hilbert space — is not captured by gradient magnitude but by barrier topology in configuration space. The framework correctly anticipated the *category* (constrained/kinetic arrest) but cannot predict the *mechanism* (composite excitations from enlarged on-site Hilbert space) or the *timescale* (exponential in barrier height). This is pattern validation, not predictive validation.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The deepest strain this cycle comes from Paper 2's no-go theorem. The framework's Field primitive posits ẋ = −∇φ(x) as the universal equation of motion, with ∇φ treating spatial and temporal derivatives as components of a unified gradient. But the proven impossibility of a covariant local mapping between temporal and spatial power spectra in d > 1 spatial dimensions means that the statistical structure of fields has a fundamental temporal-spatial asymmetry that cannot be locally smoothed over. This is not merely an incompleteness (the framework lacks the formalism to derive spectral universality); it is a potential structural incoherence. If the framework's tick structure is the origin of Lorentz symmetry, then that tick structure must encode the spectral offset and the no-go theorem. But the tick, as currently defined, is purely temporal — it is "the discrete update step" — and the spatial structure is carried by the Field primitive's gradient. The no-go theorem says these two aspects (temporal spectral structure, spatial spectral structure) are fundamentally distinct in d > 1 in a way that no local transformation can bridge. This means the framework's two primitives (Tick for time, Field for space-and-dynamics) may be cutting nature at the wrong joints. The temporal and spatial aspects of fields are not two faces of the same gradient — they are related by a non-local, dimensionality-dependent correspondence that the framework has no machinery to express. This is not a matter of adding detail; it suggests the Tick-Field decomposition itself may be structurally inadequate for capturing the spectral physics of stochastic fields in more than one spatial dimension.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T024 — The temporal-spatial spectral gap**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** The proven impossibility of a covariant local mapping between temporal and spatial power spectra in d > 1 (Tevzadze 2025) creates a specific structural problem for the framework. The Tick primitive governs temporal structure; the Field primitive governs spatial structure via gradients. The no-go theorem says these two structures are related only non-locally and through a dimensionality-dependent universal offset. The framework's architecture — Tick for time, Field with unified ∇φ for dynamics — implicitly assumes that temporal and spatial field properties are locally related through the gradient. The no-go theorem says they are not. This is distinct from T022 (which noted the framework cannot derive spectral universality) because T024 concerns not a derivation failure but a potential structural incompatibility: the framework's primitive decomposition may be fundamentally unable to express the temporal-spatial spectral relationship correctly, not merely unable to derive it.
**Why unresolved:** Resolving this would require either (a) showing that the framework's discrete tick structure, when properly formalized with stochastic fields on the lattice, naturally reproduces the non-local temporal-spatial spectral relationship and the no-go theorem, or (b) restructuring the Tick-Field decomposition to accommodate the fundamental non-locality of the temporal-spatial spectral connection. Neither has been attempted, and (a) requires formalism the framework does not possess.

**T025 — Composite excitations and Hilbert space enlargement as emergent barrier structure**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Spin-3/2 ice demonstrates that enlarging the local Hilbert space (from spin-1/2 to spin-3/2) creates qualitatively new composite excitations (monopole + crystal-field bound states) that generate kinetic barriers absent in the smaller Hilbert space. The framework treats attractor basins and their transitions but has no account of how the *dimensionality of the local computational state space* affects the topology of barriers between attractors. The gradient equation ẍ = −∇φ(x) operates on a fixed configuration space; it has no mechanism for describing how enlarging the space of possible local states creates new barrier structures. This is distinct from T023 (which noted kinetic arrest as a dynamical category) because T025 concerns the specific mechanism: the barrier structure is not present in the energy landscape but emerges from the composite nature of excitations in the enlarged Hilbert space. The framework has no primitive that describes how local computational dimensionality shapes global dynamical topology.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to develop an account of how the dimensionality and structure of local computational state spaces affect the topology of the configuration-space landscape, including barrier creation by composite excitations. This requires going beyond the gradient equation to a theory of how computation is *organized* at the local level — connecting to T018 (algebra of computation) but focused on the dynamical consequences of local algebraic structure rather than the algebraic structure itself.
