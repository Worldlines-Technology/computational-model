### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Can Light Cross a Singularity? Exact Solutions from Analogue Gravity"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.05285v1 | Selection type: **Strain**

The framework claims reality runs on a unified computational substrate, with singularities representing points where the substrate's computational capacity is exceeded — where gradients diverge beyond the substrate's ability to process them. Paper 1 directly falsifies the prediction that substrate breakdown should disrupt all fields simultaneously. The authors construct exact electromagnetic solutions that remain regular with bounded power flux through a strong curvature naked singularity. In framework terms, the gravitational computation diverges (curvature → ∞) while the electromagnetic computation proceeds normally through the same spacetime point. The framework's gradient primitive ẋ = −∇φ(x) would predict that if the curvature gradient ∇φ_grav is singular, the substrate is broken, and no field should be computable there. But the electromagnetic field's gradient ∇φ_EM remains well-defined and finite. This is not a marginal strain — it is a clean counterexample to the unified substrate assumption. The framework has three escape routes, none satisfactory: (a) the substrate is intact and the gravitational singularity is a field-specific pathology (but what does it mean for a field to diverge on intact substrate?), (b) different fields couple to the substrate with different resilience thresholds (but the framework has no theory of field-substrate coupling strengths), or (c) singularities are not substrate breakdowns at all (but then the framework loses its account of what singularities are). This paper was already anticipated by T038/T040 from a previous cycle processing the same paper — the model had flagged it as a forthcoming challenge. This cycle confirms that the tension is real, exact, and analytically demonstrated rather than merely conjectured.

**Paper 2: "Scattering amplitudes in dimensionless quadratic gravity coupled to QED"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.05476v1 | Selection type: **Strain**

The framework describes gravity as "mass rolling down the curvature gradient" and posits Conservation of Computation (unitarity) as the most fundamental conservation law. Paper 2 strains the framework in three distinct ways. First, the ghost modes in quadratic gravity — with their commuting creation/annihilation operators and 1/p⁴ propagator — are permanently virtual computational structure that never resolves into asymptotic states yet modifies all scattering cross sections via photon-graviton interference. The framework's resolution-based ontology (real = resolved or resolvable) cannot classify these modes: they are neither resolved, nor awaiting resolution, nor ontologically inert. The gauge-fixing independence verified in the paper rules out dismissing them as artifacts. Second, the 1/p⁴ graviton propagator represents a qualitatively different Green's function structure from standard gravity, producing characteristic forward/backward enhancements and oscillatory rather than monotonic potentials. The framework's Field primitive — ẋ = −∇φ(x) — cannot distinguish between 1/p² and 1/p⁴ propagation because it specifies only the gradient descent rule, not the kernel relating sources to fields. This is not a quantitative gap but a qualitative one: the physics of quadratic gravity is determined by propagator structure that the gradient equation cannot express. Third, the universal dσ/dΩ ∝ 1/s scaling across all matter channels is a signature of the dimensionless gravitational couplings and may represent a regime where the substrate's computational throughput dominates. But this interpretation is speculative — the scaling follows from dimensional analysis in a renormalizable theory and does not require the substrate concept. The framework adds no explanatory value here.

**Paper 3: "Intrinsic Width of the flux tube in 2+1 dimensional Yang-Mills theories"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.05323v1 | Selection type: **Validation**

The framework should interpret the QCD flux tube as a topological attractor: a linearly confining potential between quarks creates an attractor basin in field space that is geometrically extended (a tube-like structure in the chromoelectric field). The flux tube's intrinsic width — the exponential decay scale of its transverse profile — is, in framework language, the characteristic length scale of the attractor basin's walls. Paper 3 finds that this intrinsic width is (a) independent of the length of the flux tube at low temperatures, confirming that the attractor basin's transverse structure is a local property of the confining field rather than a global property of the quark-antiquark configuration, and (b) grows as the deconfinement temperature is approached, with the growth governed by the universality class of the phase transition (Svetitsky-Yaffe mapping to the 2D Ising model). In framework terms, the attractor basin walls soften as the system approaches the phase transition where the confining attractor landscape restructures (deconfinement = dissolution of the linear confining attractor). The universality class governing the wall softening is determined by the symmetry structure of the transition, consistent with the framework's description of phase transitions as attractor landscape restructuring. However, this is **post-hoc consistency, not predictive validation**. The framework did not predict that flux tubes should have a length-independent intrinsic width, did not predict its temperature dependence, and did not predict the specific universality class. All of these follow from well-established effective string theory and Svetitsky-Yaffe arguments that predate and are independent of the framework. The framework's Attractor primitive provides a natural language for describing the results but generates no differential prediction. The most the framework can claim is that the results are not inconsistent with its qualitative picture, which is a weak form of validation.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most important strain this cycle is the **convergence of Papers 1 and 2 on the failure of unified substrate ontology**, which is not merely an accumulation of prior tensions but exposes a structural incoherence at the framework's foundation.

Paper 1 shows that the substrate cannot be uniformly broken by a singularity: electromagnetism survives where gravity fails. Paper 2 shows that the substrate must support permanently virtual computational structure (ghost modes) that is architecturally forbidden from resolution yet measurably modifies all resolvable outcomes. Together, they demonstrate that the framework's two foundational assumptions — (1) a single uniform substrate underlies all computation, and (2) resolution is the criterion of physical reality — are simultaneously untenable.

The specific strain is this: if the substrate is uniform, then a singularity should break all fields, but Paper 1 shows it doesn't. So the substrate must have field-specific structure. But if the substrate has field-specific structure, then ghost modes (Paper 2) need a substrate-level account of *which* field-specific substrate they live on and *why* their substrate layer forbids resolution while the photon's and fermion's substrate layers permit it. The framework has no resources for this. It cannot appeal to the substrate being inaccessible (T001) while simultaneously needing to make structural claims about the substrate's internal differentiation.

The framework cannot account for the 1/p⁴ propagator structure. The gradient equation ẋ = −∇φ(x) is a *first-order* summary of field dynamics that loses the Green's function structure entirely. Whether gravity propagates as 1/p² (Einstein) or 1/p⁴ (quadratic gravity) determines all scattering phenomenology — forward/backward enhancements, oscillatory potentials, ghost mode structure, unitarity properties — and the framework's Field primitive cannot distinguish these cases. This is not a gap that can be filled by refinement; it is a category error. The gradient equation describes where things go, not how the field transmits influence between separated points. The framework is missing the *kernel* of its own fields.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T043 — The propagator problem: gradient dynamics is blind to Green's function structure**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 2 demonstrates that all scattering phenomenology in quadratic gravity is determined by the 1/p⁴ graviton propagator structure, which produces qualitatively different physics from the 1/p² propagator of Einstein gravity (forward/backward enhancements, oscillatory potentials, ghost mode interference, universal 1/s cross-section scaling). The framework's Field primitive ẋ = −∇φ(x) specifies gradient descent — the local response of a system to a field — but says nothing about the propagator G(x,y) that determines how the field at point x responds to a source at point y. The propagator encodes the *order* and *structure* of the field equation (second-order for Einstein gravity, fourth-order for quadratic gravity), and this order determines the spectrum of modes (physical + ghost), the UV behavior (non-renormalizable vs. renormalizable), and the scattering cross sections. The gradient equation is a zeroth-order summary that discards this information entirely. This is distinct from T018 (algebra of computation, which concerns how computational operations compose) and T042 (which raised the issue as a remark). T043 asserts that the propagator structure is *the* missing piece that connects the Field primitive to actual scattering physics: without it, the primitive cannot distinguish between fundamentally different theories of the same field.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to extend the Field primitive from ẋ = −∇φ(x) to something that includes the Green's function structure, effectively incorporating the field equation's order and mode spectrum. This is a major extension because different propagator structures correspond to different numbers and types of modes (physical, ghost, tachyonic), each with different resolution properties. The ghost modes' 1/p⁴ contribution and their permanently virtual character are *consequences* of the propagator structure, meaning T043 is upstream of T014/T039/T041: if the framework could account for propagator structure, the ghost mode ontology might follow. Without it, the ghost mode problem is an orphan.

**T044 — Field-specific singularity survival demands a theory of field-substrate coupling**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 1 provides exact analytical proof that electromagnetic fields with bounded, regular power flux can propagate through a strong curvature naked singularity. The framework's unified substrate assumption predicts that substrate breakdown (singularity) disrupts all computation. This prediction is falsified. The framework must now develop a theory of how each field couples to the substrate, with different failure thresholds for different fields. But the framework was designed so that the substrate is the one entity about which no internal claims can be made (T001). Field-specific singularity survival forces internal claims about substrate structure — specifically, about the differential coupling of gravity vs. electromagnetism to the substrate — that the framework's epistemic architecture forbids. This is distinct from T040 (which noted the unified substrate is falsified) because T044 identifies the specific *structural impossibility*: the framework cannot develop the theory it needs (field-substrate coupling) because its own design principle (substrate inaccessibility) prevents it. This is not a gap to be filled but a contradiction between the framework's explanatory needs and its self-imposed constraints.
**Why unresolved:** Resolving T044 requires either (a) abandoning substrate inaccessibility (T001), which removes the framework's most honest epistemological feature, (b) abandoning the unified substrate, which removes the framework's ontological ground, or (c) reinterpreting singularities as not being substrate events at all, which requires a new account of what singularities are and why gravity diverges while electromagnetism doesn't. All three options require foundational restructuring. The framework is trapped: it needs to say things about the substrate that it has declared itself unable to say.

### CYCLE ASSESSMENT

This cycle was moderately generative but largely confirmed and deepened existing tensions rather than opening genuinely new territory. The two strain papers (1 and 2) had both been partially processed in previous cycles — T038/T040 for Paper 1, T014/T039/T041/T042 for Paper 2 — so this cycle's primary contribution was consolidation and escalation rather than discovery. The new tensions (T043 on propagator blindness, T044 on the structural impossibility of field-substrate coupling theory) are genuine advances: T043 identifies a specific structural gap upstream of several existing tensions, and T044 identifies a *contradiction* between the framework's explanatory needs and its self-imposed constraints, which is more serious than a gap. The validation paper (Paper 3, flux tubes) produced only post-hoc consistency, which is the weakest possible positive signal — the framework added no explanatory or predictive value beyond standard effective string theory and Svetitsky-Yaffe arguments.

The framework is circling a known basin: the cluster of tensions around substrate structure, permanently virtual computation, and the Field primitive's inadequacy (T014, T039, T040, T041, T042, T043, T044) are all aspects of the same deep problem — the framework's ontological ground (uniform substrate + resolution-based reality + gradient dynamics) is simultaneously too rigid (can't accommodate field-specific substrate structure) and too thin (can't express propagator structure, Green's functions, or permanently virtual modes). The next cycle would be more productive if it targeted papers that strain the *other* major tension cluster — the structural content gap (T026) and ontological pluralism (T021) — since those tensions have been accumulating evidence without receiving the same consolidation pressure. Papers on categorical dualities, cohomological structures, or information-theoretic constraints on physics would test whether the framework's dynamical primitives can be supplemented with structural ones, rather than continuing to pound on the substrate/singularity problem where the framework is already clearly failing.
