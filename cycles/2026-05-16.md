**Paper 1: Non-Invertible Symmetries on Tensor-Product Hilbert Spaces and Quantum Cellular Automata**
ArXiv ID: 2605.15194v1 | Selection type: strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the pre-computational consistency problem (T107) and the scale-dependent symmetry type problem (T049): what kinds of symmetry structures can be realized on tensor-product Hilbert spaces — the natural substrate for lattice computation? The framework would interpret tensor-product Hilbert spaces as the computational substrate's local architecture and fusion category symmetries as organizational constraints on the computation. The paper proves two results that strain the framework severely. First, the QCA-refined realization theorem shows that non-invertible symmetries — symmetries that are not conservation laws and cannot be expressed as "computation is conserved" — can be systematically realized on lattice Hilbert spaces, but only when mixed with quantum cellular automata (QCA), which are locality-preserving automorphisms forming a group under composition. The QCA component is essential: the symmetry operators alone do not close into a consistent structure on the tensor-product space without QCA dressing. The framework's Conservation primitive treats symmetry as derived from conservation; this paper demonstrates that the physically realizable symmetries on lattice substrates require *automorphisms of the computational substrate itself* (QCAs) as an integral component. The QCA index — an integer-valued invariant from the GNVW classification — is determined by the categorical data (Frobenius-Perron dimensions), meaning the substrate's automorphism structure is dictated by the symmetry category, not by any dynamical primitive. Second, the weak integrality condition (that all Frobenius-Perron dimensions squared must be integers) is confirmed as the necessary condition for realizability on tensor-product spaces, meaning the substrate architecture imposes an arithmetic constraint on which symmetries are physically possible. The framework has no mechanism for deriving this constraint — it is a number-theoretic condition on categorical data that determines which organizational structures the substrate can support. The explicit Tambara-Yamagami construction demonstrates that the realization requires choosing specific algebraic data (bicharacter, Frobenius-Schur indicator) and constructing specific lattice operators — structural engineering that the framework's dynamical primitives cannot express.

**Paper 2: Extensive Long-Range Magic in Non-Abelian Topological Orders**
ArXiv ID: 2605.15150v1 | Selection type: strain

The Worldlines framework would interpret topological order as a type of attractor basin — a stable ground state of a gapped Hamiltonian. The framework's Attractor primitive describes basins by depth (stability/gap) and width (reach/correlation length), and the existing model has noted that attractor basins can be topologically distinguished (T048, noting cohomological classification of SPT phases). This paper reveals a new dimension of attractor characterization that the framework entirely lacks: *magic* — non-stabilizerness, the resource-theoretic distance from the set of efficiently classically simulable states. The central result is that ground states of non-Abelian string-net models possess extensive (scaling with system volume) long-range (surviving constant-depth local unitary circuits) magic. This is a no-go theorem: stabilizer states cannot even approximate these ground states up to constant-depth circuits. The framework's Attractor primitive distinguishes basins by energy depth and topological invariants but has no concept of the computational complexity resource content of the states within a basin. Magic is neither energy, nor topology, nor entanglement — it is a distinct resource that quantifies how far a quantum state is from classical simulability. The paper further proves that Abelian string-net phases realizable by stabilizer states have mutual braiding phases quantized by the on-site qudit dimension, and violation of this quantization condition *implies* extensive magic. This means a categorical/algebraic property of the anyonic excitations (non-Abelian fusion rules, non-quantized braiding) directly determines an information-theoretic resource property (magic) of the ground state. The framework has no mechanism connecting the algebraic properties of excitations to the computational resource content of the ground state — this is a new type of structural content (T026) that is simultaneously categorical, information-theoretic, and resource-theoretic. The higher-dimensional extension (any area-law state with nontrivial fusion spaces has extensive magic) means this is not a 2D curiosity but a dimensional-general structural theorem. The framework's claim that "consciousness is what sufficiently complex self-referential computation feels like from inside" implicitly assumes a uniform notion of computational complexity; magic provides a specific, quantifiable measure of "how non-classical" a computation is, and the result shows that certain attractor basins are constitutively non-classical in a way that scales with system size.

**Paper 3: Can a late-time cosmological model based on baby universe absorption explain the z-variation of w?**
ArXiv ID: 2605.15045v1 | Selection type: validation

The Worldlines framework claims dark energy is "the computational cost of instantiating new substrate" — as the universe expands, new computational volume must be created, manifesting as a repulsive cosmological effect. This paper proposes that exponential expansion arises not from a cosmological constant but from the absorption of "baby universes" — small closed spacetime bubbles that merge with our universe, transferring their spacetime volume. Through the framework's lens, this would be interpreted as: the computational substrate grows not by intrinsic generation of new computational volume but by *merging with external computational domains* (baby universes as independent computational patches that are absorbed). The model produces w(z) < −1 for sufficiently large z, crossing the phantom divide — a feature the framework's dark energy interpretation does not predict or constrain. The framework's claim is post-hoc consistent with this paper in only the weakest possible sense: one could narrate baby universe absorption as "new substrate being added to the computation," but this narration is entirely post-hoc and non-predictive. The framework did not predict that dark energy could arise from topology change (baby universe absorption), did not predict the z-dependence of w, did not predict the phantom crossing, and cannot distinguish this mechanism from a cosmological constant or from the other alternative dark energy mechanisms already catalogued (T086, T007). The paper actually *strains* the framework's dark energy claim more than it validates it: baby universe absorption requires topology change (new spatial topology created by the merger), which the framework has no mechanism for (T087), and the baby universes themselves are independent computational domains whose existence and properties are unexplained by any primitive. The framework's "computational cost of new substrate" picture imagines substrate being generated uniformly; baby universe absorption is a discrete, stochastic process of merging independent computations. The validation here is at the level of vocabulary application ("the universe expands, and something causes this"), not at the level of predictive content.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 2's demonstration that **non-Abelian topological order carries extensive long-range magic** — a computational resource property of ground states that is:

1. **Invisible to every primitive.** Magic is not energy (Attractor), not a conserved quantity (Conservation), not a gradient (Field), not a resolution event (Resolution), not a propagation rate (Tick). It is a resource-theoretic property measuring distance from classical simulability.

2. **Determined by categorical/algebraic data the framework cannot express.** The presence or absence of extensive magic is determined by whether the anyonic excitations have non-Abelian fusion rules and whether braiding phases violate a quantization condition set by the on-site Hilbert space dimension. These are categorical properties of the fusion category describing the topological order — precisely the kind of structural content T026 has been accumulating as missing.

3. **Constitutive and irremovable.** This is not a property that can be "resolved away" or that depends on how you look at the state. Extensive long-range magic survives any constant-depth local unitary circuit. It is a permanent, scale-extensive, structurally determined property of the attractor basin's ground state that the framework has no vocabulary for.

4. **Connects the attractor's algebraic type to its computational complexity in a way the framework cannot mediate.** The result establishes: non-Abelian fusion rules → extensive magic → constitutive non-classicality. The framework describes attractors by depth and width; it now needs to describe them by *magic content*, which is determined by the categorical symmetry structure, not by any dynamical feature.

Paper 1 compounds this strain by showing that the *realizability* of symmetry structures on the computational substrate requires QCA automorphisms whose indices are fixed by categorical data (Frobenius-Perron dimensions). The substrate's own automorphism group is not a free architectural parameter but is constrained by the symmetry category it hosts. The framework treats the substrate as a fixed platform; Paper 1 shows the substrate's automorphism structure is *determined by* the categorical symmetry — another instance of T059 (ontological inversion) at the substrate level.

Together, these papers demonstrate that the framework is missing an entire axis of physical characterization: the *computational resource content* of quantum states (magic, non-stabilizerness) and the *categorical constraints on substrate automorphisms* (QCA indices from fusion categories). Both are structural, both are physically consequential, and neither has any point of contact with the five dynamical primitives.
