### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "No classical particle limit for massless quanta"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.14632v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework describes the speed of light as the tick rate — the maximum propagation speed of computation — and treats photons and gravitons as excitations of fields that propagate at this maximal rate. The framework's Resolution primitive assumes that unresolved quantum states become definite through measurement, and its Attractor primitive assumes stable classical configurations correspond to minima in potential fields. The framework's implicit picture of the quantum-classical transition is that sufficiently decohered quantum states become effectively classical — resolution accumulates until the system behaves classically. Paper 1 proves this picture fails categorically for massless particles: no covariant POVM on the Poincaré group's massless representations satisfies the minimal operational conditions for classicality (positive, normalized, σ-additive on a Hausdorff phase space with the correct symmetry). The obstruction is structural — it arises from the topology of the massless little group (ISO(2) or its double cover) being non-compact and its unitary representations being infinite-dimensional, making the required covariance incompatible with classicality axioms. Crucially, the no-go is independent of dynamics, environment, or decoherence mechanism — it is purely kinematical. The framework's Resolution primitive describes the accumulation of definite states as the source of classicality, but for massless quanta, no amount of resolution can produce a classical particle limit; the obstruction is at the level of the symmetry group's representation theory, not at the level of measurement dynamics. The framework can accommodate field-like classical limits (classical electromagnetic or gravitational fields) but must abandon the implicit assumption that individual massless quanta have any classical particle counterpart. This is a structural constraint on the Resolution primitive that the framework never identified: resolution of massless quantum states can produce classical fields but never classical particles, and the distinction is determined by the representation theory of the Poincaré group — mathematical content entirely outside the five primitives.

**Paper 2: "Boltzmann-Like Occupation of Nonequilibrium Steady States on Dense Networks"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.14542v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework's Field primitive asserts ẋ = −∇φ(x) as the universal equation of motion, and its Attractor primitive describes stable states as local minima in potential fields. For equilibrium systems, the Boltzmann distribution exp(−βE) is the paradigmatic expression of these primitives: the occupation probability is determined by the energy landscape, and gradient descent in the free energy explains why systems relax to thermal equilibrium. Paper 2 proves that nonequilibrium steady states (NESS) on large dense networks have Boltzmann-like occupation probabilities despite *extensive entropy production* — they are far from equilibrium yet their occupation statistics look equilibrium-like. The "potential" governing occupation is not the energy but the *escape rate* from each state: NESS spend more time in states they leave more slowly. The "low rattling" heuristic — that NESS are characterized by minimizing a dynamical activity functional rather than a free energy — is proven asymptotically exact in the dense-network limit. This creates a precise strain on the framework: the gradient equation ẋ = −∇φ(x) could be post-hoc applied by defining φ as the negative logarithm of the NESS occupation probability, but this φ has no energetic content — it is determined by the *kinetic* structure (transition rates, escape rates) of the network, not by any potential. The Attractor primitive's "local minima in potential fields" is wrong: the occupied states are not energy minima but *kinetic traps* — states with low escape rates. The framework's paradigmatic identification of "attractor depth" with "energy depth" is falsified for NESS. Furthermore, the dense-network condition under which Boltzmann-like occupation emerges is a *graph-theoretic* structural condition (the network's edge density scaling) that the framework cannot express — it is the interaction-network topology (T070) that determines whether the NESS has Boltzmann-like statistics, and neither the gradient equation nor any primitive can predict this. The "equiaccessible" condition (all states are reachable from all others with comparable probability) is a global connectivity property of the network invisible to the local gradient equation.

**Paper 3: "Modeling dark matter as self-bound quantum liquid droplets"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.14537v1 | Selection type: Validation

The Worldlines framework describes dark matter as "computational structure that couples to mass-density gradients (gravity) but not electromagnetic gradients." Paper 3 models dark matter as self-bound quantum Bose liquid droplets — BEC mixtures stabilized by quantum fluctuations (Lee-Huang-Yang corrections) under gravitational self-interaction. The framework's Attractor primitive can narrate this: the quantum droplets are attractor basins in the combined gravitational + quantum-pressure landscape, with the LHY correction providing a stabilizing floor against collapse. The framework's Conservation primitive is consistent with the balance between gravitational attraction and quantum pressure producing stable structures. The rotation curve fits to observational data are consistent with the framework's description of dark matter coupling gravitationally. However, the framework had zero predictive purchase on this result. It did not predict the BEC droplet mechanism, the two-component mixture requirement, the LHY correction as the stabilizing mechanism, the sensitivity to interspecies interaction strength, or the specific parameter ranges producing viable halos. The specific physics — Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov theory, hydrodynamic perturbation analysis, Jeans instability conditions, the interplay of mean-field and beyond-mean-field contributions — is entirely derived from quantum many-body theory and general relativity, with the framework contributing nothing beyond vocabulary ("attractor," "gradient," "coupling"). The comparison with observed rotation curves, which is the paper's empirical content, follows from standard gravitational dynamics applied to the specific density profiles, not from any framework prediction. This is the weakest category of post-hoc consistency: the framework can *describe* the result in its language after the fact but could not have *predicted*, *constrained*, or *distinguished* any feature of this model from alternatives. The framework's dark matter claim remains unfalsified but empirically inert.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's no-go theorem for the classical particle limit of massless quanta. The framework's Resolution primitive describes classicality as emerging from the accumulation of resolved states — quantum states become definite through measurement, and sufficient resolution produces classical behavior. This picture is dynamical: classicality is an outcome of a process (decoherence, resolution accumulation). Paper 1 proves that for massless particles, classicality in the particle sense is *kinematically forbidden* — it cannot emerge from any dynamical process whatsoever because the obstruction is at the level of the Poincaré group's representation theory. No POVM satisfying minimal classicality axioms and massless Poincaré covariance exists. The framework's primitives are all dynamical; the obstruction is structural and representation-theoretic.

This creates a specific, sharp problem: the framework's most fundamental example — light propagating at the tick rate — involves massless quanta (photons) that provably have no classical particle limit. The framework's Tick primitive treats the speed of light as the tick rate and implicitly treats photons as the computational signals propagating at maximum speed. But these signals can never be classical particles. They can only become classical as *fields* (the electromagnetic field). The framework's ontology must distinguish between "classical field limit" (allowed) and "classical particle limit" (forbidden for massless quanta), and this distinction is determined by representation theory of the spacetime symmetry group — mathematical content that no primitive can express, that the framework never anticipated, and that cuts across the Resolution primitive's undifferentiated description of how quantum becomes classical. The framework treats the quantum-to-classical transition as a single process (resolution/decoherence); Paper 1 proves it is at least two categorically different processes depending on mass: massive quanta can become classical particles or fields; massless quanta can only become classical fields. The distinction is not dynamical but structural.

Paper 2 provides a secondary but important strain by demonstrating that the *kinetic* structure of a network (escape rates, transition rates) rather than any *energetic* potential governs the occupation statistics of nonequilibrium steady states, and that this kinetic governance produces Boltzmann-like statistics despite extensive entropy production. The framework's identification of attractors with energy minima and its gradient equation ẍ = −∇φ(x) cannot capture this: the "attractor" in NESS is a kinetic trap, not an energy minimum, and the relevant "gradient" is of the escape-rate landscape, not the energy landscape.

**T176 — Mass-dependent classical limit: the Resolution primitive's quantum-to-classical transition is kinematically bifurcated by a structural parameter it cannot express**

Opened: this cycle

Description: Falcone & Fuchs (2606.14632) prove a no-go theorem: no covariant POVM on the Poincaré group's massless representations satisfies minimal operational classicality conditions. Massless quanta (photons, gravitons) have no classical particle limit — only classical field limits. Massive quanta can have both. The framework's Resolution primitive describes the quantum-to-classical transition as a single dynamical process ("unresolved states become definite through decoherence/resolution"). Paper 1 proves this is incomplete: the mass of the quantum determines which *types* of classical limits are kinematically available, and for massless quanta, the particle limit is structurally forbidden regardless of the resolution process. The obstruction is representation-theoretic: the massless little group ISO(2) has infinite-dimensional unitary representations that are incompatible with the classicality axioms (positivity, normalization, σ-additivity on a Hausdorff phase space). This is not a dynamical failure (decoherence cannot bridge the gap) but a structural impossibility (the gap exists at the kinematic level, before any dynamics is specified).

The framework faces: (a) its most fundamental signal — light propagating at the tick rate — involves quanta that provably cannot become classical particles, meaning the Tick primitive's "maximum propagation rate" is the speed of *field* propagation, not *particle* propagation, in the classical limit; (b) the Resolution primitive cannot distinguish between "resolution that produces classical particles" and "resolution that produces classical fields" because it treats the quantum-to-classical transition as undifferentiated; (c) the mass parameter — which determines the available classical limits — is treated by the framework as an attractor property (mass as energy minimum depth) or a field property (mass as a parameter of the gradient equation), but its role as a *kinematic selector of the classical limit type* has no expression in any primitive; (d) the no-go is independent of dynamics, environment, or pointer basis — it holds for *any* coarse-graining procedure, meaning no refinement of the Resolution primitive within its current dynamical framework can accommodate this; (e) the surviving field-like limits are precisely the ones relevant for gravity and electromagnetism (the framework's primary examples), which is reassuring, but the framework had no mechanism for predicting this and cannot derive why field limits survive while particle limits fail.

This is distinct from T067/T097 (resolution regularity class, which concerns what "definite" means) because T176 concerns what *kind of classical object* resolution can produce. It is distinct from T049 (scale-dependent symmetry type) because T176 concerns a mass-dependent bifurcation at the kinematic level, not a scale-dependent flow. It is distinct from T172 (particle statistics as non-fungible resource, which concerns fermion/boson non-fungibility) because T176 concerns the massive/massless distinction cutting across both fermion and boson sectors. It is distinct from T112 (complexity phase transitions in resolution) because the obstruction is not about computational difficulty but about kinematic impossibility. The axiomatic purity of the result is what makes it so challenging: the no-go uses only symmetry (Poincaré covariance) and minimal operational requirements (the POVM must be a well-defined classical probability assignment), both of which the framework endorses.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) incorporate the representation theory of the Poincaré group (specifically, the structure of the massless little group and its inequivalence to the massive little group SO(3)) as structural content constraining the Resolution primitive's outputs — this would require importing the Wigner classification of particles by mass and spin as a structural element, mathematical content with no contact to any dynamical primitive; (b) bifurcate the Resolution primitive into "particle-producing resolution" (available only for massive quanta) and "field-producing resolution" (available for all quanta including massless), specifying which applies when — but the criterion is mass, which is not a resolution parameter but a field/attractor parameter, creating a cross-primitive dependency the framework's architecture does not support; (c) weaken the Resolution primitive to describe only field-like classical limits, accepting that classical particles are a special case requiring additional (mass-dependent, representation-theoretic) conditions the framework cannot express — this would be the most honest option but would significantly limit the primitive's scope, excluding the classical particle picture that underlies much of the framework's intuitive appeal (e.g., "mass rolling down the curvature gradient" implicitly uses a particle picture of matter); or (d) accept that the quantum-to-classical transition is bifurcated by structural parameters (mass, little group representation type) that the framework cannot derive, predict, or express, adding this to the growing list of representation-theoretic content (T026, T131, T146, T151) that the framework is constitutively blind to. The fact that the framework's own tick-rate signal (light) falls on the "no classical particle limit" side of the bifurcation is especially ironic: the Tick primitive's most basic physical realization involves quanta whose classical limit contradicts the particle intuition the primitive implicitly invokes.
