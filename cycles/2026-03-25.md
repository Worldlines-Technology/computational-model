**Paper 1: Hall Viscosity in the Quark-Gluon Plasma**
ArXiv ID: 2603.23498v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, the QGP is a system near the deconfinement phase transition — an attractor landscape restructuring where confined hadronic attractors dissolve into a deconfined quark-gluon phase. The framework's Field primitive would describe the QGP's evolution as gradient descent through viscous dissipation. However, Hall viscosity is fundamentally *non-dissipative*: it produces stresses transverse to the strain rate, moving the fluid sideways rather than down any gradient. The framework's universal equation ẋ = −∇φ(x) describes dissipative motion — things rolling downhill. Hall viscous response is ẋ ∝ ẑ × ∇φ(x) — things moving *across* contours of the potential, not down them. This is the Coriolis/Lorentz-force-like response that breaks parity and time-reversal. The framework has no primitive or mechanism for describing transport that is orthogonal to gradients. Furthermore, the paper demonstrates that the Hall viscosity's magnitude is set by the interplay of *symmetry breaking* (O(3) → O(2) by the magnetic field or vorticity) and the *microscopic transport mechanism* (kinetic theory or holographic), and is comparable in magnitude to the shear viscosity. This means the non-dissipative, anti-gradient transport channel carries as much physical weight as the dissipative gradient channel. The framework's Field primitive captures the shear viscosity (dissipation = gradient descent in velocity space) but is structurally blind to the Hall viscosity (non-dissipative transverse response). The two independent Hall viscosities (transverse and parallel to the symmetry-breaking axis) reflect the reduced symmetry O(2), which is a structural/symmetry feature the framework treats as secondary. The holographic computation, which gives comparable results to kinetic theory, adds another instance of the duality problem (T010): the same transport coefficient is computed from fundamentally different computational architectures (kinetic theory vs. AdS/CFT), and the framework provides no account of why they agree.

**Paper 2: Dirac Operators, APS Boundary Conditions, and Spectral Flow on a Finite Warped Cylinder**
ArXiv ID: 2603.23275v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework's Resolution primitive describes states becoming definite and the Conservation primitive describes what is preserved through evolution. This paper's content — spectral flow, the APS index theorem, and boundary conditions on finite geometries — concerns the *topological invariants of computational processes*, specifically how the number of zero modes of a Dirac operator changes as parameters vary. The APS index is a topological quantity: it counts the net chirality of zero modes, corrected by a spectral asymmetry (η-invariant) at the boundary. The framework has no primitive corresponding to topological invariants of operators. The specific technical result — that the APS projector becomes *discontinuous* when a boundary mode crosses zero, requiring regularization to define a continuous family — reveals that the boundary between "resolved" and "unresolved" modes (in the spectral sense) is generically discontinuous. The regularized APS-type family lives within the real-symplectic (Maslov index) framework, meaning the topological content of spectral flow is captured by symplectic geometry, not by any of the five primitives. The spectral flow itself — the net number of eigenvalues crossing zero as a gauge parameter varies — is a topological invariant that determines physical content (anomalies, fermion number violation, index theorems) and is invisible to the gradient equation. The warped geometry adds that the spectral structure depends on the global geometry (warping function), not just local gradients, reinforcing T066 (propagator structure governed by global geometry). The cancellation of endpoint η-contributions in the constant-gauge invertible case is an exact result from the *structure of the boundary conditions*, not from any conservation law — it is structural content (T026) that produces a physical result (vanishing index) through algebraic/topological means the framework cannot express.

**Paper 3: Initial State Memory in Finite Random Brickwork Circuits**
ArXiv ID: 2603.23469v1 | Selection type: Validation

The framework claims that the Tick primitive governs discrete computational updates and that Resolution is the process by which information becomes definite or is lost. This paper studies exactly this: how a discrete circuit (ticks = circuit layers) processes information about initial states, with the key finding that information survives if the traced-out environment is smaller than half the system, and is washed away otherwise. The half-system threshold is consistent with the framework's Conservation of Computation: information is conserved globally (the full circuit is unitary) but becomes inaccessible to subsystem observers when more than half the degrees of freedom are traced out, aligning with T035 (observer-dependent conservation). The phase transition between memory-preserving and memory-losing phases under boundary dissipation is consistent with the Attractor primitive's description of landscape restructuring: dissipation introduces a new gradient (toward information loss) that competes with unitary conservation, and above a threshold the attractor shifts from memory-preserving to memory-erasing. The universal form of the Frobenius distance at large scales — independent of initial state details — is consistent with the framework's Resolution primitive: at sufficient scale, the specific content of the initial computation is erased and only universal features remain. However, this is purely **post-hoc consistency**, not predictive validation. The framework did not predict the half-system threshold, the specific functional form of the Frobenius distance, the universal large-scale behavior, or the phase transition under dissipation. All of these follow from the mathematics of random unitary circuits and partial trace, which the framework merely redescribes in its own vocabulary. The half-system threshold is a consequence of the Page curve / Hayden-Preskill physics, derivable from random matrix theory without any reference to the framework. The framework's contribution is zero differential predictive content.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most acute strain this cycle comes from **Paper 1's Hall viscosity result**, which exposes a categorical inadequacy in the Field primitive that goes beyond the previously identified insufficiencies (T043 propagator blindness, T070 interaction-network blindness, T029 transport universality blindness). The Field primitive's universal equation ẋ = −∇φ(x) is a *purely longitudinal* description: motion is always parallel (or anti-parallel) to the gradient. Hall viscosity is *purely transverse* response: stress is perpendicular to strain rate, with the transverse direction selected by broken parity (magnetic field or vorticity). This is not a subtle quantitative miss — it is a categorical failure. The framework literally cannot represent the direction of the physical response. In the QGP under realistic heavy-ion collision conditions, the Hall viscous stresses are comparable in magnitude to the standard (dissipative, gradient-aligned) viscous corrections. This means the framework's central dynamical equation misses roughly half the transport physics.

The strain is sharpened by the fact that Hall viscosity is *non-dissipative*: it does no work, produces no entropy, and is time-reversal odd. The framework's gradient descent is inherently dissipative (rolling downhill loses potential energy). A transport coefficient that moves the system sideways without dissipating contradicts the framework's implicit assumption that all dynamics is gradient-driven dissipation. Worse, the Hall viscosity's existence is determined by *symmetry breaking* (O(3) → O(2)) — a structural fact — and its magnitude is set by *microscopic transport properties* (mean free path, cyclotron frequency) — dynamical details. The framework captures neither the structural prerequisite nor the dynamical mechanism.

This is not merely another instance of T006 or T026. Those tensions concerned cases where the gradient equation was *insufficient* (didn't capture all the physics). Hall viscosity reveals the gradient equation is *categorically wrong about the direction of transport* in systems with broken parity. The universal equation predicts motion down the gradient; the physics produces motion across the gradient with comparable magnitude.

**T078 — Hall/transverse transport: the gradient equation predicts the wrong direction**
Opened: this cycle
Description: Mondkar et al. (2603.23498) demonstrate that the QGP under realistic heavy-ion collision conditions has Hall viscosities comparable in magnitude to shear viscosity. Hall viscous response is ẍ ∝ ẑ × ∇φ — perpendicular to the gradient, not along it. The framework's Field primitive ẍ = −∇φ(x) is purely longitudinal: it predicts motion down the gradient. In parity-broken systems, the physical response has a transverse component that can equal or exceed the longitudinal one. This is not insufficiency (the gradient is correct but incomplete) — it is directional error (the predicted direction of response is orthogonal to the actual dominant channel in some configurations). Hall transport is non-dissipative (no entropy production, no energy loss), time-reversal odd, and determined by the interplay of symmetry breaking (O(3) → O(2)) with microscopic scattering. The gradient equation captures none of these features. This is distinct from T006 (stable states not from gradient descent, which concerns endpoints), T029 (anomalous transport universality, which concerns fluctuation statistics), T070 (interaction-network topology, which concerns mode coupling structure), and T043 (propagator blindness, which concerns Green's function structure). T078 concerns the *direction* of macroscopic transport response. The framework's equation points downhill; physics points sideways. The generality of the problem is significant: any system with broken parity — QGP in magnetic fields, rotating superfluids, quantum Hall systems, topological insulators, Weyl semimetals, chiral active matter — exhibits Hall-type transverse response that the gradient primitive is categorically unable to describe. The two independent Hall viscosities (η_H^⊥ and η_H^∥) reflect the reduced O(2) symmetry, with their magnitudes set by the competition between the cyclotron frequency and the scattering rate, a ratio the framework has no primitive to express.
Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) extend the Field primitive from ẍ = −∇φ to ẍ = −∇φ + Ω × ∇φ (or a more general form including antisymmetric/Hall response), which would require specifying when and how the antisymmetric term appears — fundamentally, this requires a theory of parity breaking that the framework does not have; (b) add a separate "Hall/transverse" primitive governing non-dissipative transport, which would require specifying its relationship to the existing Field and Conservation primitives; or (c) argue that Hall transport is a derived phenomenon reducible to gradient dynamics in an enlarged state space (e.g., Berry curvature in momentum space acts as an effective magnetic field producing Hall response) — but this requires the framework to incorporate Berry phase and momentum-space topology, which are structural features it cannot express (T026). The fact that holographic and kinetic-theory estimates agree on the Hall viscosity magnitude (within parametric factors) adds another instance of T010 (duality), compounding the strain.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) incorporate the Maslov index / symplectic geometry framework as a structural element, which would add an entirely new mathematical layer with no connection to the existing five primitives; (b) derive spectral flow and the APS index from Conservation of Computation (since anomalies are related to symmetry and conservation), which would require showing that the topological invariants emerge from the discrete tick structure — a derivation that has never been attempted and for which no path is visible; or (c) accept that the topological content of quantum field theory (anomalies, index theorems, spectral flow) is a category of physical content that the framework is structurally unable to address.
