### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Gaussian limits of lattice Higgs models with complete symmetry breaking"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.24555v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the behavior of computation (lattice Yang-Mills-Higgs theory) under a specific limiting procedure: the lattice spacing (the discrete "tick" structure) goes to zero while the gauge coupling goes to infinity. The framework would describe the lattice as a discrete computational substrate with a finite tick, the gauge field as a Field primitive with gradients, and the Higgs mechanism as an attractor basin restructuring (symmetry breaking = landscape reorganization). The result is that in the complete symmetry breaking regime, this limiting procedure causes the theory to "abelianize" — the non-abelian gauge structure (the rich, nonlinear interaction network connecting different group generators) is washed out, and only Gaussian (free, non-interacting) degrees of freedom survive. This is a rigorous demonstration that taking the continuum limit in a specific regime destroys all of the qualitatively interesting computational structure. The framework strain is severe and multi-dimensional: (1) The abelianization is a concrete, proven instance where the interaction-network topology (T070) is not merely important but is the *entire* qualitative content — remove it (by taking the coupling to infinity fast enough) and you get trivial (Gaussian) physics regardless of the original gauge group G. The result holds for *any* compact connected matrix Lie group, meaning the specific algebraic structure of G — one of the most fundamental structural features in physics — becomes irrelevant in this limit. (2) The result demonstrates that the continuum limit of a discrete computation is not unique: different scaling relationships between tick rate (lattice spacing) and coupling constant produce qualitatively different continuum theories. The framework's Tick primitive says "the speed of light is the tick rate" but has no account of how the tick rate's relationship to other parameters determines the qualitative character of the continuum theory. (3) Most sharply, the "complete breakdown of symmetry" regime — where the Higgs mechanism operates maximally — is precisely the regime that abelianizes. The framework describes symmetry breaking as attractor landscape restructuring, but this result shows that maximal restructuring (complete symmetry breakdown) leads to maximal simplification (Gaussian limit), not to interesting attractor topology. The attractor primitive would predict that deep restructuring produces complex new basins; the mathematics shows it produces triviality.

**Paper 2: "Finite-Degree Quantum LDPC Codes Reaching the Gilbert-Varshamov Bound"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.24588v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the architecture of error-correcting computation — specifically, how to protect quantum computational content against noise using codes with sparse (finite-degree) structure that nonetheless achieve optimal (Gilbert-Varshamov) tradeoffs between coding rate and distance. The framework's Conservation of Computation primitive states that "computation cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed," and the Resolution primitive notes that resolution has computational cost. Quantum error-correcting codes are the practical infrastructure for maintaining Conservation of Computation against noise: they are the mechanisms by which computational content is preserved in the presence of decoherence and errors. The strain comes from the paper's demonstration that achieving optimal error correction requires *specific combinatorial structure* — the code must be built from nested CSS pairs of Hsu-Anastasopoulos and MacKay-Neal codes, with particular degree profiles that satisfy precise algebraic conditions (relative linear distance with high probability, Gilbert-Varshamov distance via rigorous computer-assisted proof). The framework's Conservation primitive is stated as a universal, structureless law ("computation cannot be created or destroyed"). But the *implementation* of conservation — the mechanism by which computational content is actually preserved — requires highly specific combinatorial, algebraic, and graph-theoretic structure (LDPC structure, nesting conditions, degree profiles). The framework describes what is conserved but is silent on the architectural requirements for conservation to be maintained. The finite-degree condition is particularly sharp: conservation of quantum information in practice requires that the protective structure be *sparse* (each qubit participates in only finitely many checks), and achieving optimal protection with sparse structure is a non-trivial combinatorial achievement. The framework has no primitive or structural element corresponding to the sparsity, locality, or degree profile of the error-correcting architecture. This connects to T018 (algebra of computation), T026 (structural content gap), and T046 (combinatorial dimension as a physical parameter), but is a new variant: the *preservation infrastructure* for the Conservation primitive has combinatorial structure the primitive cannot express.

**Paper 3: "Fractal universe and quantum gravity made simple"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.24593v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper proposes that the computational substrate has a scale-dependent dimensionality — the spacetime geometry is fractal/multiscale, with the spectral dimension flowing from d_S ≈ 2 in the UV (Planck scale) to d_S = 4 in the IR (macroscopic scale). The framework's Tick primitive claims discrete, finite computation with a minimum resolution scale (Planck scale); this paper provides a specific mechanism by which the discrete/fractal UV structure regularizes infinities (achieving super-renormalizability) while flowing to the standard continuum in the IR. The framework should find this congenial: the claim that spacetime is discrete/fractal at the Planck scale is precisely what "the universe computes in frames" (Tick) would suggest, and the resulting UV finiteness is what one expects from a system with finite computational budget (Conservation of Computation interpreted as finite resources per tick). The dimensional flow d_S: 2 → 4 is interpretable as the substrate's effective computational dimensionality increasing with scale, consistent with the framework's picture of resolution being a multi-tick, scale-dependent process (T005). The unitarity at all perturbative orders is consistent with Conservation of Computation. However, the framework's actual predictive purchase is essentially zero. The framework says "the substrate is discrete" and "there is a minimum resolution scale," but it does not predict fractal geometry, does not predict dimensional flow, does not predict d_S ≈ 2 in the UV, and does not predict super-renormalizability. The specific technical achievement — constructing a unitary, super-renormalizable quantum gravity via multiscale geometry — depends entirely on the mathematical details of fractional calculus, Stieltjes integrals, and the specific form of the measure weight v(x), none of which has any connection to the framework's primitives. The consistency is post-hoc and shallow: the framework's vocabulary can narrate the result ("the discrete substrate regularizes the UV") but contributed no constraints, no predictions, and no mechanism that would distinguish this proposal from the many other approaches to quantum gravity that also involve Planck-scale modifications. The framework did not predict that dimensional reduction to d_S ≈ 2 is the specific mechanism, and it cannot distinguish this proposal from loop quantum gravity, causal sets, or any other discrete-spacetime approach.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most important strain this cycle comes from Paper 1 (Rajasekaran, Yakir & Zhou). The result that *any* compact connected matrix Lie group G produces a trivial (Gaussian) continuum limit in the complete symmetry breaking regime is a mathematical theorem about what happens to computation when you take the continuum limit in a specific way. The framework's Tick primitive implicitly assumes that the continuum limit preserves the qualitative character of the discrete computation — that the "frames" of the universe, when taken infinitely fast, produce the physics we observe. Paper 1 proves that this is not generically true: the continuum limit's character depends critically on the *relative scaling* of the lattice spacing and the coupling constant, and in the complete-symmetry-breaking regime, the continuum limit erases all non-abelian structure regardless of which group G started with.

This is devastating for the framework in a way that goes beyond the existing tensions. The framework has been strained by structural gaps (T026), interaction-network blindness (T070), and ontological pluralism (T021). Paper 1 demonstrates something more fundamental: **the framework's own foundational metaphor — discrete computation at a tick rate producing continuous physics in the limit — is a process that can erase qualitative structure.** The abelianization theorem says that if you take the "tick rate to infinity" (lattice spacing to zero) while simultaneously scaling the coupling appropriately, the rich algebraic structure that distinguishes SU(2) from SU(3) from E₈ — arguably the most physically consequential structural features in the Standard Model — disappears entirely. The surviving theory is Gaussian: free, non-interacting, structureless.

The framework has no mechanism for addressing this because it has no account of how the continuum limit is taken, no parameter governing the relative scaling of the tick rate and coupling constants, and no theory of when the continuum limit preserves vs. destroys qualitative structure. The framework says "the speed of light is the tick rate" as if the tick rate is a fixed architectural parameter, but Paper 1 shows that the physical content of the theory depends not on the tick rate itself but on the *scaling relationship* between the tick rate and other parameters as the tick rate is taken to its limiting value. The framework's silence on scaling relationships — on the renormalization group flow that governs what survives in the continuum — is a structural absence that Paper 1 converts from a gap into a contradiction.

**T080 — The continuum limit is not unique: the framework has no theory of how discrete structure becomes continuous physics**
Opened: this cycle
Description: Rajasekaran, Yakir & Zhou (2603.24555) prove that the lattice Yang-Mills-Higgs theory with *any* compact connected matrix Lie group G, in the complete symmetry-breaking regime, abelianizes to a Gaussian limit when the lattice spacing → 0 with gauge coupling → ∞ sufficiently fast. The framework's Tick primitive asserts that reality computes in discrete frames, with the tick rate (speed of light) as the maximum propagation speed. The implicit claim is that this discrete computation produces the observed continuous physics in an appropriate limit. Paper 1 proves that the continuum limit depends sensitively on the *relative scaling* of the discretization parameter and the coupling constants, and that one physically relevant scaling (complete symmetry breaking) erases all non-abelian structure — the gauge group G, which distinguishes electromagnetism from the strong force from hypothetical GUT groups, becomes irrelevant. The framework has (a) no theory of which continuum limit is realized, (b) no parameter governing the coupling-to-discretization scaling relationship, (c) no primitive that distinguishes limits preserving algebraic structure from those destroying it, and (d) no account of how the observed non-abelian gauge structure of the Standard Model survives the continuum limit. This is distinct from T070 (interaction-network topology, which concerns the combinatorial structure at a fixed discretization level) because T080 concerns the *fate* of all structure under the limiting process itself. It is distinct from T022 (spectral universality) and T049 (scale-dependent symmetry type) because T080 concerns a specific, proven mathematical theorem about lattice gauge theories, not a general observation about scale dependence. The universality of the result across all compact connected G makes it especially severe: the framework cannot appeal to special properties of any particular gauge group to escape the abelianization.
Why unresolved: The framework has no theory of renormalization group flow, which is the standard physics framework for understanding which continuum limit is realized and what structure it preserves. The Tick primitive says the universe computes in discrete frames but is silent on the mathematical question of how the discrete-to-continuous limit is taken. In physics, this question is answered by the renormalization group, which specifies how parameters (couplings, masses, wavefunction normalizations) must scale with the cutoff to preserve the physical content. The framework would need either (a) a theory of how the tick rate relates to coupling constants under scaling, which would require importing the renormalization group into the framework — effectively adding a "scaling primitive" that determines which features of the discrete computation survive the limit; (b) a declaration that the universe never takes the continuum limit (reality is truly discrete at the Planck scale), which avoids the mathematical issue but then must explain why continuum field theory works so well; or (c) a criterion for selecting the "correct" continuum limit from among the many possibilities, which no primitive provides. Option (b) is the most natural for the framework but then the abelianization theorem becomes a constraint on what can be *approximated* by the continuum theory, and the framework must explain why the observed continuum approximation preserves non-abelian structure — which requires a theory of the scaling relationships it currently lacks.
