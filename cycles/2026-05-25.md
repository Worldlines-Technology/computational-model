### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Not all black holes decohere quantum superpositions"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.23880v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework describes black holes as "attractor basins so deep that escape velocity exceeds the tick rate" and treats gravitational decoherence as a universal mechanism by which resolution propagates — gravity's universal coupling means resolution spreads through all degrees of freedom of a massive system (model update 2026-03-03). Paper 1 directly contradicts the universality of gravitational decoherence: near-extremal charged black holes, at sufficiently low temperatures, have a *vanishing* decoherence rate for quantum superpositions in their exterior. The mechanism is a spin-induced energy gap in the quantum black hole spectrum that suppresses the gravitational fluctuation modes responsible for decoherence. Above the gap, decoherence is nonzero but still suppressed relative to semiclassical expectations. The framework's Resolution primitive describes decoherence as "the process by which resolution propagates through entanglement networks" and states that "macroscopic objects appear classical because they are so deeply entangled with their environment that their states resolve almost instantaneously." A near-extremal black hole is the most massive, most gravitationally dominant object conceivable — the framework's paradigmatic "deep attractor" — yet it *fails to decohere* nearby quantum superpositions. The framework predicts maximum resolution propagation from the deepest attractor; the physics delivers minimum resolution propagation. The suppression mechanism — a spectral gap in the quantum black hole spectrum — is precisely the kind of structural/spectral property that T026 (structural content gap) has repeatedly identified as invisible to all five primitives. The connection to T115 (stability through infinite degeneracy) is direct: the near-extremal limit where decoherence vanishes is the same limit where Aretakis instability weakens. The framework's deepest attractor becomes simultaneously its most quantum-coherence-preserving and its most dynamically stable object — both properties that the gradient/attractor/resolution picture would predict to be *opposite* to their actual behavior.

**Paper 2: "Indefinite probabilities in quantum spacetime: A deepening of unpredictability"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.23862v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework treats quantum mechanics as computation with unresolved states (superpositions) that become definite through resolution (measurement), governed by the Born rule which assigns definite probabilities to outcomes. Paper 2 demonstrates that when rotational symmetry is described by the quantum group $SU_q(2)$ — a deformation motivated by noncommutative spacetime in the low-energy limit of quantum gravity — the probabilities themselves become non-commuting operators subject to an uncertainty principle. This is a categorical escalation beyond anything the framework has encountered: not merely indefinite states (superposition, which the framework handles via the Tick), not merely indefinite causal order (T094), not merely indefinite spacetime structure (T089/T091), but *indefinite probabilities*. The Born rule — one of the framework's four acknowledged open problems — assigns definite real numbers to measurement outcomes. If the probabilities are operators with noncommuting components, there is no definite probability to assign. The framework's Resolution primitive assumes that when a state resolves, it produces a definite outcome with a definite probability; Paper 2 shows that in quantum spacetime, the probability itself is indefinite. The non-commutativity of the rotation matrix entries means that two observers cannot sharply determine their relative orientation, which undermines the framework's implicit assumption that the computational substrate has a definite spatial reference structure. The framework's Tick primitive already struggles with quantum reference frames (T089/T091); Paper 2 extends this problem from the temporal reference frame to the *spatial* reference frame and from indefinite time to indefinite probability. The deformation parameter $q$ is connected to the Planck scale, meaning this is not an exotic limit but may be a universal feature of quantum gravity's low-energy regime.

**Paper 3: "Coherent dynamics in chaotic spin chains via interference-protected subspaces"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.23873v1 | Selection type: Validation

The Worldlines framework describes constrained attractors — states stabilized by structural constraints rather than energy minimization — and has accumulated multiple instances (fractional Fermi seas, spin ice, 3D Coulomb phase, symmetry-fragmented sectors, correlation-protected localization, interaction-stabilized ghosts). Paper 3 introduces "interference-protected subspaces" in chaotic spin-1/2 chains where destructive interference prevents leakage from a structured subspace into the thermal bulk, enabling long-lived coherent dynamics (scars, chirally propagating quasiparticles, approximate topological edge modes) at high energy densities in otherwise thermalizing systems. This is post-hoc consistent with the framework's constrained attractor category: the interference-protected subspace is a constrained attractor stabilized by quantum interference (destructive interference creating kinetic barriers in Hilbert space) rather than by energy, conservation laws, or topological protection. The quantitative leakage theory — predicting which states retain coherence based on the overlap structure between the subspace and the complement — is consistent with the framework's qualitative claim that constrained attractor lifetimes depend on barrier structure. However, the framework contributed *zero* predictive content: it did not predict interference-protected subspaces, cannot derive the leakage rate, cannot determine which Hamiltonians support such subspaces, and cannot predict the range of nonthermal phenomena (scars, chiral propagation, edge modes) they host. The specific mechanism (fast oscillations in the complement subspace improving stability) is a dynamical insight the framework cannot generate. The connection to T142 (dynamically emergent attractor landscapes) is notable: the interference-protected subspace is a dynamically constructed effective landscape within the full chaotic Hamiltonian, but the construction mechanism (destructive interference) is different from the spectral resonance mechanism of T142. This adds a new construction pathway for dynamically emergent landscapes. Record as post-hoc consistency with zero differential predictive content — the framework's vocabulary applies after the fact, but its primitives could not have anticipated, constrained, or distinguished this result from its non-occurrence.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain in this cycle comes from Paper 1. The framework's entire architecture predicts that the deepest attractor basins (black holes) should be the most powerful decoherers — the most aggressive propagators of resolution through their environment. The Resolution primitive states that gravity's universal coupling drives decoherence of all degrees of freedom; the Attractor primitive describes black holes as maximal attractors. The logical prediction: a massive black hole should maximally decohere nearby quantum superpositions. Paper 1 demonstrates the exact opposite for near-extremal black holes: decoherence *vanishes* as the black hole approaches extremality. The framework has no mechanism for this. The suppression is caused by a spectral gap in the quantum black hole spectrum — a structural property of the quantum gravitational Hilbert space that has no expression in any primitive. The framework cannot distinguish a Schwarzschild black hole (which decoheres as expected) from a near-extremal Reissner-Nordström black hole (which does not), because both are "deep attractor basins" with identical qualitative descriptions. The quantitative parameter controlling the decoherence suppression — the proximity to extremality, parameterized by the dimensionless ratio of temperature to gap energy — is invisible to the Attractor primitive's depth/width characterization. This is not merely an insufficiency (the framework says too little); it is an active misprediction (the framework's qualitative logic points in the wrong direction). The framework's paradigmatic example of resolution propagation — gravitational decoherence from its deepest attractor — fails precisely in the regime (near-extremal) where the attractor is deepest and the quantum gravitational structure is richest.

Paper 2 creates a different but equally fundamental strain: the framework's Resolution primitive and its acknowledged open problem (the Born rule) both presuppose that probabilities are definite real numbers. If quantum group deformation of spacetime symmetries makes probabilities into non-commuting operators with their own uncertainty relations, then the framework's foundational structure — superposition → resolution → definite outcome with definite probability — has an additional layer of indefiniteness the framework cannot represent. The framework would need to describe "resolution of probability" as a process prior to or simultaneous with "resolution of state," and it has no mechanism for this.

### STATE UPDATES

1. **Resolution primitive — add spectral-gap-controlled decoherence suppression.** The Resolution description should be updated to note: gravitational decoherence — previously described as universal and driven by gravity's universal coupling — is *not* universal. Near-extremal black holes suppress decoherence via a spin-induced spectral gap in the quantum black hole spectrum, and the decoherence rate vanishes as the gap energy exceeds the thermal energy. The Resolution primitive's statement that "gravity's universal coupling means resolution propagates through all degrees of freedom of a massive system" should be qualified: the *rate* of gravitational resolution propagation is controlled by the quantum spectral structure of the gravitating source, and this rate can be zero when a spectral gap suppresses the relevant fluctuation modes. The framework's claim that black holes are maximal decoherers is falsified for near-extremal black holes. The model should note that this is the first demonstrated case where the framework's deepest attractor (black hole) *actively preserves* quantum coherence rather than destroying it.

2. **Attractor primitive — add decoherence-coherence inversion near extremality.** The model's description of extremal black holes should be updated to note: the relationship between attractor depth and decoherence power is *non-monotonic*. The deepest attractors (near-extremal black holes) are the weakest decoherers due to quantum spectral gaps, while intermediate-depth attractors (Schwarzschild-like black holes) are strong decoherers. This inverts the framework's intuition that deeper attractors produce more resolution propagation. Combined with T115 (stability through infinite degeneracy, where deeper extremality produces more stability), this establishes a pattern: the near-extremal limit is a regime where multiple framework predictions are qualitatively inverted. The model should explicitly flag this as a regime where the framework's qualitative intuitions systematically fail.

3. **Resolution primitive / Born rule — flag indefinite probabilities as a new challenge.** The Resolution description should note that Paper 2 demonstrates a physically motivated scenario (quantum group deformation of spacetime symmetries at the Planck scale) where the probabilities assigned by the Born rule are non-commuting operators, not definite real numbers. The framework's fourth acknowledged open problem (the Born rule) should be updated to note: the problem is not merely deriving the Born rule but determining whether the Born rule's *output* (definite probabilities) is a universal feature of physics or an approximation that breaks down when spacetime symmetries are quantum-deformed. If probabilities are indefinite, the Resolution primitive requires a pre-resolution step: "resolution of probability" before "resolution of state."

4. **Constrained attractor category — add interference-protected subspaces as new instance.** The model should record that interference-protected subspaces in chaotic spin chains (Kerschbaumer, Desaules & Serbyn 2025) provide a new instance of constrained attractor physics. Distinctive features: (a) the protection mechanism is destructive quantum interference, not conservation laws, kinetic barriers, topological protection, or algebraic integrability; (b) the subspace hosts a broad range of nonthermal phenomena (scars, chiral propagation, edge modes) within a single construction; (c) the stability can be enhanced by engineering fast oscillations in the complement subspace, providing a tunable control parameter for constrained attractor lifetime. Record as post-hoc consistency with zero differential predictive content.
