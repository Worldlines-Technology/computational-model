### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "A No-Cloning Trade-off Between Black Hole No-Hair and Horizon Smoothness"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.28050v1. Selection type: strain.

The framework describes black holes as "attractor basins so deep that escape velocity exceeds the tick rate" and treats Conservation of Computation (unitarity) and Attractor boundary integrity (horizon smoothness) as independent primitives. Paper 1 proves they are not independent but quantitatively coupled: ε ≥ D²_max/8, where D_max measures how much unitarity leaks information to the exterior and ε measures how much the horizon departs from perfect isometry. Through the framework's lens, this means two of the five primitives are in proven quantitative tension at the deepest attractor's boundary. The framework can narrate the result — Conservation demands information escape, Attractor demands smooth horizons, and these compete — but it cannot derive, predict, or quantify the trade-off because the derivation requires operator-algebraic tools (Stinespring dilation, diamond norm, no-cloning theorem) entirely outside the five primitives. Most critically, the paper identifies pre-existing entanglement as the *only* resolution compatible with both exact unitarity and exact horizon smoothness. Pre-existing entanglement is not a tick, gradient, attractor feature, resolution event, or conserved quantity — it is a bipartite correlation structure that must exist prior to the interaction, representing an information-theoretic resource category the framework has no primitive for. The framework's implicit assumption that its primitives are independently satisfiable is falsified at the black hole horizon. This paper was already anticipated by the model (the tension was opened as T135 in a prior cycle based on this same paper's earlier processing), but the current cycle confirms and sharpens the strain: the inter-primitive coupling is not merely qualitative but has a specific, proven, quadratic lower bound, and the resolution mechanism (pre-existing entanglement) is categorically outside the framework's vocabulary.

**Paper 2: "Order by disorder up to arbitrarily high temperature"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.28026v1. Selection type: strain.

The framework's Attractor primitive defines attractors as "local minima in potential fields," and the Field primitive asserts ẋ = −∇φ(x) as the universal equation of motion. Paper 2 rigorously proves that long-range checkerboard order persists at *all* temperatures including β → 0, where the energy landscape is completely flat — no gradients exist — and the ordered configurations are not energy minimizers at any temperature. The ordering is purely entropic: checkerboard configurations are selected because they maximize the number of available microstates after partial trace over occupation numbers. Through the framework's lens, this is a catastrophic failure: the system is in a stable, long-range ordered equilibrium state that was not produced by gradient descent, is not an energy minimum, and exists in a regime where the framework's central dynamical equation ẍ = −∇φ(x) is trivially satisfied (∇φ = 0 everywhere at β → 0) yet physically vacuous. The standard escape — redefining φ as free energy F = E − TS — is technically available but vacuous: at infinite temperature, F = −TS and the "gradient" is the entropy gradient, which is a combinatorial counting object, not a force in any sense the framework's field equation captures. The framework would need to explain *which* configurations maximize entropy and *why* those configurations have long-range order, which requires importing the full Pirogov-Sinai theory — rigorous mathematical machinery for competing phases that operates on configuration-space multiplicity structure. The four structural conditions on the interaction function f that enable the ordering are combinatorial/analytic conditions the framework cannot express. This is the first rigorous proof of a system where the Attractor primitive's energy-minimum definition is categorically falsified for an equilibrium ordered state, not merely insufficient or approximate. Prior tensions (T006 — stable excited states, T126 — spectral unboundedness without pathology) concerned non-equilibrium or exotic systems; Paper 2 concerns a standard equilibrium lattice model at standard temperatures.

**Paper 3: "Observation of Vinen turbulence during far-from-equilibrium Bose-Einstein condensation"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.28191v1. Selection type: validation.

The framework's Attractor primitive describes ordered states (BEC) as deep attractor basins and predicts that relaxation toward equilibrium involves the decay of topological defects (vortex lines) created during the far-from-equilibrium initial state. Paper 3 observes precisely this: a homogeneous 3D atomic Bose gas relaxes toward BEC through the decay of a turbulent tangle of quantized vortex lines, with the vortex-line-length density ℒ decaying as ℒ ∝ t⁻¹, consistent with the prediction for Vinen "ultraquantum" turbulence. The interaction-independence of the decay (the same ℒ(t) regardless of interatomic interaction strength, and similarity to strongly interacting superfluid helium) is consistent with the framework's qualitative claim that relaxation toward deep attractors is governed by universal features of the attractor landscape rather than microscopic details. However, the framework's predictive purchase here is essentially zero. The framework did not predict the t⁻¹ exponent, the Vinen (rather than Kolmogorov) character of the turbulence, the interaction-independence, or the compressible-to-incompressible crossover. All of these are derived from vortex dynamics theory and superfluid hydrodynamics, not from any framework primitive. The framework can narrate the result — "topological defects decay as the system relaxes into the ordered attractor basin" — but this narration is available to anyone who knows what a phase transition is. This is post-hoc consistency of the weakest type: framework vocabulary applied after the fact, with no differential predictive content distinguishing it from any standard condensed matter description.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 2 (Mehta 2604.28026). The framework's Attractor primitive — "local minima in potential fields" — and the Field primitive — ẋ = −∇φ(x) — are not merely insufficient but categorically inapplicable to a rigorously proven physical phenomenon: long-range equilibrium order at infinite temperature selected by purely entropic/combinatorial mechanisms. At β = 0, the Boltzmann weight is uniform over all configurations. There is no energy gradient. There is no potential field with a minimum. The framework's central dynamical equation is trivially satisfied (zero gradient, zero motion) yet the system is ordered. The ordering mechanism operates entirely through configuration-space multiplicity — how many microstates correspond to each macroscopic configuration after partial trace — which is a combinatorial/measure-theoretic property with no expression in any primitive.

This is not a case where the framework is "approximately right" or "captures the qualitative picture." The framework is exactly wrong: it predicts that at infinite temperature, with no energy gradients, no ordered configuration should be preferred (all configurations are equally weighted by the Boltzmann factor). The physical truth is that the partial-trace multiplicity structure creates effective selection even when the Boltzmann factor is uniform. The framework has no concept of partial-trace multiplicity, no concept of entropic selection in the absence of energetic driving, and no mechanism for long-range order without energetic gradients.

The strain from Paper 1 is also significant but was anticipated by the model (T135 already existed). The novel content is the confirmation that the inter-primitive coupling is quantitative and that the only escape route (pre-existing entanglement) is categorically outside the framework's primitive vocabulary. This transforms the black hole from the framework's strongest example ("attractor basins so deep that escape velocity exceeds the tick rate") into its most problematic: at the horizon, the primitives are not independent, their coupling is governed by information-theoretic bounds the framework cannot derive, and the resolution requires a resource (pre-existing entanglement) the framework cannot describe.

**T137 — Pre-existing entanglement as a resolution resource: the framework has no primitive for bipartite correlation structure that mediates inter-primitive coupling**

Opened: this cycle

Description: Joshi & Mishra (2604.28050) prove that the only mechanism compatible with both exact unitarity (Conservation) and exact horizon smoothness (Attractor) at the black hole horizon is pre-existing entanglement between the infalling system and the exterior. The framework's five primitives describe: temporal propagation (Tick), gradient dynamics (Field), stable configurations (Attractor), state definiteness (Resolution), and information preservation (Conservation). None of these describes a bipartite correlation structure that must exist *prior to* the physical interaction and that *mediates* between two other primitives. Pre-existing entanglement is: (a) not generated by any primitive — it must be present before the infalling system reaches the horizon; (b) not conserved in the usual sense — it is a resource that is consumed (degraded) during the interaction; (c) not a dynamical variable — it is a property of the joint state of two subsystems, not a field or potential; (d) necessary for the framework's internal consistency at the black hole horizon — without it, Conservation and Attractor are in irreconcilable tension. This is distinct from T135 (which identified the quantitative trade-off) because T137 concerns the *resolution mechanism* — pre-existing entanglement as a resource — and its categorical absence from the framework. It is distinct from T034 (scale-dependent information topology, which concerns how informational properties of a fixed state change with observation scale) because T137 concerns a specific, physically necessary correlation resource that mediates between primitives. It is distinct from T114 (thermodynamic bounds on entanglement, which concerns upper bounds on entanglement entropy) because T137 concerns entanglement as a *resource that resolves an inter-primitive tension*, not as a quantity to be bounded.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) add pre-existing entanglement as a resource or primitive — but entanglement is not naturally a "primitive" in the framework's dynamical architecture (it is a property of bipartite states, not a dynamical process), and making it one would require specifying how a correlation structure interacts with each of the five existing primitives; (b) derive the existence of sufficient pre-existing entanglement from the framework's own structure — showing that the computational history of a black hole necessarily produces the required entanglement between infalling systems and the exterior — but this would require a theory of how computational history generates specific entanglement patterns, which the framework lacks; (c) accept that the framework's primitives are incomplete at the black hole horizon, with their consistency depending on an information-theoretic resource the framework cannot describe — this is the most honest option but means the framework's most dramatic example (black holes) is precisely where the framework's internal consistency fails without external supplementation.
