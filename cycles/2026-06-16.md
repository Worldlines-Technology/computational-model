**Paper 1: "The Optimal Rate Function in Covariant Quantum State Tomography"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.16948v1. Selection type: strain.

Through the Worldlines lens, quantum state tomography is the problem of reconstructing the pre-resolution computational state from the outcomes of resolution events applied to multiple copies. The framework's Resolution primitive says states become definite, and Conservation of Computation says the computational content is preserved. This paper reveals that the *rate* at which resolution outcomes concentrate around the true state — the exponential distinguishability between the true state ρ and an incorrect estimate σ — depends critically on the *protocol choice* (which multi-copy measurement is performed) and is bounded above by the quantum relative entropy D(σ‖ρ). The proof of Keyl's conjecture establishes that among basis-independent (covariant) protocols, Schur sampling is uniquely optimal, with a rate function that is an *annealed* version of the quantum relative entropy — reduced from the ideal D(σ‖ρ) by the cost of learning the eigenbasis. The framework strains in several specific ways: (1) The Resolution primitive treats resolution as producing definite outcomes but has no concept of *protocol optimality* — the fact that different resolution strategies extract information at fundamentally different rates, with the optimal rate governed by representation-theoretic structure (Schur-Weyl duality, Young diagram combinatorics) invisible to all primitives. (2) The "annealing" cost — the gap between the optimal covariant rate function and the quantum relative entropy — is the information-theoretic price of ignorance about the eigenbasis, a structural cost determined by the geometry of the state space (specifically, the Haar measure on the unitary group) that no primitive can express. (3) The rate function itself is a large-deviation quantity — it governs the probability of rare events in the N → ∞ limit — and the framework has no mechanism for large-deviation structure of resolution outcomes. (4) The Schur sampling protocol's optimality is proven via representation-theoretic arguments (decomposition of the N-copy Hilbert space into irreducible representations of the symmetric and unitary groups), adding another instance to the growing catalogue of representation-theoretic content the framework cannot access (T127, T131, T146, T151, T172).

**Paper 2: "Fixed-quadrupole static tidal response of Schwarzschild black holes in a cubic Weyl effective field theory"**
