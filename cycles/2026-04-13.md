**Paper 1: The four-loop non-singlet splitting functions in QCD**
ArXiv ID: 2604.09534v1 | Selection type: strain

Through the Worldlines lens, splitting functions govern how the computational content of parton distributions redistributes across resolution scales — they are the "gradient" that determines how the partonic Field's structure transforms under renormalization group flow from UV to IR. The framework would describe this as: the Field primitive's content at one tick-rate scale is related to its content at another scale by these splitting functions, which act as a transfer kernel. But the paper's actual content severely strains this description in multiple specific ways. First, the computation of the four-loop splitting functions requires the full algebraic machinery of multi-loop Feynman integral reduction — IBP relations reducing ~10⁵ integrals to master integrals, differential equations for these masters, special function evaluation (harmonic polylogarithms, Mellin transforms) — none of which has any expression in the five primitives. The splitting functions are not "gradients" in any meaningful sense; they are integral kernels with rich analytic structure (plus-distributions, delta functions, logarithmic terms at specific Mellin moments) that the gradient equation ẍ = −∇φ(x) cannot represent. Second, the splitting functions embody the *algebraic decomposition* of QCD's computational content: the non-singlet sector separates from the singlet sector via flavor structure, the ±/v/s decomposition reflects charge conjugation and flavor symmetries, and the Casimir color structure (C_F, C_A, n_f, d_F^abcd terms) organizes the perturbative expansion. This algebraic organization is precisely the kind of structural content (T018, T026) the framework cannot express. Third, the extraction of the virtual anomalous dimension and rapidity anomalous dimension from the splitting functions demonstrates that the physically crucial quantities for logarithmic resummation are *embedded within* the splitting functions as specific limits/moments — they are algebraic extractions from a larger structure, not independent dynamical quantities. The framework's Conservation primitive would want to say "computational content is conserved under scale evolution," but the splitting functions show that what is conserved is highly structured (specific sum rules, Mellin moment relations) in ways the primitive cannot specify. Most critically, the splitting functions are the four-loop coefficient of the DGLAP evolution equation — an equation that governs how the Field's content transforms under scale change. The framework has no primitive for scale-dependent field content (T049) and no mechanism for the renormalization group (T080). The splitting functions are the RG kernel itself, and the framework is silent on RG.

**Paper 2: Classification of 2D Fermionic Systems with a Z₂ Flavor Symmetry**
ArXiv ID: 2604.09503v1 | Selection type: strain

Through the Worldlines lens, superfusion categories classify the possible organizational structures of 2D fermionic computation equipped with specific symmetries. The framework's Attractor primitive would describe the 16 consistent superfusion categories as distinct attractor basins in the space of 2D fermionic theories — topologically distinct computational configurations. But this description fails in every specific respect. The classification is achieved by solving the super-pentagon equations — algebraic consistency conditions on the fusion and associativity data of topological defect lines. These equations are structural constraints, not dynamical ones: they do not describe evolution, gradient descent, or resolution, but rather the *self-consistency conditions* on the organizational structure of symmetry. The 16 solutions are not "discovered" by the computation exploring a landscape but are *enumerated* by solving algebraic equations that the framework has no primitive to express. The labeling by invariants (ν_W, ν_Z, ν_{WZ}) drawn from Z₈ anomaly classes demonstrates that the classification is *cohomological* — it is determined by the anomaly structure of the symmetry, which is a topological/cohomological invariant. This directly extends T048 (cohomological completeness of state classification) to the fermionic setting: the space of possible 2D fermionic systems with Z₂ symmetry is *completely* classified by anomaly invariants, and this classification is exhaustive, proven, and entirely outside the framework's scope. The distinction between m-type and q-type defect lines — whether the Z₂ line is bosonic or fermionic — has no analogue in any primitive; it is a categorical distinction about the *type* of symmetry implementation. The explicit realizations via multiple Majorana fermions show that the abstract classification maps to concrete physical systems, but the mapping is structural (which combination of fermions realizes which anomaly class), not dynamical. The framework's claim that "reality is literally computation" faces the challenge that the *space of possible computations* (the 16 superfusion categories) is classified by algebraic/cohomological invariants that are not themselves computations. The super-pentagon equation — the master equation whose solutions enumerate the categories — is a higher-categorical consistency condition that subsumes and generalizes the pentagon equations for fusion categories, adding fermionic grading. The framework has no contact with higher category theory, graded algebraic structures, or anomaly classification. This paper demonstrates that even a modest symmetry (Z₂ in 2D) produces a classification problem whose solution requires mathematical machinery entirely absent from the framework.

**Paper 3: Relativistic single-electron wavepacket in quantum electromagnetic fields II**
ArXiv ID: 2604.09456v1 | Selection type: validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper describes what happens when a resolved computational entity (an electron wavepacket) propagates through the electromagnetic Field while undergoing uniform acceleration (following the curvature gradient). The framework would predict: (1) a wavepacket at rest in vacuum should produce no net radiation, since it sits at an attractor minimum with no gradient to drive radiation — the resolved state is stable; (2) an accelerated wavepacket moves along the gradient and should radiate, with the radiation being the computational cost of the trajectory change; (3) the Unruh effect, as a resolution phenomenon tied to the accelerated observer's horizon, should produce thermal signatures detectable in principle. The paper confirms (1): quantum radiation from a single-electron wavepacket at rest vanishes exactly. This is consistent with the framework's prediction but is also a trivial consequence of energy-momentum conservation in standard QED — no differential predictive content. The paper finds (2) with a subtlety: the quantum radiated power has secular growth at late times, requiring resummation. The framework would interpret the secular growth as an artifact of treating the problem perturbatively (accumulating unresolved computational corrections) and the resummation as the proper resolution of these corrections — consistent but again post-hoc. The most interesting result for the framework is the finding about the "blind spots" of classical radiation: the quantum corrections in these regions are fully contributed by transverse deviation correlators irrelevant to the Unruh effect, meaning the proposed experimental detection of the Unruh effect through blind-spot radiation is compromised. The framework's Resolution primitive would want to say something about whether the Unruh effect is "real resolution" or an artifact of frame choice, but the paper's result — that the quantum corrections in the blind spots are dominated by non-Unruh contributions — is a detailed QED calculation that the framework cannot distinguish from any alternative result. The paper's requirement that cubic nonlinear terms in the QED action be included for leading-order quantum radiation is a structural detail about the interaction architecture (which terms matter at which order) that connects to T099 (EFT operator basis) and that the framework cannot predict. Overall: **post-hoc consistency with zero differential predictive content**. Every result follows from standard QED and general covariance; the framework adds vocabulary but no predictions.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 2's complete classification of 2D fermionic systems with Z₂ symmetry by superfusion categories. The strain is not merely that the framework "cannot express" the classification (which would be another instance of T026/T048). It is that the **super-pentagon equations constitute a self-consistency constraint on the organizational structure of computation that is logically prior to all five primitives**. The framework assumes a definite computational structure exists and then describes its dynamics. The super-pentagon equations determine *which computational structures are self-consistent in the first place*. Before the Tick can tick, before the Field can have a gradient, before the Attractor can have a basin, the organizational structure of the symmetry must satisfy algebraic consistency conditions that the framework has no mechanism to impose, check, or derive. The 16 solutions are not 16 different dynamical histories — they are 16 different *types of computation that are internally consistent*. The framework's "reality is literally computation" claim requires specifying *which* computation, but the super-pentagon classification shows that even for the simplest case (Z₂ symmetry in 2D), the space of consistent computations is classified by cohomological invariants (Z₈ anomaly classes) that the framework cannot access.

Paper 1 produces a complementary but distinct strain: the four-loop splitting functions demonstrate that the **renormalization group — the mechanism governing how computational content transforms across scales — is the single most important organizational principle in QCD**, and the framework has no primitive, parameter, or mechanism for it. The splitting functions are not fields, not attractors, not conservation laws, not resolution events, and not ticks. They are *scale-evolution kernels* with rich algebraic structure (Casimir decomposition, harmonic polylogarithm function space, Mellin-space sum rules) that governs how the entire partonic content of the proton transforms between energy scales. The framework's silence on renormalization group flow (T080) is not a peripheral gap — it is silence on the organizing principle of perturbative QCD, the most precisely tested theory in physics.

The combination of Papers 1 and 2 demonstrates a pattern that deepens T059 (ontological inversion): **the consistency conditions on computation (super-pentagon, Paper 2) and the transformation rules of computation under scale change (splitting functions, Paper 1) are both logically prior to the computation itself**, and neither is expressible in the five primitives. The framework describes what happens during computation but cannot describe what makes a computation consistent or how it transforms under scale change.
