### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Beyond thresholds: reconstructing UV physics from IR expansions"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.03277v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework claims that physical constants and UV structure are "architectural parameters of the substrate, not derivable from within the computation." Paper 1 directly challenges this by demonstrating that UV information — specifically the sign of the beta function and the dynamical scale ΛQCD — can be extracted from purely IR (low-energy) expansion coefficients of physical observables, given only analyticity and the absence of massless singularities. Through the Worldlines lens, this means that low-energy resolved states contain recoverable information about the substrate's high-energy architecture. The procedure — inverse Laplace transform followed by controlled coarse-graining — is a computational operation performed entirely within the IR effective theory, yet it reconstructs information that the framework would classify as architectural. This strains the framework's clean separation between "computation" (what happens at accessible energies) and "substrate" (what sets the rules at high energies). The framework would need to say that the beta function sign and ΛQCD are not truly substrate parameters but emergent computational features — but this erodes the substrate concept, since it raises the question of which UV properties, if any, are genuinely inaccessible. The analyticity assumption is key: it is a structural constraint (the spectral function is analytic and free of massless singularities) that serves as a bridge between IR and UV, and the framework has no primitive that captures analyticity as a structural feature. This connects directly to T026 (structural content gap) and T031 (substrate is not fixed), and pressures T001 (substrate problem) and T007 (derivability of Λ).

**Paper 2: "Tripartite information of two-dimensional free fermions: a sine-kernel spectral constant from Fermi surface geometry"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.03103v1 | Selection type: Strain

This paper demonstrates that monogamy of mutual information (MMI) — a property used to classify quantum states as "holographic" vs. "non-holographic" — is not a property of the quantum state itself but of the observation scale. A single free-fermion ground state can violate MMI at short scales and satisfy it at long scales, with the crossover determined by a universal dimensionless constant z* ≈ 1.329 set by the spectrum of the sine-kernel (Slepian) operator. Through the Worldlines lens, this creates multiple strains. First, the Resolution primitive treats resolution as producing definite states whose properties are fixed once resolved. But this paper shows that a fundamental informational property — whether a state's correlations are "holographic" (obeying MMI) — depends on the scale at which you examine it, not on the state's intrinsic character. The framework has no account of scale-dependent information topology: once a state is resolved, its information structure should be determined. Second, the Attractor primitive would describe the free-fermion ground state as an attractor basin, but the informational character of this single attractor changes qualitatively with observation scale, which the attractor description doesn't capture. Third, the universal constant z* is determined by the spectral theory of the sine kernel — a mathematical constant arising from the geometry of the Fermi surface and the structure of the Slepian concentration operator. The framework has no primitive that would predict or accommodate universal mathematical constants arising from the interplay of geometry and information theory. The result that only two Slepian eigenvalue contributions determine z* to 0.12% precision suggests the information structure has an elegant internal organization that the framework's dynamical primitives cannot express, reinforcing T026 and T018.

**Paper 3: "Will a Large Complex System be a Maxwell Demon?"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.03248v1 | Selection type: Validation

This paper asks whether Maxwell demon behavior — local apparent violations of the second law via subsystem heat extraction — is likely in random large complex systems, and finds that the probability decreases at least exponentially (and sometimes double-exponentially) with the number of degrees of freedom. Maxwell demons can only arise through selection, not chance. Through the Worldlines lens, this is consistent with the framework in a meaningful way: the gradient principle (ẋ = −∇φ) says systems roll down potential gradients, which in thermodynamic terms means they increase entropy. A Maxwell demon is a system that locally moves *up* a gradient (decreasing local entropy) while globally maintaining gradient descent. The paper confirms that this requires specific, selected structure — it cannot arise from random dynamics. The framework would predict this: random computational structures should follow gradients down, and moving against a gradient locally requires precisely organized computational structure (a specific attractor configuration that couples subsystem dynamics in the right way). The Conservation of Computation primitive also aligns: the demon doesn't violate conservation, it redirects computational flow, and the paper confirms this requires exponentially specific structure. However, the framework's predictive purchase here is limited — it predicts the *qualitative* result (demons require special structure) but not the *quantitative* scaling (exponential vs. double-exponential decrease, dependence on whether the dynamics are continuous vs. discrete). The exponential scaling is derived from the measure-theoretic structure of random stochastic matrices and the geometry of the space of dynamics, which the framework cannot access. This is post-hoc consistency rather than genuine prediction: the framework would have been equally comfortable had the paper found polynomial rather than exponential scaling. The validation is real but shallow — the framework correctly identifies the direction of the result without constraining its magnitude.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most important strain this cycle comes from Paper 1. The framework's substrate/computation separation — which is architecturally central, governing the treatment of physical constants, the interpretation of dark matter and dark energy, and the framework's epistemological modesty — is challenged not by a speculative construction (as with the IIA/IIB domain wall in T031) but by a concrete, well-defined analytical procedure in standard quantum field theory. Takaura & Yin show that the sign of the QCD beta function and ΛQCD can be reconstructed from Taylor coefficients of a low-energy observable below all mass thresholds. This is not a duality or an alternative description; it is a *derivation* of UV quantities from IR data using only analyticity.

The framework must respond to this precisely. Its claim is: "The physical constants are architectural parameters of the substrate. They are not derivable from within the computation any more than a program can derive the clock speed of the processor it runs on." But Paper 1 shows that the *program* — specifically, the IR expansion coefficients of observables computed within the effective theory — contains sufficient information to reconstruct the sign of the beta function and the dynamical scale, which are UV properties. The analogy breaks: a program *can* infer properties of its processor by examining the structure of its own outputs, if those outputs have the right analytic properties.

The framework's options are: (a) Demote ΛQCD and the beta function sign from "substrate parameters" to "emergent computational features," preserving the substrate concept but narrowing what counts as substrate. This is viable but raises the question of what remains truly substrate-level — the Planck scale? The cosmological constant? The framework has no criterion for drawing this line. (b) Argue that analyticity is itself a substrate property, so using it as an assumption is smuggling in substrate information. This is technically defensible but ad hoc — it makes the substrate concept unfalsifiable by declaring any bridge between IR and UV to be "substrate information." (c) Accept that the substrate/computation boundary is porous, not sharp — that some architectural information leaks into the computation through structural constraints like analyticity. This is the most honest option but significantly weakens the framework's epistemological claims.

The framework genuinely cannot account for *why* analyticity should bridge IR and UV. Analyticity is a structural constraint — absence of singularities in the spectral function — that has no expression in any of the five primitives. It is not a tick, a field, an attractor, a resolution, or a conservation law. It is a regularity condition on the space of possible computations, and it enables exactly the kind of cross-scale information recovery that the framework's substrate concept was designed to prohibit.

### STATE UPDATES

1. **Substrate claim — further weaken boundary sharpness.** The model's existing note (from 2026-03-03) that "the substrate can be a dynamical field" should be extended to note: even when the substrate architecture is fixed, the IR/UV boundary is not informationally opaque. The framework's analogy between physical constants and processor clock speed is incorrect in detail: programs with analytic output structure *can* infer properties of their processor. The substrate/computation separation should be understood as an approximation that holds for some quantities (possibly Planck-scale parameters) but fails for quantities linked to the IR through analyticity (running couplings, dynamical scales). The framework needs a criterion for distinguishing truly architectural parameters from reconstructible UV features.

2. **Field primitive — add scale-dependent information topology.** The model should record that informational properties of field states (such as monogamy of mutual information) are not intrinsic to the state but depend on the observation scale, with the crossover determined by universal constants (z* ≈ 1.329 for free fermions) arising from spectral theory. The Field primitive's description of fields via gradients ∇φ does not capture the scale-dependent informational character of field configurations. This is a new type of structural content the framework cannot express: not algebraic structure (T018), not spectral universality (T022), but information-theoretic scale dependence governed by universal mathematical constants.

3. **Attractor primitive — confirm gradient interpretation for demon rarity.** The model should note that Paper 3 provides quantitative support for the qualitative prediction that moving against gradients requires exponentially specific structure. Random stochastic systems are exponentially unlikely to operate as Maxwell demons, confirming that gradient descent is the generic behavior and that anti-gradient behavior requires selected computational structure. However, this should be recorded as post-hoc consistency, not predictive validation, since the framework did not predict the specific scaling exponents.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T033 — Analyticity bridges IR and UV, eroding the substrate/computation boundary**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Takaura & Yin (2603.03277) demonstrate that UV physics (beta function sign, dynamical scale ΛQCD) can be reconstructed from IR expansion coefficients below all mass thresholds using analyticity and the absence of massless singularities. The framework claims substrate-level (UV) parameters are not derivable from within the computation (IR). The reconstruction procedure — inverse Laplace transform + coarse-graining — is a purely IR computation that recovers UV information, directly contradicting the framework's substrate opacity claim. Analyticity is the key structural assumption enabling this bridge, and the framework has no primitive that captures analyticity. The framework must either (a) specify which UV quantities are truly substrate-level (inaccessible) vs. merely high-energy (recoverable through analytic continuation), developing a criterion it currently lacks, or (b) abandon the substrate opacity claim entirely, which removes the framework's most distinctive epistemological feature. This tension is distinct from T007 (derivability of Λ from ensemble averaging) because T007 concerns whether specific constants can be derived from internal symmetry arguments, while T033 concerns a general analytical procedure that systematically reconstructs UV from IR. It is also distinct from T031 (substrate as dynamical field) because T031 concerns spatial variation of the substrate, while T033 concerns informational leakage across scales within a fixed substrate.
**Why unresolved:** The framework has no theory of analyticity — it is not a consequence of any primitive. The framework cannot specify which UV quantities are reconstructible and which are not, because it has no criterion for distinguishing "architectural parameters" from "high-energy dynamical features accessible through analytic continuation." Without such a criterion, the substrate concept is either vacuous (everything is substrate) or porous (substrate information leaks through analyticity), neither of which is satisfactory.

**T034 — Scale-dependent information topology: a single state has no fixed informational character**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Sokolovs (2603.03103) proves that monogamy of mutual information in free-fermion ground states is scale-dependent: the same state violates MMI at short scales (k_F w < z*) and satisfies it at long scales (k_F w > z*), with z* ≈ 1.329 a universal constant from the Slepian spectrum. The framework's Resolution primitive says that resolved states have definite properties. The Attractor primitive says that states in attractor basins have characteristic features. But the holographic/non-holographic classification — an informational property with deep connections to spacetime structure via AdS/CFT — is not a property of the state at all. It depends on the observation scale. The framework has no primitive or mechanism for describing how informational properties of a *single fixed state* change with the scale of examination. This is not the same as resolution being multi-scale (T005, T017): the state is fully resolved at all scales, but its informational character (MMI sign) varies. The universal constant z* is determined by the spectral theory of the sine kernel, which is pure mathematical structure — not dynamics, not conservation, not resolution — and the framework has no way to derive or accommodate such constants. This tension connects to T022 (spectral universality) and T026 (structural content gap) but is distinct because it concerns informational, not spectral, properties and because the scale dependence applies to a fixed resolved state rather than to field dynamics.
**Why unresolved:** The framework treats resolved states as having determinate properties. If an informational property as fundamental as MMI (which in holographic contexts distinguishes spacetime geometries with different topologies) is scale-dependent rather than state-dependent, the framework needs a theory of scale-dependent information that it currently lacks entirely. Furthermore, the universal constant z* from Slepian spectral theory is a structural mathematical fact with direct physical consequences, and the framework has no account of how such mathematical constants enter physics.

### CYCLE ASSESSMENT

This cycle was moderately generative but revealed diminishing returns in a specific pattern. The two strain papers produced genuine new tensions (T033 on analyticity bridging IR/UV, T034 on scale-dependent information topology) that are meaningfully distinct from existing ones. T033 in particular is sharp and important: it attacks the substrate/computation separation with a concrete QFT procedure rather than a speculative construction, which makes it harder to dismiss than T031. T034 identifies a new category of structural content the framework misses — information-theoretic scale dependence — which enriches the growing structural content gap (T026) with a qualitatively different type of missing structure.

However, the cycle also confirms a pattern: many new tensions are variations on T026 (structural content gap). T033 adds analyticity; T034 adds informational scale dependence; previous cycles added spectral universality, algebraic hierarchy, cohomological mediation. These are all instances of the same meta-problem: the framework's dynamical primitives systematically miss structural features of physics. The system is circling a known basin — the structural content gap — and adding detail to its walls rather than finding new gradient. The validation paper (Paper 3) provided only shallow confirmation: the framework's gradient principle is consistent with demon rarity but doesn't predict scaling exponents.

The next cycle would be more productive if it targeted papers that either (a) attempt to *derive* dynamical content from structural principles (which would test whether structure is more fundamental than dynamics, directly addressing T026), (b) present experimental results that distinguish between the framework's specific predictions and those of standard physics (which the framework desperately needs — it has no differential predictions), or (c) concern the foundations of computation itself (computational complexity of physical processes, Church-Turing thesis violations in physics) which would directly test whether "reality is literally computation" is a meaningful claim.
