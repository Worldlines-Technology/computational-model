### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "The Exact Replica Threshold for Nonlinear Moments of Quantum States"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.22627v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the Resolution primitive's cost structure (T009, T019): how much computational resource is required to extract specific information from quantum states. The framework's Resolution primitive treats resolution as a binary event — unresolved states become definite — and the model's updates have added cost structure (resolution is not free) and a precision hierarchy (Clifford hierarchy). Paper 1 reveals something sharper: the number of *coherent copies* of a quantum state constitutes a genuinely discrete, quantized informational resource with an exact threshold. Below ⌈t/2⌉ replicas, estimating tr(ρ^t) requires dimension-growing sample complexity; at or above ⌈t/2⌉, polynomial samples suffice. This is a *sharp step function* in information-extraction capacity as a function of replica number — not a continuous cost gradient, not an asymptotic scaling relation, but an exact integer threshold separating qualitatively different regimes. The framework's Field primitive would describe information extraction as rolling down a gradient, but there is no gradient here: one additional coherent replica flips the system between qualitative regimes. The framework's Conservation of Computation would suggest that computational content is fungible and transformable, but replicas are a *non-fungible* resource: you cannot substitute more samples for fewer replicas below the threshold. The result demonstrates that the information-theoretic structure of quantum states has discrete resource thresholds that are invisible to gradient dynamics, cannot be derived from conservation, and are not described by attractor topology. The threshold ⌈t/2⌉ is determined by the algebraic structure of symmetric subspaces and Schur-Weyl duality — mathematical machinery entirely outside the framework's primitives. The framework can narrate the result (resolution has cost, more copies enable more resolution) but cannot predict, derive, or explain the specific threshold or its universality.

**Paper 2: "No-Go Theorem for Quantum Heat Engines Powered Purely by Quantum Measurements in the Steady Regime"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.22376v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework would interpret this through the Resolution and Conservation primitives: measurements (resolution events) inject energy into a quantum system via backaction, and one might ask whether this energy can be systematically extracted as work. The paper proves that in the steady regime, measurement-powered engines cannot extract work without an entropy-decreasing process (feedback control or thermal contact). The mechanism is a Poincaré-like recurrence theorem for quantum channels: repeated application of the measurement channel drives the working substance to a fixed point where the measurement becomes non-disturbing (no further energy injection). This result strains the framework in a specific way: the Resolution primitive describes resolution as irreversible state-fixing that generates the arrow of time through accumulation. Paper 2 shows that *repeated* resolution applied cyclically to a finite-dimensional system is *not* cumulative — it converges to a fixed point where resolution ceases to change anything. The "accumulation of resolved states" picture fails for cyclic measurement processes: the system reaches a state where measurement is perfectly non-disturbing, which means resolution events occur (the measurement is performed) but produce no physical change — resolution without resolution's consequences. The Poincaré recurrence for channels means the Resolution primitive's irreversibility is a transient phenomenon for finite-dimensional cyclic processes, not an eternal accumulation. Furthermore, the result sharply delineates the boundary between what resolution alone can achieve (energy injection during the transient, but not sustained work extraction) and what requires additional structure (entropy decrease via feedback or thermal contact). The framework's implicit picture — resolution as a one-way ratchet generating the arrow of time — is revealed as applicable only to the transient regime; in steady state, resolution becomes dynamically inert. The necessity of an entropy-decreasing process for sustained work extraction connects to T120 (the reversible/irreversible boundary is gauge-dependent) by showing that measurement alone cannot sustain thermodynamic irreversibility without structural supplementation.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The deepest strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's demonstration that **coherent replica number is a genuinely discrete, non-fungible informational resource with an exact threshold**. The framework's architecture is built on continuous primitives: continuous gradient descent, continuous resolution of probability distributions, continuous conservation of computational content. The replica threshold ⌈t/2⌉ is none of these — it is a discrete, integer-valued, algebraically determined boundary that separates qualitatively different information-extraction regimes, and it applies universally to all quantum states regardless of their dynamical origin, attractor structure, or conservation properties.

The framework has no mechanism for discrete resource thresholds in information extraction. The Conservation of Computation says computational content is conserved and transformable, implying a kind of fungibility — if you have enough total computation, you can extract what you need. Paper 1 proves this is false for replica-limited quantum estimation: below the threshold, no amount of additional classical processing (more samples) compensates for the missing coherent replica. The discrete resource is not convertible, not fungible, and not expressible as a continuous quantity. It is determined by the representation theory of the symmetric group acting on tensor product Hilbert spaces (Schur-Weyl duality), which is algebraic structure of the type the framework has repeatedly been shown unable to express (T026).

The strain is compounded by Paper 2's demonstration that **resolution becomes dynamically inert in the steady regime of cyclic measurement**. The Poincaré recurrence for quantum channels means the Resolution primitive's claim — "the arrow of time is the accumulation of resolved states" — has a structural expiration date for finite-dimensional cyclic processes. The system converges to a measurement-invariant state where resolution events occur but produce no physical effect. The framework describes resolution as irreversible and cumulative; the paper shows it is transient and self-neutralizing in the cyclic steady state. This does not merely weaken the Resolution primitive — it identifies a regime where the primitive applies formally (measurements happen) but has no physical content (nothing changes), creating a category the framework cannot distinguish from genuine resolution.
