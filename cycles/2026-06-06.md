**Paper 1: "Breakeven demonstration of quantum low-density parity-check codes"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.06455v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper is a direct experimental engagement with the Conservation of Computation primitive and its enforcement infrastructure. The framework claims "computation cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed." This paper demonstrates the first breakeven performance of qLDPC codes on a trapped-ion platform — meaning the conservation-enforcement infrastructure (the error-correcting code) now preserves logical quantum information at least as long as the raw physical substrate preserves it. The framework can narrate this as "conservation enforcement achieving substrate-level fidelity." However, the strain is severe and multi-layered. First, nine categorically different code families (qLDPC, topological, concatenated) are demonstrated on a single physical device without hardware reconfiguration, confirming T081's observation that conservation enforcement is a *software/structural* choice, not a substrate-level architectural parameter — the same substrate supports radically different conservation architectures. Second, the specific performance variations across codes (the [[18,4,4]] qLDPC code achieves 9× improvement over superconducting implementations, while other codes on the same device perform differently) demonstrate that the *effectiveness* of conservation enforcement depends on the match between code structure and hardware noise structure — an interaction the Conservation primitive cannot express. Third, the OMG (optical-metastable-ground) architecture for mid-circuit measurement uses a physically distinct metastable state as an auxiliary computational register, demonstrating that conservation enforcement requires substrate features (multiple atomic energy levels with different decoherence properties) that are invisible to the Conservation primitive's universal formulation. The framework's claim that conservation is a *primitive* — simple, universal, structureless — is contradicted by the demonstration that its enforcement is complex, code-family-dependent, hardware-architecture-dependent, and requires multi-level atomic physics for its realization.

**Paper 2: "$E_\infty^{1,2}$-type Lieb-Schultz-Mattis anomalies, deconfined quantum critical points, and non-invertible symmetry breaking"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.06343v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the Attractor primitive's description of phase transitions as "attractor landscape restructuring" and the Conservation primitive's treatment of symmetry constraints. The paper demonstrates that a specific class of 1D quantum phase transitions — deconfined quantum critical points (DQCPs) of "type II" — are classified by a precise location within the Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence ($E_\infty^{1,2}$ page), and that gauging the internal symmetry at such anomalies *necessarily* produces a non-invertible dual symmetry ($\mathrm{Rep}(H_8)$ for the specific $D_8$ example). The framework strains at every level. First, the classification of the critical point's type by its position in a spectral sequence — a specific homological-algebraic construction involving iterated filtrations of group cohomology — is structural content categorically beyond all five primitives. The Attractor primitive's description of the transition as a "landscape restructuring at a saddle point" is not merely qualitatively weaker than the truth (as T129 noted for conformal enrichment) but is in a different mathematical universe from the spectral sequence classification. Second, the *necessity* of non-invertible symmetry emergence upon gauging — the main physics result — is a structural entailment (gauging an $E_\infty^{1,2}$ anomaly → non-invertible dual symmetry) that involves no dynamics. The framework's dynamical primitives are entirely idle. Third, the type-I/type-II distinction for DQCPs ($E_\infty^{2,1}$ vs. $E_\infty^{1,2}$) classifies the *kind of phase transition* by the algebraic structure of its anomaly, not by any property of the attractor landscape, the gradient dynamics, or the resolution process. Fourth, the non-invertible $\mathrm{Rep}(H_8)$ symmetry that emerges is precisely the category of constraint that T111 identified as invisible to the Conservation primitive — it is not a conservation law, cannot be expressed as preservation of any quantity, and produces Hilbert space fragmentation and constraints that the Conservation primitive's architecture cannot represent. The numerical evidence for a $c \approx 1$ CFT at the DQCP connects to T129 (conformal enrichment) by demonstrating that the critical point has specific CFT data (central charge, scaling dimensions) determined by the algebraic structure of the anomaly, not by any attractor landscape property.

The paper classifies the *kind* of phase transition — deconfined vs. conventional, type-I vs. type-II — by algebraic invariants in a spectral sequence that the framework cannot even formulate, let alone derive.

3. 4. The spectral sequence classification is a *refinement* of the cohomological classification already flagged by T048 and T131. It is not merely "cohomology classifies phases" but "the specific page of the spectral sequence where the anomaly lives determines the kind of phase transition and the type of emergent symmetry." The compound effect with Paper 1 is also significant: Paper 1 demonstrates that the Conservation primitive's enforcement is a software-level structural choice (nine codes on one device), while Paper 2 demonstrates that the Conservation primitive's *scope* is categorically inadequate (non-invertible symmetries are constraints that are not conservation laws).
