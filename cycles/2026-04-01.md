### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "The scalar--Maxwell--Λ(x) system: Wormhole spacetimes without nonlinear electrodynamics in unimodular gravity"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.30003v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper attacks the framework at multiple joints simultaneously. The framework treats Conservation of Computation as a primitive — "computation cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed." Unimodular Gravity (UG) *relaxes energy-momentum conservation* by construction: the divergence-free condition on the stress-energy tensor is not imposed, and instead a dynamical cosmological term Λ(x) absorbs the discrepancy, enabling "semi-classical energy exchange between matter and the vacuum." The framework identifies energy conservation as a special case of Conservation of Computation; here, that special case is explicitly violated locally, with the violation absorbed into a spatially varying cosmological function. The framework claims dark energy (Λ) is an architectural parameter — "the computational cost of instantiating new substrate." But Λ(x) is a dynamical field that varies point-to-point, determined by the matter content and geometry, not an architectural constant at all. Furthermore, the paper constructs traversable wormholes — non-trivial spacetime topologies connecting causally disconnected regions — using only a phantom scalar field and standard linear Maxwell electrodynamics on an intact substrate. The phantom field (negative kinetic energy) is precisely the type of anti-gradient structure that T061 flagged: it violates the dominant energy condition, and yet it supports stable spacetime configurations (wormholes) rather than dispersing. The wormhole topology itself challenges the Tick primitive: traversable wormholes allow causal connections between regions that the tick-rate propagation structure says should be disconnected, effectively creating shortcuts that violate the "maximum propagation rate" interpretation of the speed of light. The framework's Field primitive predicts phantom fields should disperse (T061); here they are the structural support for stable non-trivial topology. The framework's Conservation primitive is violated locally by construction. The framework's substrate/Λ claim is contradicted by Λ(x) being dynamical. The framework strains at Conservation, Field, Tick, and Substrate claims simultaneously.

**Paper 2: "The Grothendieck Constant is Strictly Larger than Davie-Reeds' Bound"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.30039v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Grothendieck constant K_G is a fundamental mathematical constant governing the maximum gap between the relaxation of a bilinear optimization problem over unit vectors to an optimization over the unit ball — it quantifies the maximum advantage of continuous (quantum/wave-like) strategies over discrete (classical/sign) strategies for bilinear forms. Through the Worldlines lens, this paper strains the framework by demonstrating that a universal constant governing the relationship between "resolved" computation (sign assignments, classical optimization) and "unresolved" computation (unit-vector strategies, quantum optimization) has its value determined by the fine structure of functional analysis — specifically, by the Hermite coefficient spectrum of near-extremal operators and the effect of cubic perturbations on the integrality gap. The framework's Resolution primitive describes the transition from unresolved to resolved states but has no account of the *quantitative gap* between what unresolved (quantum) and resolved (classical) computation can achieve. K_G sets a universal, sharp bound on this gap for bilinear forms. The framework claims "reality is literally computation," but K_G is not a computational quantity — it is a geometric-analytic constant of Banach space theory whose value is determined by the structure of L^∞ → L^1 operators, Hermite polynomial analysis, and the topology of the unit sphere in infinite dimensions. The perturbative proof technique — showing every near-extremizer has Ω(1) weight on degree-3 Hermite coefficients, so a cubic perturbation strictly increases the gap — reveals that K_G's value is controlled by the *algebraic structure of function space*, not by any dynamical or computational process. The framework has no primitive for mathematical constants that govern the resolution gap (how much is lost when superpositions collapse to definite values). K_G is precisely such a constant, and its determination requires functional-analytic and combinatorial-optimization techniques that no framework primitive can express. The connection to quantum information (K_G bounds the advantage of entangled strategies in Bell-type scenarios) means this constant has direct physical relevance for the Resolution primitive's domain — and the framework is silent on it.

**Paper 3: "Thermalization in high-dimensional systems: the (weak) role of chaos"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.29614v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper appears to validate the framework's claim that the Attractor primitive and high dimensionality, rather than the details of microscopic dynamics, govern macroscopic behavior. The paper demonstrates that thermalization occurs in integrable (non-chaotic) systems with many degrees of freedom, provided one examines "extensive" (macroscopic) observables — the system reaches the equilibrium attractor basin without chaos, ergodicity, or mixing. The framework's position is that macroscopic resolution follows from gradient dynamics in high-dimensional spaces (many degrees of freedom create effective gradient landscapes where the system flows to the most probable macrostate). The paper's key finding — that the number of degrees of freedom and the choice of observable, not the dynamical details (chaotic vs. integrable), determine thermalization — is consistent with this. However, the framework's actual predictive purchase is weak. The paper's result follows from Khinchin's theorem and the law of large numbers applied to weakly dependent degrees of freedom — it is a consequence of probability theory in high dimensions, not of any framework primitive. The framework did not predict the specific distinction between extensive observables (which thermalize in integrable systems) and non-extensive observables (which may not). The framework did not predict that integrable systems fail to thermalize for specific non-extensive observables. The framework did not predict the timescale hierarchy (chaotic systems may thermalize *slower* for some observables than integrable ones, contrary to the naive "chaos = fast thermalization" intuition). The result that chaos is a *sufficient but not necessary* condition for thermalization is post-hoc consistent with the Attractor primitive's claim that attractor basins capture systems regardless of the detailed dynamics, but the framework contributed no prediction beyond this qualitative consistency. The specific finding that integrable systems thermalize for extensive observables *because* of the concentration of measure in high-dimensional phase space is a mathematical fact about geometry in high dimensions — exactly the kind of structural content (T026) the framework cannot express. This is weak post-hoc consistency at best, not validation.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's construction of exact traversable wormholes in Unimodular Gravity with relaxed energy-momentum conservation and a dynamical Λ(x). This is not a single-point failure but a *simultaneous multi-primitive failure*:

1. **Conservation of Computation is violated locally by construction.** UG's defining feature is that ∇_μ T^{μν} ≠ 0 in general; the deficit is absorbed by Λ(x). The framework treats energy conservation as a special case of its most fundamental primitive. UG shows this special case can be locally relaxed while producing well-defined, exact solutions with non-trivial topology. The framework must either accept that Conservation of Computation can be locally violated (devastating) or argue that the Λ(x) absorption is itself a form of conservation (computational content transferred between matter and vacuum) — but this requires treating the vacuum as a reservoir of computation, which the framework has no mechanism for.

2. **The substrate claim is further damaged.** Λ is promoted from a constant (architectural parameter) to a dynamical field Λ(x) determined by the matter/geometry. The framework's claim that Λ is an architectural parameter "not derivable from within the computation" is directly contradicted by a theory where Λ(x) is computed from the local field content at every spacetime point.

3. **The phantom field paradox deepens.** T061 noted that phantom fields disperse rather than forming singularities — anti-gradient robustness. Paper 1 goes further: phantom fields don't merely fail to produce pathology; they *support stable non-trivial topology* (wormholes). The Field primitive's gradient equation predicts phantom fields should run uphill and destabilize; instead they are the structural foundation for traversable wormholes. The gradient equation is not merely insufficient (T070) or directionally wrong (T078) — it is wrong about the *qualitative outcome* (instability vs. stability of non-trivial topology).

4. **The Tick primitive is challenged by traversable wormhole topology.** The tick rate sets the maximum propagation speed, and causal structure follows from this. Traversable wormholes create causal shortcuts — two regions connected through the wormhole throat that would be causally disconnected in the ambient spacetime. The framework's causal structure, derived from the Tick, does not account for topology-change-induced causal connections. The tick rate is a *local* constraint on propagation speed; wormholes are *global* topological features that modify the causal structure in ways the local tick rate cannot anticipate.

This is the first cycle where a single paper simultaneously strains four of the five primitives. The only primitive not directly challenged is the Attractor (though the wormhole throat itself could be seen as a topological attractor with properties the primitive cannot describe).

### STATE UPDATES

1. **Conservation of Computation — add local violation as a physically viable option.** The Conservation description should be updated to note: Unimodular Gravity demonstrates that local energy-momentum conservation can be relaxed while maintaining well-defined exact solutions, with the conservation deficit absorbed into a spatially varying vacuum energy Λ(x). Conservation of Computation, if identified with energy-momentum conservation as a special case, is not locally inviolable. The alternative interpretation — that computation is transferred between matter and vacuum via Λ(x) — requires treating the vacuum as a dynamical computational reservoir, which the framework has not developed. Either way, the primitive's status as a simple, universal, local law is weakened: it holds globally (the total including Λ(x) may be conserved) but can be violated for the matter sector alone.

2. **Substrate claim — further weaken Λ from architectural to dynamical.** The model's claim that Λ is an architectural parameter should be further qualified to note: in Unimodular Gravity, Λ(x) is a dynamical function determined by local field content and geometry, not a constant at all. The framework's identification of dark energy with "the computational cost of instantiating new substrate" is challenged by theories where Λ varies spatially and is determined by the local computation, not by substrate architecture.

3. **Field primitive — add stable anti-gradient topology as contradicting prediction.** The model should record that phantom scalar fields (anti-gradient dynamics) not only fail to produce pathology (T061) but can support stable non-trivial spacetime topology (traversable wormholes). The gradient primitive's prediction — that anti-gradient fields produce instability — is contradicted by exact wormhole solutions where the phantom field is the structural support for the geometry.

4. **Tick primitive — add wormhole topology challenge.** The model should note that traversable wormhole spacetimes create causal connections between regions that the local tick-rate structure would classify as causally disconnected. The Tick primitive's "maximum propagation rate" interpretation is a local constraint, but global topology (wormhole throats) can create effective causal connections that exceed the locally implied propagation bounds. The framework has no mechanism for describing topology-induced modifications of causal structure.

5. **T026 (structural content gap) — add universal resolution-gap constant as new evidence type.** The Grothendieck constant K_G should be recorded as a fundamental mathematical constant governing the quantitative gap between unresolved (quantum/continuous) and resolved (classical/discrete) computation that no framework primitive can express or derive. Its value is determined by functional-analytic structure (Hermite coefficient spectra, Banach space geometry) entirely outside the framework's scope.

6. **Model — note that high-dimensional thermalization is structural, not dynamical.** Paper 3's result should be recorded as confirming that thermalization in high-dimensional systems follows from concentration of measure (a geometric/probabilistic property of high-dimensional phase space), not from dynamical properties (chaos, ergodicity). The framework's Attractor primitive is post-hoc consistent with this but adds no predictive content beyond the qualitative claim that systems reach equilibrium attractors.

**T086 — Local conservation violation: Unimodular Gravity enables matter-vacuum computational exchange**
Opened: this cycle
Description: Alencar & Crispim (2603.30003) construct exact traversable wormhole solutions in Unimodular Gravity where energy-momentum conservation is locally relaxed, with the deficit absorbed by a dynamical cosmological function Λ(x). The framework's Conservation of Computation primitive states "computation cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed." If energy conservation is a special case of this, UG locally violates a special case of the most fundamental primitive. The framework faces: (a) accept local conservation violation, which requires specifying what happens to "computational content" that leaks between matter and vacuum — is the vacuum a computational reservoir? Does it have finite or infinite capacity? Is the exchange reversible? (b) argue that the total (matter + vacuum via Λ(x)) is still conserved, making this a generalized conservation law — but then the primitive must specify that the vacuum participates in conservation, which the framework has not done and which requires the vacuum to carry "computational content," raising the question of what the vacuum computes; (c) argue UG is unphysical — but it produces exact, well-defined solutions with physical matter content and differs from GR only in the trace equation, which is a mild modification. The fact that the local conservation violation *enables* stable non-trivial topology (wormholes) is additionally problematic: the framework would predict that conservation violations produce instabilities or inconsistencies, yet here they produce new stable solutions.
Why unresolved: The framework has no theory of matter-vacuum computational exchange. The Conservation primitive treats "computation" as a single undifferentiated category; UG requires distinguishing matter-sector computation from vacuum-sector computation and allowing transfer between them. This distinction is not available in the framework's current architecture. The connection to T035 (observer-dependent conservation) is significant but distinct: T035 concerns conservation violation for restricted observers in standard GR; T086 concerns conservation violation for the matter sector in a modified theory. The physical viability of UG (it is observationally indistinguishable from GR for most phenomena, differing only in the treatment of the cosmological constant) makes this tension empirically grounded rather than merely theoretical.

**T087 — Topology-induced causal shortcuts: the Tick primitive's local propagation bound is insufficient for global causal structure**
Opened: this cycle
Description: Paper 1 constructs traversable wormholes — spacetimes where two asymptotic regions are connected by a throat, allowing causal signals to travel between regions that would be causally disconnected in the ambient manifold. The Tick primitive sets the speed of light as the maximum propagation rate, and the framework derives causal structure from this local constraint. But wormhole topology creates *global* causal connections that the local tick rate does not determine. Two observers on opposite sides of a wormhole can communicate through the throat at subluminal speeds while being separated by arbitrarily large distances in the ambient geometry — the causal structure is determined by the global topology, not just the local propagation rate. The framework's implicit derivation of causal structure from the Tick is: "tick rate → light cone structure → causal future/past of each event." Wormholes show this is incomplete: "tick rate → local light cone structure" is correct, but "local light cone structure → global causal structure" fails when the topology is non-trivial. The framework needs a global topological input in addition to the local tick rate to determine causal structure, and it has no primitive for global topology. This is distinct from T036 (multi-signature computation, which concerns the signature of spacetime) and T053 (Euclidean nucleation, which concerns the absence of time). T087 concerns a fully Lorentzian spacetime with standard signature and a well-defined tick rate, where the global causal structure differs from what the local tick rate implies due to topological non-triviality.
Why unresolved: The framework would need either (a) a primitive or structural element governing spacetime topology, specifying which global topologies are allowed and how they modify the causal structure determined by the local tick rate; (b) a derivation of topology from the existing primitives (which seems impossible — topology is not a consequence of local dynamics in any known framework, and the framework's primitives are all local); or (c) acceptance that the Tick primitive determines only *local* causal structure and that global causal structure requires additional topological input the framework does not provide. Option (c) is honest but means the framework's causal account is fundamentally incomplete for any spacetime with non-trivial topology (wormholes, cosmological horizons, spatial compactification, topology change).
