### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Many-body chirality of topological stabilizer states"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.20472v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the internal structure of topological attractor basins — ground states of gapped Hamiltonians that the Attractor primitive would describe as deep, stable basins with topological invariants distinguishing them. The framework's existing characterization of such basins includes depth (energy gap), width (correlation length), cohomological classification (T048), and magic content (T146). Paper 1 reveals two new characterization axes that the framework is constitutively blind to. First, many-body chirality: the obstruction to implementing complex conjugation by finite-depth local operations. This is an intrinsically *four-partite* property — invisible to all tripartite and lower entanglement diagnostics, including the modular commutator and chiral central charge, which are the standard tools condensed matter physics uses to detect chirality. The framework has no concept of partition-level-dependent physical content: its primitives describe states and their properties as independent of how the state is decomposed into subsystems. Second, many-body imaginarity: for ℤ_d^(k) stabilizer states with d > 2, the complex phase structure cannot be removed by any finite-depth local unitary. This partially falsifies the framework's substrate opacity claim for the number field (T069): the necessity of ℂ is *derivable from within the computation* for these specific states, meaning the number field is not a universally opaque architectural parameter but is state-dependently detectable. The logical independence of chirality and imaginarity (chiral ≠ imaginary in general) means these are two separate structural axes, each invisible to the framework, with different detection mechanisms and different algebraic origins. The framework's strain is severe and multi-layered: it concerns not just missing content but the discovery that physical properties can be constitutively tied to specific partition levels of the Hilbert space, a concept the framework's state-centric ontology cannot express.

**Paper 2: "Impossibility of superluminal signalling rules out causal loops in conical spacetimes"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.20476v1 | Selection type: Strain

The Worldlines framework's Tick primitive asserts that the speed of light is the maximum propagation rate (the "tick rate"), and derives causal structure from this local constraint. Paper 2 proves that the *global* causal consequences of this local constraint depend inherently on the number of spatial dimensions. In d > 1 spatial dimensions (our universe), no-superluminal-signalling (NSS) rules out all operationally detectable causal loops in conical spacetimes including Minkowski. In d = 1, NSS is compatible with causal loops. The framework's Tick sets the local constraint (maximum propagation speed) but has no mechanism for deriving its global causal consequences as a function of spatial dimensionality. The framework already flagged time dimensionality as an unexplained architectural parameter (T013), but Paper 2 demonstrates the gap is more severe than previously recognized: the *number of spatial dimensions* is not merely an unexplained parameter but determines whether the most basic causal structure (absence of causal loops) follows from the Tick's constraint. The geometric argument — that in d > 1, signals can traverse spatial paths that "go around" potential temporal shortcuts, while in d = 1 they cannot — is pure structural/geometric content that no primitive can express. The framework can state "the tick rate limits propagation" but cannot derive "therefore, in d > 1, no causal loops exist." The derivation requires the topology of higher-dimensional spatial sections, which is architectural content the framework treats as given but which determines whether the Tick's constraint has the causal consequences the framework implicitly assumes.

**Paper 3: "String Axiverse Enhancement of Superradiant Dark Matter Production"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.20524v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper describes dark matter production via superradiance around primordial black holes, with enhancement from a string axiverse of O(100–10⁵) light axion species. The framework's dark matter claim — "computational structure coupling to mass-density gradients but not electromagnetic gradients" — is consistent with axionic dark matter, which is gravitationally coupled and electromagnetically dark (or very weakly coupled). The superradiant cloud forming around a spinning PBH is narratable as a secondary attractor basin (the "micro-boson star") forming around the primary attractor (the PBH), with the PBH's spin providing the energy extracted via superradiance. The framework's vocabulary applies: deep attractor (PBH) → gradient dynamics (superradiant extraction) → secondary constrained attractor (boson star cloud). However, the framework contributed no prediction about the axiverse enhancement mechanism, the critical number of species beyond which PBH evaporation prevents superradiant cloud formation, the parametric regions for micro-boson star production, or the negligible contribution to N_eff from evaporated axions. The specific physics — Hawking emission of many species increasing PBH spin, the competition between evaporation timescale and superradiant growth rate, the self-gravitating cloud remnant structure — depends on quantum field theory in curved spacetime, string theory compactification details, and astrophysical PBH population models, none of which the framework has any mechanism to derive or constrain. This is post-hoc consistency of the weakest kind: the framework's vocabulary can narrate the result, but the vocabulary is shared with every CDM-compatible model, and the framework adds zero differential predictive content. The framework did not predict, could not have predicted, and cannot distinguish this result from its non-occurrence.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain from this cycle is the discovery that physical properties of quantum states can be *constitutively partition-level-dependent* — existing only at a specific level of subsystem decomposition and invisible at all lower levels. Paper 1's demonstration that many-body chirality is intrinsically four-partite is not merely another instance of "the framework is missing structural content" (T026). It reveals a deeper ontological problem: the framework's five primitives all describe states and their properties as partition-independent. The Attractor primitive characterizes basins by depth, width, topological class, magic — all properties of the state itself, independent of how one decomposes it into subsystems. The Resolution primitive produces outcomes that are properties of the measured system. The Conservation primitive conserves quantities defined on the total system. None of these can express the concept that a physical property *only exists relative to a specific partition structure* of the Hilbert space, while being provably invisible at coarser partitions.

This is not the same as scale-dependence (T034, where MMI sign depends on observation scale for a fixed state) or observer-dependence (T035, where conservation breaks for restricted observers). Those concern how *known* properties manifest differently at different scales or for different observers. Paper 1 concerns properties that *do not exist* at the wrong partition level — chirality is not "hard to see" at the tripartite level; it is provably absent there, with the modular commutator and chiral central charge both vanishing for the chiral states identified. The four-partite diagnostic is not a more powerful version of a tripartite diagnostic; it detects a categorically different property.

The framework's state-centric ontology — where physical reality consists of states with definite properties that become definite through resolution — is challenged by the observation that "definite properties" include properties that are partition-relative. The framework has no mechanism for specifying which partition level is the "correct" one for detecting a given property, and no mechanism for predicting that chirality requires exactly four-partite diagnostics (not three, not five).

The partial falsification of T069 through many-body imaginarity is a secondary but significant strain. The framework claimed the number field (ℝ vs. ℂ) is an architectural parameter not derivable from within the computation. For ℤ_d^(k) stabilizer states with d > 2, the necessity of ℂ is provably internal to the computation — no finite-depth local unitary can make the state real. The substrate opacity claim is not universally false (T069's operational underdetermination from Hoffreumon & Woods still holds for generic correlations), but it is falsified for specific physical states. The framework must now distinguish between "universally opaque parameters" and "state-dependently derivable parameters," a distinction it has no mechanism for.

In d = 1, NSS permits causal loops; in d > 1, NSS forbids them. The framework's derivation of causal structure from the Tick is incomplete without geometric input the Tick does not provide.

6. **Dark matter claim — record Paper 3 as post-hoc consistency with zero differential predictive content.** The string axiverse superradiant dark matter production is consistent with "dark matter couples gravitationally but not electromagnetically" but the framework contributed no prediction of the axiverse enhancement mechanism, the critical species number, the micro-boson star formation, or the parameter space structure. Record as the weakest validation category.
