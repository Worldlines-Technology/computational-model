### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: Leading singularities of Wilson loop correlators from twistor Wilson loop diagrams**
ArXiv ID: 2602.18423v1 | Selection type: Strain

This paper demonstrates that one-loop leading singularities of Wilson loop expectation values in planar 𝒩=4 super Yang-Mills factorize into products of tree-level objects, and that this factorization is *manifest* from the twistor Wilson loop perspective — arising simply from planarity, without needing to invoke unitarity from the amplitude side of the amplitude/Wilson-loop duality. Through the Worldlines lens, this is a statement about computational structure: the one-loop computation decomposes cleanly into tree-level sub-computations, and this decomposition is visible from either the amplitude or the Wilson loop perspective. The framework would say: the computation has a modular structure that is preserved under duality, and the factorization reflects the conservation of computational content (the same information content is present in tree-level building blocks regardless of which formulation you use). However, the strain comes from the *duality itself*. The amplitude/Wilson-loop duality in 𝒩=4 SYM means two radically different-looking computational processes (scattering amplitudes as local vertex computations vs. Wilson loops as holonomy computations over closed contours) produce identical results. The framework's ẋ = −∇φ(x) wants a single field with a single gradient. But here there are two entirely different field descriptions — one in momentum twistor space, one in configuration/twistor space — that are exactly equivalent. The framework has no natural mechanism for explaining *why* dual computational descriptions should exist. Moreover, the result that planarity alone suffices to produce factorization — without unitarity — strains the Resolution primitive. Unitarity is the framework's Conservation of Computation expressed as information conservation. If factorization doesn't need unitarity as an input, then the computational structure is more constrained by topology (planarity) than by information conservation. This suggests the framework may be missing a primitive related to topological or combinatorial structure that is independent of the conservation principle.

**Paper 2: Bell-GHZ nonclassicality of many-observer interwoven frustrated down conversions**
ArXiv ID: 2602.18381v1 | Selection type: Strain

This paper extends frustrated parametric down-conversion to multi-observer settings and demonstrates violations of WWWZB Bell inequalities and GHZ/Hardy-type arguments for nonclassicality. The key mechanism is "path identity" — when emission modes from different crystals are made to overlap so that the origin of photon pairs becomes fundamentally indistinguishable, interference between joint emission processes produces correlations that violate classical bounds. Through the Worldlines lens, this is a clean demonstration of what unresolved computation looks like: before resolution (detection), the system exists in superposition over which crystal pair produced the photons, and the interference is between computational paths that remain unresolved. The Bell/GHZ violations confirm that resolution cannot be explained by pre-existing definite states (hidden variables). The framework handles this naturally — it's precisely the Tick and Resolution primitives at work. The strain, however, is subtle but important. The frustrated down-conversion setup shows that the *topology of how unresolved computational paths are interwoven* matters critically. It's not just that states are unresolved; it's that the pattern of which paths are made indistinguishable — the "interwoven" structure — determines what nonclassical correlations emerge. The framework's Resolution primitive says states become definite, and between ticks they're unresolved distributions. But it says nothing about the *topology of indistinguishability*. The paper shows you can engineer specific nonclassical phenomena by controlling which computational paths are made indistinguishable. This is a design space for resolution that the framework doesn't acknowledge: there's structure in the *pre-resolution* state that goes beyond "unresolved probability distribution." The GHZ/Hardy argument is especially pointed: it shows that the paradox's strength depends on the specific interwoven configuration, meaning the framework needs a theory of *how* unresolved paths combine, not just that they're unresolved.

**Paper 3: The Emergence of Measured Geometry in Self-Gravitating Systems**
ArXiv ID: 2602.18115v1 | Selection type: Validation

This paper argues that in self-gravitating N-body Newtonian systems, measured geometry is not a fixed background but emerges from gravitational interactions — nearest-neighbor separations systematically vary with radial distance from the center of mass in central configurations, reflecting a context-dependent effective geometry. Through the Worldlines lens, this is strongly consonant with the framework's position: geometry is not substrate but is the result of computation. The Field primitive says gravity is mass rolling down curvature gradients, and the Attractor primitive says central configurations are attractor basins. This paper adds that the attractor structure itself defines an emergent geometry — the "shape" of space as measured by physical rods is determined by the gravitational field, not given a priori. This aligns with the framework's claim that physical constants and structures emerge from computational dynamics rather than from a pre-given container. However, the validation is weak in a critical sense: the framework did not *predict* this specific result. The finding that nearest-neighbor separations vary systematically with radial position in central configurations is a concrete, quantitative result about Newtonian gravity that the framework, at its current level of specificity, could not have derived. The framework says "geometry emerges from computation" but doesn't specify *how* or *what geometry*. The paper's result is post-hoc consistent with the framework but would be equally consistent with general relativity's lesson that geometry is dynamical (which long predates this framework), or with Poincaré's operationalism about geometry (which is 120 years old). The framework adds no predictive value beyond what these established perspectives already provide. The genuine validation would require the framework to have predicted a specific scaling relation for the nearest-neighbor variation — which it did not.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most significant strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's demonstration that computational duality — the exact equivalence of amplitude and Wilson loop descriptions — produces factorization from planarity alone, without invoking unitarity (information conservation). This is uncomfortable for the framework in two specific ways:

First, the framework lacks any primitive for **computational duality**. Two completely different-looking computations (amplitudes computed via Feynman-like diagrams in momentum space vs. Wilson loop expectation values computed via holonomies in twistor space) give identical results. The framework says "reality is computation," but it has no account of *why* the same physical reality admits radically different but exactly equivalent computational descriptions. This isn't just gauge redundancy — the amplitude and Wilson loop pictures involve different fundamental objects (particles vs. loops), different spaces (momentum vs. position/twistor), and different intuitions (scattering vs. parallel transport). If reality is literally computation, which computation is it? The framework implicitly assumes there's one computation happening. Duality says there are multiple equivalent ones. This challenges the ontological weight the framework places on "the computation."

Second, and more technically precise: the factorization arising from **planarity rather than unitarity** suggests that *topological/combinatorial* structure can do work that the framework currently assigns to Conservation of Computation (unitarity). The framework treats conservation of computation as the master conservation law from which all others derive. But here, a structural property of the diagram topology (planarity = being embeddable on a sphere) produces the same factorization that unitarity would produce, independently. This means there's a constraint on the computation that isn't captured by any of the five primitives. It's not the Tick (propagation rate), not the Field (gradient structure), not the Attractor (stability), not Resolution (state-fixing), and not Conservation (information preservation). It's something about the *topology of the computational graph itself* — how the computation is wired together. The framework may need a sixth structural element related to the topology or combinatorics of computational connections.

### STATE UPDATES

1. **Resolution primitive — add pre-resolution topology.** The Resolution description should note that unresolved states have internal *topological* structure: the pattern of which computational paths are indistinguishable matters and determines what correlations emerge upon resolution. Resolution is not merely "unresolved → definite"; the structure of the superposition's indistinguishability graph constrains and shapes the resolution outcomes. This is demonstrated by interwoven frustrated down-conversion (Paper 2), where engineering specific path-identity topologies produces specific nonclassical signatures. The framework should distinguish between the *content* of superposition (which outcomes are possible) and the *topology* of superposition (which paths are indistinguishable from which).

2. **Field/Attractor — note emergent geometry.** The model should record that the attractor structure of self-gravitating N-body systems defines an emergent measured geometry (Paper 3), providing a concrete example of geometry emerging from field dynamics rather than being background. However, this should be noted as post-hoc consistent rather than predicted — the framework needs to develop the specificity to derive geometrical consequences of attractor structures, not merely claim they exist.

3. **Conservation of Computation — note potential incompleteness.** The conservation principle may not be the only fundamental constraint on computation. Paper 1 shows that topological/combinatorial constraints (planarity) can independently produce results that would otherwise require conservation (unitarity). The model should note that the five primitives may be missing a structural element related to the topology of computational connections, and that conservation may be necessary but not sufficient to characterize all constraints on how computation proceeds.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T010 — The duality problem: which computation is reality?**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** The amplitude/Wilson-loop duality in 𝒩=4 SYM (Paper 1) demonstrates that the same physical observables can be computed from radically different computational architectures — local scattering vertices vs. holonomy integrals over closed loops — in different mathematical spaces. The framework claims "reality is literally computation," but duality means the physical content is invariant under exchange of completely different computational descriptions. If reality *is* computation, it should have a definite computational structure. Duality says the structure is not unique. Either: (a) reality is the equivalence class of dual computations rather than any single computation (but what is an equivalence class ontologically?), (b) there is a preferred computation and the dual description is merely a calculational convenience (but the framework provides no criterion for choosing), or (c) the framework's identification of reality with computation is too literal and needs to be weakened to "reality is *described by* computation" (which significantly dilutes the framework's core claim). This tension is distinct from gauge redundancy because the dual descriptions involve fundamentally different objects and spaces, not just reparameterizations of the same objects.
**Why unresolved:** The framework has no account of duality. Its five primitives describe the dynamics of a single computation but say nothing about whether or why equivalent computational descriptions should exist. Resolving this would require either identifying the invariant structure that all dual descriptions share (which might be the true "computation") or modifying the core claim about reality being computation.

**T011 — Planarity as a missing structural primitive**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 1 shows that factorization of one-loop leading singularities follows from planarity alone in the twistor Wilson loop formalism, without requiring unitarity. In the framework, unitarity corresponds to Conservation of Computation — the most fundamental conservation law. If planarity (a topological property of how the computation is wired) independently produces the same result, then either (a) planarity and unitarity are secretly the same constraint in different guises, (b) there's a deeper constraint from which both follow, or (c) the five primitives are incomplete and a topological/combinatorial primitive is needed. This tension is sharpest in its implication for Conservation of Computation: if the same physical constraints can arise from graph topology without invoking information conservation, then Conservation of Computation may be less fundamental than the framework claims.
**Why unresolved:** Determining whether planarity and unitarity are equivalent constraints, or whether planarity is independent and more primitive, requires a formal analysis that the framework currently cannot perform. This is also currently specific to planar 𝒩=4 SYM and may not generalize, but the existence of even one case where topology does unitarity's work is significant.

**T012 — Pre-resolution structure and the topology of indistinguishability**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 2 demonstrates that engineering specific patterns of path indistinguishability in interwoven frustrated PDC produces specific and different nonclassical signatures (Bell violations, GHZ paradoxes). The framework's Resolution primitive treats pre-resolution states as "unresolved probability distributions," but this is insufficient: the *topology* of which paths are indistinguishable is a structural feature of the pre-resolution state that determines the character of the resolution outcomes. This is not just the quantum state (which encodes amplitudes) but the connectivity pattern of which processes can interfere with which — the indistinguishability graph. The framework needs a theory of pre-resolution topology, not just pre-resolution probability.
**Why unresolved:** The framework has no formalism for describing the topology of indistinguishability. The Tick primitive says states are "unresolved probability distributions between ticks," but this conflates the amplitude information with the structural information about which computational paths are connected. Developing this formalism would require specifying how the indistinguishability graph relates to the other primitives and whether it should be considered part of the Field, a feature of the Tick, or something new.

The system is finding new gradient in the direction of structural/topological incompleteness — the framework may be missing something about the *wiring* of computation that is distinct from all five current primitives. The next cycle would be most productive if it targeted papers on holographic duality, topological quantum computation, or categorical/operadic descriptions of quantum theory, which would directly pressure T010 and T011 and test whether the framework genuinely needs a topological primitive or whether the existing primitives can absorb this strain.
