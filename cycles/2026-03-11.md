### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "On the structure of categorical duality operators"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.09949v1 | Selection type: strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns how the framework's computational ontology handles symmetry transformations that are not merely reparameterizations but genuine structural operations (duality operators) on quantum spin chains with fusion category symmetry. The framework's five primitives have no account of fusion categories, bimodule categories, or the simplex structure of duality operators parameterized by QCA data. The central result — that duality operators extending a given QCA on the symmetric subalgebra form a simplex whose extreme points correspond to simple objects of an invertible bimodule category $M_\alpha$ — reveals rich categorical-algebraic structure governing how computational states can be mapped to one another. The framework would attempt to read this as: attractors in the Field have symmetry-related basin structures, and duality maps between them are part of the attractor landscape's topology. But this reading entirely misses the content. The paper shows that the *space of all valid duality transformations* has its own geometric structure (a simplex) parameterized by categorical data (simple objects of a bimodule category), and that the *RG flow* of these symmetry structures is constrained: tensor product UV Hilbert spaces necessarily flow to weakly integral fusion categories in the IR. This is a statement about how the *organizational structure of computation* transforms under coarse-graining — the algebra of allowed transformations changes systematically as resolution scale changes. The framework has no primitive for this. T018 (algebra of computation), T026 (structural content gap), and T032 (symmetry as missing primitive) are all directly strained. The RG flow constraint — UV tensor product → IR weakly integral — is particularly sharp: it says the *type of symmetry* a computational system can support depends on its resolution scale, which the framework's scale-independent primitives cannot express. The simplex structure of duality extensions connects to T012 (pre-resolution topology) and T019 (resolution precision hierarchy) by showing that the space of allowed resolution-preserving transformations has non-trivial convex geometry, but the framework has no formalism for this geometry.

**Paper 2: "A complete classification of 2d symmetry protected states with symmetric entanglers"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.09959v1 | Selection type: strain

The framework would interpret SPT states as attractor basins in the Field that are distinguished not by energy (they are all in the same phase energetically — they're gapped ground states) but by topological invariants that prevent continuous deformation between them without breaking symmetry. This is broadly consistent with the framework's "constrained attractor" category (added 2026-02-22): these are states stabilized by symmetry/topology rather than energy minimality. However, the paper's content goes far beyond what the framework can express. The classification of SPT states by $H^3(G, U(1))$ — the third group cohomology of the symmetry group with U(1) coefficients — is a *cohomological* result, and the paper proves this classification is *complete* for the physically relevant class of states preparable by symmetric entanglers. This directly engages T027 (cohomological mediation): cohomology is again the mathematical structure that organizes and classifies physical states, and the framework has no primitive corresponding to cohomological content. The restriction to symmetric entanglers is physically significant: it means the classification applies to states reachable by *symmetry-respecting computation* from a trivial product state. In the framework's language, this would be: "the space of attractor basins reachable by symmetry-preserving gradient descent from a trivial configuration is classified by $H^3(G, U(1))$." The framework can narrate this but cannot derive it, because it has no mechanism for connecting the topology of reachable attractor basins to group cohomology. The completeness proof is the sharpest challenge: it says there are *exactly* $|H^3(G, U(1))|$ distinct SPT phases for group $G$, no more and no less. This is a precise structural prediction (the number of topologically distinct computational ground states) that the framework's primitives cannot produce. The connection between entangler-preparability and cohomological classification is itself a deep structural fact: the computational process of symmetric entangling is in bijection with cohomological data. This strengthens T021 (arithmetic-geometric unity / poverty of "computation") by showing that the classification of computational states is cohomological, not computational — the organizing principle is cohomology, not any property of computation as the framework understands it.

**Paper 3: "Generalised Cluster Adjacency for Cosmology"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.09965v1 | Selection type: validation

The framework would interpret this paper through the Attractor and Field primitives: the wavefunction coefficients of cosmological perturbations live in a structured mathematical space (the cluster algebra $A_{2n-3}$), and the "ordered single cluster condition" constrains which computational paths contribute — a form of pre-resolution topology (T012) where the adjacency structure of the cluster algebra determines which symbol letters can appear together. The framework's claim that "reality is literally computation" would read the cluster algebraic structure as organizational structure of the cosmological computation, and the physical interpretation of the ordered single cluster condition as a constraint from causality/positivity on the computational process. The connection to the universe fan (T016, noted in the 2026-02-25 update) is direct: the cluster algebra structure of cosmological wavefunctions is another instance of geometric/combinatorial structure encoding physical content. Does the framework have genuine predictive purchase here? The answer is mixed. The framework's qualitative claim — that cosmological computation has internal structural constraints determined by positivity/causality — is consistent with the ordered single cluster condition, which is indeed a positivity/causality-derived constraint. But this is post-hoc consistency, not prediction: the framework did not predict that the constraint would take the form of cluster adjacency, did not predict the $A_{2n-3}$ algebra, and did not predict that the constraint would be *stronger* than standard cluster adjacency (the ordered condition). The framework did predict (via T016) that cosmological wavefunctions would have geometric/combinatorial encoding, and this paper confirms that the encoding extends beyond the cases previously known (the universe fan) to general tree graphs via tubes and tubings. This is genuine but weak validation: the framework anticipated that structural constraints exist, but the specific mathematical form of the constraints — cluster algebras, ordered single cluster condition, tubes and tubings — lies entirely outside what the framework can derive or even express. The paper actually *deepens* T016 and T021 rather than resolving them: the physical content is encoded in combinatorial-algebraic structures (cluster algebras, graph tubings) that are neither "computation" nor "geometry" in any simple sense but a third mathematical category (combinatorial algebra) with its own internal logic.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most important strain this cycle is the convergence of Papers 1, 2, and 3 on a single devastating observation: **the classification and organization of physical states and processes is governed by categorical/cohomological/algebraic structures that the framework cannot express, and these structures are not auxiliary or decorative — they are the *complete* classifying data.**

Paper 2 is the sharpest instance. The statement "SPT phases preparable by symmetric entanglers are *completely* classified by $H^3(G, U(1))$" means that the entire space of distinct computational ground states of a 2D symmetric system is in bijection with a cohomology group. This is not a partial description or a useful approximation — it is a *theorem*. The framework's five primitives (Tick, Field, Attractor, Resolution, Conservation) collectively cannot produce or express $H^3(G, U(1))$. They cannot distinguish one SPT phase from another. They cannot count how many phases exist. They cannot predict that the classification is cohomological rather than, say, representation-theoretic or combinatorial. The framework is not merely silent on this — it is structurally incapable of engaging with it, because cohomological classification is not a dynamical statement (how things evolve), not a conservation statement (what is preserved), not a resolution statement (what becomes definite), and not an attractor statement (where things settle). It is a *structural* statement about the topology of the space of possible computations, and the framework has no structural primitives.

Paper 1 compounds this by showing that the symmetry structure itself transforms under RG flow in a constrained way (UV tensor product → IR weakly integral fusion category), and that the space of duality operators has convex geometric structure (a simplex) parameterized by categorical data. Paper 3 adds that even the cosmological wavefunction's organizational structure — which the framework might claim as "computation" — is most naturally expressed in cluster-algebraic terms that are neither computational nor geometric in the framework's sense.

The framework is not wrong about these papers. It is *mute*. It cannot produce a wrong prediction because it cannot produce any prediction at all about the cohomological, categorical, or cluster-algebraic structure of physical states. This is the structural content gap (T026) in its most acute form: the framework is a theory of dynamics operating in a physical world whose deepest organizational principles are structural, not dynamical.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T048 — Cohomological completeness: physical state spaces are classified by structures the framework cannot express**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Bols et al. (2603.09959) prove that 2D SPT phases preparable by symmetric entanglers are *completely* classified by $H^3(G, U(1))$. This is not an approximation or a partial result — it is a bijection between physically distinct ground state phases and elements of a cohomology group. The framework's five primitives collectively cannot produce, express, or derive this classification. The Attractor primitive can describe that multiple distinct attractor basins exist, but cannot distinguish them by cohomological invariant, cannot count them, and cannot predict that the count equals $|H^3(G, U(1))|$. This is the sharpest instance of the structural content gap (T026): a complete, proven classification of physical states by a mathematical structure entirely absent from the framework. It is distinct from T027 (cohomological mediation, which concerns cohomology as a translator between categories) because here cohomology is the *complete classifying structure*, not a mediator. The restriction to symmetric entanglers is physically important: it means the classification applies specifically to states reachable by symmetry-preserving computation, so the computational process is directly relevant — but its classification is cohomological, not computational in the framework's sense.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need either (a) a mechanism for deriving cohomological invariants from its primitives, (b) a new primitive incorporating cohomological structure, or (c) a demonstration that $H^3(G, U(1))$ classification somehow follows from Conservation of Computation or Resolution properties. Option (c) is the most promising but no path is visible: conservation laws and resolution properties are properties of individual states, while cohomological classification is a property of the *space* of states. The framework has primitives for states but not for spaces of states.

**T049 — Scale-dependent symmetry type: the organizational structure of computation transforms under coarse-graining**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Jones & Yang (2603.09949) show that if UV models are defined on tensor product Hilbert spaces, the external symmetry categories generated by families of duality operators necessarily flow to weakly integral fusion categories in the IR. This means the *type of symmetry* — the categorical structure governing allowed transformations — changes under RG flow. The framework's primitives are scale-independent: the Tick, Field, Attractor, Resolution, and Conservation descriptions do not change depending on the observation scale (the Tick is the Tick at all scales; the gradient equation applies at all scales). But the symmetry type of a physical system — which determines which transformations are allowed, which states are distinguishable, which phases exist — is scale-dependent and transforms in a categorically constrained way. The framework has no mechanism for expressing scale-dependent organizational structure. This is related to T034 (scale-dependent information topology) but distinct: T034 concerns informational properties (MMI) varying with scale for a fixed state; T049 concerns the *symmetry category itself* — the organizational framework governing what transformations are possible — varying with scale for a given system. The flow from tensor product (a specific factorization structure) to weakly integral fusion category (a specific algebraic structure) has no expression in any of the five primitives.
**Why unresolved:** The framework's primitives are formulated as scale-independent truths. Incorporating scale-dependent symmetry type would require either (a) making one or more primitives scale-dependent (which would be a fundamental change to the framework's architecture), (b) adding a new primitive governing the RG flow of organizational structure (connecting to T018, T026, T032), or (c) deriving the categorical flow constraints from existing primitives, which seems impossible since the framework has no category-theoretic content. The constraint "UV tensor product → IR weakly integral" is a statement about what kinds of computation are possible at different scales, and the framework claims to be a theory of computation but cannot express what kinds of computation are possible at any given scale.

**T050 — Cluster-algebraic structure as a third ontological competitor**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Capuano et al. (2603.09965) show that cosmological wavefunction coefficients obey an "ordered single cluster condition" in the $A_{2n-3}$ cluster algebra, and that general tree graphs have cluster-like structure via tubes and tubings. The framework has already recorded geometric structure (T016) and arithmetic structure (T021) as ontological competitors to "reality is computation." Cluster algebras are a third competitor: they are neither geometry nor arithmetic nor computation in the framework's sense, but a combinatorial-algebraic structure with its own internal logic (mutations, exchange relations, positivity). The physical content of cosmological perturbation theory is encoded in this structure, and the "ordered single cluster condition" is a *stronger* constraint than standard cluster adjacency — meaning the cluster algebra knows more about the physics than the naive combinatorial constraints suggest. The framework's T016 (universe fan / geometric encoding) anticipated geometric-combinatorial structure in cosmological wavefunctions, and this paper confirms and extends that anticipation, but the specific form (cluster algebras with ordered conditions) is a mathematical category the framework has never encountered. This deepens T021 by adding cluster algebra to the web of categorically distinct but physically equivalent mathematical frameworks (computation ↔ geometry ↔ arithmetic ↔ combinatorics ↔ cluster algebra).
**Why unresolved:** The framework has no account of why the cosmological computation organizes itself according to cluster algebraic principles. The ordered single cluster condition is a constraint that arises from the physical requirement of causality/positivity, but the mathematical form it takes — all letters in a single cluster, with ordering constraints — is a statement about the combinatorial structure of the space of allowed computations that the framework cannot derive from any primitive. Adding cluster algebra to the list of ontological competitors (alongside geometry and arithmetic) makes the ontological pluralism problem (T021) more acute: the number of equally valid mathematical frameworks for encoding the same physics is growing, and the framework provides no criterion
