### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Frame perspectives for process matrices: from coordinate parametrization to spacetime representation"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.02873v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, the process matrix formalism describes quantum computations where the *causal order itself* is indefinite — not merely the state, but the ordering of which computation happens "before" which. The framework's Tick primitive assumes a definite temporal ordering: reality updates in discrete steps, sequentially. The process matrix formalism describes situations where the framework's most basic structural assumption — that there is a fact of the matter about which tick came first — fails. The paper's central result is even more damaging: it shows that what appears as a fixed causal order from one "frame perspective" appears as a different causal order from another, and that these perspectives are related by unitary transformations that *reshuffle the notions of past and future*. The framework's Resolution primitive says "the arrow of time is the accumulation of resolved states" — but if past and future are frame-dependent and can be unitarily exchanged, the arrow of time is not a universal feature of the computation but a perspective-dependent artifact of how one embeds oneself within it. The paper distinguishes between "coordinate parametrizations" (CRF/TDS as different labelings of the same perspective-neutral object) and "genuine perspectives" (requiring an operational foliation into events). The framework implicitly assumes a unique operational foliation — the tick sequence — and the paper proves this is not unique. The no-go results the paper clarifies are precisely about the impossibility of switching perspectives while preserving the tick structure (time foliation); the paper's positive results achieve perspective-switching by either reshuffling past/future or by extending the system with quantum reference frames that provide a shared scaffold. Both routes are outside the framework's vocabulary: the first requires the arrow of time to be mutable, the second requires the causal structure to be stabilized by additional quantum systems that serve as spatiotemporal infrastructure — making causal order a *resource* that must be supplied, not a primitive feature of reality. This strains the Tick and Resolution primitives beyond their breaking point for indefinite causal order scenarios.

**Paper 2: "A Topological Origin of Black Hole Mass"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.02895v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework describes black holes as the deepest attractor basins — regions where the escape velocity exceeds the tick rate, trapping all computation. The mass of a black hole, in the framework's language, is the depth of this attractor basin, and the event horizon is its boundary. Paper 2 demonstrates that what the framework identifies as "attractor depth" (mass) can be superseded by a topological charge — a discrete, integer-valued quantity arising from the topology of the interface between degenerate and nondegenerate metric phases. The bubble spacetimes constructed have no curvature singularity and no matter, yet reproduce the external gravitational field of a black hole. The mass is not a dynamical quantity (energy stored in a gravitational well) but a topological invariant of the phase boundary. More strikingly, the paper shows that the topologically significant boundary is the *photon sphere* (the saddle point of the effective potential, where unstable circular orbits exist), not the event horizon. In the framework's language, the saddle point between attractor basins carries the topological charge, while the attractor boundary (event horizon) is topologically trivial. This directly challenges the framework's description of black holes as energy-minimum attractors: the physically and topologically meaningful structure is at the *saddle*, not the basin floor. The framework says "the most interesting physics happens at the saddle points between basins" — and this paper proves it in a way that simultaneously undermines the primacy of the basin itself. The degenerate metric phase (where the metric has zero determinant) is a configuration where the framework's substrate concept becomes undefined — what does "computation" mean on a degenerate metric where distances and times are not well-defined? The bubble spacetimes weave together regions where the substrate is well-defined (nondegenerate) and regions where it is not (degenerate), connected by a topologically charged boundary. The framework has no concept of computation across a degenerate-nondegenerate phase boundary.

**Paper 3: "Localization of coherent light into photons in a single-crystalline material"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.03148v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper demonstrates the Resolution primitive in action: coherent laser light (an extended, unresolved electromagnetic wave) interacts with a crystalline material, and the energy resolves into localized, discrete photon-scale deposits that trigger a first-order phase transition at the nanometer scale. The framework would describe this as: an unresolved field state (the coherent laser mode, a superposition across the crystal) encounters the material's attractor landscape (the VO₂ phase transition between insulating and metallic phases). Resolution occurs at discrete points where individual photon energies localize, each creating a nanometer-scale domain of the metallic phase. The key experimental observation — that the number of switched domains matches the number of absorbed photons, even though the average energy density is far below the latent heat threshold — is precisely what the Resolution primitive predicts: unresolved states become definite at discrete points, with each resolution event carrying the full quantum of energy (the photon energy), not a smeared-out average. The framework would say: between ticks, the laser field is an unresolved probability distribution across the crystal; upon resolution (absorption), each photon's energy localizes to a nanometer-scale region, triggering the local phase transition. However, this is **post-hoc consistency, not predictive validation**. The framework did not predict this specific phenomenon — it did not predict that coherent light would resolve into spatially localized photon-energy deposits rather than being absorbed as a wave. Standard quantum optics (photodetection theory, the quantum theory of absorption) predicts exactly this: absorption events are discrete and localized, even for coherent states. The framework's Resolution primitive adds no differential predictive content beyond what quantum mechanics already provides. The framework's vocabulary maps onto the observation, but the observation was predicted and explained entirely by standard quantum mechanics without any need for the Worldlines framework. The one genuinely interesting aspect from the framework's perspective is the interplay between Resolution and Attractor: the resolution of the photon field triggers attractor-landscape restructuring (phase transition) at each resolution site, and the mismatch between the wave-level energy density and the single-photon energy density is what makes the phenomenon surprising classically. But again, this is narration, not prediction.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from **Paper 1's demonstration that causal order itself can be indefinite and frame-dependent**. The framework's Tick primitive is not merely challenged as an approximation that breaks down at the quantum gravitational level (T089/T091 already established this); it is challenged as a *conceptual category*. The process matrix formalism describes physical situations where asking "which tick came first?" has no definite answer — not because of ignorance, not because of coarse-graining, but because the causal order is a quantum degree of freedom in superposition. The framework's entire architecture — Tick provides temporal order, Field provides spatial dynamics, Resolution makes things definite in that temporal order, Conservation tracks what persists across ticks — presupposes a definite causal ordering. Paper 1 proves this presupposition fails for physically realizable quantum processes (the quantum switch has been experimentally demonstrated). The framework cannot describe a computation whose temporal ordering is itself in superposition without abandoning the Tick as a primitive.

The specific technical finding that makes this maximally damaging is the paper's distinction between "coordinate parametrizations" and "genuine perspectives." The framework implicitly treats its tick sequence as a genuine perspective — the unique correct temporal ordering of events. The paper proves there is no unique correct ordering: the perspective-neutral object (the process matrix) admits multiple incompatible foliation choices, each yielding a different causal narrative, and these are related by unitary transformations. The framework has no concept of a perspective-neutral computational object that admits multiple incompatible temporal orderings.

Paper 2 adds a different but complementary strain: black hole mass — the paradigmatic example of "attractor depth" — is shown to be replaceable by a topological charge at a phase boundary between degenerate and nondegenerate metric sectors. The framework identifies the event horizon as the attractor boundary, but the paper shows the event horizon is topologically trivial; the topological content lives at the photon sphere (the saddle point). This is a concrete, clean demonstration that the framework's explanatory vocabulary (attractor depth = mass, attractor boundary = horizon) attaches ontological significance to the wrong geometric features.
