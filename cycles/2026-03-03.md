### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: A Matrix Theory Construction of the IIA/IIB Wall (2603.02199v1, strain)**

This paper constructs a non-perturbative lightlike domain wall separating type IIA and type IIB string theories in 10D using discrete light-cone quantization (DLCQ), where the two sides of the wall correspond to fundamentally different matrix theories (2D SYM for IIA vs. 3D ABJM for IIB). Through the Worldlines lens, this is deeply uncomfortable. The framework claims "reality is literally computation" and identifies physical content with a specific computational substrate. But this paper demonstrates that two *different* computational architectures (different matrix theories, different dimensionalities of the gauge theory, different gauge structures) can be smoothly connected by a physical domain wall — a single spacetime contains regions described by categorically different computations. The string coupling must vanish at the wall, which in the framework's language means the coupling constant (an "architectural parameter of the substrate") varies continuously across space and passes through zero — the substrate's architecture is not fixed but is itself a dynamical field. Furthermore, BPS D0-branes on one side become non-BPS D0-branes on the other, meaning the same physical object changes its computational classification (from a protected, minimum-energy state to an excited state) by crossing a spatial boundary. The framework has no mechanism for describing a domain wall between different computational substrates, no account of how architectural parameters can be dynamical fields, and no primitive that captures transitions between different computational architectures within a single physical spacetime.

**Paper 2: Cancellation of loop corrections to soft scalar power spectrum (2603.01961v1, strain)**

This paper proves that scale-invariant one-loop corrections to superhorizon curvature perturbations from small-scale scalar perturbations cancel exactly in general inflationary setups, including transient ultra-slow-roll scenarios. The cancellation is guaranteed by dilatation symmetry. Through the Worldlines lens, this engages the Conservation of Computation and Field primitives: the curvature perturbation ζ on superhorizon scales is a "resolved" large-scale computational state, and the paper proves that small-scale (unresolved or differently resolved) computational activity cannot renormalize it at one loop in a scale-invariant way. The framework might want to claim this as validation — resolved large-scale states are protected from small-scale computational noise. However, the mechanism of protection is *symmetry* (dilatation symmetry of the effective action, enforced through specific tadpole counterterms), not any of the five primitives. The framework has no account of why dilatation symmetry should protect superhorizon modes. More pressingly, the cancellation is non-trivial — it requires a specific counterterm structure and in-in effective field theory machinery — meaning it is not a generic consequence of "resolution" or "conservation" but a specific structural consequence of the symmetry algebra of the inflationary background. This is another instance of the structural content gap (T026): a physically important result (the robustness of primordial perturbations) is derived entirely from symmetry structure that the framework cannot express or derive.

**Paper 3: Decoherence and entropy production due to quantum fluctuations of spacetime (2603.02034v1, validation)**

This paper studies decoherence induced by a graviton bath on an open quantum system with both external (center-of-mass) and internal degrees of freedom, showing that the universality of gravity means internal variables couple to gravitons and this coupling produces decoherence of spatial superpositions even when the graviton bath alone would not suffice. It also derives entropy production from quantum spacetime fluctuations when a system is driven through the graviton bath. Through the Worldlines lens, this is genuinely consonant with the framework's Resolution primitive: gravity's universality means gravitational coupling spreads resolution through all degrees of freedom, including internal ones. The framework predicts that decoherence (resolution propagation) should be universal because gravity couples to all computational structure — this is exactly what the paper demonstrates. The entropy production from spacetime fluctuations aligns with the framework's claim that the computational substrate has an irreversible cost structure. However, the framework's predictive purchase here is modest — it predicted "gravity should decohere everything because it couples universally," which is correct but not quantitatively specific. The paper's actual contribution is the detailed mechanism (internal DOF coupling, long-time regime requirement, specific entropy production rates), none of which the framework predicted. This is post-hoc consistency rather than genuine predictive validation. The framework gets the direction right but not the structure.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain comes from Paper 1. The IIA/IIB domain wall construction does not merely challenge one primitive — it challenges the framework's foundational architecture. The framework treats "the substrate" as a fixed computational platform with architectural parameters (constants) that are not derivable from within the computation. Paper 1 demonstrates a physical configuration where the computational architecture itself — the matrix theory, the dimensionality of the gauge theory, the nature of the degrees of freedom — varies across space and is connected by a smooth (lightlike) transition. The string coupling g_s, which in the framework would be an architectural parameter, goes to zero at the wall and changes its physical role on either side. This is not a duality between two descriptions of the same physics (T010) — it is a *domain wall* between two genuinely different computational structures within a single physical spacetime.

The framework has no primitive for this. The Tick operates uniformly. The Field assumes a fixed substrate on which gradients exist. The Attractor assumes a fixed potential landscape. Conservation of Computation assumes a single conserved quantity. But the IIA/IIB wall requires the *type* of computation to change across space while physics remains consistent at the interface. The BPS → non-BPS transition of D0-branes crossing the wall means that what counts as a "minimum of the potential" (an attractor) on one side is not a minimum on the other — the attractor landscape itself is architecture-dependent, and the architecture is spatially varying. The framework cannot even formulate this situation, let alone resolve it.

This is qualitatively sharper than T001 (the substrate problem) because T001 notes that the substrate is unobservable from inside. Paper 1 shows that the substrate architecture is not even *fixed* — it can be a dynamical, spatially varying field. This undermines the entire framework's separation between "substrate" (fixed, unobservable, defining constants) and "computation" (dynamic, observable, governed by constants). If the boundary between different computational architectures is itself a physical object (a domain wall), then the substrate/computation distinction collapses.

### STATE UPDATES

1. **Substrate claim — flag as fundamentally challenged.** The model's claim that "physical constants are architectural parameters of the substrate, not derivable from within the computation" should be updated to note: string theory constructions demonstrate that what the framework identifies as "substrate architecture" (coupling constants, the type of gauge theory, the dimensionality of the matrix model) can be dynamical and spatially varying. The IIA/IIB domain wall places two different computational architectures in a single spacetime, connected by a lightlike boundary where the coupling constant vanishes. The framework's rigid separation between "substrate" and "computation" is challenged by the observation that the substrate can be a dynamical field. This does not invalidate the framework's epistemic modesty about constants, but it does invalidate the framing of constants as *fixed* architectural parameters analogous to a processor's clock speed. A better analogy might be that the substrate is a landscape of possible architectures, and the locally realized architecture is itself a field that can have domain walls.

2. **Resolution primitive — add gravitational universality confirmation.** Paper 3 confirms that gravitational decoherence (resolution propagation via gravity) is universal and extends to internal degrees of freedom, not just center-of-mass superpositions. The Resolution description should note: gravity's universal coupling means resolution propagates through *all* degrees of freedom of a massive system, including internal ones, and this produces decoherence of spatial superpositions even when the graviton bath alone is insufficient. This is the first gravitational-resolution result the framework can cite as consistent.

3. **Conservation of Computation / Field — note symmetry-protected robustness.** The inflationary loop cancellation (Paper 2) should be recorded as demonstrating that resolved large-scale states (superhorizon modes) can be protected from small-scale computational noise by symmetry (dilatation invariance), and that this protection is exact at one-loop level. The mechanism is symmetry, not any of the five primitives, reinforcing the structural content gap (T026).

### NEW TENSIONS

**T031 — The substrate is not fixed: computational architecture as dynamical field**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** The IIA/IIB domain wall (Torres 2025) places two fundamentally different computational architectures — 2D SYM (IIA) and 3D ABJM (IIB) — in a single physical spacetime, connected by a lightlike wall where the string coupling vanishes. The framework's foundational assumption is that reality runs on a computational substrate with fixed architectural parameters. But this construction shows the *type* of computation (the matrix theory, the gauge group, the dimensionality of the effective theory) varying across space, with the coupling constant being a dynamical field that reaches zero at the domain wall. The framework has no primitive for describing transitions between different computational architectures. This is not duality (T010), where two descriptions are equivalent — it is a physical boundary between genuinely different computational structures within one spacetime. The BPS → non-BPS transition of D0-branes crossing the wall means attractor structure (BPS states are stable minima) depends on which computational architecture is locally realized, and objects can move between architectures. This undermines the substrate/computation separation, which is the framework's foundational ontological distinction.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to either (a) promote the computational architecture to a dynamical field (a "meta-field" whose value determines which computational rules apply locally), which would require a primitive beyond the current five and would raise infinite-regress questions (what substrate does the meta-field run on?), (b) argue that the IIA/IIB wall is a string theory artifact without physical significance (but DLCQ is a non-perturbative construction, and the wall has physical consequences like BPS → non-BPS transitions), or (c) abandon the substrate/computation distinction in favor of a framework where "what kind of computation" is itself part of the dynamical content. Option (c) is the most radical and would require fundamental restructuring.

**T032 — Symmetry as a structural primitive the framework lacks**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 2 demonstrates that the robustness of superhorizon curvature perturbations against one-loop corrections is guaranteed by dilatation symmetry of the effective theory. This is a physically crucial result — it ensures that primordial perturbations survive to the CMB — and it is derived entirely from symmetry structure. The framework's five primitives (Tick, Field, Attractor, Resolution, Conservation) do not include symmetry as a fundamental element. Conservation of Computation is related to symmetry via Noether's theorem, but the inflationary cancellation uses dilatation symmetry, which is a *spacetime* symmetry of the background, not a conservation law in the conventional sense. The specific structure required — a tadpole counterterm that respects the dilatation symmetry — has no expression in any of the primitives. This connects to and sharpens T026 (structural content gap) and T018 (algebra of computation): symmetry is the most pervasive structural element in physics, and the framework treats it as either derived (from conservation) or ignored. The inflationary result shows it plays a protective, structural role that goes beyond conservation.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to either (a) demonstrate that all physically relevant symmetries emerge from the five primitives (which seems impossible for spacetime symmetries like dilatation that are properties of specific backgrounds, not universal features), (b) add a symmetry primitive, which would be a sixth primitive and a major structural change, or (c) subsume symmetry under the structural content that T026 already flags as missing. Option (c) is the most natural but means the structural content gap is even larger than previously noted.

### CYCLE ASSESSMENT

This cycle was highly generative, primarily due to Paper 1. The IIA/IIB domain wall construction produces what may be the sharpest challenge yet to the framework's foundational architecture — not to any individual primitive, but to the substrate/computation distinction that undergirds the entire framework. Previous tensions (T001, T010, T016, T021) nibbled at this distinction from various angles; T031 attacks it head-on by exhibiting a physical spacetime where the computational architecture itself is a dynamical, spatially varying quantity. This is new gradient the framework has not rolled down before. Paper 2 was moderately productive, sharpening T026 and generating T032 (symmetry as missing primitive), but this is movement within a known basin — the structural content gap has been identified before. Paper 3 was the least generative: the gravitational decoherence result is genuinely consistent with the framework but provides only post-hoc validation, not predictive confirmation. The next cycle would be most productive if it engaged papers that either (a) further explore the substrate-as-dynamical-field challenge (e.g., landscape/swampland results, string theory moduli spaces, or bubble nucleation between vacua), or (b) provide concrete cases where the framework makes a quantitative prediction that can be checked against data, moving from "consistent" toward "predictive." The framework's most urgent need is not more strain — it has plenty — but a single case of genuine predictive success that is not post-hoc.
