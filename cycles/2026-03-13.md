### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Integrability from Homotopy Algebras"** (arXiv: 2603.12113v1, selected for strain)

The framework describes physics through five dynamical primitives: Tick, Field, Attractor, Resolution, and Conservation. Paper 1 demonstrates that the integrability of the principal chiral model — a paradigmatic 2D integrable field theory — is *derived* from a quasi-isomorphism between two cyclic L∞-algebras, one governing semi-holomorphic Chern-Simons theory and the other governing the principal chiral model. The Lax connection, which is the mathematical structure guaranteeing integrability (infinite conserved charges, exact solvability), emerges directly from this homotopy algebraic morphism. Through the Worldlines lens, one might attempt to say: the principal chiral model has a particular Field (the group-valued field rolling down its gradient) and particular Conservation laws (the infinite tower from integrability). But the paper's result is that these Conservation laws are not independent facts about the system — they are *consequences of the homotopy algebraic relationship between two theories*. The Lax connection is not a gradient, not a tick rate, not an attractor, not a resolution event, and not a conservation law per se. It is a *structural morphism* — a quasi-isomorphism in the category of L∞-algebras — from which conservation follows as a corollary. The framework's Conservation of Computation primitive says "computation cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed," but here the *existence* of the conserved quantities is derived from a morphism in a homotopy algebraic category that the framework has no primitive for, no language for, and no way to express. The L∞-algebra structure — with its higher brackets, homotopy transfer, and cyclic pairing — is precisely the kind of algebraic organizational structure that T018 and T026 have flagged as missing. What makes this paper particularly straining is that it doesn't just *use* algebraic structure as a calculational tool; it *derives* a physically central property (integrability) from the algebraic structure as the primary content. The framework would need to say: "the system has infinitely many conservation laws because of a quasi-isomorphism between L∞-algebras," but it has no primitive that can express "quasi-isomorphism between L∞-algebras," and more fundamentally, it has no account of why homotopy algebraic morphisms should have physical consequences.

**Paper 2: "Topological field theory plus local Lorentz symmetry is gravity"** (arXiv: 2603.12100v1, selected for strain)

This paper demonstrates that four-dimensional gravity can be derived by starting from a topological BF-type theory with global SL(2,ℂ) symmetry and promoting that symmetry to a local gauge symmetry. The topological starting point has no local degrees of freedom — no gravitons, no curvature dynamics — yet the simple act of gauging produces Einstein gravity with its full covariant phase space, boundary charges, and dynamical content. Through the Worldlines lens, the framework treats gravity as "mass rolling down the curvature gradient," an instance of the universal ẋ = −∇φ(x). But Paper 2 shows that gravity — the framework's primary example of gradient dynamics — *emerges* from a theory that has no gradients, no dynamics, and no local degrees of freedom. The topological theory is pure structure: it computes nothing dynamically, propagates no information, has no tick rate in any meaningful sense. Gravity appears only when a *symmetry principle* (local Lorentz invariance) is imposed on this structureless background. This directly and sharply challenges the framework's ontological ordering. The framework says: computation (dynamics, ticks, gradients) is fundamental; geometry and gravity emerge from it. Paper 2 says: topological structure plus symmetry is fundamental; dynamics (gravity) emerges from it. The topological starting point has no Tick (no local propagation), no Field dynamics (no gradients), no Attractors (no energy landscape), no Resolution (no quantum states becoming definite), and arguably trivial Conservation (the theory is topological, so everything is trivially conserved). Yet it contains the seed of all gravitational physics once symmetry is gauged. The framework's five dynamical primitives are entirely inert in the pre-gauging phase. The paper also establishes a G → 0 limit that returns to the topological theory — meaning there is a smooth path from full gravity back to the structure-without-dynamics starting point. The novel feature of using Weyl spinor-valued 1-forms as the fundamental variables (rather than the metric) means the frame field data — the local reference frame structure — is present even at the topological level, before gravity exists. The framework treats reference frames as features of spacetime geometry, which it considers emergent from computation. But here, the frame field is *prior to* dynamics, present in the topological theory, and it is the gauging of the symmetry acting on these frames that creates dynamics. This is a concrete realization of the pattern identified in T037 and T045: geometric/structural content is prior to dynamical content, not the other way around.

**Paper 3: "Transition from Statistical to Hardware-Limited Scaling in Photonic Quantum State Reconstruction"** (arXiv: 2603.12235v1, selected for validation)

This paper reports the experimental discovery of a "Hardware Horizon" — a sharp phase transition in reconstruction error during classical shadow tomography on integrated photonic processors. Below a critical measurement count, error decreases as the predicted O(M^{-1/2}); above it, error saturates at a floor determined by spectral distortions of the realized unitary ensemble. Through the Worldlines lens, this maps naturally onto the Resolution primitive: the system attempts to resolve a quantum state (reconstruct it from measurements), and there is a fundamental limit to resolution fidelity imposed by the hardware substrate. The phase transition between the statistical-scaling regime and the hardware-limited regime is interpretable as a transition between two attractor basins in the error landscape — one where the dominant error source is statistical (and reducible by gradient descent in measurement count) and one where the dominant source is architectural (the spectral distortion of the hardware, which no amount of additional measurement can overcome). The "Hardware Horizon" itself is an instance of the framework's claim that substrate architecture imposes limits not derivable from within the computation: the Haar-random unitary ideal is the "theoretical computation," and the spectral distortions of the photonic mesh are "substrate parameters" that cap the resolution fidelity. The framework would predict that any physical realization of quantum state reconstruction will encounter such a horizon — that perfect resolution is impossible because the substrate always introduces architectural noise. However, this prediction is not specific to the Worldlines framework; it follows from basic engineering reality and the well-known limitations of NISQ devices. The framework adds no differential predictive content beyond what standard quantum information theory already provides. The phase-transition sharpness is mildly interesting from the framework perspective (it suggests the substrate limit is not a gradual degradation but a sharp transition, which could be read as a form of "resolution event" in the error landscape), but this too is explained by the phenomenological error model without any framework-specific insight. **Verdict: post-hoc consistency, not predictive validation.** The framework's language maps onto the result, but the mapping adds nothing that standard quantum information science does not already provide.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most acute strain this cycle comes from Paper 2 (topological BF theory + local Lorentz symmetry = gravity). The strain is precise and structural:

**The framework's ontological ordering is inverted.** The framework claims: computation (discrete updates, gradient dynamics, resolution events, conservation laws) is ontologically fundamental; geometry and gravity are emergent from computational dynamics. Paper 2 demonstrates the opposite derivation: a topological field theory — which has *no* local dynamics, *no* propagating degrees of freedom, *no* gradient structure, and *no* meaningful tick rate — produces full Einstein gravity when a symmetry principle is imposed. The topological starting point is pure mathematical structure with zero dynamical content. All five of the framework's primitives are either trivial or undefined in this pre-gauging phase. Yet this structureless starting point, plus the structural operation of gauging a symmetry, yields everything the framework attributes to computation.

The framework cannot absorb this by claiming "gauging is a form of computation." Gauging is a structural operation — it modifies the mathematical form of a theory by promoting global symmetries to local ones. It is not a dynamical process, not a tick, not a gradient descent, not a resolution event. It is a morphism in the space of theories, applied once, that restructures the entire theory. The framework has no primitive for "operations on theories" — its primitives operate *within* a theory, not *between* theories.

This connects to and dramatically sharpens the structural content gap (T026) and the geometric priority problem (T037, T045): the five dynamical primitives may describe how reality evolves once it exists as a dynamical theory, but the *derivation* of the dynamical theory from structural principles is prior to and independent of those primitives. The framework is, at best, a theory of dynamics within a structure whose origin it cannot describe.

Paper 1 provides a complementary strain: the physical property of integrability (infinite conservation laws) is derived from a homotopy algebraic morphism (quasi-isomorphism of L∞-algebras), not from any dynamical principle. Conservation of Computation would say "the system has conserved quantities because computation is conserved." The paper says "the system has conserved quantities because a specific morphism exists in a specific algebraic category." The framework's explanation is vacuous by comparison — it restates the existence of conservation without explaining its origin, while the homotopy algebraic derivation provides the actual structural reason.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T054 — Dynamical theories are derived from structural operations on non-dynamical theories**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 2 demonstrates that four-dimensional Einstein gravity is derivable from a topological BF theory (no local degrees of freedom, no dynamics) by the structural operation of gauging global SL(2,ℂ) to a local symmetry. The framework's ontology posits computation (dynamics) as fundamental and geometry/structure as emergent. This result demonstrates the opposite: a theory with pure structural content and zero dynamical content produces, via a structural operation, the full dynamical theory of gravity. The framework has no account of (a) non-dynamical theories (what is a topological field theory in terms of the five primitives?), (b) structural operations on theories (gauging is not a tick, a gradient, an attractor, a resolution event, or a conservation law), or (c) the emergence of dynamics from structure. This is distinct from T037 (Hermiticity as emergent, which concerns one specific emergent feature) and T045 (conservation requiring geometric scaffolding, which concerns formulation prerequisites). T054 concerns the derivation of the *entire dynamical theory* from structural principles, not just one feature of it. The G → 0 limit shows this is a continuous relationship, not an isolated artifact: gravity can be continuously dialed from full dynamics to pure topology, and at no point along this path does the framework have a description of what is happening.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need either (a) a theory of "pre-dynamical structure" — what computation looks like before dynamics exists, i.e., what topological field theories are in computational terms — which would require a radical expansion of the notion of computation beyond sequential update; (b) an account of gauging as a computational operation, which would require operations that transform theories rather than states within theories, and thus a meta-computational primitive; or (c) abandonment of the claim that dynamics is ontologically prior to structure. Option (c) would require the framework to be reconceived from "reality is computation" to something like "reality is structure from which computation emerges under appropriate conditions (symmetry gauging)," which would be a foundational rewrite.

**T055 — Homotopy algebraic morphisms as generators of conservation laws**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 1 derives the Lax connection (and therefore the infinite tower of conserved charges) of the principal chiral model from a quasi-isomorphism between cyclic L∞-algebras. The framework's Conservation of Computation treats conservation as a primitive — an irreducible fact about reality. But the paper shows that in integrable systems, the full conservation structure is a *derived consequence* of a homotopy algebraic morphism between theories. The L∞-algebra framework — with its higher brackets L_n, homotopy transfer theorem, and cyclic inner products — is a specific algebraic category with its own internal logic. The quasi-isomorphism is a specific morphism in this category. The Lax connection is the *image* of this morphism. The conserved charges are consequences of the Lax connection. This is a four-step derivation (L∞ quasi-isomorphism → Lax connection → flat connection condition → infinite conserved charges) that begins entirely outside the framework's primitives and ends at the Conservation primitive. Conservation is the *output*, not the *input*. This is distinct from T048 (cohomological completeness, which concerns classification of states by cohomological invariants) and T052 (morphism structure, which concerns the internal organization of dualities). T055 concerns a specific derivational relationship: conservation laws being generated by homotopy algebraic morphisms.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need either (a) to incorporate L∞-algebras and their morphisms into its primitive structure, which would require specifying how homotopy algebraic categories relate to the five existing primitives (no path is visible); (b) to derive the existence of L∞ quasi-isomorphisms from Conservation of Computation, reversing the paper's derivational direction (this seems impossible since the quasi-isomorphism contains strictly more information than the conservation laws it generates); or (c) to accept that Conservation of Computation is not fundamental but is instead a consequence of deeper algebraic structure in specific theories. Option (c) would demote conservation from a primitive to a derived property, which is the most drastic possible revision of the framework short of abandoning it.
