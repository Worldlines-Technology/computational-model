**Paper 1: Holographic Derivation of BPZ-Type Null State Equations in Higher Dimensional CFTs**
ArXiv ID: 2605.04032v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework would narrate this as: a computation running on the gravitational substrate (AdS bulk) produces specific differential equations governing the boundary computation (CFT correlators), with the "decoupling mechanism" at special conformal dimensions being an instance of the computational structure simplifying at particular architectural parameters. The Attractor primitive would describe the black hole background as a deep attractor basin, and the near-boundary expansion as probing the attractor's asymptotic structure. But the paper's actual content strains the framework in a specific and important way: the BPZ-type equations are *structural constraints on the CFT* derived from the bulk geometry. They are not dynamical outputs of a computation — they are differential equations that the correlation functions must satisfy, derived from the condition that certain bulk perturbative series decouple at specific conformal dimensions. The decoupling mechanism is algebraic: at conformal dimensions Δ = d − 2 (and higher cases), the perturbative expansion truncates, isolating lower-order equations. This truncation is a structural/algebraic property of the bulk-boundary map, not a dynamical process. More critically, the paper demonstrates that bulk geometry *derives* boundary algebraic structure (the null-state equations), extending the pattern of T059 (ontological inversion) and T085 (extra-dimensional geometric determination of 4D structure): the CFT's computational constraints are determined by the geometry of the bulk spacetime, not by the CFT's own dynamical content. The "resummed contributions from minimal-twist multi-stress tensor operators" are an infinite class of CFT data organized by a single bulk equation — the framework's gradient equation cannot express this organizational compression, and the framework has no account of how a higher-dimensional geometry determines the complete algebraic structure of a lower-dimensional computation. The extension "beyond the near-lightcone regime" demonstrates that the bulk geometry contains more information about the boundary computation than the leading (lightcone) kinematics suggest, meaning the geometric determination is not merely asymptotic but penetrates into the full kinematic space.

**Paper 2: A Berry-Esseen Bound for Quantum Lattice Systems**
ArXiv ID: 2605.03829v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework would narrate this as: local resolution events (measurements of local observables) on a many-body quantum computation produce statistics that converge to Gaussian form, with the convergence rate governed by the system size and correlation structure. The Resolution primitive describes individual measurements as "unresolved states becoming definite." The result shows that the *collective statistics* of many resolution events converge to a universal (Gaussian) form with a specific, optimal convergence rate O(N^{-1/2} polylog(N)). The framework should be comfortable with this — Gaussian emergence from many independent-ish resolution events is a natural consequence of statistical mechanics. However, the paper strains the framework in a precise way: the convergence rate and its proof depend on structural properties of the quantum state and Hamiltonian that no primitive can express. The proof requires: (a) finite correlation length of the quantum state — an information-theoretic property of the pre-resolution state that the framework's T012 (pre-resolution topology) gestures toward but cannot quantify; (b) the locality structure of the Hamiltonian — a constraint on which degrees of freedom interact with which, extending T070's observation that interaction-network topology determines physics; (c) the polylogarithmic correction factor, which encodes the dimensional and structural details of the lattice. The result is a *universality theorem* — it holds for any local Hamiltonian and any finite-correlation-length state — but the universality is determined by the lattice structure, locality, and correlation properties, not by any dynamical primitive. The framework's Field primitive (gradient dynamics) and Conservation primitive (information preservation) are both operative but insufficient: the gradient equation says nothing about the statistical convergence rate of resolution outcomes, and conservation says nothing about the form of the limiting distribution. The Berry-Esseen bound is a structural constraint on the *collective statistics of resolution events* that is determined by the spatial organization of the computation (lattice geometry, locality, correlation decay), not by any of the five primitives individually or collectively. This is a new type of structural content the framework lacks: not the organization of a single computation's output (T026's existing categories) but the *statistical universality of many resolution outcomes* governed by the spatial structure of the computation.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most important strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's holographic derivation of BPZ-type null-state equations. The paper demonstrates a specific, technically precise instance of a pattern the framework has been accumulating evidence against itself for weeks: **bulk geometry derives boundary computational constraints**. The decoupling mechanism — where at specific conformal dimensions (Δ = d − 2, etc.), the bulk perturbative series truncates and produces isolated differential equations that the boundary CFT correlators must satisfy — is not a dynamical process expressible in any primitive. It is a structural/algebraic property of the AdS/CFT map. The framework claims "reality is literally computation," but the paper shows that the *rules constraining the computation* (the null-state differential equations governing CFT correlators) are derived from the *geometry of a higher-dimensional space* (the AdS black hole background). The computation does not derive the rules; the geometry derives the rules. This is T059 (ontological inversion) in its sharpest higher-dimensional form yet, because:

1. The derived objects (null-state equations) are **complete structural constraints** on the CFT — they determine the form of heavy-light correlators, the structure of multi-stress-tensor contributions, and the resummation of an infinite tower of operators. The framework's Field primitive cannot produce these constraints; the framework's Conservation primitive cannot derive them; no primitive can express what "decoupling at specific conformal dimensions" means.

2. The derivation is **constructive and extends beyond known regimes** — the holographic calculation produces additional CFT equations "beyond the near-lightcone regime" that were not previously known from the CFT side. The geometry contains more information about the boundary computation than the boundary computation knew about itself. If reality is computation, the computation does not know its own constraints; the geometry does.

3. The minimal-twist multi-stress tensor resummation demonstrates that an **infinite class of computational data** (all minimal-twist contributions at all orders) is organized by a single geometric object (the bulk scalar field equation in the black hole background). The framework has no mechanism for a single geometric equation compressing infinite computational data.

The framework cannot account for this. It can narrate ("the bulk is a deeper level of computation that constrains the boundary computation") but this narration inverts the framework's own ontological hierarchy: the bulk's geometric content is prior to and determines the boundary's computational content. The framework claims geometry emerges from computation; the paper demonstrates computation's constraints emerge from geometry.
