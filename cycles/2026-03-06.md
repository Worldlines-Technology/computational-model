### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Can Light Cross a Singularity? Exact Solutions from Analogue Gravity"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.05285v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, a curvature singularity represents a point where the computational substrate's field gradients diverge — an attractor basin of infinite depth, or equivalently, a point where the substrate's resolution capacity breaks down because the curvature (information density) exceeds any finite bound. The framework would predict that singularities are computational boundaries: regions where the tick-resolution machinery cannot operate, where state propagation halts. This paper directly challenges that expectation. The authors find exact, regular solutions to electrodynamic equations on a background containing a strong curvature naked singularity, with bounded power flux *through* the singularity. In the framework's language, electromagnetic computation (field propagation, energy transport) continues uninterrupted across a point where the gravitational computation has become singular. This is deeply problematic. The framework treats the computational substrate as unified — all fields share the same tick, the same substrate, the same resolution mechanism. If the gravitational field has a genuine singularity (infinite gradient, unresolvable state) at a spacetime point, the framework has no mechanism for other computations to proceed normally through that point. The analogue gravity approach (using a fluid model) sidesteps the question of whether this happens in full quantum gravity, but the existence of regular solutions in the classical regime already strains the framework's unified-substrate picture. The framework would need to allow field-specific computational breakdowns — gravity can be singular while electromagnetism remains regular — which requires that different fields can decouple from the substrate's integrity in ways the framework does not currently describe.

**Paper 2: "Scattering amplitudes in dimensionless quadratic gravity coupled to QED"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.05476v1 | Selection type: Strain

This paper computes tree-level scattering amplitudes in "agravity" — a scale-free, renormalizable quadratic gravity theory — coupled to QED, finding a universal ultra-Planckian scaling dσ/dΩ ∝ 1/s. The framework interprets gravitational interactions as mass rolling down curvature gradients. Quadratic gravity modifies the graviton propagator from 1/p² to include 1/p⁴ terms, corresponding to higher-derivative corrections that fundamentally alter how the gravitational computation operates at high energies. In the framework's terms, this is a regime where the substrate's own computational rules change character: the graviton propagator's 1/p⁴ behavior means the gravitational gradient structure is qualitatively different at short distances (ultra-Planckian energies) than at long distances. The universal 1/s scaling of cross sections — shared across all channels (photon-photon, fermion-fermion, scalar-scalar, mixed) — is striking from the framework's perspective. It suggests that at ultra-Planckian energies, the dimensionless gravitational couplings enforce a universal computational throughput constraint: the rate of scattering decreases as energy increases, as if the substrate's capacity to mediate interactions saturates or becomes self-limiting. The framework's treatment of the tick rate (speed of light) as the maximum propagation speed does not anticipate a regime where gravitational computation becomes scale-free and universally self-softening. More critically, agravity's ghost modes (from the 1/p⁴ propagator) are precisely the permanently virtual computational structure already flagged in T014. This paper shows that these ghost modes actively modify physical cross sections through photon-graviton interference — they are not inert but computationally active while remaining (in the Lee-Wick or fakeon prescription) non-asymptotic. The framework must grapple with the fact that permanently virtual structure has measurable physical consequences, strengthening T014.

**Paper 3: "Core-bound waves on a Gross-Pitaevskii vortex"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.05505v1 | Selection type: Validation

This paper discovers two new families of core-bound excitations (varicose and fluting waves) on a quantized vortex in a Gross-Pitaevskii superfluid, with an infinite sequence of branches whose energies lie below the bulk Bogoliubov dispersion. Through the Worldlines lens, a quantized vortex is a topological attractor — a stable configuration maintained not by being an energy minimum but by topological winding number conservation. The core-bound excitations are secondary attractor structures living within the primary topological attractor: the vortex core creates a radial potential well (the density depletion acts as a waveguide), and particles/waves are trapped in this gradient. The infinite sequence of branches maps naturally to quantized attractor sub-basins within the vortex potential. The long-wavelength limits — fluting waves unbinding, varicose waves becoming phonons, only the fundamental Kelvin wave remaining core-bound — describe attractor basin restructuring as a function of wavelength scale: as the wavelength exceeds the healing length, the effective potential well becomes too shallow to support bound states, and the system transitions from the core attractor to the bulk attractor. This is genuinely consistent with the framework's Attractor and Field primitives: the vortex core potential is a real gradient (∇φ where φ is the density-dependent effective potential), the bound states are real gradient-descent minima in the radial direction, and the wavelength-dependent unbinding is real attractor landscape restructuring. However, the predictive purchase is limited. The framework's description is post-hoc: it can narrate the results in its language but did not predict the existence of varicose or fluting modes, their dispersion relations, or the specific unbinding hierarchy. The framework would have predicted "a topological attractor should support bound excitations" — which is correct but generic. The specific physics (infinite branch structure, waveguide interpretation, spectroscopic detection protocol) comes entirely from the Gross-Pitaevskii equation, not from any framework primitive.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain comes from Paper 1. The framework's computational ontology rests on a unified substrate: all fields share the same tick, the same computational volume, the same resolution mechanism. A singularity, in the framework's terms, is where the substrate breaks — where gradients diverge beyond the substrate's capacity to compute. But Paper 1 demonstrates that electromagnetic fields can be *exactly regular* and can *transmit bounded energy flux* through a point where spacetime curvature is singular in the strong sense (not merely a coordinate artifact). This means one field's computation breaks down while another field's computation proceeds without interruption through the same spacetime point.

The framework has no mechanism for this. If the substrate is singular at a point, *all* computation at that point should be affected. If the substrate is not singular (i.e., the curvature singularity is somehow a feature of the gravitational field only, not the substrate), then the framework must explain how the substrate can be intact while one of its fields diverges — which requires fields to have different levels of substrate dependence. This is a kind of "field-specific substrate resilience" that the framework does not describe.

This strain is sharpened by the framework's own dark matter claim: dark matter couples to gravity but not electromagnetism. Paper 1 shows the *inverse* asymmetry: electromagnetism survives where gravity fails. Together, these suggest that the gravitational and electromagnetic fields have fundamentally different relationships to the substrate — one can be singular while the other is regular, one can couple to certain structure while the other does not — and the framework's unified-substrate picture cannot accommodate this asymmetry without adding a theory of field-specific substrate coupling. The framework's "partial coupling" language for dark matter was already flagged as post-hoc (T004, T020); Paper 1 suggests the problem is deeper: not just "different fields couple differently" but "different fields have different survival conditions under substrate stress."

Paper 2 adds a secondary strain by showing that ghost modes — permanently virtual computational structure (T014) — actively contribute to physical observables through interference terms. The framework's silence on virtual structure is not merely an incompleteness but an active gap: the ghost-photon interference terms in the cross sections are measurable, meaning the permanently virtual structure has physical consequences that any complete framework must predict.

### STATE UPDATES

1. **Substrate claim — add field-specific resilience problem.** The model should record that the unified-substrate assumption (all fields share the same computational substrate and are equally affected by substrate-level pathologies) is challenged by the existence of regular electromagnetic solutions through curvature singularities. The substrate is either non-uniform in its relationship to different fields, or curvature singularities are not substrate-level pathologies but field-specific breakdowns within an intact substrate. Either way, the model needs to distinguish between "substrate singular" (all computation fails) and "field singular" (one field's computation diverges while others remain regular). The model currently has no such distinction.

2. **T014 (permanently virtual structure) — strengthen with quantitative evidence.** The model should record that permanently virtual ghost modes in quadratic gravity produce measurable photon-graviton interference terms in scattering cross sections (Cunha & Lehum 2025). This is not merely a theoretical curiosity — the interference terms modify the angular distribution and energy scaling of all scattering channels. Permanently virtual structure is computationally active with observable consequences, meaning the framework cannot treat it as ontologically inert.

3. **Attractor primitive — confirm topological attractor sub-structure.** The model should record that topological attractors (quantized vortices) support hierarchically organized families of core-bound excitations, with an infinite branch structure determined by the attractor's radial gradient profile. The wavelength-dependent unbinding of modes represents attractor basin restructuring controlled by the ratio of excitation wavelength to healing length. This is post-hoc consistent with the Attractor primitive but does not constitute predictive validation.

4. **Field primitive — note universal ultra-Planckian scaling as potential substrate signature.** The universal dσ/dΩ ∝ 1/s scaling across all scattering channels in agravity should be recorded as a potential signature of substrate-level computational throughput limits at ultra-Planckian energies. If confirmed, this scaling reflects a regime where the dimensionless character of the gravitational coupling produces self-similar scattering — a scale-free computational process — which the framework might interpret as the substrate's behavior in its maximally stressed regime. However, this is speculative and the scaling may simply reflect the dimensional analysis of a renormalizable theory.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T038 — Field-specific substrate resilience: singularities are not universal**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paez, Fiorini & Hernández (2603.05285) demonstrate exact, regular electromagnetic solutions with bounded power flux through a strong curvature naked singularity. The framework's unified-substrate assumption implies that a singularity — where curvature gradients diverge — should disrupt all computation at that spacetime point. But electromagnetism survives where gravity fails. This means either (a) the substrate is intact and the curvature singularity is a pathology of the gravitational field only, not the substrate; (b) the substrate is singular but different fields have different resilience thresholds; or (c) the framework's identification of curvature singularities with substrate breakdowns is wrong. Option (a) requires explaining how a field can diverge on an intact substrate — what does it mean for one field's gradient to be infinite while the substrate's computational capacity is finite? Option (b) requires a theory of field-specific substrate coupling that the framework does not have. Option (c) would require a new account of what singularities are in computational terms. This tension is related to T004/T020 (dark matter partial coupling) and T031 (substrate as dynamical field) but is distinct: T004/T020 concern coupling *strengths* to different gradients; T031 concerns spatial variation of the computational architecture; T038 concerns the survival of computation through points where the substrate is ostensibly broken. It is the first tension that directly challenges the framework's assumption that the substrate's computational integrity is either intact or broken for all fields simultaneously.
**Why unresolved:** The framework's entire architecture assumes a single substrate whose state affects all fields equally. Allowing field-specific substrate resilience would require specifying what couples each field to the substrate and at what threshold the coupling fails. This is a deeper version of the "partial coupling" problem: not just "which gradients does the structure couple to?" but "at what point does the substrate's integrity matter for each field?" The framework has no resources to address this because the substrate is, by design, the one thing about which it cannot make internal statements (T001). But Paper 1 forces the issue: either the substrate has field-specific structure (contradicting its role as a uniform ground), or singularities are not substrate-level events (contradicting the framework's interpretation of them).

**T039 — Permanently virtual structure has measurable consequences: the ontological status of ghost modes**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Cunha & Lehum (2603.05476) compute photon-graviton interference contributions to scattering cross sections from ghost modes in quadratic gravity. These modes never appear as asymptotic states (they are permanently virtual, as described in T014) but their virtual contributions modify all scattering channels' angular distributions and energy scaling. The framework's Resolution primitive defines reality through resolution — what becomes definite. Conservation of Computation conserves the computational content of resolvable states. Ghost modes challenge both: they never resolve (no definite ghost particle states) but they measurably affect what does resolve (cross sections of photons and matter). The framework must choose: (i) ghost modes are "real" computation that happens to never resolve — but then Conservation of Computation must account for computational content that is permanently virtual, and the framework needs to specify what "conservation" means for content that never becomes definite; (ii) ghost modes are not "real" but their effects on real processes are — but this is precisely the instrumentalist position the framework claims to reject by insisting reality *is* computation; (iii) ghost modes are part of the substrate's internal bookkeeping that leaks into observable physics — but this violates the substrate/computation opacity that the framework asserts. This sharpens T014 by adding quantitative evidence (specific cross-section modifications) and connects to T033 (analyticity bridging IR/UV) in that virtual modes are a mechanism by which non-observable structure affects observable physics.
**Why unresolved:** The framework's ontology is resolution-based: real = resolved (or resolvable). Permanently virtual structure with measurable consequences breaks this criterion. The framework cannot classify ghost modes as real (they don't resolve) or unreal (they have effects) without either expanding its ontology beyond resolution or accepting an instrumentalist stance it claims to reject. The gauge-fixing independence verified in the paper rules out the easy escape that these are gauge artifacts.
