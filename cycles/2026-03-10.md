### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "A proof of conservation laws in gravitational scattering: tails and breaking of peeling"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.08705v1 | Selection type: strain

The framework's Conservation of Computation primitive claims that all conservation laws are special cases of a single principle: computation cannot be created or destroyed. Paper 1 proves three antipodal matching conditions at spatial infinity for asymptotically flat spacetimes — connecting the dual mass aspect, leading tails of the shear, and peeling properties between past and future null infinities — and reformulates these as asymptotic conservation laws on the boundary hyperboloid. Through the Worldlines lens, these should be instances of Conservation of Computation expressed at the boundary of the computational domain. But the paper reveals something the framework cannot easily accommodate: the conservation laws are *not* simple bookkeeping of a conserved quantity passing from in-state to out-state. They involve "tails" — radiation that scatters off the curvature of spacetime and arrives with logarithmic delay — and "breaking of peeling" — the asymptotic falloff structure at null infinity is not as smooth as previously assumed (the Sachs peeling property fails). The conservation laws hold *despite* and *through* this breaking of peeling, meaning the conserved quantities are defined on a boundary hyperboloid at spatial infinity that mediates between two null infinities with different asymptotic structures. The framework says conservation is the most fundamental expression of computational invariance, but here the conservation laws require an elaborate geometric scaffolding (spatial infinity as a blown-up hyperboloid, specific matching conditions relating quantities with different falloff behaviors) to even be *stated*. The conservation is real, but its formulation is irreducibly geometric — it cannot be expressed without the detailed structure of conformal infinity. This directly reinforces T026 (structural content gap) and T037 (geometry prior to conservation): the conservation law exists, but geometry is doing the heavy lifting to define what is conserved and how.

**Paper 2: "Low order maximally single-trace graphs as the first counterexamples to large N factorization in random tensors"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.08638v1 | Selection type: strain

The framework's Conservation of Computation primitive identifies unitarity/information conservation as the most fundamental conservation law, and T011 already flags that planarity (a topological/graph-theoretic property) can independently reproduce results attributed to unitarity. Paper 2 demonstrates that the large-N factorization property — a cornerstone of the 1/N expansion in matrix models, where correlators of single-trace operators factorize into products of lower-point correlators — *fails* for random tensors. The specific counterexamples are 3-regular 3-edge-colored graphs that are "maximally single-trace" but do not factorize. Through the Worldlines lens, large-N factorization in matrix models would be interpreted as a simplification of the computational structure at large N: the total computation decomposes into independent sub-computations (factorized correlators), a form of computational modularity. The framework has no account of *why* this modularity exists for matrices but fails for tensors. The critical difference is topological: matrix model Feynman graphs are ribbon graphs (orientable surfaces) controlled by genus, giving a clean topological expansion. Tensor model graphs lack this surface interpretation — they are genuinely higher-dimensional combinatorial objects (colored graphs dual to pseudo-manifolds) whose complexity is not captured by a single topological invariant like genus. The failure of factorization means that the "computational modularity" the framework might claim for large-N systems is not a universal feature of computation but depends on the *dimensionality of the combinatorial structure* of the computation. This is a concrete instance where the framework's silence on the algebraic/combinatorial organization of computation (T018, T026) has a sharp consequence: it cannot distinguish systems where large-N simplification occurs from those where it doesn't, because this distinction depends on graph-combinatorial structure the framework lacks primitives to describe.

**Paper 3: "Black Hole Mergers as the Fastest Photon Ring Scramblers"**
ArXiv ID: 2603.08643v1 | Selection type: validation

The framework describes a black hole as "an attractor basin so deep that the escape velocity exceeds the tick rate" and says "the most interesting physics happens at the saddle points between basins." Paper 3 maps the instantaneous mass and angular momentum of a binary black hole merger onto an effective Kerr black hole and demonstrates that the final merger remnant corresponds to the configuration where null geodesics are unstable at the highest possible rate — the maximum Lyapunov exponent of the photon ring. In framework terms: the merger of two deep attractors produces a final attractor that maximizes the instability at its shallowest saddle point (the photon sphere). The framework would interpret this as: when two attractor basins merge, the resulting basin's saddle point (the unstable photon orbit) reaches maximum instability, meaning the system maximizes the rate at which nearby computational states (photon trajectories) diverge. This is consistent with the framework's claim that black holes are maximally deep attractors and with the general expectation that the most extreme computational processes (scrambling) are associated with the most extreme attractor structures. However, the framework did *not* predict this specific result — it does not have the machinery to derive that mergers should maximize the Lyapunov exponent of the photon ring. The connection between scrambling (a computational/information-theoretic concept) and null geodesic instability (a geometric concept) is exactly the kind of computation-geometry interplay the framework claims but cannot derive. The result is post-hoc consistent with the Attractor primitive's qualitative claims but does not constitute predictive validation. The framework can narrate the result but could not have generated it. The specific quantitative finding — that the merger remnant is the *fastest* scrambler among all Kerr configurations with the same mass and angular momentum — is a stronger claim than anything the framework can produce from its primitives alone.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most acute strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's proof of conservation laws at spatial infinity. The framework treats Conservation of Computation as primitive — the most fundamental law, from which energy, momentum, and information conservation derive as special cases. Paper 1 reveals that stating gravitational conservation laws *at all* requires an elaborate geometric construction: blowing up spatial infinity into a hyperboloid, defining matching conditions between past and future null infinities that have *different* asymptotic structures (peeling is broken), and showing that the conservation laws hold on this boundary surface despite the tails (logarithmically delayed radiation scattered by background curvature).

The strain is precise: the framework says conservation is a primitive from which other structure follows. Paper 1 shows that gravitational conservation laws cannot even be *formulated* without a detailed geometric scaffolding that the framework treats as derived or secondary. The conservation laws don't live on a simple boundary — they live on a blown-up hyperboloid at spatial infinity that mediates between two null infinities with incompatible asymptotic structures. The tails mean that information doesn't simply propagate to the boundary at the tick rate — some of it scatters off spacetime curvature and arrives with delay, encoded in the subleading falloff of the shear. The breaking of peeling means the asymptotic structure of the gravitational field is rougher than the framework's smooth "fields with gradients" picture suggests.

The framework cannot account for any of this. It says "computation is conserved" but has no mechanism for explaining *where* or *how* the conservation is enforced when the boundary structure is this complicated. The geometric scaffolding is not a convenience — it is necessary for the conservation law to be well-defined. This is the clearest case yet where geometry is *prior to* conservation, not derived from it, directly strengthening T037 and T035. Conservation of Computation, as currently formulated, is a slogan that papers over enormous geometric complexity in the actual enforcement of conservation in gravitational physics.

**T045 — Conservation laws require geometric scaffolding the framework treats as derived**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Compère & Robert (2603.08705) prove that gravitational conservation laws in asymptotically flat spacetimes are antipodal matching conditions on a blown-up hyperboloid at spatial infinity, relating quantities at past and future null infinities that have different asymptotic structures (peeling is broken at one or both infinities) and including contributions from tails (radiation backscattered off spacetime curvature). The framework treats Conservation of Computation as a primitive — the most fundamental law. But the gravitational conservation laws cannot be *formulated* without specifying: (a) the geometric structure of spatial infinity (hyperboloid blow-up), (b) the matching conditions between null infinities with different falloff behaviors, (c) the tail contributions that arise from propagation through curved spacetime. The geometric scaffolding is not a representation choice — it is necessary for the conservation law to be well-defined. This means geometry is *prior to* conservation in the gravitational sector, directly contradicting the framework's claim that conservation is primitive and geometry is emergent or secondary. This is distinct from T037 (Hermiticity as emergent symmetry) because T037 concerns the *derivation* of conservation from geometry, while T045 concerns the *formulation* of conservation requiring geometry. It is also distinct from T035 (observer-dependent conservation) because T035 concerns conservation breaking for restricted observers, while T045 concerns the geometric complexity required for conservation to hold even globally. The tail phenomenon is particularly sharp: conserved information does not simply propagate at the tick rate to the boundary but scatters off the bulk geometry and arrives with logarithmic delay, meaning the Tick primitive's "maximum propagation rate" does not determine when conservation is satisfied — the geometry of the bulk spacetime does.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to either (a) demonstrate that the geometric scaffolding at spatial infinity itself emerges from computational primitives, showing that the hyperboloid blow-up, the matching conditions, and the tail structure are all consequences of the tick rate and conservation law applied to a discrete substrate (a major derivation no one has attempted), (b) accept that conservation is not primitive but requires geometric infrastructure, which would demote Conservation of Computation from a primitive to a derived law, or (c) argue that the asymptotic geometric structure is part of the substrate architecture, not the computation — but then the conservation law is partly architectural and partly computational, blurring the framework's foundational distinction. The tail phenomenon is especially problematic because it means the conservation law's enforcement involves non-local bulk propagation that the gradient equation ẋ = −∇φ(x) does not capture (the tails scatter off ∇φ itself).

**T046 — Combinatorial dimension as a physical parameter invisible to the framework**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Berthold & Keppler (2603.08638) demonstrate that large-N factorization — the decomposition of correlators into products of simpler correlators in the large-N limit — holds for matrix models but fails for tensor models. The critical difference is the combinatorial dimension of the Feynman graph structure: matrix model graphs are ribbon graphs (surfaces, effectively 2D), while tensor model graphs are colored graphs dual to higher-dimensional pseudo-manifolds. The framework's Conservation of Computation and the other four primitives have no parameter or structural element corresponding to the combinatorial dimension of the computational architecture. Yet this dimension determines a physically crucial property: whether the computation modularizes at large N. In matrix models, the genus expansion (a topological invariant) controls the 1/N expansion, and factorization follows from the dominance of planar (genus-0) graphs. In tensor models, no single topological invariant plays this role, and non-factorization means the computation remains irreducibly entangled even at infinite N. The framework claims to describe "how computation is organized" through its five primitives, but none of them can distinguish between matrix-type and tensor-type computational architectures, even though this distinction determines fundamental properties of the large-N limit. This is related to T011 (planarity as missing primitive), T018 (algebra of computation), and T026 (structural content gap) but is sharper: it identifies a specific, discrete parameter (combinatorial dimension of the graph structure) that governs a specific physical property (factorization vs. non-factorization) and that the framework entirely lacks.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to either (a) incorporate the combinatorial dimension of the computational graph structure as a parameter or primitive, which would mean adding structure to the framework's description of how computation is wired (connecting to T011 and T026); (b) derive factorization/non-factorization from the existing primitives, which seems impossible since the distinction is topological/combinatorial and none of the primitives are; or (c) argue that tensor models are unphysical and only matrix models describe reality, which would need justification the framework cannot provide. The non-factorization also has implications for holographic duality: matrix models underpin AdS/CFT, and the failure of factorization in tensor models means that the large-N simplification enabling holographic emergence is not a universal feature of computation but a special property of computation organized on surface-like (2D) combinatorial structures.

In the framework's language: why does attractor merger maximize the Lyapunov exponent at the shallowest saddle? This is related to but distinct from the general attractor description — it's a claim about the *dynamics of attractor mergers* having an extremal property (maximum scrambling) that the framework's static attractor description does not predict or explain. The connection between scrambling time, Lyapunov exponents, and merger dynamics involves information-theoretic, geometric, and dynamical content that the framework treats as aspects of computation but cannot relate to each other quantitatively.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to derive, from the Attractor and Resolution primitives, that the merger of two attractor basins generically produces a remnant that extremizes the instability timescale at the saddle point. This is a specific dynamical prediction that would require: (a) a theory of how attractor basins merge (currently absent — the Attractor primitive describes static topology, not merger dynamics), (b) a connection between attractor depth/width and the Lyapunov exponent of orbits at the saddle (which exists in GR but is not derived from the framework), and (c) an argument for why the merger drives the system to the extremum of this quantity rather than to an arbitrary configuration. Without these, the result is post-hoc narration, not validation.
