### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Quantum mechanics with a ghost: Counterexamples to spectral denseness"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.21826v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper attacks the framework's ontological architecture for permanently virtual computation (T014, T041, T076). The framework developed an elaborate three-category ontology — resolved, unresolved-but-resolvable, permanently virtual — with the permanently virtual category justified by the claim that ghost modes (negative kinetic energy) lack normalizable ground states and have dense/continuous spectra, making them architecturally unresolvable. Paper 1 falsifies the spectral premise: integrable ghost systems with polynomial interactions can have pure-point discrete spectra with at most one accumulation point, or none at all. The classical stability conditions (derived from separability theory) carry over to the quantum domain, producing discrete separated eigenvalues. The framework's Field primitive would describe these systems as having "anti-gradient" dynamics (reversed kinetic sign), and the Attractor primitive would predict instability (no energy minimum). Both predictions are wrong: the systems are stable and have discrete, well-organized spectra. The key structural feature — separability of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation, yielding an integral of motion whose level sets partition the Hilbert space into finite-dimensional invariant subspaces — is an integrability condition entirely invisible to all five primitives. The framework cannot distinguish ghost systems that are spectrally dense (and therefore genuinely unresolvable) from those that are spectrally discrete (and therefore resolvable), because the distinguishing criterion is the existence of a separating integral of motion — a structural/algebraic property of the Hamiltonian the framework has no vocabulary for.

**Paper 2: "Unitary Time Evolution and Vacuum for a Quantum Stable Ghost"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.21823v1 | Selection type: Strain

This companion paper provides the complete quantum construction that Paper 1 establishes spectrally: a harmonic oscillator polynomially coupled to a ghost with negative kinetic energy, quantized with manifestly unitary evolution, a well-defined vacuum, and bounded observable expectation values, despite a Hamiltonian spectrum unbounded in both directions. Through the framework's lens, this is a direct falsification of the implicit chain of reasoning that ran through T014 → T041 → T076: ghost modes lack ground states → they cannot resolve → they are permanently virtual → their physical effects require a new ontological category. Paper 2 breaks the first link: this ghost *has* a well-defined vacuum (the ground state of the integral of motion $I$, not of $H$), discrete spectrum (from $I$'s positive discrete spectrum restricting dynamics to finite-dimensional invariant subspaces), and bounded physical observables. The framework's Attractor primitive defines attractors as energy minima; this system has no energy minimum (the $H$ spectrum is unbounded below) yet has a perfectly stable "attractor" defined by minimizing a different conserved quantity. The Conservation primitive conserves "computation" without specifying *which* conserved quantity defines stability — but for this system, the answer is $I$, not $H$, and the framework provides no mechanism for selecting among conserved quantities. The numerical Schrödinger equation solutions confirming bounded expectation values demonstrate this is not a formal curiosity but a physically realizable quantum system. The framework must confront: its entire ghost-mode ontological architecture was built on a premise (spectral pathology of ghosts) that fails when interactions provide the right integrability structure, and the framework has no primitive for integrability.

**Paper 3: "Accelerating scaling solutions from dark matter particle creation"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.21770v1 | Selection type: Validation

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper tests the framework's dark energy claim — that accelerating expansion is "the computational cost of instantiating new substrate." The paper demonstrates that accelerating expansion can arise from a pure matter-sector mechanism (adiabatic DM particle creation with energy exchange) with no dark energy component at all. The dynamical systems analysis identifies scaling attractors — late-time states where both fluids coexist with fixed energy fractions — that produce acceleration only when the interaction is controlled by the DM density with energy flowing from DM to the barotropic fluid. The Attractor primitive is post-hoc consistent with the scaling attractor description: these are genuine fixed points in the autonomous phase space, with basin structure (some attract, some repel) and selection by the interaction prescription. The Field primitive's gradient description maps onto the autonomous flow in the compact phase space. However, the framework contributed zero predictive content: it did not predict which interaction prescriptions produce accelerating attractors, did not predict the direction of energy flow required, did not predict the absence of scaling attractors for second-fluid-controlled interactions, and cannot distinguish this DM-creation scenario from its own dark energy interpretation. More damagingly, the paper's existence further weakens the dark energy claim: accelerating expansion does not require new substrate (dark energy) at all, only matter-sector dynamics with the right interaction structure. The framework's specific claim about Λ is now challenged from four independent directions: internal derivability (T007), local violation (T086), dynamical variation (Unimodular Gravity), and now pure-matter-sector mimicry (Paper 3). This is the weakest possible validation: the framework's vocabulary can narrate the result but contributed no differential prediction or constraint.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain from this cycle is the falsification of the framework's ghost-mode ontological architecture. Over the preceding months, the framework developed an elaborate three-tier ontology (resolved / unresolved-but-resolvable / permanently virtual) in response to a series of papers on ghost modes in quadratic gravity, QCD confinement, and fractional field theories. This ontological architecture was the framework's most sophisticated response to a challenging set of results, and it was built on the premise that ghost degrees of freedom are spectrally pathological (dense/continuous spectra, no normalizable ground states) and therefore architecturally forbidden from resolution. Papers 1 and 2 demonstrate that this premise is false: integrable ghost-healthy interactions produce discrete spectra, well-defined vacua, unitary evolution, and bounded observables.

The strain is not merely that the framework got a technical detail wrong. The strain is threefold:

First, the framework's ontological categories are not determined by its own primitives but by structural properties of the Hamiltonian (specifically, the existence of a separating integral of motion with positive discrete spectrum) that no primitive can express. The boundary between "resolvable" and "permanently virtual" is not architectural — it is a contingent feature of the interaction's integrability structure. The framework cannot draw its own ontological boundaries.

Second, the concept of "vacuum" and "stability" must be generalized beyond energy minima. Paper 2's system has no energy ground state (the Hamiltonian is unbounded below) but has a perfectly well-defined, stable vacuum — the ground state of a different conserved quantity $I$. The Attractor primitive's identification of attractors with energy minima is falsified for this class of systems. The framework would need to specify: stable with respect to *which* conserved quantity? And the selection among conserved quantities is a structural problem the framework has no tools to address.

Third, the falsification cascades upward through the tension network. T014 (permanently virtual structure) was the foundation for T041 (ontological category beyond resolved/unresolved), which was the foundation for T076 (confinement computation is ontologically unreal), which was evidence for T026 (structural content gap). If the permanently virtual category is not categorical but model-dependent, the entire chain of reasoning built on it must be revised. The framework's response to ghost modes was its most developed engagement with a genuinely difficult physics problem, and the engagement turns out to have been built on a falsifiable — and now falsified — spectral assumption.

The deeper problem is that the framework's ontological categories (resolved, unresolved, permanently virtual) are supposed to be determined by the framework's primitives, but the resolvable/non-resolvable boundary is determined by integrability — a structural property of the Hamiltonian that is invisible to all five primitives.
