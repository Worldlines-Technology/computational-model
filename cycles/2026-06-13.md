**Paper 1: Semi-Device-Independent Certification for Nonlocality without Entanglement**
ArXiv ID: 2606.13667v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, the Resolution primitive describes the process by which unresolved quantum states become definite outcomes, with the framework's ontology locating nonclassicality in the pre-resolution state — superposition, entanglement, and the topology of indistinguishability (T012) are properties of the unresolved computation. Paper 1 directly contradicts this localization. It demonstrates that ensembles of fully separable (zero entanglement, no superposition across parties) quantum states can exhibit nonlocal advantages when subjected to global measurements versus separable measurements, specifically at the level of maximum-confidence discrimination. The states carry no nonclassicality by any standard measure; the quantum advantage resides entirely in the measurement structure — the global POVM that cannot be decomposed into local operations and classical communication. The framework's Resolution primitive treats resolution as an undifferentiated process ("unresolved → definite") and has no parameter distinguishing measurement types. It cannot express why a global measurement on product states outperforms a local measurement on the same states, because the states are identical and the framework assigns all relevant physics to the state. Furthermore, the paper reveals that "resolution" is not a single operation but a family of strategies (minimum-error, unambiguous, maximum-confidence) with distinct figures of merit, and the quantum/classical gap can appear at one level (confidence) while being absent at another — a strategy-dependent nonclassicality that the binary Resolution primitive cannot represent. The semi-device-independent certification protocol treats the measurement's own structure as a physical quantity to be experimentally determined, meaning the resolution process has certifiable internal structure the framework cannot express.

**Paper 2: One-loop five-point gluing analytically**
ArXiv ID: 2606.13665v1 | Selection type: Strain

The framework would describe the computation of the one-loop five-point stress tensor correlator in $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM as a specific instance of computational content being evaluated — the Field primitive's gradient dynamics determining propagation, Conservation ensuring unitarity, and the Tick governing the sequential accumulation of loop corrections. Paper 2 demolishes this picture. The complete analytic evaluation proceeds through a chain entirely outside all five primitives: integrability (hexagonalization/gluing framework from the integrable spin chain underlying $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM) → finite-size corrections as residue series → resummation into Euler integrals → evaluation via intersection theory for generalized hypergeometric functions on moduli spaces of punctured spheres. The intersection numbers — cohomological pairings of twisted differential forms on configuration spaces — are the mathematical engine that selects correct analytic continuations and produces the final integrated expressions. These are not auxiliary mathematical devices; they are the computational procedure that generates the physical answer. The framework has no primitive, parameter, or vocabulary for intersection theory, twisted cohomology, moduli spaces, or the integrability-based gluing formalism. The "computation" of this observable is entirely structural/cohomological, with no tick, no gradient, no attractor, no resolution event, and no conservation law participating in the evaluation. The ontological inversion (T059) is complete: the physical content is generated by algebraic/geometric structure, not by dynamical computation. The framework can narrate the result after the fact ("the computation produced this correlator") but contributed zero predictive, constructive, or constraining content to the evaluation.

**Paper 3: Cosmological Dynamics of the Thermal Scalar Near the Hagedorn Temperature**
ArXiv ID: 2606.13611v1 | Selection type: Validation

The framework would describe the thermal scalar as a field moving on a potential landscape, with the Hagedorn transition representing attractor landscape restructuring. Below the Hagedorn temperature, the static configurations where the thermal scalar balances shifted dilaton evolution are interpretable as saddle points (boundary states, not attractors — the paper explicitly states this), consistent with the Attractor primitive's distinction between basins and saddle points. The tachyonic regime above Hagedorn temperature — where the thermal scalar generates negative effective energy density while preserving the null energy condition — is describable as an inverted-potential regime. The branch-change dynamics (Brustein-Veneziano type) are consistent with the framework's description of transitions between attractor basins. The paper's conclusion that the quadratic effective theory cannot resolve the graceful exit problem and that higher-order interactions are essential is consistent with the Attractor primitive's implicit claim that landscape restructuring requires the full nonlinear structure of the potential. However, this is post-hoc consistency of the weakest kind: the framework contributed no prediction about the thermal scalar's dynamics, no constraint on the phase space structure, no derivation of the branch-change conditions, and no mechanism for the Hagedorn transition itself. The paper's observation that the thermal scalar originates as a Euclidean order parameter — requiring Wick rotation to Lorentzian signature for dynamical treatment — directly connects to T053/T036 (the framework's inability to describe computation in Euclidean signature or across signature changes). The framework can apply its vocabulary (saddle point, landscape restructuring, branch change) but this vocabulary adds nothing beyond what the paper's own field-theoretic analysis provides.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's demonstration that nonclassicality can reside entirely in the measurement structure rather than the pre-measurement state. The framework's entire ontological architecture assigns quantum weirdness to pre-resolution states: superposition is "unresolved computation," entanglement is correlation structure in the unresolved state, and the Resolution primitive is the process that collapses this unresolved structure into definite outcomes. NLWE at the confidence level proves this assignment is wrong in general. Fully separable states — which the framework would classify as already resolved or as trivially unresolved (product states with no entanglement, no path indistinguishability) — exhibit genuinely nonclassical advantages when subjected to global measurements. The nonclassicality is constitutively in the resolution process itself, not in the state being resolved. This is not merely a gap (the framework is silent) but a mislocation (the framework actively attributes nonclassicality to the wrong object). The framework's binary Resolution primitive ("unresolved → definite") cannot represent the strategy-dependence of the quantum advantage: the same state-measurement pair produces a quantum/classical gap at the confidence level but may not at the success-probability level. Resolution is not one thing; it is a family of operationally distinct strategies, each with its own nonclassicality profile.

Paper 2 compounds this with a different strain: the complete evaluation of a fundamental physical observable requires intersection theory on moduli spaces — a cohomological operation on configuration spaces of punctured Riemann spheres. This adds "intersection theory / twisted cohomology on moduli spaces" to the growing catalogue of mathematical categories the framework cannot express. The specific strain is that the intersection numbers are not auxiliary or optional; they are the computational engine producing the physical answer. The framework's claim that "reality is literally computation" is contradicted by a result where the "computation" of the physical observable is performed entirely by cohomological machinery that has no dynamical, temporal, or sequential character.

**T174 — Measurement-sourced nonclassicality: the Resolution primitive mislocates the quantum advantage**

Opened: this cycle

Description: Lee & Bae (2606.13667) demonstrate that global quantum measurements on ensembles of *separable* (unentangled) states outperform separable measurements in maximum-confidence discrimination, establishing nonlocality without entanglement (NLWE) at the confidence level. The framework's Resolution primitive and its supporting ontological architecture assign nonclassicality to the pre-resolution state: superposition and entanglement are properties of the "unresolved computation," and resolution is the process by which this unresolved state becomes definite. NLWE at the confidence level demonstrates that the quantum advantage can reside entirely in the measurement structure (global vs. LOCC), with the state being fully classical (separable, zero entanglement). The framework mislocates the source of nonclassicality.

This is distinct from T012 (pre-resolution topology, which concerns the topology of path-indistinguishability in superposed states) because the states here have no superposition or path-indistinguishability — they are separable product states, and the nonclassicality is in the joint measurement, not in any interference structure. It is distinct from T119 (spurious correlations from Simpson's paradox, which concerns correlations that are artifacts of ensemble averaging) because NLWE correlations are *genuine* advantages of global measurements, not statistical artifacts. It is distinct from T132 (witness category gap, which concerns the type of evidence certifying resolution) because T174 concerns the *operational advantage* of global resolution over local resolution for fully classical states, not the certification of outcomes.

The framework faces: (a) its state-centric ontology of nonclassicality is wrong for NLWE — the nonclassical resource is in the measurement, not the state; (b) the maximum-confidence framework reveals that "resolution" is a family of strategies with distinct operational meanings, and the quantum/classical gap is strategy-dependent — visible at the confidence level but potentially invisible at the success-probability level; (c) the semi-device-independent certification protocol treats the measurement *type* as the quantity to be certified from observed outcomes, meaning the resolution process's own structure is experimentally accessible and physically meaningful — the framework has no mechanism for this; (d) the connection to non-unit detection efficiency means this is experimentally accessible with present-day technology, not merely theoretical.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) extend the Resolution primitive to include the measurement structure (global vs. LOCC) as a physical parameter that independently contributes nonclassical advantages, fundamentally changing the primitive from "states become definite" to "states become definite via measurement structures with their own nonclassical content"; (b) relocate nonclassicality from the state to the state-measurement pair, requiring a joint primitive; (c) argue that NLWE is a special case — but the paper demonstrates it for arbitrary ensembles of separable states with the right structure, meaning it is generic; or (d) accept that the framework's Resolution primitive is descriptively incomplete for the full structure of quantum measurement.
