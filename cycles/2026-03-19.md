### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Non-linear instability of the Kerr Cauchy horizon near $i_+$"** (arXiv 2603.17911v1, selected for strain)

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper addresses the interior structure of the framework's deepest attractor basin — the black hole. The framework describes a black hole as "an attractor basin so deep that the escape velocity exceeds the tick rate." The Cauchy horizon is the boundary beyond which deterministic computation breaks down — it is where the framework's Resolution primitive faces a fundamental crisis, because beyond the Cauchy horizon, initial data no longer uniquely determines the future. Strong Cosmic Censorship (SCC) is the conjecture that this breakdown is pathological: the spacetime cannot be smoothly extended past the Cauchy horizon, so the indeterminate region is physically inaccessible. Gurriaran proves a Lipschitz version of SCC for Kerr: the Cauchy horizon exists as a piece of null boundary, curvature blows up there, and the metric cannot be extended with Lipschitz regularity. The framework should welcome this as a structural result — the attractor's interior pathology is real but quarantined, preserving the global coherence of resolution. However, the strain is precise and sharp: the result relies on nonlinear Price's law decay (a specific quantitative rate at which perturbations die off along the event horizon toward timelike infinity $i_+$), which is a *dynamical* result about the propagation of perturbations in the black hole exterior that the framework's gradient equation cannot derive or even express. The curvature blow-up at the Cauchy horizon is the *consequence* of a delicate interplay between the decay rate of perturbations (Price's law), the blue-shift instability at the inner horizon, and the global causal structure of the Kerr interior — all of which involve the propagator/Green's function structure (T043, T066) that the gradient equation is blind to. Furthermore, the Lipschitz inextendibility criterion (Sbierski's result) is a *regularity class* statement: the metric is continuous ($C^0$ stable, per Dafermos-Luk) but not Lipschitz-extendible. The framework's Resolution primitive, which describes states becoming "definite," has no concept of regularity classes — it cannot distinguish between $C^0$ and Lipschitz extensions, yet this distinction is precisely what determines whether Strong Cosmic Censorship holds in the physically relevant sense. The decomposition of the black hole interior into multiple regions with different gauge choices also demonstrates that no single computational frame covers the interior — the "computation" must be stitched together from patches, with the stitching itself being a non-trivial geometric operation invisible to the framework.

**Paper 2: "The full strong coupling expansion of the cusp anomalous dimension"** (arXiv 2603.17943v1, selected for strain)

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper provides the complete nonperturbative specification of a fundamental observable — the cusp anomalous dimension in $\mathcal{N}=4$ SYM at strong coupling — via a full transseries. This directly engages T008 (infinite specification from finite computation): the transseries has infinitely many nonperturbative sectors, classified by partitions into distinct non-negative odd integers. The framework must confront whether this is finite or infinite computation. The key structural features strain the framework in several ways. First, the "fermionic-type" resurgence pattern means the nonperturbative sectors combine with exclusion (odd-partition classification, analogous to fermionic statistics), not with arbitrary superposition. The framework has no account of why the organizational structure of nonperturbative computation should mimic fermionic statistics — this is algebraic/combinatorial structure (T018, T026) governing the transseries that the five primitives cannot generate. Second, the representation as a ratio of two determinants with "particularly simple" strong-coupling expansions means the physical observable's computational complexity is determined by the algebraic structure of the exact integral representation, not by any dynamical principle. The Stokes constants — which govern how nonperturbative sectors contribute across different regions of the coupling plane — are computed iteratively, meaning the full nonperturbative content is generated from finite data, consistent with the Kolmogorov-complexity interpretation of T008 (option (c)). However, the iterative generation of Stokes constants is governed by resurgence relations that are algebraic/analytic in character, not dynamical. The framework's Conservation of Computation should mean the total computational content is invariant, yet the transseries *generates* new sectors (exponentially suppressed contributions) that are not present in the perturbative expansion. Either these sectors are "always there" (and the perturbative expansion is an incomplete resolution of the full computation) or they are generated by the resurgence structure. The framework has no mechanism for describing how the organizational structure of a computation (the resurgence pattern) determines which contributions exist.

**Paper 3: "Universal scaling of transport coefficients near the liquid-gas critical point"** (arXiv 2603.17874v1, selected for validation)

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper computes universal scaling functions for thermal diffusivity and shear viscosity near the liquid-gas critical point (Model H universality class) using real-time FRG. The framework predicts that near attractor landscape restructuring (phase transitions), dynamics should exhibit universal features determined by the topology of the restructuring rather than microscopic details. This is precisely what the paper demonstrates: the transport coefficients have universal scaling functions depending only on the universality class (Model H), not on the specific fluid. The framework's Attractor primitive describes phase transitions as landscape restructuring, and the universal scaling of transport near the critical point is consistent with the claim that the restructuring topology determines observable dynamics. The paper further confirms the multi-tick resolution picture (T005): the transport coefficients (thermal diffusivity, shear viscosity) are precisely the quantities that govern how resolution propagates through the system — they set the timescale for the system to reach local equilibrium, which in the framework's language is the rate at which unresolved states resolve to definite macroscopic behavior. The universal scaling of these coefficients near criticality means the resolution dynamics have their own critical behavior, consistent with the model's existing note that resolution is a multi-tick process with thermodynamically determined minimum duration. However, the validation is weak for several reasons: (a) the framework did not predict the specific scaling functions, the path dependence of the non-perturbative results, or the deviations from the Kawasaki approximation; (b) the real-time FRG formalism that produces these results is a sophisticated field-theoretic tool with no derivation from the framework's primitives; (c) the mild path dependence of the non-perturbative scaling functions (which differs from the Kawasaki approximation) is a quantitative result the framework cannot produce or explain. The path dependence specifically means that the approach to the critical point along different thermodynamic trajectories yields slightly different transport coefficients — the Attractor primitive's description of landscape restructuring does not distinguish between different approach paths. This is post-hoc consistency with the framework's qualitative claims about universality near phase transitions, not predictive validation.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The sharpest strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's Lipschitz inextendibility result at the Kerr Cauchy horizon. The strain is not merely that the framework cannot derive this result — that is true of most results. The strain is that the framework's Resolution primitive fundamentally lacks the concept of *regularity class*, and this concept is precisely what determines the physical answer to one of the framework's own core claims.

The framework asserts that a black hole is "an attractor basin so deep that the escape velocity exceeds the tick rate." The Cauchy horizon is where this attractor description faces its ultimate test: inside the Cauchy horizon, deterministic evolution fails — the past no longer uniquely determines the future. Strong Cosmic Censorship is the assertion that this failure is quarantined by the spacetime's own structure. Gurriaran proves that the quarantine holds at Lipschitz regularity but *not* at $C^0$ regularity (the Dafermos-Luk result shows $C^0$ stability — the Cauchy horizon exists and the metric is continuous across it). The physical content of SCC thus lives in the gap between $C^0$ and Lipschitz: the metric is continuous but its first derivatives blow up.

The framework's Resolution primitive says states become "definite." It has no concept of *how definite* — there is no resolution regularity class, no distinction between "the state is defined as a continuous function" and "the state is defined with bounded derivatives." Yet this is exactly the distinction that determines whether the black hole interior preserves or destroys deterministic computation. If the framework claims reality is computation, it must specify what regularity class "resolution" demands. $C^0$ resolution allows the spacetime to continue past the Cauchy horizon into indeterminacy. Lipschitz resolution forbids it. The framework is silent on this distinction, which means it cannot adjudicate its own claim about black holes as attractor basins — it cannot say whether the attractor's interior is a place where computation breaks down or merely where it becomes less smooth.

This exposes a deeper problem: the framework's binary resolution model (unresolved → definite) cannot express the physically crucial gradations of definiteness. Physical theories operate in specific regularity classes ($C^0$, Lipschitz, $C^1$, $C^2$, smooth, analytic), and the physical content of theorems like SCC depends essentially on which class is demanded. The framework has nothing to say about this, and adding it would require a fundamental rethinking of what "resolution" means — not a binary event but a regularity spectrum.

**T067 — Resolution regularity: the framework cannot specify what "definite" means**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Gurriaran (2603.17911) proves that the Kerr Cauchy horizon is $C^0$-stable but Lipschitz-inextendible, and this regularity gap is the physical content of Strong Cosmic Censorship. The framework's Resolution primitive describes states becoming "definite" without specifying the regularity class of definiteness. Yet the most fundamental physical question about the black hole interior — whether deterministic evolution survives at the Cauchy horizon — depends entirely on whether "definite" means $C^0$, Lipschitz, $C^1$, or smoother. A $C^0$-definite state allows passage through the Cauchy horizon into a region of indeterminacy; a Lipschitz-definite state does not. The framework's binary resolution (unresolved/definite) collapses a physically crucial spectrum into a single concept. This is distinct from T017 (criticality as continuous sharpening, which concerns the *transition process*) and T005 (tick-resolution gap, which concerns *timing*). T067 concerns the *endpoint specification*: when a state is "resolved," what does the framework mean? Without a regularity class, the primitive is underspecified in exactly the regime (black hole interiors) where the framework makes its strongest claim (black holes as maximal attractors).
**Why unresolved:** Introducing a resolution regularity class would require specifying: (a) what physical principle selects the regularity class (why Lipschitz and not $C^1$?), (b) whether different systems resolve to different regularity classes, (c) how regularity class connects to the Tick primitive's discrete structure (a discrete computation naturally produces distributions, not smooth functions — what regularity does a discrete lattice computation converge to in the continuum limit?). The framework has no resources for any of these. The question "what regularity class does physics demand?" is answered in GR by the Einstein equations' well-posedness requirements, which are geometric/PDE-theoretic constraints entirely outside the framework's scope.

**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to either (a) derive the fermionic resurgence pattern from Conservation of Computation or some other primitive (no path is visible — the combinatorial structure of partitions into distinct odd integers has no connection to conservation, gradients, ticks, attractors, or resolution), (b) add a primitive governing the resurgent/transseries structure of physical quantities (which would be highly specific and might not generalize), or (c) accept that the nonperturbative organizational structure of physics is determined by algebraic properties of exact solutions that the framework is structurally unable to address. Option (c) is the most honest and aligns with the accumulating evidence for T026/T059 (the structural content gap / ontological inversion).
