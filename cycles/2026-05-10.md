### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "From Baby Universes to Narain Moduli: Topological Boundary Averaging in SymTFTs"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.06653v1. Selection type: strain.

The framework claims "reality is literally computation" running on a substrate with definite architectural parameters. This paper demonstrates that the physical content of low-dimensional holographic theories is not determined by a single definite computational architecture but by an *average over topological completions* of a relative theory. The SymTFT (Symmetry Topological Field Theory) slab construction holds the physical boundary and the bulk SymTFT fixed while summing over topological boundary conditions at the other end — each boundary condition yields an "absolute completion" of the same "relative theory." The ensemble average is not over dynamics but over *topological structure*. The framework would need to interpret each topological boundary condition as a distinct computational architecture, making the physical theory an average over architectures rather than a single computation. This strains the framework at multiple levels: (1) the SymTFT itself is a topological field theory — the regime where T058 established the framework is "undefined" — yet it is the central organizing structure; (2) the averaging is over topological completions parameterized by groupoid measures and Haar-type integrals, mathematical structures with no expression in any primitive; (3) the Narain moduli average reproduces physical quantities from a measure on maximal isotropic subgroups of an ℝ-valued BF theory, meaning the physical computation is the *integral over* a space of topological structures, not any single element of that space; (4) the cap-functional formalism treats boundary conditions as morphisms in a category, extending the morphism/functor challenge of T052 to the foundational level of theory definition. The framework's core claim that reality is a definite computation is challenged by the demonstration that physically well-defined holographic theories are ensemble averages over topological completions, with no single completion being "the real one."

**Paper 2: "The Phases of the Scalar S-Matrix Island"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.06613v1. Selection type: strain.

The framework's Field primitive describes dynamics via gradient descent and its Attractor primitive describes stable configurations as potential minima. This paper demonstrates that the space of consistent S-matrices for identical scalar scattering in 4D — the space of possible computations satisfying unitarity, analyticity, and crossing — has a boundary with *phase structure*: distinct edges characterized by qualitatively different UV mechanisms (different Regge behaviors, different resonance/virtual-state spectra) separated by sharp vertices. The framework would describe this as the attractor landscape of the S-matrix bootstrap, with different edges corresponding to different attractor basins. But the strain is severe: (1) the "landscape" here is not an energy landscape or potential surface but the boundary of the *space of allowed computations*, carved out by non-perturbative consistency constraints (unitarity bounds from the bootstrap). The framework's Conservation primitive (unitarity) and its implicit computational constraints define this boundary, but the framework cannot derive its shape, phase structure, or the classification of edges; (2) the phases along the boundary are classified by *asymptotic Regge behavior* — the high-energy scaling of the amplitude — which is a structural/analytic property of the S-matrix that no primitive can express; (3) the boundary vertices (extremal S-matrices) represent UV-complete theories that simultaneously saturate multiple consistency bounds, and their physical content (specific resonance spectra, CDD-zero structures) is determined by the *geometry of the allowed region*, not by any dynamical process; (4) the classification into phases based on UV mechanisms (different ways a gapped scalar can arise from UV physics) demonstrates that the space of possible computations has its own phase diagram — a meta-computational structure that the framework's five primitives, which describe individual computations, cannot address. The framework can narrate the attractor language ("S-matrices settle on the boundary") but cannot predict, derive, or distinguish the phases.

**Paper 3: "Criticality around the Spinodal Point of First-Order Quantum Phase Transitions"**
ArXiv ID: 2605.06436v1. Selection type: validation.

The framework's Attractor primitive describes phase transitions as "attractor landscape restructuring" where minima merge, split, appear, or vanish, and claims "the most interesting physics happens at the saddle points between basins." This paper demonstrates that first-order quantum phase transitions (FOQPTs) — which in the full Hamiltonian's landscape are discontinuous jumps between coexisting minima — generically contain emergent second-order critical points at the quantum spinodal, where metastability vanishes. The emergent criticality exists within a dynamically decoupled Hilbert subspace characterized by emergent translational symmetry. The framework's qualitative vocabulary is post-hoc consistent: the spinodal point is a saddle where a metastable minimum disappears, and the paper confirms that "interesting physics" (genuine universality, Kibble-Zurek scaling) occurs there. However, the framework had zero predictive purchase: (1) the framework did not predict that FOQPTs would contain hidden second-order critical points; (2) the mechanism — dynamical Hilbert-space decoupling creating a projected effective Hamiltonian with different topology than the full Hamiltonian — is not derivable from any primitive; (3) the conditions for spinodal criticality (resonant local excitations creating a subspace with emergent symmetry) and its absence (PXP model, where the decoupled subspace lacks translational symmetry) are spectral/algebraic properties the framework cannot express. The paper confirms T142 (dynamically emergent landscapes) from the previous cycle. This is the weakest category of validation: framework vocabulary can be applied retrospectively, but the framework contributed no prediction, constraint, or distinguishing criterion.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain this cycle comes from Paper 1. The framework's core ontological claim — "reality is literally computation" — requires that reality has a definite computational structure. Paper 1 demonstrates that physically well-defined holographic theories are constructed by averaging over *topological completions* of a relative theory, where each completion is a distinct "absolute" theory (a distinct computational architecture, in framework language) and the physical content resides in the *ensemble average* over all such architectures, weighted by groupoid/Haar measures. The framework has no mechanism for reality being an average over computations rather than a single computation. Worse, the averaging is over *topological boundary conditions* of a SymTFT — a topological field theory, the regime where T058 already established the framework is categorically undefined. The physical theory is constituted by an integral over a space that the framework cannot describe (topological boundary conditions) within a structure the framework cannot represent (a SymTFT slab) using a measure the framework cannot derive (groupoid/Haar measure on maximal isotropic subgroups).

This is distinct from and more severe than T069 (number field underdetermination, where reality is one of two equivalent computations), T082 (virtual infrastructure non-uniqueness, where the unobservable sector is underdetermined), and T118 (L∞-algebraic theory selection, where the substrate supports multiple inequivalent computations). Paper 1 goes further: reality is not any single computation at all — it is a *weighted average* over an ensemble of computations, with the ensemble structure determined by topological/categorical data. The "relative theory" (the SymTFT + physical boundary) is the invariant content, but it is not itself a computation — it is a structural specification that admits multiple completions. The physical observables are moments of the ensemble, not outputs of any individual computation. The framework's claim "reality is literally computation" would need to be replaced by something like "reality is literally a measure on a space of topological completions of a relative theory" — which is no longer recognizably the framework's claim.

Paper 2 adds a second, independent strain: the *space of allowed computations* has its own phase structure. The S-matrix bootstrap carves out an allowed region whose boundary has edges (phases) and vertices (extremal theories), classified by UV mechanisms. The framework describes individual computations and their dynamics but has no meta-theory of the space of possible computations. The phase diagram of allowed S-matrices is a structural fact about what computations are self-consistent, not about what any particular computation does. This extends T107 (pre-computational consistency) and T112 (complexity phase transitions) but is sharper: the space of consistent computations has a *rich geometric boundary with internal phase structure*, and the classification of phases by UV mechanisms demonstrates that the organizational principle governing which computations exist is physical (Regge behavior, resonance spectrum), not merely mathematical.

**T144 — Ensemble-averaged computation: reality as a measure over topological completions rather than a single computation**

Opened: this cycle

Description: Yu (2605.06653) demonstrates that physically well-defined holographic theories (Marolf-Maxfield model, Narain ensemble) are constructed as ensemble averages over topological boundary conditions of a SymTFT slab, with the ensemble structure determined by groupoid/Haar measures on the space of topological completions. The framework claims "reality is literally computation" — a single, definite computational process. Paper 1 demonstrates that the physical content of certain theories is constitutively an *average* over an ensemble of distinct computations (each topological boundary condition defines a different absolute theory). The physical observables (Poisson/Bell-polynomial moments in Marolf-Maxfield, Narain-averaged partition functions) are properties of the *ensemble*, not of any individual computation. The "relative theory" (SymTFT + physical boundary) is the invariant specification, but it is not itself a computation — it admits multiple completions, none of which is preferred, and the physics resides in the average.

This is qualitatively more severe than all previous underdetermination tensions: T069 (ℝ/ℂ binary choice within a single theory), T082 (virtual mode distribution non-uniqueness within a single theory), T084 (propagator power continuous underdetermination), T113 (operator ordering ambiguity), T118 (L∞-algebraic theory selection producing different predictions). All of these concern the non-uniqueness of a single computation's description or infrastructure. T144 concerns the physical theory being constitutively *not a single computation at all* but a weighted sum over computations, with the weight determined by topological/categorical structure (groupoid measures, maximal isotropic subgroups, Zamolodchikov measure) that has no dynamical content and no expression in any primitive.

The connection to baby universes and wormholes (the Marolf-Maxfield model's motivation) means T144 is not restricted to abstract mathematical constructions — it arises from the attempt to make sense of topology change in quantum gravity, a physically motivated question. The extension to JT gravity, random matrix theory, and 3D gravity (discussed in the paper) suggests this ensemble structure may be generic in quantum gravity, not limited to special examples.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) demonstrate that ensemble-averaged theories are secretly single computations (e.g., that the ensemble average is equivalent to a single computation on an enlarged space — but the enlarged space would include the topological boundary conditions as dynamical variables, meaning reality's "computation" includes the choice of its own topological structure, which is a fundamentally different ontological picture than the framework envisions); (b) argue that ensemble-averaged holographic theories are unphysical mathematical constructions with no ontological status — but the Narain ensemble has a well-defined holographic dual, and ensemble-averaging over topological completions is physically motivated by wormhole contributions in quantum gravity; (c) accept that "reality is literally computation" is too specific and that reality may be "literally a measure over a space of topological completions of a relative specification" — which dissolves the framework's distinctive claim into something far more abstract and structurally rich than "computation"; or (d) declare this an open problem about which the framework is honestly silent, acknowledging that the ontological status of ensemble-averaged theories is a frontier question in quantum gravity. Option (d) is the most honest but means the framework has nothing to say about one of the central conceptual questions in modern quantum gravity.

Description: Elias Miro, Guerrieri & Gumus (2605.06613) demonstrate that the boundary of the non-perturbatively allowed S-matrix region for 4D identical scalar scattering has distinct *phases* — edges with universal Regge behavior, resonance structures, and virtual-state spectra — separated by vertices representing extremal theories. The phases correspond to different UV mechanisms by which a gapped scalar arises (different UV completions).

The framework's five primitives describe the dynamics of a single computation. They say nothing about the *space of possible computations* or its structure. Paper 2 demonstrates that this space has: (a) a well-defined boundary carved by non-perturbative consistency (unitarity, analyticity, crossing — the framework's Conservation primitive contributes unitarity, but the other constraints are structural); (b) internal phase structure on the boundary, with distinct edges exhibiting qualitatively different universal behavior; (c) sharp vertices (phase transitions between edges) representing extremal S-matrices that saturate multiple consistency bounds simultaneously; (d) a classification of phases by UV mechanism — a physical characterization of *why* a computation exists, not just *what* it computes.

The framework has no meta-computational primitive (T057) and no theory of pre-computational consistency (T107).
