**Paper 1: "On the spanning cuts consistency problem in the IBP reductions of Feynman integrals"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.02485v1 | Selection type: strain

Through the Worldlines lens, IBP (integration-by-parts) reduction is the process by which the framework's Conservation of Computation manifests at the level of Feynman integral computation: the total derivative identities that generate IBP relations are expressions of the fact that computational content is conserved under shifts in loop-momentum space, allowing the decomposition of any Feynman integral into a finite basis of master integrals (T018). The paper reveals that this decomposition can become *inconsistent* when evaluated on different cuts — different ways of projecting the computation onto on-shell subspaces. The mechanism is precise and physically sharp: the Feynman iε prescription, which the framework would interpret as part of the substrate's causal architecture (determining the direction of temporal propagation and enforcing the arrow of time), carries "hidden terms" that are formally zero in the uncut integral but become finite on certain cuts because cut-induced pinch singularities cancel the vanishing iε parameters. The framework strain is severe and specific: the iε prescription is not merely a regularization convenience but carries physically essential information that becomes visible only when the computation is projected onto specific subspaces. The framework's Conservation primitive states computation is conserved, but the IBP relations that enforce this conservation are *violated* on cuts when hidden iε-dependent terms are dropped. The consistency of conservation depends on tracking infinitesimal causal-structure information (iε) through the computation, and this information has a structured relationship to the singularity structure of the propagators (pinch singularities from "hidden relations between propagators"). The framework has no primitive for the iε prescription, no account of how causal-structure information at the infinitesimal level mediates conservation, and no mechanism for the "hidden relations between propagators" that the paper identifies as the source of the inconsistency. This is a new category of structural content: the *causal microstructure* of conservation enforcement, where the conservation law's validity depends on infinitesimal prescription data that is invisible to the conservation law's statement but essential to its application.

**Paper 2: "Dynamics of the Density Cube"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.02421v1 | Selection type: strain

The Worldlines framework treats quantum mechanics as the behavior of unresolved computation between ticks, with the density matrix ρ_{ij} encoding the state of unresolved computation and the Born rule governing resolution outcomes. The density cube ρ_{ijk} is a radical extension: it adds a third index to the state description, encoding "triple-path interference" — correlations between three computational paths that are not reducible to pairwise interference. The framework's Resolution primitive and its pre-resolution topology (T012) describe the structure of indistinguishability between pairs of computational paths; the density cube demands a *ternary* indistinguishability structure that the framework cannot express. The equation of motion is derived not from standard Hamiltonian (binary) dynamics but from *ternary Nambu dynamics* — a generalization of Hamiltonian mechanics where the fundamental bracket involves three quantities rather than two, and the evolution is governed by two Hamiltonians rather than one. The framework's Tick primitive assumes binary composition: at each tick, the state is updated by a single Hamiltonian generating a unitary transformation on pairs of indices. Nambu dynamics replaces this with ternary composition, where the fundamental update involves triplets. The framework's entire architecture — sequential binary update (Tick), binary gradient descent (Field), pairwise interference (pre-resolution topology), binary conservation (unitarity as inner-product preservation) — is built on binary mathematical structures. The density cube operates in a ternary mathematical universe. The framework cannot represent ternary dynamics, ternary interference, or ternary conservation. The finding that "pairs of triple-path interferences oscillate into each other" describes a dynamical phenomenon (ternary coherence oscillation) that has no expression in any of the five primitives. This is not merely a structural gap (T026) or an algebraic insufficiency (T018) — it is a challenge to the *arity* of the framework's mathematical foundation. If physical reality admits ternary interference and ternary dynamics, the framework's binary architecture is not just incomplete but categorically wrong about the mathematical structure of computation.

**Paper 3: "Teleocosmology and quantum post-selection"**
ArXiv ID: 2606.02514v1 | Selection type: validation

The Worldlines framework claims dark energy is "the computational cost of instantiating new substrate," with accelerating expansion being a consequence of substrate expansion. Paper 3 proposes an entirely different mechanism: cosmic acceleration arises from quantum post-selection — a final boundary condition on the wave function of the universe (a Chern-Simons soliton) — with zero cosmological constant in the forward Hamiltonian. Through the framework lens, this would be interpreted as: the attractor landscape of the universe has a future boundary condition (a "teleological attractor") that shapes the intermediate dynamics, causing the universe's trajectory to deviate from the radiation-dominated path and enter an accelerating regime without any local source of dark energy. The framework's Resolution primitive and Attractor primitive can narrate this in vocabulary terms: the final state acts as an attractor in the space of cosmological wavefunctions, and the intermediate acceleration is the trajectory's response to this future boundary condition. However, the framework has no mechanism for future boundary conditions — the Tick primitive generates a forward arrow of time, the Resolution primitive accumulates states irreversibly, and causality flows from past to future. A teleological mechanism (future-to-past influence on the intermediate trajectory) is not merely absent from the framework but contradicts its temporal architecture. The paper's demonstration that a free particle with a final boundary condition exhibits apparent acceleration — mimicking a "contrived classical force" — is precisely the framework's dark energy scenario inverted: the framework posits a real computational cost (dark energy as substrate expansion cost); the paper shows the same phenomenology from a quantum boundary-condition effect with no local source. This further weakens the framework's dark energy claim (already the most repeatedly weakened specific claim, documented across T007, T086, and multiple model updates). The framework has zero predictive purchase here — it cannot predict, constrain, or distinguish post-selection-driven acceleration from its own "substrate cost" mechanism or from any other dark energy model. This is post-hoc consistency at best (the framework can narrate acceleration), but the specific mechanism (future boundary conditions, two-boundary amplitudes, Chern-Simons soliton as final state) is entirely outside the framework's vocabulary and contradicts its temporal architecture.

The framework cannot express any of these. This is not a gap in the framework's coverage of known physics (like T026's catalogue of missing structural categories) — it is a challenge to the framework's implicit assumption that the mathematical arity of physical law is binary. The framework has no escape route: it cannot absorb ternary dynamics by rewriting it in binary terms (Nambu dynamics is not reducible to Hamiltonian dynamics in the general case), cannot dismiss it as unphysical (triple-path interference is a well-defined operational concept with proposed experimental tests, e.g., Sorkin's third-order interference), and cannot add a "ternary primitive" without fundamentally restructuring all five existing primitives which presuppose binary composition.

Paper 1 contributes a subtler but precise strain: conservation enforcement depends on infinitesimal causal-structure data (iε prescriptions) that the framework treats as part of the substrate architecture but that turns out to carry essential, context-dependent information mediating between different projections of the computation. Conservation is not a simple bookkeeping identity but requires a causal-microstructural infrastructure the framework cannot express.
