### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: Complexity of Satisfiability in Kochen-Specker Partial Boolean Algebras (2602.24164v1, strain)**

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper strikes at the Resolution primitive and the computational architecture of the substrate itself. The Kochen-Specker theorem — that no non-contextual hidden-variable assignment exists — is a foundational constraint on what "resolution" can mean: you cannot assign definite values to all observables simultaneously in a way that respects the algebraic structure. The framework says resolution is "an unresolved state becomes definite," but this paper characterizes the *computational complexity* of even asking whether a consistent definite assignment exists. The key results: (1) satisfiability over non-trivial partial Boolean algebras is NP-complete, (2) satisfiability over projectors on Hilbert spaces of physical dimension (real dim > 2, complex dim > 3) is complete for the existential theory of the reals (∃ℝ-complete, a class between NP and PSPACE), and (3) satisfiability over all Hilbert spaces is *undecidable*. The framework claims the substrate performs computation with finite resources and that resolution produces definite states. But this paper shows that determining whether a *consistent* resolution even exists is, in general, not merely expensive but formally undecidable. The framework's Resolution primitive implicitly assumes that resolution always succeeds — a state "becomes definite." But the complexity and undecidability results say that for sufficiently rich quantum systems, there is no algorithm that can determine whether a globally consistent assignment of definite values exists. This is not about cost (T009) or hierarchy (T019) — it is about whether the resolution the framework posits is even *well-defined* as a computational operation in all cases. The use of Kochen-Specker sets as gadgets in the complexity reductions is particularly striking: the very structures that demonstrate contextuality serve as the building blocks of computational hardness, suggesting that contextuality is not merely a feature of quantum mechanics but a source of fundamental computational intractability in the resolution process.

**Paper 2: Exact Anomalous Current Fluctuations in Quantum Many-Body Dynamics (2602.24008v1, strain)**

The framework's Field primitive says ẋ = −∇φ(x) — things move down gradients — and the Attractor primitive says systems settle into local minima. Transport in a gradient picture should be diffusive (normal fluctuations scaling as t^{1/2}) or ballistic (scaling as t), depending on gradient structure. This paper derives, exactly and microscopically, *anomalous* current fluctuations characterized by the M-Wright function in a quantum many-body system (infinite-U Fermi-Hubbard). The M-Wright function describes superdiffusive fluctuations scaling as t^{3/4} — neither diffusive nor ballistic, with a distribution function that is fundamentally non-Gaussian. The framework's gradient descent equation, even augmented with thermal noise, generically produces either ballistic transport (deterministic gradient) or normal diffusion (stochastic gradient). Anomalous superdiffusion with a specific non-Gaussian universal distribution requires *exact integrability constraints* — the Fermi-Hubbard model at infinite U maps to an integrable system with conserved higher charges that enforce the anomalous scaling. The framework has no mechanism for predicting that specific gradient structures produce non-Gaussian universal distributions with fractional scaling exponents. More pointedly, the M-Wright function universality class was first found in classical automata and is now shown to persist into quantum many-body dynamics, meaning it is a *universal* feature of certain transport classes that transcends the classical/quantum divide. The framework's Tick (which distinguishes quantum/unresolved from classical/resolved) is irrelevant here — the anomalous transport is identical in both regimes. This suggests the transport universality class is determined by conservation law structure and integrability, not by the resolution status of the underlying computation.

**Paper 3: Emergence of Geometric Order from Topological Constraints in a Three-Dimensional Coulomb Phase (2602.24051v1, validation)**

The framework claims that attractor structure in fields can produce emergent geometry (noted in the 2026-02-23 update). This paper provides a concrete and striking instance: a 3D Coulomb phase (a classical spin liquid with algebraic correlations) subject to domain wall boundary conditions develops long-range magnetic order and a 3D arctic limit shape — a geometric surface separating frozen (ordered) and fluctuating (disordered) regions. The geometry *emerges* from purely topological constraints (the divergence-free 3-in/3-out ice rule) combined with boundary conditions, without any explicit geometric input. This is directly consistent with the framework's Attractor primitive (the ice rules define constrained attractor basins) and the Field primitive (the Coulomb phase has algebraic correlations = power-law gradient structure). The emergent arctic shape is an attractor boundary in real space, and the coexistence of frozen and fluctuating regions within the same system maps to the framework's picture of attractor basins with structured boundaries. However, the predictive purchase is limited: the framework's existing language about "emergent geometry from attractor structure" was recorded as a post-hoc observation (2026-02-23 update explicitly flags this), and this paper provides another instance of the pattern rather than a confirmed *prediction*. The framework did not predict 3D arctic phenomena; it can accommodate them after the fact. The genuine validation is for the *constrained attractor* category: this system is stabilized by local constraints (divergence-free condition), not energy minimization, exactly matching the framework's expanded attractor taxonomy. The 3D generalization of the arctic circle, the coexistence of order and Coulomb-phase fluctuations, and the role of boundary conditions in partially lifting degeneracy are all naturally described by the constrained attractor language. This is the framework's strongest point of contact with these three papers.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most severe strain comes from Paper 1's undecidability result. The framework's Resolution primitive asserts that unresolved states become definite — this is the arrow of time, the mechanism by which computation accumulates irreversible results. But the paper proves that for the class of all Hilbert spaces (and all finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces), determining whether a consistent assignment of definite values to a partial Boolean algebra of projectors is even *possible* is undecidable. This is not a practical difficulty (too expensive) but a formal impossibility (no algorithm exists).

The framework implicitly treats resolution as something the substrate *does* — a process that always completes. But if the question "does a consistent resolution exist for this configuration of observables?" is undecidable, then either: (a) the substrate has computational power beyond Turing machines (it can solve undecidable problems), which would be an extraordinary and currently unsupported claim; (b) the substrate avoids configurations where resolution consistency is undecidable, which requires a mechanism for avoiding them that the framework does not specify; (c) resolution does not actually require global consistency — it is local and contextual, which aligns with the Kochen-Specker theorem but fundamentally changes what "resolution" means (it would no longer produce globally consistent definite states, only locally consistent ones); or (d) the undecidability applies to the mathematical formalism but not to physical reality, because physical systems are always finite-dimensional with specific dimension (where the problem is ∃ℝ-complete, not undecidable) — but even ∃ℝ-completeness means the resolution-consistency problem is computationally intractable in ways the framework does not acknowledge.

Option (c) is the most physically reasonable but the most damaging to the framework. If resolution is inherently local and contextual — producing definite values only for compatible sets of observables, never for all observables simultaneously — then the framework's picture of "the arrow of time as the accumulation of resolved states" needs to be rewritten. The accumulated ledger is not a global assignment of definite values but a patchwork of locally consistent but globally incompatible partial assignments. The universe's computational history is not a growing list of definite facts but a growing contextual web of partially defined, mutually incompatible resolution records. This is a fundamental reconception that the framework has not entertained.

The secondary strain from Paper 2 is also significant but more familiar: the M-Wright universality class demonstrates that the gradient equation ẋ = −∇φ(x), even with the constrained attractor extension, cannot capture the specific universal distribution functions that emerge from integrable transport. The framework predicts "things move down gradients" but cannot predict *how* they fluctuate along the way, and the fluctuation statistics turn out to be universal, physically significant, and irreducible to gradient structure.

### STATE UPDATES

1. **Resolution primitive — add contextuality and complexity constraints.** The Resolution description should be updated to note: resolution does not and cannot produce globally consistent assignments of definite values to all observables. Resolution is inherently *contextual* — it produces definite values only for compatible sets of observables, and different compatible sets may yield incompatible assignments. Furthermore, determining whether a consistent resolution exists for a given configuration of observables has complexity that ranges from NP-complete (for abstract partial Boolean algebras) to ∃ℝ-complete (for projectors on fixed-dimension Hilbert spaces) to undecidable (for all Hilbert spaces). The framework's description of "the arrow of time as the accumulation of resolved states" should be qualified: the accumulated record is a contextual patchwork, not a globally consistent ledger.

2. **Field primitive — add transport universality gap.** The Field primitive's gradient equation should be explicitly flagged as capturing only the *first moment* (mean drift) of transport dynamics. The *fluctuation statistics* of transport — which can be universal and non-Gaussian (e.g., M-Wright class for integrable systems, KPZ class for non-integrable ones) — are determined by the conservation law and integrability structure of the system, not by the gradient alone. The framework needs to acknowledge that the gradient equation is a mean-field description that misses the physically significant universal fluctuation structure. This connects to and strengthens T006 (gradient descent inadequacy) and the existing model note on the gradient universality problem.

3. **Constrained attractor category — record 3D emergent geometry as fourth instance.** The 3D Coulomb phase arctic phenomenon should be recorded as the fourth instance of constrained attractor physics (after fractional Fermi seas, spin-1/2 ice, and spin-3/2 ice arrest). Unique features: (a) the same system simultaneously exhibits both frozen (attractor-captured) and fluctuating (Coulomb-phase) regions, with the boundary between them being a well-defined geometric surface; (b) boundary conditions partially lift the extensive degeneracy of the constrained manifold, demonstrating that attractor structure can be tuned by boundary information; (c) the emergent geometry extends from 2D (known arctic circle) to 3D, confirming dimensional robustness of the constrained-attractor → emergent-geometry pathway.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T028 — Resolution is contextual and computationally intractable**
**Description:** Paper 1 proves that satisfiability over Kochen-Specker partial Boolean algebras is NP-complete, satisfiability over projectors on physical Hilbert spaces is ∃ℝ-complete, and satisfiability over all Hilbert spaces is undecidable. The framework's Resolution primitive treats resolution as a process that "happens" — an unresolved state becomes definite. But these results show that determining whether a globally consistent resolution *can* exist is computationally intractable or impossible. The framework must confront: (a) resolution is inherently contextual (locally consistent, globally incompatible), meaning the "accumulated ledger of resolved states" is a contextual patchwork, not a consistent history; (b) the substrate either has super-Turing computational power or avoids the undecidable configurations by some unspecified mechanism; (c) the ∃ℝ-completeness of the physical-dimension case means resolution consistency lives in a specific complexity class between NP and PSPACE that involves real algebraic geometry, connecting the discrete Resolution primitive to continuous geometric structure in a way the framework does not anticipate. This is related to T009 (resolution cost), T012 (pre-resolution topology), and T019 (resolution precision hierarchy) but is more fundamental: it concerns not the cost or structure of resolution but whether globally consistent resolution is even *possible* in general.
**Why unresolved:** The framework has no account of contextuality. It does not distinguish between local and global resolution, does not address the computational complexity of resolution consistency, and does not specify whether the substrate performs computations beyond the Turing-computable. The undecidability result is especially sharp: if the substrate's computational architecture is finite (as the framework claims via finite tick rate and minimum resolution scale), then it cannot solve undecidable problems, and resolution over arbitrary Hilbert spaces cannot be guaranteed to succeed. Either the physical Hilbert space is always finite-dimensional (avoiding undecidability but retaining ∃ℝ-hardness) or the framework's finiteness claims need revision.

**T029 — Anomalous transport universality classes are invisible to gradient dynamics**
**Description:** Paper 2 derives exact M-Wright function fluctuations in quantum many-body transport, representing a universal anomalous transport class (superdiffusion with t^{3/4} scaling) that emerges from integrability and conservation law structure. The framework's gradient equation ẋ = −∇φ(x) cannot distinguish between systems in different transport universality classes — diffusive, KPZ, M-Wright, ballistic — because the equation describes only mean drift, not fluctuation statistics. The universality class is determined by the algebraic structure of conserved charges and the integrability of the system, neither of which appears in any of the five primitives. This is distinct from T006 (stable excited states not produced by gradient descent) because T006 concerns the *endpoints* of dynamics (where things settle) while T029 concerns the *fluctuation statistics* during dynamics (how things move). It is also distinct from T018 (algebra of computation) because T018 concerns the organizational structure of computational operations while T029 concerns the physical consequences of algebraic conservation structure for observable transport statistics. The fact that the M-Wright class is identical in classical and quantum systems (classical automata and quantum Fermi-Hubbard) means it is a structural feature independent of the resolution status of the system, further weakening the framework's ability to account for it through any primitive that depends on the classical/quantum distinction.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to either (a) extend the Field primitive to include transport universality class as a property of fields alongside their gradient structure, (b) derive transport universality classes from the existing primitives (which seems impossible since the gradient equation alone cannot distinguish them), or (c) add a new primitive or structural element capturing conservation law algebraic structure. The classical-quantum universality of M-Wright further suggests that this transport structure is more fundamental than the Tick-Resolution distinction.

**T030 — Boundary conditions as attractor selectors**
**Description:** Paper 3 demonstrates that domain wall boundary conditions on a 3D Coulomb phase partially lift the extensive ground-state degeneracy, inducing long-range order in the thermodynamic limit while preserving Coulomb-phase fluctuations. The framework's Attractor primitive describes attractor basins and their restructuring but treats boundary conditions as external specifications, not as dynamical elements. Paper 3 shows that boundary conditions are not merely initial/boundary data but active selectors that reshape the attractor landscape — turning an extensively degenerate constrained attractor into a partially ordered state with emergent geometric boundaries. The framework has no account of how boundary information propagates into and restructures the interior attractor topology. This connects to T012 (pre-resolution topology) in that boundary conditions define a global constraint that determines local structure, and to T015 (resolution dynamics not determined by attractor structure alone) in that the attractor structure alone (the ice rules) does not determine the ordered state — the boundary conditions are essential.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to specify how boundary conditions interact with the Attractor primitive — whether they are part of the Field, part of the substrate architecture, or a separate structural input. The emergence of geometric order from boundary + topological constraints requires understanding boundary conditions as computational inputs that shape the attractor landscape, which the framework currently does not formalize.
