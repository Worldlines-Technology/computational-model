**Paper 1: "En Route to a Standard QMA1 vs. QCMA Oracle Separation"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.26921v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the computational cost and structure of resolution (the Resolution primitive) and the nature of quantum witnesses — pre-resolution computational structures that certify the outcome of a resolution event. The central result is that quantum witnesses (QMA₁) are provably more powerful than classical witnesses (QCMA) under specific oracle constraints, even with perfect completeness. The framework's Resolution primitive treats resolution as "unresolved → definite" and has been progressively refined to acknowledge cost structure (T009), precision hierarchy (T019), contextuality (T028), and complexity phase transitions (T112). This paper strains the framework in a new direction: the *witness structure* — what kind of pre-resolution information is needed to verify a resolution outcome — is a physically meaningful distinction that the framework has no mechanism to express. The quantum witness is not merely "more information" than the classical witness; it is a categorically different *type* of pre-resolution certification, and the separation between them persists even when the verifier has exponentially many parallel queries per round and polynomially many adaptive rounds. The framework's binary resolution (unresolved → definite) cannot distinguish between "resolution verifiable by classical certificate" and "resolution verifiable only by quantum certificate." The sparse Hamiltonian ground-state preparation consequence is especially relevant: it demonstrates that preparing a state near an attractor basin minimum (ground state) from oracle access to the Hamiltonian is subject to the same quantum/classical witness distinction, meaning the framework's Attractor primitive's "things settle into minima" elides the fact that *finding* the minimum has witness-type-dependent complexity. The bounded-adaptivity frustration-free variant connects to T114 (frustration-free area law independence from spectral gap) by showing that even in the "simplest" attractor landscapes, the witness structure matters for computational accessibility.

**Paper 2: "Anomalous Transport and Explicit Symmetry Breaking in Holography"**
ArXiv ID: 2604.26949v1 | Selection type: Strain

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper concerns the Field primitive's gradient dynamics and its interaction with symmetry structure. The paper studies anomaly-induced transport (chiral magnetic effect, chiral vortical effect) in a holographic (5D) system with explicit symmetry breaking via a scalar field mass term. The framework's Field primitive ẍ = −∇φ(x) describes transport along gradients. The paper demonstrates two phenomena the framework cannot account for: (1) Anomalous transport is perpendicular to gradients (chiral magnetic current flows along the magnetic field, not along any thermodynamic gradient), extending T078 (Hall/transverse transport) to the anomalous sector — but here the transverse transport is not merely a Hall effect but a topological phenomenon rooted in quantum anomalies (Chern-Simons terms in 5D), making it structurally deeper than T078's parity-breaking mechanism. (2) Explicit symmetry breaking causes anomaly-induced transport to *leak* into the non-anomalous sector — transport coefficients that should be zero by symmetry become nonzero when the symmetry is explicitly broken by the mass parameter. The framework has no mechanism for describing how symmetry breaking in one sector (the anomalous/chiral sector) modifies transport in a different sector (the non-anomalous/vector sector). The holographic derivation (5D Einstein-Maxwell-Chern-Simons with full backreaction) provides the computation entirely through extra-dimensional geometry, reinforcing T083/T085 (vacuum and transport structure as extra-dimensional geometry). The mass-parameter dependence of all transport coefficients — including the anomalous ones — means the gradient structure is not merely supplemented by anomalous contributions but is *modified* by an explicit symmetry-breaking parameter that the framework treats as part of the attractor landscape but not of the transport dynamics. The interplay between anomaly and explicit breaking is a structural coupling between two categories of physics (topological/anomalous and dynamical/symmetry-breaking) that no primitive can express.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most significant strain this cycle comes from Paper 1's quantum vs. classical witness separation. The framework's Resolution primitive describes states becoming definite and has been progressively refined through dozens of tensions (T009, T019, T028, T067, T097, T112, T123, T128), but all these refinements concern properties of the *resolution process itself* — its cost, its precision, its contextuality, its regularity, its phase structure, its transient character. Paper 1 reveals a categorically new dimension: the *certification structure* of resolution outcomes. The question is not "what does the system resolve to?" or "how hard is it to resolve?" but "what type of evidence is needed to *verify* that a resolution has occurred correctly?" The QMA₁ vs. QCMA separation proves that quantum witnesses are strictly more powerful than classical witnesses for certifying resolution outcomes, even with perfect completeness and even when the classical verifier has massively parallel oracle access. This means the Resolution primitive is not merely underspecified (as T067/T097 argued) or cost-structured (as T009/T112 argued) but is missing an entire dimension: the *witness category* — whether the certificate attesting to a resolution outcome is classical or quantum — determines what resolutions are verifiable at all. The framework has no concept of "what type of evidence certifies a resolution." The ground-state preparation consequence makes this concrete for the Attractor primitive: "things settle into energy minima" elides the fact that whether a claimed minimum *is* a minimum requires certification, and the type of certification needed (quantum or classical witness) is a structural property of the Hamiltonian that no primitive addresses.

Paper 2 creates a secondary but important strain by demonstrating that anomalous transport — which is already beyond the gradient equation (T078) — is further modified by explicit symmetry breaking in a way that causes anomalous physics to *contaminate* the non-anomalous sector. This is not merely "the gradient equation misses transverse transport" (already known) but "the boundary between anomalous and non-anomalous transport is itself permeable, controlled by a symmetry-breaking parameter that the framework has no mechanism to track." The holographic origin of this effect (5D Chern-Simons terms with full backreaction) further reinforces the pattern that transport physics is determined by extra-dimensional geometric/topological structure.

**T132 — Witness category gap: the Resolution primitive lacks the concept of what type of evidence certifies a resolution outcome**

Opened: this cycle

Description: Miloschewsky, Podder & Rudolph (2604.26921) prove that QMA₁ (quantum witnesses with perfect completeness) is strictly more powerful than QCMA (classical witnesses) relative to a classical oracle, even when the QCMA verifier has polynomially many adaptive rounds and exponentially many parallel queries per round. The framework's Resolution primitive describes states becoming definite but has no concept of the *type of evidence* required to certify that a resolution outcome is correct. The quantum/classical witness distinction is a structural property of the physical problem (the Hamiltonian, the oracle) that determines which resolution outcomes can be verified. The framework faces: (a) the Resolution primitive treats all resolution events as equivalent (unresolved → definite), but the verifiability of resolution depends on whether the certifying evidence is quantum or classical — a categorical distinction invisible to the primitive; (b) the ground-state preparation consequence means the Attractor primitive's "things settle into energy minima" requires certification that the minimum has been reached, and the certification type needed is a structural property of the Hamiltonian; (c) the bounded-adaptivity frustration-free result connects to T114 (entanglement/thermodynamic bounds) and T112 (complexity phase transitions) but adds a new dimension: even "easy" attractor landscapes (frustration-free Hamiltonians) have witness-type-dependent accessibility; (d) the exponential-parallel-query robustness of the separation means this is not a resource-limitation issue — massive classical computational power does not substitute for quantum witness structure.

This is distinct from T009 (resolution cost, which concerns the computational resources for resolution), T019 (resolution precision hierarchy, which concerns the levels of resolution precision), T028 (resolution contextuality, which concerns global consistency of resolution), T112 (complexity phase transitions, which concerns the computational difficulty of determining resolution outcomes), and T123 (AoT phase structure, which concerns the temporal asymmetry of resolution). T132 concerns a new axis entirely: the *certification category* of resolution outcomes — whether the evidence attesting to a resolution is classical or quantum — and the provable separation between these categories.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) incorporate witness type (quantum vs. classical) as a parameter of the Resolution primitive, specifying what determines which witness type is needed for a given system — this would require importing the QMA/QCMA complexity-theoretic hierarchy as structural content; (b) derive the witness-type dependence from existing primitives, which is impossible since the distinction depends on the algebraic structure of the verification circuit and the oracle (structural content no primitive can express); or (c) add a meta-resolution primitive governing what types of evidence are valid for certifying different resolution events. The separation's survival against exponentially many parallel classical queries is especially severe: it means the gap is not a resource issue (more computation closes it) but a *type* issue (the wrong kind of computation, no matter how much, cannot close it). The framework's implicit assumption that resolution is a single, undifferentiated operation is challenged at the level of what counts as evidence for resolution having occurred.

Description: Tamang, Rai, Landsteiner & Megías (2604.26949) demonstrate in a holographic Einstein-Maxwell-Chern-Simons model that explicit breaking of an axial symmetry (via a scalar field mass) causes anomaly-induced transport to contaminate the non-anomalous (vector) sector: transport coefficients that are zero in the unbroken theory become nonzero and mass-dependent when the symmetry is broken. The framework's Field primitive describes transport via gradient dynamics; T078 already established that the gradient equation misses transverse/anomalous transport. T133 goes further: the *boundary between anomalous and non-anomalous transport* is itself a dynamical, symmetry-breaking-dependent feature that the framework cannot express. The framework has no concept of "transport sectors" (anomalous vs. non-anomalous), no mechanism for inter-sector contamination, and no parameter tracking how explicit symmetry breaking controls this contamination.

This is distinct from T078 (Hall/transverse transport, which concerns the direction of transport in parity-broken systems) because T133 concerns the *crossing of sector boundaries* — transport phenomena that belong to the anomalous sector leaking into the non-anomalous sector — not merely the direction of transport within a single sector. It is distinct from T032 (symmetry as missing primitive) because T133 concerns the *consequences of symmetry breaking for transport sector structure*, not symmetry as a general missing element. It is distinct from T083/T085 (extra-dimensional geometry determining vacuum/attractor structure) because T133 concerns transport coefficients, not vacuum structure, though the mechanism is holographic.

Why unresolved: The framework would need to either (a) develop a theory of transport sectors and their coupling under symmetry breaking, which requires importing the full apparatus of anomalous transport (Kubo formulae for mixed correlators, Chern-Simons terms, Ward identity structure) — mathematical machinery categorically absent from the five primitives; (b) extend the Field primitive to include sector-dependent transport with symmetry-breaking-controlled cross-coupling, fundamentally transforming the primitive from a scalar gradient equation to a matrix transport equation with off-diagonal terms controlled by symmetry parameters; or (c) accept that anomalous transport and its symmetry-breaking modifications are structural physics the framework cannot address, further deepening the pattern where the physically most interesting transport phenomena (Hall viscosity T078, anomalous transport, sector contamination) are precisely those the gradient equation misses. The full backreaction treatment in the paper means this is not a perturbative artifact but a robust feature of strongly coupled matter with broken symmetries.
