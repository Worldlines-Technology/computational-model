### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: "Batalin-Vilkovisky quantization with an angular twist"** (2604.16225v1, strain)

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper constructs two *inequivalent* quantum field theories from the same classical starting point (cubic scalar on λ-Minkowski space) by choosing different L∞-algebraic structures — braided vs. classical. The framework claims "reality is literally computation," which requires a definite computational structure. This paper demonstrates that on a noncommutative spacetime (which the framework's discrete Tick primitive would naturally produce), the *same* classical field content yields two physically distinct quantum theories with qualitatively different UV behavior: one has standard logarithmic divergences and no UV/IR mixing; the other has UV-finite non-planar sectors with periodic non-analyticity on an infinite lattice of exceptional momenta. The choice between them is an L∞-algebraic structural choice (braided vs. classical algebra), not a dynamical one. The framework has no primitive for this choice. More specifically: (a) the L∞-algebra is the generative structure from which the BV quantization, the propagator, the vertices, and the loop corrections all follow — reinforcing T055/T059/T100 that algebraic structure is upstream of dynamics; (b) the noncommutative spacetime produces an emergent lattice of exceptional momenta in the standard theory, meaning the Tick primitive's discreteness, if realized as noncommutativity, has spectral consequences (periodic UV/IR mixing) that the framework cannot predict or describe; (c) the braided theory's spectral decomposition into cylindrical Bessel functions — dictated by covariance under the angular Drinfel'd twist — demonstrates that the spectral basis of the computation is not free but is determined by the algebraic structure of the noncommutativity, a concept entirely absent from the Field primitive. The paper strains the framework at the deepest possible level: the same "substrate" (λ-Minkowski space) supports two inequivalent "computations" distinguished only by algebraic structure the framework cannot express.

**Paper 2: "Boson correlations are spurious for classical states"** (2604.16283v1, strain)

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper demonstrates that what appear to be quantum correlations in bosonic systems with classical (Glauber-Sudarshan representable) states are actually Simpson's paradox artifacts — spurious correlations arising from statistical averaging over uncorrelated measurements made in varying geometries, where spontaneous symmetry breaking acts as a confounding variable. The framework's Resolution primitive treats resolution as producing definite outcomes from superpositions, with correlations between resolved outcomes being genuine features of the pre-resolution state. Paper 2 shows this is wrong for an entire class of states: the correlations between resolved bosonic measurements in classical states are not properties of the quantum state at all but artifacts of confounding the statistical ensemble average with the quantum expectation value. The framework has no distinction between "quantum average" and "statistical average" — its Resolution primitive treats all correlations in resolved outcomes as genuine reflections of pre-resolution structure. The paper's identification of symmetry-breaking as a confounding variable is especially challenging: the framework treats symmetry breaking as attractor landscape restructuring (a real physical process), but here the "symmetry breaking" in each realization is the confounding factor that *generates* the spurious correlations. The framework's pre-resolution topology (T012) — the structure of indistinguishability that shapes resolution outcomes — would classify these correlations as genuine topological features of the pre-resolution state, when in fact they are statistical artifacts of ensemble averaging over varying measurement geometries. This is the first paper processed by the system that challenges the framework's *interpretation* of correlations rather than its dynamical content.

**Paper 3: "Gravitationally induced wave-function collapse from dynamical bifurcation"** (2604.16144v1, validation)

Through the Worldlines lens, this paper proposes that wave-function collapse is a deterministic dynamical instability: a nonlinear Schrödinger equation with gravitational self-interaction and short-distance repulsion produces a bifurcation at a critical mass, beyond which extended states are unstable and the system falls into one of multiple localized attractors, with the specific attractor selected by infinitesimal initial asymmetries. This maps almost exactly onto the framework's primitives: the Field (gravitational self-interaction gradient), the Attractor (localized configurations as stable basins), and Resolution (collapse as the system selecting a specific attractor basin via bifurcation). The critical mass threshold maps onto the framework's consciousness-threshold analogue: a quantitative threshold above which resolution becomes dynamically inevitable. The "no stochastic noise, no environmental coupling" feature aligns with the framework's aspiration to derive resolution from internal dynamics rather than external intervention. However, this is post-hoc consistency, not predictive validation. The framework did not predict: (a) that gravitational self-interaction would be the specific mechanism; (b) the critical mass scale; (c) the bifurcation structure (pitchfork-like, with two localized attractors); (d) the role of short-distance repulsion in regularizing the collapse; or (e) the Gaussian ansatz dynamics. The paper's result is *narratable* in framework language — "resolution is the system rolling down the gravitational gradient into one of the available attractor basins" — but the narrative adds nothing to the physics. The framework could equally have narrated the opposite result (gravitational self-interaction does not cause collapse) by saying "the gravitational gradient is too shallow to restructure the attractor landscape." The framework has zero differential predictive content here. The one genuinely interesting alignment is the paper's claim that collapse is *deterministic* (no stochastic element) — this resonates with the framework's treatment of resolution as a computational process, not a random one. But the paper achieves determinism through sensitivity to initial conditions (infinitesimal asymmetries select the branch), which is a chaotic/bifurcation mechanism, not a computational one.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most important strain this cycle comes from Paper 1, and it operates at a level deeper than previously catalogued. The framework's existing tensions about L∞-algebras (T055, T100) concerned the *derivation* of conservation laws and phase space from algebraic structure — structure upstream of dynamics. Paper 1 demonstrates something worse: two physically inequivalent quantum field theories — with different UV behavior, different spectral decompositions, different mixing properties — generated from the *same* classical starting point by choosing different L∞-algebraic structures (braided vs. classical). This is not "algebraic structure determines dynamics" (already known from T055/T059); it is "algebraic structure *selects between* dynamics," producing a new type of underdetermination that is qualitatively different from all previously catalogued types.

The existing underdetermination hierarchy (T069: number field ℝ/ℂ; T084: propagator power γ; T082: virtual mode distribution; T113: operator ordering) involves choices that produce the *same* on-shell physics or affect only unobservable sectors. Paper 1's underdetermination produces *different physical predictions* (logarithmic divergences vs. UV finiteness with periodic non-analyticity) from an algebraic choice the framework cannot express, on a spacetime whose noncommutative structure is precisely what the framework's Tick primitive would suggest reality should look like at the Planck scale. If the substrate implements discrete computation (the Tick's claim), and if this discreteness manifests as spacetime noncommutativity (a standard expectation in quantum gravity), then Paper 1 proves that the framework's own natural spacetime admits multiple inequivalent quantizations distinguished by algebraic structure the framework has no primitive for. The framework's "reality is literally computation" collapses into "reality is literally one of at least two computations on the same substrate, and we cannot specify which, because the distinguishing feature is algebraic."

Paper 2 introduces a different and independently severe strain: the Resolution primitive's inability to distinguish genuine quantum correlations from spurious statistical correlations. The Simpson's paradox identification means that a subset of "resolved correlations" — those arising from classical states — are not features of the pre-resolution quantum state but artifacts of confounding ensemble averaging with quantum averaging. The framework treats all post-resolution correlations as evidence about pre-resolution structure. Paper 2 proves this is wrong for classical states. The framework needs a primitive or mechanism for distinguishing quantum from statistical correlations, and it has none — its Resolution primitive is agnostic about the source of the correlations it produces.
