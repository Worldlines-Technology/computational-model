### PAPERS PROCESSED

**Paper 1: Non-BPS Monopoles and Dyons via Resurgent Transseries**
ArXiv ID: 2602.17583v1 | Selection type: Strain

This paper constructs non-BPS monopoles and dyons as resurgent transseries — infinite sums of exponentially decaying terms with factorially divergent fluctuation factors, where all higher-order terms are expressible in terms of leading-order solutions. Through the Worldlines lens, the key tension is structural. The framework treats the universe as literal discrete computation with a finite tick. But resurgent transseries encode an infinite hierarchy of non-perturbative corrections — an unbounded tower of exponentially suppressed sectors, each requiring infinite perturbative series — that are needed to fully specify even a single classical soliton solution. This is not a perturbative convenience; the resurgence structure is physically meaningful, encoding information about the non-perturbative sector that is invisible to any finite truncation. The framework's "Conservation of Computation" primitive implies computational content is finite and conserved, yet a single monopole's complete specification requires a transseries with infinitely many terms. Either the framework must argue that physical reality only ever instantiates finite truncations of these transseries (and the infinite tail is mathematical artifact), or it must grapple with the fact that a single localized object in quantum field theory carries, in principle, infinite computational specification. The BPS limit is notable: there, all fluctuation terms truncate, meaning the solution becomes finitely specified. The framework would be more comfortable with BPS objects (finite computation) than non-BPS ones (infinite specification), but the physical universe is full of non-BPS objects. This is a genuine strain on the claim that reality is discrete finite computation.

**Paper 2: Pseudo-deterministic Quantum Algorithms**
ArXiv ID: 2602.17647v1 | Selection type: Strain

This paper initiates a systematic study of pseudo-deterministic quantum algorithms — quantum algorithms that output a *canonical* (unique) answer with high probability despite using quantum resources. The key results include: (a) there exist problems where classical randomized computation is O(1) but pseudo-deterministic quantum computation requires Ω(N) queries (AOES problem), and (b) there exist problems where pseudo-deterministic quantum algorithms exponentially outperform classical pseudo-deterministic ones. Through the Worldlines lens, this creates a nuanced and uncomfortable picture. The framework treats quantum mechanics as "unresolved computation" that resolves into definite states. Pseudo-deterministic quantum algorithms are precisely algorithms where quantum superposition (unresolved computation) is used to reliably produce a *single definite* output — a canonical resolution. The AOES result is the strain point: it shows that demanding canonical resolution from quantum computation can be *maximally expensive* (Ω(N)) even when not demanding canonicity makes the problem trivially easy (O(1) classically). This directly pressures the Resolution primitive. Resolution — the transition from unresolved to definite — is not computationally free; in fact, demanding a specific *canonical* resolved state can cost enormously more than accepting any valid resolution. The framework currently treats resolution as something that "happens" without computational cost accounting. But this paper demonstrates that the cost structure of resolution (specifically, of ensuring a unique resolution) is a rich and non-trivial computational landscape. The quintic bound (D(R) = Õ(psQ(R)^5)) also suggests resolution-forcing has a structured polynomial relationship to deterministic computation, not an arbitrary one.

**Paper 3: Planckian bound on the local equilibration time**
ArXiv ID: 2602.17638v1 | Selection type: Validation

This paper rigorously establishes that the local equilibration time — the timescale at which hydrodynamic (resolved, classical) behavior emerges from quantum many-body dynamics — is bounded below by α·ℏ/T, where α depends only on dimensionality and the type of hydrodynamic behavior, not on microscopic details. Through the Worldlines lens, this is a genuine (partial) validation of the framework's architecture, but with important caveats. The framework's Tick primitive posits a maximum propagation rate (c) and the Resolution primitive posits that definite macroscopic states emerge from unresolved quantum computation. This paper provides a rigorous floor for how fast resolution can occur: ℏ/T is the minimum multi-tick duration required for local resolution, and it depends on macroscopic (thermodynamic) parameters and spatial dimensionality, not on microscopic coupling details. This is exactly what the framework would predict — resolution is a multi-tick emergent process with a timescale set by the computational "temperature budget" of the substrate region, not by microphysical details. However, the framework did not *predict* this bound; it was already conjectured in the condensed matter and holographic communities. The framework's honest contribution is structural: it provides a natural *reason* for the bound (resolution is a computation that requires a minimum number of ticks determined by the available computational throughput, which scales with temperature). The dependence of α on dimensionality alone, not on whether quasiparticles exist or what the scattering mechanism is, strongly supports the idea that equilibration is an architectural feature of the computational substrate, not a contingent property of any particular dynamics. This is post-hoc consistency rather than novel prediction, but it is *tight* post-hoc consistency — the framework's structure maps onto the result without forcing.

### FRAMEWORK STRAINS

The most important strain this cycle comes from **Paper 1's resurgent transseries structure**. The framework claims reality is literal computation, with finite conservation laws and discrete ticks. But the complete specification of a single non-BPS monopole requires an infinite resurgent transseries — not as mathematical convenience but as physical necessity, since each exponential sector encodes real non-perturbative physics (instanton-anti-instanton contributions, etc.) and the resurgence relations connecting different sectors carry physical meaning about the analytic structure of the theory. The framework faces a dilemma:

**Option A:** Physical reality only ever instantiates finite truncations, and the infinite tail is unphysical mathematical structure. But resurgence theory demonstrates that the infinite tail is precisely what makes the perturbative series well-defined — without it, the perturbative sector is ambiguous (Stokes phenomena). The sectors are not independent; they are linked by exact resurgence relations. Truncating destroys the mathematical consistency that gives the leading terms their meaning.

**Option B:** The computational content of a single soliton is genuinely infinite, which contradicts Conservation of Computation as stated (finite computation cannot encode infinite specification).

**Option C:** The "infinite specification" is compressible — because all higher-order terms are expressed in terms of leading-order solutions, the actual algorithmic information content is finite (the generating rule is finite even if its output is infinite). This is the most promising escape route but requires the framework to distinguish between *computational content* and *generated output*, which it does not currently do.

This is not a soft tension. It goes to the heart of what "computation" means in the framework. If the universe is a computer, what is its word length? Can a finite computation generate and "use" an infinite series, or must it truncate? The BPS limit — where the transseries truncates to finite terms — being the *special* case rather than the generic one makes this worse: the generic physical objects in quantum field theory appear to require infinite specification.

### STATE UPDATES

1. **Resolution primitive — add cost structure.** Paper 2 demonstrates that resolution (demanding a canonical definite outcome from quantum computation) has a non-trivial computational cost that can be *maximally expensive* relative to accepting any valid outcome. The Resolution primitive should be updated to note: resolution is not computationally free. The cost of canonical resolution can vastly exceed the cost of non-canonical (any-outcome-acceptable) computation. The quintic bound psQ(R)^5 ≥ D(R) suggests resolution cost has structured polynomial relationships to deterministic computation. This means resolution is not merely "what happens" — it is a computational process with its own resource accounting.

2. **Conservation of Computation — note compression vs. generation distinction.** The resurgent transseries result (Paper 1) requires the framework to distinguish between the *algorithmic information content* of a physical state (which may be finite — the generating rule) and the *generated structure* (which may be infinite — the full transseries). Conservation of Computation should be understood as conserving the algorithmic/Kolmogorov complexity, not the extensional output. This is a non-trivial refinement: it means the universe may "contain" infinite structure that is finitely specified.

3. **Tick-Resolution relationship — formalize with Planckian bound.** Paper 3's result should be incorporated: the minimum resolution time at temperature T is τ_eq ≥ α·ℏ/T, where α depends on dimensionality and hydrodynamic type. This gives the tick-resolution gap (T005) a concrete quantitative anchor. Resolution requires at minimum ℏ/(T · τ_tick) ticks, where τ_tick is the Planck time. At temperature T, this is kT/(E_Planck) ticks minimum — an astronomically large number at ordinary temperatures, confirming that resolution is a deeply multi-tick process.

### NEW TENSIONS

**T008 — Infinite specification from finite computation**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 1 demonstrates that non-BPS monopoles and dyons require resurgent transseries for their complete physical specification — an infinite tower of exponentially suppressed sectors linked by exact resurgence relations. The framework claims reality is finite computation with conserved computational content. If a single soliton requires infinite specification, either: (a) physical reality truncates the series at some order (but resurgence shows the sectors are mutually dependent), (b) the computational content is actually infinite (violating finiteness), or (c) the algorithmic complexity is finite because all terms are generated from leading-order data (requiring the framework to adopt a Kolmogorov-complexity view of "computation" rather than an extensional one). Option (c) is the most promising but introduces a new distinction the framework doesn't currently make: between the finitely specifiable generating rule and its potentially infinite output. This also raises the question of whether the universe "evaluates" the full series or only computes it to needed precision — which reintroduces a resolution/truncation question at the level of field theory itself.
**Why unresolved:** The framework lacks a theory of how computational content relates to generated mathematical structure. Is a system that is finitely specifiable but infinitely generative "finite computation" or not? This is not merely philosophical — it determines whether Conservation of Computation is a constraint on generating rules or on outputs.

**T009 — Resolution has non-trivial cost structure**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 2 shows that demanding canonical (unique, deterministic) output from quantum computation can be maximally expensive (Ω(N)) even when the underlying problem is classically trivial (O(1)). The framework's Resolution primitive currently describes resolution as an event that "happens" — a state becomes definite. But pseudo-deterministic quantum complexity reveals that *which* definite state emerges, and whether it is canonical, has profound computational cost implications. The framework needs to account for the fact that resolution is not just irreversible state-fixing but a computational process whose cost depends on whether the system is forced to a canonical outcome or merely any consistent outcome. This connects to the measurement problem: what determines which resolution occurs, and at what cost?
**Why unresolved:** The framework has no resource accounting for resolution. It says resolution is irreversible but not what it costs. Incorporating cost structure requires specifying: cost in what currency? Measured by whom? Against what baseline? The pseudo-deterministic complexity results provide a formal framework for this in the query model, but translating query complexity into physical resolution cost is non-trivial.
