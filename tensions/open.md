**T005 — The tick-resolution gap**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 1 (Planckian bound) establishes that the minimum time for local equilibration — the emergence of resolved hydrodynamic behavior — scales as ℏ/T, depends on dimensionality, and is independent of microscopic details. The framework currently conflates the tick (maximum propagation rate) with resolution (states becoming definite). But if resolution requires a temperature-dependent, system-structure-dependent number of ticks, then the tick and resolution are distinct processes with different timescales. The framework needs a theory of how resolution unfolds *across* ticks — a dynamics of resolution itself. Without this, the Tick and Resolution primitives are underspecified in their relationship to each other.
**Why unresolved:** The framework has no internal account of multi-tick resolution dynamics. Adding one requires specifying what happens during the intermediate ticks — are states partially resolved? Is there a continuous resolution parameter? This risks undermining the discrete character of the tick.

**T008 — Infinite specification from finite computation**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 1 demonstrates that non-BPS monopoles and dyons require resurgent transseries for their complete physical specification — an infinite tower of exponentially suppressed sectors linked by exact resurgence relations. The framework claims reality is finite computation with conserved computational content. If a single soliton requires infinite specification, either: (a) physical reality truncates the series at some order (but resurgence shows the sectors are mutually dependent), (b) the computational content is actually infinite (violating finiteness), or (c) the algorithmic complexity is finite because all terms are generated from leading-order data (requiring the framework to adopt a Kolmogorov-complexity view of "computation" rather than an extensional one). Option (c) is the most promising but introduces a new distinction the framework doesn't currently make: between the finitely specifiable generating rule and its potentially infinite output. This also raises the question of whether the universe "evaluates" the full series or only computes it to needed precision — which reintroduces a resolution/truncation question at the level of field theory itself.
**Why unresolved:** The framework lacks a theory of how computational content relates to generated mathematical structure. Is a system that is finitely specifiable but infinitely generative "finite computation" or not? This is not merely philosophical — it determines whether Conservation of Computation is a constraint on generating rules or on outputs.

**T009 — Resolution has non-trivial cost structure**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 2 shows that demanding canonical (unique, deterministic) output from quantum computation can be maximally expensive (Ω(N)) even when the underlying problem is classically trivial (O(1)). The framework's Resolution primitive currently describes resolution as an event that "happens" — a state becomes definite. But pseudo-deterministic quantum complexity reveals that *which* definite state emerges, and whether it is canonical, has profound computational cost implications. The framework needs to account for the fact that resolution is not just irreversible state-fixing but a computational process whose cost depends on whether the system is forced to a canonical outcome or merely any consistent outcome. This connects to the measurement problem: what determines which resolution occurs, and at what cost?
**Why unresolved:** The framework has no resource accounting for resolution. It says resolution is irreversible but not what it costs. Incorporating cost structure requires specifying: cost in what currency? Measured by whom? Against what baseline? The pseudo-deterministic complexity results provide a formal framework for this in the query model, but translating query complexity into physical resolution cost is non-trivial.

**T010 — The duality problem: which computation is reality?**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** The amplitude/Wilson-loop duality in 𝒩=4 SYM (Paper 1) demonstrates that the same physical observables can be computed from radically different computational architectures — local scattering vertices vs. holonomy integrals over closed loops — in different mathematical spaces. The framework claims "reality is literally computation," but duality means the physical content is invariant under exchange of completely different computational descriptions. If reality *is* computation, it should have a definite computational structure. Duality says the structure is not unique. Either: (a) reality is the equivalence class of dual computations rather than any single computation (but what is an equivalence class ontologically?), (b) there is a preferred computation and the dual description is merely a calculational convenience (but the framework provides no criterion for choosing), or (c) the framework's identification of reality with computation is too literal and needs to be weakened to "reality is *described by* computation" (which significantly dilutes the framework's core claim). This tension is distinct from gauge redundancy because the dual descriptions involve fundamentally different objects and spaces, not just reparameterizations of the same objects.
**Why unresolved:** The framework has no account of duality. Its five primitives describe the dynamics of a single computation but say nothing about whether or why equivalent computational descriptions should exist. Resolving this would require either identifying the invariant structure that all dual descriptions share (which might be the true "computation") or modifying the core claim about reality being computation.

This tension is sharpest in its implication for Conservation of Computation: if the same physical constraints can arise from graph topology without invoking information conservation, then Conservation of Computation may be less fundamental than the framework claims.
**Why unresolved:** Determining whether planarity and unitarity are equivalent constraints, or whether planarity is independent and more primitive, requires a formal analysis that the framework currently cannot perform. This is also currently specific to planar 𝒩=4 SYM and may not generalize, but the existence of even one case where topology does unitarity's work is significant.

**T014 — Permanently virtual computational structure**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 2 shows that ghost modes in quadratic gravity can be quantized such that they never appear as free asymptotic states — their creation/annihilation operators commute, so they propagate only virtually. The framework's Resolution primitive assumes all computational states are at least potentially resolvable. Permanently virtual structure — computation that participates in intermediate processes but is architecturally forbidden from resolving — is a new category. The ghost modes' commuting statistics ([a, a†] = 0) are neither bosonic nor fermionic, meaning they don't fit the framework's existing categories of resolved states. Conservation of Computation (unitarity) is preserved precisely *because* these modes never resolve, but the framework has no account of what permanent virtuality means ontologically. Is permanently virtual structure "real" computation? Does it carry computational content that must be conserved? Or is it bookkeeping that the substrate uses internally but that has no resolution-level reality?
**Why unresolved:** The framework identifies reality with computation and computation with states that resolve. Structure that computes but never resolves challenges this identification. If virtual structure is "real," the framework's ontology is broader than resolution. If virtual structure is not "real," then Conservation of Computation doesn't apply to it and the framework needs to specify which computation is conserved and which is not. Either option requires significant conceptual work.

**T016 — Geometric encoding vs. computational ontology**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 1 (The Universe Fan) demonstrates that the cosmological wavefunction is determined by a polyhedral fan — a static combinatorial-geometric object defined by positivity conditions on a lattice. The physical content (singularity structure, factorization, residues = matroid amplitudes) is encoded in the face lattice and cone structure without invoking temporal evolution or sequential computation. The framework claims "reality is literally computation," but the universe fan suggests the physical content may be more naturally understood as geometry than as computation. This is related to but distinct from T010 (which concerns dual computational descriptions) and T011 (which concerns planarity as a structural constraint). T016 concerns a more fundamental issue: whether the ontological category "computation" is the right one, or whether "geometric structure defined by positivity/causality" is a competing ontological category that the framework has no grounds for dismissing. The cosmological polytopes of Arkani-Hamed et al., the amplituhedron, and now the universe fan all point toward physics being encoded in geometric objects whose structure is defined by positivity rather than by dynamical evolution.
**Why unresolved:** The framework could attempt to absorb this by arguing that geometric structure *is* a form of computation, but this risks trivializing the computational claim. Alternatively, it could argue that the geometric encoding is a mathematical convenience while the physical reality is computational, but it has no criterion for distinguishing "mathematical convenience" from "ontological ground." The tension cannot be resolved without either (a) a principled argument for why computation is ontologically privileged over geometry, or (b) a reconceptualization of the framework's core claim to accommodate geometric structure as foundational.

Option (c) would significantly limit the primitive's scope.

**T018 — The algebra of computation**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Papers 1 and 2 jointly reveal that computation has rich algebraic structure — decomposition into master integrals via IBP, hierarchical organization of quantum gates via Pauli periodicity — that is not captured by any of the five primitives. The framework describes the *dynamics* of computation (propagation, gradient descent, attractor capture, resolution, conservation) but not its *algebra* (how computational operations compose, decompose, and organize into hierarchies). IBP relations show that the space of field-theoretic computations has a finite-dimensional basis; the Clifford hierarchy shows that quantum operations form a rigid infinite hierarchy with exponential cost barriers between levels. These are fundamental organizational features of computation that the framework ignores. This is distinct from T010 (duality) and T011 (planarity) because it concerns not alternative descriptions of the same physics or topological constraints, but the internal compositional and hierarchical structure of the computational operations themselves.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need either (a) a sixth primitive describing algebraic/compositional structure, (b) a demonstration that algebraic structure emerges from the existing five primitives in a non-trivial way, or (c) an argument for why algebraic structure is not ontologically fundamental but merely a mathematical convenience. None of these has been attempted.

**T019 — Resolution precision hierarchy**

Level 1 (Pauli measurements) represents the coarsest resolution; each subsequent level enables finer angular precision in state manipulation. The framework's Resolution primitive treats resolution as binary (unresolved → definite) and makes no distinction between different kinds or precisions of resolution. The controlled-jump rule shows that climbing the resolution hierarchy by one level requires coherent control and that the target-qubit cost grows exponentially with the desired level. This connects to T009 (resolution cost structure) but is more specific: T009 notes that resolution has costs; T019 asserts that resolution has a hierarchically organized internal structure with quantifiable resource requirements at each level.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to either (a) incorporate a notion of "resolution depth" or "resolution precision" into the Resolution primitive, specifying that resolution comes in levels with different costs and capabilities, or (b) argue that the Clifford hierarchy is a feature of quantum computational models that does not map onto physical resolution. Option (b) seems difficult given that the Clifford hierarchy directly governs what measurements and transformations are physically implementable.

**T021 — Arithmetic-geometric unity and the poverty of "computation"**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 1 demonstrates that the zeta function of a Calabi-Yau variety encodes topological invariants (via Weil conjectures) through arithmetic over finite fields, with the connection mediated by cohomology (Monsky-Washnitzer) and geometric operations (crepant resolution). This instantiates a pattern seen throughout modern mathematical physics: the same invariant content is accessible through categorically different mathematical frameworks — arithmetic, algebraic geometry, topology, combinatorics — that are provably equivalent but conceptually distinct. The framework claims reality is "literally computation," privileging one mathematical category as ontologically fundamental. But arithmetic-geometric correspondences (Weil conjectures, Langlands program, mirror symmetry) suggest that the deep structure of mathematical physics lives in the *relationships between* categories, not in any single category. The framework provides no justification for privileging computation over arithmetic, geometry, or topology, and the existence of categorical equivalences suggests that the invariant content — which is what physics cares about — is not inherently computational.
**Why unresolved:** This is deeper than T010 (duality between computational descriptions) or T016 (geometric encoding vs. computational ontology). Those tensions concern specific alternative descriptions of specific physical systems. T021 concerns the observation that the entire edifice of mathematical physics is characterized by inter-categorical equivalences, and asks whether "computation" names anything ontologically distinctive or is merely one of many equivalent windows into the same structure. Resolving this would require either (a) demonstrating that computation is the unique category from which all others can be derived (which would be an extraordinary mathematical claim), (b) identifying what specific feature of computation earns it ontological privilege, or (c) abandoning the claim that reality is literally computation in favor of a more category-neutral ontological claim.

**T022 — Spectral universality as a constraint the framework cannot derive**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 2 derives a universal relationship between temporal and spatial spectral indices for Lorentz-homogeneous stochastic fields, with the offset determined purely by spacetime dimensionality and Lorentz symmetry. This is a structural constraint on the statistical properties of fields that follows from symmetry alone. The framework's Field primitive describes dynamics (ẋ = −∇φ) but not the statistical/spectral properties of fields. The Tick primitive posits discrete updates and finite propagation speed, which should in principle constrain spectral structure, but the framework cannot derive the specific universal offset or the no-go theorem for local spectral mappings. More fundamentally, the result shows that Lorentz symmetry — which in the framework is a consequence of the tick rate being the maximum propagation speed — has non-trivial implications for the spectral structure of stochastic fields that the framework never anticipated and currently cannot reproduce. If the framework's "tick rate = speed of light" is correct, it should be possible to derive these spectral constraints from the discrete tick structure, but the framework lacks the formalism to do so.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to develop a theory of stochastic fields on its discrete substrate and show that the spectral universality emerges from the tick structure + Lorentz invariance. This is in principle possible but has not been attempted. Furthermore, the no-go result (no covariant local spectral mapping in d > 1) might constrain the framework's Field primitive: if temporal and spatial field structures cannot be locally mapped, the framework's treatment of fields as having unified gradients ∇φ that combine spatial and temporal derivatives may be too naive.

**T023 — Kinetic arrest as a distinct dynamical category**
**Opened:** this cycle
**Description:** Paper 3 demonstrates that a disorder-free, short-range interacting system can exhibit dynamical arrest with exponentially long-lived athermal plateaus. The arrest mechanism — composite excitations creating kinetic barriers in an enlarged Hilbert space — represents a dynamical phenomenon that is distinct from both attractor capture (the system is not in a minimum) and resolution dynamics (the system is classical, so quantum resolution is not involved). The framework currently has three categories for stable states: energy attractors, constrained attractors, and driven attractors. Kinetically arrested states don't fit cleanly into any of these: they are not true attractors (the system would eventually relax), they are not constrained by conservation laws or integrability (the ice rules are not symmetries in the traditional sense), and they are not driven (no external energy input). They represent a fourth category: states that are metastable for exponentially long times due to the structure of the dynamical landscape (barriers in configuration space) rather than the structure of the energy landscape (minima). The framework's gradient equation ẋ = −∇φ(x) would describe the system as slowly rolling down a gradient, but the exponential timescale is set by barrier structure, not gradient magnitude, and the framework has no account of barrier-dominated dynamics as distinct from gradient-dominated dynamics.
**Why unresolved:** The framework's "everything moves down gradients" claim technically accommodates exponentially slow motion (small gradients), but this misses the physical content: the dynamics are barrier-dominated, not gradient-dominated. The exponential timescale comes from the Arrhenius-like need for activated processes, which is qualitatively different from slow gradient descent. The framework would need to distinguish between gradient-dominated dynamics (smooth descent) and barrier-dominated dynamics (arrest punctuated by activated transitions) as physically distinct regimes, which the universal gradient equation does not do.

**T026 — The structural content gap**
**Description:** Multiple independent tensions (T010 duality, T011 planarity, T016 geometric encoding, T018 algebra of computation, T021 arithmetic-geometric unity, T022 spectral universality, T024 temporal-spatial spectral gap) are converging on a single meta-problem: the framework's five primitives are all *dynamical* — they describe how computation propagates (Tick), how things move (Field), where things settle (Attractor), when things become definite (Resolution), and what is conserved (Conservation). But physics also has *structural* content: categorical equivalences (arithmetic ↔ geometry ↔ topology), spectral universalities (Lorentz-covariant spectral offsets), algebraic hierarchies (Clifford hierarchy, IBP decomposition), and topological constraints (planarity, indistinguishability graphs). The framework has no primitive that captures structural content. This is not the same as adding a sixth primitive — it may require reconceiving the framework's ontological basis. If reality has both dynamical and structural aspects, and the framework captures only the dynamical, it is at best half a theory.
**Why unresolved:** This is a meta-tension that subsumes several existing tensions but is larger than any of them. Resolving it would require either (a) demonstrating that structural content emerges from the five dynamical primitives (which seems unlikely given that the no-go theorem for spectral mappings is a structural result the primitives cannot even express), (b) adding structural primitives to the framework (which would be a major extension), or (c) reconceiving the framework's ontological basis so that structure and dynamics are unified at a deeper level. None of these has been attempted.

**T027 — Cohomological mediation and the framework's silence on mathematical depth**

The framework has no account of why cohomological structures should play this mediating role, no primitive that corresponds to cohomological content, and no mechanism for explaining the existence of cohomological equivalences. This is distinct from T021 (which notes the existence of inter-categorical correspondences) because T027 asks specifically about the *mechanism* of mediation — why cohomology is the universal translator between mathematical categories — and whether the framework's silence on this indicates a fundamental blind spot.
**Why unresolved:** The framework would need to either explain cohomological structure as emerging from its computational ontology or acknowledge that the mathematical depth of physics includes a cohomological layer that the framework cannot currently address. This is potentially the deepest version of the ontological pluralism problem: not just "which category is reality?" but "what makes categories equivalent, and why does physics care?"

**T028 — Resolution is contextual and computationally intractable**
**Description:** Paper 1 proves that satisfiability over Kochen-Specker partial Boolean algebras is NP-complete, satisfiability over projectors on physical Hilbert spaces is ∃ℝ-complete, and satisfiability over all Hilbert spaces is undecidable.
